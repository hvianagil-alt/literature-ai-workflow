# Form card — nanomedicine / drug delivery (narrative)

**Kind:** narrative technology-and-constraint review.  
**Worked example (form only):** `review/runs/2026-09-19-nanocarriers/article.md`.  
**Do not copy formulations, log reductions, or trial arms from that article into a new topic.**

## What this field’s reviews usually do

Open on the biological problem (why a free drug fails), then the **constraint** the papers actually test (release trigger, coat, mucosa, biofilm), not a catalogue of every nanoparticle type. Clinical or in-vivo papers stay ranked above formulation-only papers. A coat that “arrives” is not the same heading as a coat that **releases**.

## Example heading *shapes* (replace with this sample’s constructs)

- Why a trigger or barrier exists.
- One comparison the sample can actually support (e.g. two membrane strategies) — not an encyclopaedia of materials.
- The one clinical or late-stage case, in its own section, without inflating it.
- Surface chemistry / charge / route (GI, mucosa) as constraints.
- Combination with a second agent only if measured.
- Delivery to a difficult niche (mucosa, biofilm) if measured.
- Discussion: arrival ≠ function; what was not compared.

## First-try traps in this field

- One section per paper (“the liposome paper”, “the AgNP paper”).
- Treating mouse EPR as a human guarantee.
- Mixing antimicrobial silver papers with oncology liposomes as if they were one efficacy class.
- Skipping physiology the later sections assume (endosome, mucus, pH).
