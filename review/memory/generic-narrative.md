# Form card — generic narrative review

**Kind:** journal-style narrative review with a short Methods section.  
**Source of form:** `review/runs/2026-09-19-review-craft-study/structure-notes.md` and the fictional `examples/sample-article.md`.  
**Do not cite this card as science.**

## Title

Phenomenon first, then a colon and the kind or angle (`X: a narrative review of Y`). About 8–25 words. Not *Recent Advances*, not a database export, not a list of papers.

## Front matter

Unstructured Abstract (topic map; no `[1]`; no named papers). Keywords (four or more topic phrases). Optional Key Summary Points (claim plus evidence grade).

## Spine

1. Introduction — present-tense problem; teach what later sections assume; aim in the **last** paragraph.
2. Methods — search, eligibility, selection. Brief. How papers were found lives **only** here.
3. Numbered thematic sections — arguments or constructs, never author names, never a generic `Results` dump.
4. Discussion — interpretation, not a second Results.
5. Conclusions — next measurements in running sentences, not First/Second/Third.
6. References — Vancouver first-appearance; one reference per paragraph.

## Introduction must teach

A reader expert in a **neighbouring** field (not this subfield) should follow §§3–N after the Introduction alone: the clinical or biological problem, what current options already do and still fail, the tools later sections assume, the live controversy.

## Paragraphs

Claim first, then evidence, then contrast, then why, then what that heading cannot show. Rank design in the sentence (trial vs observational vs animal vs review). Mechanisms: condition → effect → measurement, graded (demonstrated / consistent with / proposed).

**Condition clusters (any field):** do not present results as one study and one percentage. Gather similar experiments (same endpoint family) and name why they agree or differ — population, cells or strain, dose, endpoint, geography, statistics. Cite two or more papers in that paragraph when they share a question. A last-line “Taken together” does not repair a dump.

## Tables

Numbered Markdown tables in the article. Mention “Table 1” from the prose. Do not paste the extraction worksheet.

## What this card cannot do

It cannot tell you the headings of an oncology or microbiome review. For an unseen field, copy this spine, then take **topic names** from included reviews in the current sample (`field-structure-benchmark`). If those reviews organise by matrix or by technology and your question is one process and its coefficients, a question-driven spine is allowed — record that as a justified departure.
