# Form card — neurodegeneration / cannabinoid neurology (narrative)

**Kind:** narrative clinical-and-mechanism review.  
**Worked example (form only):** `review/runs/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/article.md`.  
**Do not copy trial arms, receptor occupancy numbers, or plant-product claims from that article into a new topic.**

## What this field’s reviews usually do

Open on the disease physiology (what is lost, what usual care already replaces). Split **motor** endpoints from **non-motor** endpoints from **preclinical receptor biology**. Keep patient-use surveys from being written as efficacy. Discussion says occupancy ≠ approval.

## Example heading *shapes* (replace with this sample’s constructs)

- Introduction: native circuits → licensed replacement → a neighbouring symptom cluster → receptor classes later sections assume → aim.
- Methods.
- Human outcome evidence, ranked (placebo-controlled vs observational vs clinic synthesis).
- Receptor imaging or occupancy — only if the sample measured it, and not as a surrogate for motor benefit.
- Preclinical models in their own section.
- Discussion: what cannot be pooled; what patients already do versus what trials showed.

## Title shape in published OA reviews (form only)

In the form-model sample, Parkinson/cannabinoid reviews almost always use a **colon subtitle** and name the kind (`a narrative review`, `a systematic review and meta-analysis`). Do not copy their indications.

## First-try traps in this field

- Treating plant cannabis use prevalence as motor efficacy.
- Pooling sleep or pain signals with examiner-rated motor scales.
- Writing a CB1 occupancy paper as if it were a disease-modifying trial.
- Abstract that names a product or stacks n and p from several studies.
