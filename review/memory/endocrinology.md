# Form card — endocrinology / metabolic disease (narrative)

**Kind:** narrative clinical review.  
**Worked example (form only):** `review/runs/2026-09-19-scopus-oa-full/article.md`.  
**Do not copy numbers, drugs, or trial results from that article into a new topic.**

## What this field’s reviews usually do

Teach hormone or pathway physiology in the Introduction (what the analogue is replacing or amplifying). Split **clinical outcomes** from **delivery/engineering** from **extra-target biology**. Keep a protocol or utilisation paper from being written as if it were an outcomes trial. Discussion says what cannot be pooled.

## Example heading *shapes* (replace with this sample’s constructs)

- Introduction with nested teaching (native biology → licensed products → a neighbouring clinical use → extra-target organs → delivery barriers → aim).
- Methods.
- Clinical use (glycaemia / safety / utilisation as separate subsections if the sample actually measured them).
- Non-injectable or locally targeted delivery — only if the sample has it.
- Extra-glycaemic or organ biology — only if the sample has it.
- A mechanistic or regenerative sideline that must **not** be promoted to a pooled outcomes class.
- Discussion; Conclusions.

## First-try traps in this field

- Writing a utilisation or protocol paper as class-wide efficacy.
- Putting animal or wound-healing mechanics in the same table as human HbA1c trials.
- Abstract that names a trial or stacks effect sizes.
- Introduction that assumes the reader already knows incretin physiology.

## Title shape in published OA reviews (form only)

In the in-repo form-model sample, endocrinology review titles usually use a **colon subtitle** and often name the kind (`a narrative review`, `a systematic review`). Do not copy their drugs or endpoints.
