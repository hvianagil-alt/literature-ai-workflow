# Form card — food science / nonthermal processing (narrative)

**Kind:** narrative process review organised by **question**, not by commodity.  
**Worked example (form only):** `review/runs/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/article.md`.  
**Do not copy log reductions, legal criteria, or enzyme names from that article into a new topic.**

## What this field’s reviews often do

Many published narratives catalogue **by matrix** (milk / juice / meat) or **by technology** (HPP, then PEF, then plasma). That is field-typical. A question-driven spine is still valid when the argument is that one set-point is not one safety number — record it as a **justified departure**. Keep legal performance criteria in their own section when the sample has them. Quality endpoints that move against plate counts need their own heading.

## Example heading *shapes* (replace with this sample’s constructs)

- Why the same process setting is not the same kill (coefficients: ice, solute, injury, assay).
- Legal or 5-log performance criteria versus what studies measured.
- Spores / heat-assisted variants / cold-chain hurdles.
- Quality endpoints that do not track microbial counts.
- Neighbouring unit operations only to refuse false equivalence — not as equal heroes.
- Discussion: commercial success ≠ legal pasteurisation.

## First-try traps in this field

- One chapter per food (milk, then juice, then meat) that hides the shared coefficients.
- Equal-time chapters for technologies the sample barely measured.
- Equipment, energy, packaging, or consumer chapters with no measurements — name them as neighbouring gaps, do not invent them.
- A graphical abstract or figure step (this workflow does not produce one).
- Abstract that recites Scopus or open-access fetching.

## Title shape in published OA reviews (form only)

Food-process reviews in the form-model sample mix colon subtitles with kind-named titles. Abstracts that never mark a tension (`yet` / `however` / `do not imply`) are off the published pattern — this workflow now fails that on a full manuscript. Do not copy log reductions from the gold set.
