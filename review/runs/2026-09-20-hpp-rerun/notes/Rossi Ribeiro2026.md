# Enhancing shelf life of pumpkin pulp with natural-based preservative and high pressure processing

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Rossi Ribeiro L, Leichtweis MG, Landgräber E, Silva B, Azevedo M, Pereira C, Carocho M, Barros L, Schlüter O. Front Food Sci Technol. 2026;6:1817509. doi:10.3389/frfst.2026.1817509.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Rossi Ribeiro2026.txt (PDF sidecar also present)
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (HPP 600 MPa/4 min of formulated pumpkin pulp ± natural peel preservative versus thermal pasteurization)

## Research question
Can HPP at 600 MPa for 4 min plus a water-extracted pumpkin-peel natural-based preservative (NBP) keep formulated butternut pumpkin pulp microbiologically stable for 45 days at 5 °C while reducing or replacing potassium sorbate and avoiding the texture and carotenoid damage of a 90 °C thermal process?

## Methods
*Cucurbita moschata* butternut pulp (67% yield after peel/seed removal) blended in Berlin. Five vacuum-packed formulations: A1 pulp + industrial mix without sorbate + HPP; A2 mix with sorbate + pasteurization; A3 mix with sorbate + HPP; A4 mix without sorbate + NBP + HPP; A5 mix with 50% sorbate + NBP + HPP. Mix: natural β-carotene, starch, citric acid, citrate. NBP: freeze-dried peels, water extraction 1 g/40 mL, 84 min, 30 °C, spray-dried with maltodextrin. HPP: 600 MPa, 4 min, ambient temperature (Unipress U4000). Thermal: retort 90 °C for 3 min, 15 min total including heat-up. Storage 5 °C, dark, 45 days; analyses days 0/1, 21, 45. PCA mesophiles 37 °C/48 h; Endo agar Enterobacteriaceae; DRBC moulds/yeasts 30 °C/72 h. pH, L*a*b*, TPA, HPLC-MS/MS carotenoids/γ-tocopherol, Folin TPC, FRAP, proximate composition. Triplicate experiments; two-way ANOVA, Tukey or Tamhane T2, α = 0.05.

## Sample / data
Formulated pumpkin pulp; number of fruit not reported. Microbiological plating in duplicate from serial dilutions. Texture in triplicate. Vitamin extracts pooled from two identical samples per treatment-day before HPLC.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** factorial storage study of HPP versus thermal pasteurization of pumpkin pulp, with/without potassium sorbate and with/without peel NBP
- **Population / model:** butternut squash (*Cucurbita moschata*) pulp plus industrial acid/starch mix; NBP from pumpkin peel
- **n:** experiments performed in triplicate (mean ± SD); fruit n not reported
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 600 MPa/4 min at ambient temperature versus retort 90 °C/3 min (15 min including come-up); sorbate present, 50% reduced, or absent; NBP present or absent
- **Primary endpoint:** mesophiles, Enterobacteriaceae, moulds and yeasts (log/g); pH; L*a*b*; TPA firmness/stickiness/chewiness; β-carotene, lutein, γ-tocopherol (mg/100 g dw); TPC (mg GAE/100 g dw); FRAP (mg AAE/100 g dw)
- **Primary result:** No mesophiles, Enterobacteriaceae, or moulds/yeasts were detected in any of the five formulations throughout 45 days at 5 °C (Microbiology). Raw 100% pulp (no mix/NBP) started at 2.9 log/g mesophiles and 2.3 log/g Enterobacteriaceae. In a previous same-HPP run on ingredient-free pulp, contamination reached 4.7 log/g mesophiles and 3.12 log/g Enterobacteriaceae at days 20/34 and shelf life was 15 days; thermal-only pulp at day 34 reached 4.2, 4.3, and 4.5 log/g mesophiles, Enterobacteriaceae, and moulds/yeasts (Microbiology). Untreated pulp pH 6.5; after mix/NBP/process, pH stayed ~5.0 (≤5.03 and ≥4.93; variation ≤1.9%) (Table 4). Thermal A2 was darker (lower L*) and had much higher firmness (≤361% higher than other samples), chewiness (≤360–493% higher), and stickiness (≤670% higher) than HPP pulps (Tables 5–6). Day-0 β-carotene (mg/100 g dw): A1 150.01 ± 7.45, A2 54.98 ± 7.95, A3 138.01 ± 10.05, A4 123.32 ± 5.33, A5 91.1 ± 5.33; A2 was not detectable at day 45; 45-day β-carotene losses 42.73–66.16% where measurable; lutein losses 27.35–71.98%; γ-tocopherol losses 62.26–84.42% (Table 7). Day-0 TPC (mg GAE/100 g dw): A4 52.49 ± 4.20 and A5 52.55 ± 1.69 versus A1 48.86 ± 1.13, A2 42.52 ± 2.02, A3 38.01 ± 2.61; A5 had the smallest 45-day TPC drop (3.92%) (Table 8). FRAP remained highest in NBP samples A4 and A5 (Figure 1). Proximate macronutrient profiles stayed similar across preservatives and storage (Table 9).
- **Cannot show:** HPP-alone spore inactivation or 45-day stability of 100% pulp without mix/NBP (authors report 15-day shelf life in that prior condition); clinical safety of NBP as a labelled additive beyond this matrix; that HPP without the citric-acid mix would have given zero counts.

## Key findings
- HPP 600 MPa/4 min with the industrial mix (A1) matched sorbate + HPP (A3) and sorbate + heat (A2) for 45-day non-detectable counts, so sorbate was dispensable in this acidified, refrigerated pulp (Microbiology).
- NBP allowed full sorbate replacement (A4) or 50% reduction (A5) without detectable growth (Abstract; Conclusion).
- Quality cost of the *named thermal cycle* (90 °C) versus the *named HPP cycle* (600 MPa/4 min) was texture (starch gelatinization **proposed**) and lower carotenoids, not microbial counts in the formulated product.
- Phenolics from water-extracted peel raised TPC/FRAP but not β-carotene (lipophilic carotenoids not extracted by water; Oxidative analysis).
- Mechanism of microbial control is **combined/not isolated**: HPP + citric acid/citrate pH drop (~6.5 to ~5) + NBP antimicrobial activity previously reported at 10–2.5 mg/mL (cited Leichtweis et al. 2025).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- NBP concentration in the pulp is not stated as mg/mL in the methods excerpt.
- LOD for “no growth” is not given as a log number.
- Significant ST × PT interactions for sugars and fatty acids prevented simple main-effect interpretation (Tables 10–11).
- Thermal process (90 °C/3 min, 15 min total) is more severe than a typical juice pasteurization, so quality contrast may be process-severity as well as heat versus pressure.

## Relevance to our research question
Same 600 MPa/4 min cycle is not the same shelf-life outcome with versus without mix/NBP (15 versus 45 days). Heat is not the kill step in A1/A3/A4/A5, yet A2 heat still uniquely damages texture and carotenoids.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Whether “no growth” means <1 CFU/plate or below a stated LOD.
- Exact potassium sorbate mg/kg in A2/A3 versus A5.
