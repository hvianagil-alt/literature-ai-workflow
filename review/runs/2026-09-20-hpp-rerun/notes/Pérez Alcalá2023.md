# Effect of High Hydrostatic Pressure Processing on the Microbiological Quality and Bacterial Diversity of Sous-Vide-Cooked Cod

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Pérez Alcalá D, Grande Burgos MJ, Rodríguez López J, Lucas R, Gálvez A, Pérez Pulido R. 2023. Foods 12(6):1206. DOI:10.3390/foods12061206. PMID:36981133
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Pérez Alcalá2023.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

When sous-vide cooking of Atlantic cod is too mild to be a full kill step, which HP pressure–time–temperature combinations keep aerobic counts down during refrigeration, and how does community composition shift versus unpressurized sous-vide?

## Methods

Gadus morhua loins (~5.6–5.8 kg fish), 25 g portions plus 8 g pil-pil sauce (olive oil, garlic, chili, skin/head cooked 30 min at 72 °C then emulsified), vacuum −0.95 bar. Trial 1: sous-vide 57.1 °C, 30 min, blast chill; half then 500 MPa, 8 min, 22 °C (SFP system); storage 4 °C to 60 days. Counts on TSA, saline TSA, MacConkey; 37 °C, 24–48 h; LOD <1.30 log CFU/g. Trial 2: sous-vide 52 °C, 20 min; treatments A 300 MPa/8 min/50 °C; B 600 MPa/4 min/50 °C; C 600 MPa/8 min/22 °C; D 600 MPa/8 min/50 °C; storage to 105 days; TSA and MacConkey. Duplicate packages, plated in triplicate (stats n = 6, p < 0.05). Trial 2 homogenates: PMA treatment then 16S V3–V4 Illumina MiSeq (SILVA 132); two biological DNA extracts pooled per treatment × time. No sensory/quality chemistry.

## Sample / data

Sous-vide Atlantic cod in pil-pil sauce (human dish). HTS reads 37,162–129,557 OTUs/sample, mean 76,881. Controls without HP in both trials.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Two sequential storage trials (culture counts); trial 2 adds PMA-16S community profiling.
- **Population / model:** Vacuum-packed Atlantic cod loin in pil-pil sauce; native microbiota (not inoculated pathogens).
- **n:** Duplicate samples plated in triplicate (reported n = 6 for significance). HTS: pooled DNA of two biological replicates per cell.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Sous-vide only vs sous-vide then HP. Trial 1: 57.1 °C/30 min ± 500 MPa/8 min/22 °C. Trial 2: 52 °C/20 min ± A/B/C/D as above.
- **Primary endpoint:** Aerobic mesophiles on TSA (log CFU/g); MacConkey as presumptive Enterobacteriaceae; genus-level relative abundance.
- **Primary result:** Trial 1 controls: TSA 5.63 ± 0.06 log at day 45 and 7.33 ± 0.03 at day 60; HP 500 MPa/8 min: <1.30 through day 45, 4.92 ± 0.03 at day 60. MacConkey <1.30 in all trial 1 samples. Trial 2 controls: 2.30 ± 0.29 (day 0), 5.45 ± 0.06 (day 7), 6.45 ± 0.06 (day 14), 8.73 ± 0.03 (day 30). All HP treatments: TSA <1.30 immediately and again days 7–30; counts reappeared from day 45. Day 60 TSA: A 6.70 ± 0.04; B 4.79 ± 0.06; C 5.17 ± 0.05; D 4.26 ± 0.10. Day 75: A 8.47 ± 0.10; B 5.67 ± 0.06; C 6.54 ± 0.05; D 5.37 ± 0.07. MacConkey never recovered in pressurized trial 2 samples. Authors call 600 MPa/4 min/50 °C (B) the practical optimum for low-temperature sous-vide, though D (600 MPa/8 min/50 °C) was numerically lowest late counts. HTS controls: Proteobacteria dominant; Pseudomonas then Enterobacteriaceae succession; Anoxybacillus early, Carnobacterium late. HP samples: Burkholderiaceae (Cupriavidus, Ralstonia) early; late storage LAB (Enterococcus at 300 MPa; Aerococcus at 600 MPa/8 min/50 °C). Clostridium sensu stricto ≤0.1% in four samples.
- **Cannot show:** Listeria or C. botulinum challenge (authors explicitly request spore challenge); texture/colour/nutrition of pressurized sous-vide; that 500 MPa/8 min after 57.1 °C equals 600 MPa after 52 °C; live/dead beyond PMA; species-level pathogen absence from 16S.

## Key findings

- Measurement: 57.1 °C/30 min + 500 MPa/8 min kept TSA <LOD for 45 days; the same cook without HP exceeded 5 log by day 45 (Table 1).
- Measurement: Dropping cook to 52 °C/20 min let controls hit 5.45 log by day 7; only 600 MPa treatments held TSA <5 log to at least day 45 (Table 2).
- Measurement: 600 MPa at 50 °C beat 600 MPa at 22 °C for delayed recovery (B and D vs C) (Table 2; Discussion).
- Measurement: 300 MPa/8 min/50 °C (A) was clearly weaker (day 60 6.70 log) (Table 2).
- Measurement: HP removed detectable MacConkey growth for the whole storage window (both trials).
- Measurement: Community composition after the same 52 °C cook diverged by HP condition (Enterococcus vs Aerococcus dominance) (Figure 1).
- Author interpretation: Anoxybacillus (mostly A. flavithermus) is a new report for this food and may be a process-environment or gut contaminant (Discussion). Not proven as the spoilage agent.
- Proposed mechanism: mild heat (~50 °C) plus HP destabilizes membranes (cited, not imaged). Germination-then-HP for spores is hypothesized, not measured.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Authors: need C. botulinum endospore challenge; Aerococcus culture/biopreservation follow-up (Discussion).
- No quality (texture, colour, lipid oxidation) endpoints — only microbes.
- HTS pools two replicates; relative abundance ≠ counts.
- TSA at 37 °C under-represents psychrotrophs that spoil fish at 4 °C.
- Pil-pil sauce is a second matrix; HP effect cannot be split between fish and sauce.
- Trial 1 vs 2 used different cook intensities, so HP cycles are not a single factorial.

## Relevance to our research question

Directly answers “named pressure–time is not the same outcome”: 500 MPa/8 min after 57.1 °C/30 min ≠ 300 vs 600 MPa after 52 °C/20 min, and 600 MPa/8 min at 22 °C ≠ 50 °C, for both aerobic counts and late LAB identity in sous-vide cod.
