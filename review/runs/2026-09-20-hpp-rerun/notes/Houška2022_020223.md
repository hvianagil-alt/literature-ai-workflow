# High Pressure Processing Applications in Plant Foods

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Houška M, Silva FVM, Evelyn, Buckow R, Terefe NS, Tonello C. Foods. 2022;11(2):223. doi:10.3390/foods11020223. PMC8774875.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Houška2022_020223.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What do commercial-range HPP (300–600 MPa, typically cold) and heat-assisted HPP (HPTP) do to spoilage/pathogen cells and spores, endogenous enzymes, and quality of fruit and vegetable products, and what industrial equipment exists?

## Methods
Narrative review with an educational/industrial section. Compiles kinetic models (first-order D/z, Weibull, biphasic) for microbes in juices and vegetable products; summarises PME/PG and other enzyme studies; surveys marketed HPP juices, smoothies, and guacamole. Parts upgraded from chapters in a book edited by Houska and Silva.

## Sample / data
Fruit juices/purees (apple, orange, lime concentrate, blackcurrant concentrate, tomato, strawberry) and vegetable juices (carrot, broccoli). Commercial machine limit stated as usually 600 MPa with post-compression T < 45 °C.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** narrative review of HPP/HPTP in plant foods, including industrial products and kinetic tables
- **Population / model:** fruit and vegetable juices, purees, smoothies, guacamole, herbs
- **n:** not applicable
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 300–600 MPa (cold) vs HPTP (HPP + heat, often >60 °C) vs thermal pasteurisation (e.g. orange juice 90–99 °C/15–30 s for PME)
- **Primary endpoint:** microbial D-values or Weibull b,n; residual PME/PG; product quality (cloud, texture)
- **Primary result (compiled):** HPP alone “does not inactivate most microbial spores and enzymes”; HPTP is required (Introduction). *Alicyclobacillus acidoterrestris* spores at 600 MPa/45 °C: D = 8.6 min in 10.6 °Brix apple juice, 19.9 min in 20.2 °Brix lime concentrate, 46.1 min in 30.3 °Brix blackcurrant concentrate (Uchida and Silva). *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* ascospores in orange juice: D ≈ 0.067 min at 500 MPa, zP = 123 MPa (Parish); authors also quote D500MPa ≈ 0.07 min at ≤40 °C. *Bacillus coagulans* 185A in tomato juice, 600 MPa/95 °C, Weibull b = 1.93, n = 0.68. *B. coagulans* ATCC 7050 in tomato pulp, 600 MPa/60 °C, biphasic D = 1.6 and 6.2 min. *Byssochlamys nivea* in strawberry puree 600 MPa/75 °C, Weibull b = 0.29, n = 0.66. *Neosartorya fischeri* in apple juice 600 MPa/75 °C, b = 1.44, n = 0.35. *Eurotium repens* in apple juice 500 MPa/45 °C, biphasic D = 2.0 and 9.0 min; in broccoli juice 500 MPa/45 °C, D = 16 min then a plateau with no further inactivation. *Penicillium expansum* 350 MPa/40 °C: D <1 min then no inactivation. *Bacillus licheniformis* spores in carrot juice: D = 0.70 min at 600 MPa/60 °C. *E. coli* in carrot juice: D = 2.5 min at 600 MPa (no added heat). Estimated D600MPa for *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*: 2 s in orange juice vs 58 s in concentrate; *Lactobacillus brevis* in orange juice D600MPa = 0.12 s. At 350 MPa, vegetative D-values often 0.6–2 min (*L. brevis* and *Zygosaccharomyces bailii* in orange juice D = 0.6 min). Orange PME: 800 MPa/25 °C/1 min left 4% residual activity and cloud stable 2 months; 600 MPa/~20 °C/1 min cut PME ~40% in Navel but not significantly in Valencia. Tomato PG: 60% inactivation at 600 MPa/25 °C/1 min, complete at 800 MPa/25 °C/1 min; PME not inactivated up to 5 min and was 50% activated at 400 MPa/45 °C/5 min. Another tomato study: PG −65%/−82% and PME −22%/−43% at 300/600 MPa, ~30–40 °C, 15 min. Orange PME kinetic example: ~6% inactivation at 600 MPa/30 °C/3 min vs ~93% at 600 MPa/70 °C/3 min.
- **Cannot show:** that cold 600 MPa is a spore kill step; a single PME residual that applies to all orange cultivars; original industrial challenge tests.

## Key findings
- Measurement/modelling: vegetative inactivation in fruit is usually log-linear; spore curves are concave-up with tails (Weibull or biphasic) so extending hold time is a poor substitute for raising P or T (Section 3.1).
- Author interpretation: commercial HPP juices must stay <7 °C because spores survive; molds are recommended pasteurisation targets for cold-distributed fruit HPP/HPTP; aged mold spores were more HPP-resistant than bacterial spores in a cited study.
- Quality: HPP guacamole is cited as keeping avocado green where heat browns it (Section 2.1).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Kinetic studies often used low pressure because 600 MPa kills vegetative cells in seconds, so D600 is extrapolated via zP.
- Enzyme results vary by cultivar (Navel vs Valencia PME).
- Commercial units generally cannot supply heat; Hiperbaric experimental unit 630 MPa / 5–90 °C is an exception.

## Relevance to our research question
Intake related paper from OpenAlex related-to-seeds search (not a post-table gap-fill). Strongest sample statement that **the same 600 MPa is not one safety outcome**: D for *A. acidoterrestris* spores at 600 MPa/45 °C triples from 10.6 to 30.3 °Brix; cold 600 MPa kills vegetative cells in seconds but not spores; PME residual at 600 MPa/1 min differs between orange varieties and needs 70 °C for ~93% inactivation.

## Open questions / things to verify
Food-authority “six log” pasteurisation range of four to eight logs is a review statement, not a new measurement. Table 1 D-value 19.9 vs later prose “20 min” for lime concentrate are the same result rounded.
