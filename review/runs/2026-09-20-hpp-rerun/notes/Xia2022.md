# High Hydrostatic Pressure-Based Combination Strategies for Microbial Inactivation of Food Products: The Cases of Emerging Combination Patterns

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Xia Q, Liu Q, Denoya GI, Yang C, Barba FJ, Yu H, Chen X (2022). Frontiers in Nutrition 9:878904. doi:10.3389/fnut.2022.878904
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Xia2022.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What limitations of single HHP treatments for microbial (especially spore and barotolerant) inactivation in plant-based foods are reported, and which emerging non-thermal combination patterns (HHP + ultrasound, UV photocatalysis, and related hurdles) have been shown to raise inactivation efficiency?

## Methods
Narrative mini-review. No new experiment. Synthesizes published HHP combination studies, with Table 1 compiling pressure-assisted thermal processing, pressure ohmic thermal sterilization, HPP+ultrasound, and other hurdles on fruit/vegetable matrices.

## Sample / data
Not a primary dataset. Cited examples include table olives, brined asparagus, juices, purees. Table 1 rows are secondary reports.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative mini-review of HHP combination strategies (not a systematic review; no PRISMA counts).
- **Population / model:** Plant-based foods (juices, purees, olives, asparagus) and associated vegetative cells/spores as reported in cited studies.
- **n:** not applicable (review); inactivation numbers below are as cited, not generated here.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Single HHP vs HHP combined with heat, ultrasound/thermosonication, ohmic heating, UV/TiO2 photocatalysis, and other hurdles.
- **Primary endpoint:** Reported log reductions or residual survivors after named combination cycles (various units, matrices, organisms).
- **Primary result:** Authors state single room-temperature HHP inactivates vegetative spoilers but is limited for Gram-positive organisms and spores, with matrix- and physiology-dependent baroresistance and possible regrowth of injured cells. Cited examples: 300 MPa/5 min on Manzanilla Aloreña seasoned table olives used to control yeasts, yet residual bacteria after 600 or 700 MPa attributed to endospore-formers; 600 MPa/8 min on brined asparagus shifted community structure (lower Enterobacteriaceae relative abundance). Table 1 cited results include: PATP (preheat 98 °C/5 min then 600 MPa in a 90 °C vessel/5 min) on pumpkin, squash, pea, beetroot, purple potato purees—no detectable aerobic/anaerobic microbes, 9 log reduction of *B. amyloliquefaciens* spores; 600 MPa/10 min/75 °C apple juice—3.3 log *Neosartorya fischeri* ascospores; 600 MPa/15 min/60 °C tomato pulp—5.7 log *Bacillus coagulans*; 600 MPa/10 min/75 °C strawberry puree—1.4 log *Byssochlamys nivea* ascospores; POTS 105 °C at 600 MPa, 0–5 min, 50 V/cm—3.1 log *B. amyloliquefaciens* and 4.8 log *G. stearothermophilus* spores in tomato juice (pH 4.1); HPP 600 MPa/15 min then thermosonication 20.20 W/mL, 78 °C, 60 min—4.4 log *Alicyclobacillus acidoterrestris* in orange juice; 450 MPa/10 min plus 25 kHz, 70% amplitude, 60 min, 2 W/cm^2—complete inactivation of natural microflora in apple juice. Authors argue synergy can avoid higher HHP levels and that PATP heat may compromise nutrition relative to non-thermal combinations.
- **Cannot show:** New experimental inactivation in a food. Pooled “HPP log reduction” across Table 1 (different organisms, pH, and whether heat is part of the cycle). That non-thermal HHP alone equals PATP spore kill.

## Key findings
- Review claim: a named high-pressure cycle is not a complete safety outcome when spores or injured vegetative cells remain and can regrow. (Section “Challenges of single HHP”)
- Cited measurement: 450 MPa/10 min + ultrasound 60 min completely inactivated apple-juice natural microflora; HHP 600 MPa/15 min + thermosonication 4.4 log *A. acidoterrestris* in orange juice. (Table 1)
- Cited measurement: PATP 600 MPa with substantial heat achieved 9 log *B. amyloliquefaciens* spores in vegetable purees—heat is part of that kill step. (Table 1)
- Author interpretation: combinations exist to raise inactivation without always raising pressure; PATP nutrition cost is a reason to prefer emerging non-thermal pairs. (Introduction; ultrasound section)
- Mechanism grade: all mechanisms (cavitation, ROS, spore germination by HHP then heat) are cited from earlier work, not measured in this paper.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Mini-review without search protocol, inclusion counts, or risk-of-bias.
- Table 1 mixes thermal-assisted HHP (PATP, POTS, thermosonication at 78 °C) with non-thermal pairs; those rows are not “heat is not the kill step.”
- OCR/text artifacts in the extract (e.g., “kowsalya” appended to references).

## Relevance to our research question
Frames why a named MPa–min cycle is not a universal microbial outcome: spores, Gram-positives, low-acid juices, and injured-cell repair. Useful as combination context; spore PATP numbers must not be treated as non-thermal HPP results.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Do not import Table 1 PATP/POTS spore logs into a non-thermal HPP synthesis without labeling the heat component.
- Original cited papers should be used if a specific log reduction is quoted in the article.
