# Application of High Hydrostatic Pressure (Long Holding Time vs. Two Consecutive Short Cycles) for the Preservation of Lamb Burgers Enriched with Lupinus albus Flour

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** González-Cantillo N, Martín-Mateos MJ, Sánchez-Ordóñez M, López-Parra MM, García-Parra JJ, Matías J, Ramírez-Bernabé MR. Foods. 2026;15(4):789. doi:10.3390/foods15040789
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/González-Cantillo2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
When total HHP machine time is matched, do two 1 s cycles at 600 MPa give the same microbial and quality outcome as one 4 min cycle at 600 MPa in lupine-flour lamb burgers stored 14 days at 6 °C?

## Methods
Three independent burger batches. Formulation: 40% flank / 60% leg lamb, salt and spices, with 0 or 1% (w/w) Lupinus albus var. Orden Dorado flour from hammer milling (Flour 1) or compression milling (Flour 2). Vacuum packed. HHP on a Hiperbaric Wave 6000/55 (55 L), initial water 15 °C, come-up 3.5 min, instantaneous decompression. Treatments: untreated; HHP1 = two consecutive 600 MPa × 1 s cycles; HHP2 = one 600 MPa × 4 min cycle (authors state total HHP-unit time equivalent). Storage 6 ± 1 °C, darkness, day 1 and day 14. Endpoints: proximate composition, fatty acids, CIE colour, TBA-RS, protein carbonyls, plate counts, trained sensory panel on grilled burgers (day 1 only). n = 5 burgers per cell for physico-chemical/microbiology; n = 3 flour samples; sensory 8 panellists, 54 burgers.

## Sample / data
22 kg lamb for process development; 12 kg for the HHP storage study (115 burgers manufactured; 15 untreated day-14 burgers discarded for high load). Each burger ~100 g. Detection limits: 10 CFU g−1 except S. aureus 100 CFU g−1.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** factorial laboratory/pilot meat study (formulation × HHP schedule × storage), three independent batches
- **Population / model:** raw lamb burgers with or without 1% sweet lupine flour; background microbiota (not a pathogen challenge)
- **n:** 5 burgers per treatment–formulation–time cell for microbiology/colour/oxidation; 3 flour samples; sensory 8 trained panellists on day-1 grilled burgers only
- **Intervention / comparator:** no HHP vs two cycles 600 MPa/1 s vs one cycle 600 MPa/4 min; control vs Flour 1 vs Flour 2; day 1 vs day 14 at 6 °C
- **Primary endpoint:** mesophilic, psychrophilic, moulds/yeasts, S. aureus, C. perfringens, coliforms and E. coli as log10 CFU g−1; CIE L*a*b*; TBA-RS (mg MDA kg−1); protein carbonyls (nmol mg protein−1); grilled sensory scores 0–10
- **Primary result:** Untreated day-1 mesophiles >7 log CFU g−1 and psychrophiles 7.6–7.8 log CFU g−1. Both HHP schedules cut mesophiles to 3.7–4.5 log CFU g−1 and psychrophiles to ~1 log CFU g−1 at day 1; coliforms (untreated 2.8–3.2) and E. coli (1.2–1.4) fell below 1 log CFU g−1 and stayed ND through day 14. S. aureus fell below 2 log CFU g−1. C. perfringens remained <1 log CFU g−1 in all samples. At day 14, HHP1 vs HHP2 did not differ for mesophiles, psychrophiles, moulds/yeasts, S. aureus, C. perfringens, coliforms or E. coli (two-way ANOVA); mesophiles rose to 4.6–5.0 (HHP1) and up to 6.6 log CFU g−1 in one HHP2 formulation. Lipid oxidation at day 14 was lower with Flour 2: HHP1 0.1 vs control 0.5 and Flour 1 0.4 mg MDA kg−1; HHP2 0.3 vs Flour 1 0.6 and control 0.9 mg MDA kg−1. HHP increased L* and decreased a*; the two short cycles whitened and oxidized less than the 4 min cycle. Grilled sensory scores (appearance, odour, taste, texture, overall 6.8–7.9) did not differ by flour or HHP.
- **Cannot show:** FDA-style ≥5-log validation against L. monocytogenes, Salmonella or STEC (authors explicitly say this); spore kill (C. perfringens already <LOD before HHP); that two 1 s pulses equal one 4 min hold for every quality trait (colour and oxidation differed); 4 °C retail storage (they used 6 °C).

## Key findings
- Measurement: Flour 2 phenolics 74.0 mg/100 g vs Flour 1 36.8 mg/100 g; ABTS antioxidant activity did not differ (Section 3.1).
- Measurement: 1% flour did not change burger moisture, protein, fat or SFA/MUFA/PUFA groups (Section 3.2).
- Author interpretation: two short 600 MPa cycles match the long cycle microbiologically and better preserve colour and oxidative stability at equivalent equipment time (Conclusions).
- Mechanism grade: HHP colour change via myoglobin/metmyoglobin is **cited**; lupine phenolics as radical scavengers after pressure are **proposed**.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Not a pathogen challenge; authors warn against reading results as process validation.
- Untreated day-14 samples discarded.
- Sensory only on grilled day-1 burgers, so fresh HHP colour change was not scored by consumers at purchase.
- Protein oxidation of Flour 2 diverged between HHP1 and HHP2 (interaction), so antioxidant benefit is lipid-specific here.

## Relevance to our research question
Direct evidence that a named 600 MPa treatment is not one outcome: matched machine time, two 1 s cycles vs 4 min, similar plate-count kill but less colour loss and less TBA-RS with the short pulses, and Flour 2 further changes lipid oxidation at the same pressure.
