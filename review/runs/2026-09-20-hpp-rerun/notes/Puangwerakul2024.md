# Comparative Effects of HPP and Irradiation on Plant-Based Whole Hard-Boiled Eggs

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Puangwerakul Y, Chaisakdanukul C, Soithongsuk S, Sajjaut S, Pewlong W. Journal of Current Science and Technology. 2024;14(3):Article 66. doi:10.59796/jcst.V14N3.2024.66
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Puangwerakul2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Do gamma irradiation, electron-beam irradiation (EBI) and HPP equally inactivate Clostridium perfringens and extend shelf-life of plant-based whole hard-boiled eggs without spoiling colour, texture and sensory quality?

## Methods
Plant-based eggs (80 g; rice proteins, rice malt, vegan egg powder, bio-cellulose, yeast, gellan; steamed 100 °C/15 min) vacuum-sealed in nylon. Gamma (Co-60, 2.6 Gy/h) and EBI (10 MeV) at 0, 2, 3.5, 5 kGy. HPP (K-TKFHPP HPP600MPa, TISTR) at 0, 300, 400, 500 MPa, 30 °C, 5 min, water as transmitting medium. Colour, penetrometer firmness, aw, moisture, TBA, aerobic plate count, yeast and mould, C. perfringens (BAM). Sensory: 10 trained assessors, 9-point hedonic. ASLT at 40 °C and 50 °C with C. perfringens every 9 days; Q10/Q1 used to predict shelf-life at 30 °C. End of shelf-life indexed to RTE limit of 100 CFU/g C. perfringens. Triplicate; ANOVA/Duncan, p < 0.05.

## Sample / data
Plant-based whole hard-boiled egg analogues, not hen eggs. n = 3 analytical replicates. Sensory n = 10 panellists.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** comparative laboratory preservation study (irradiation vs HPP) with accelerated storage
- **Population / model:** vacuum-packed plant-based hard-boiled egg analogues; background flora plus C. perfringens as the storage index organism
- **n:** 3 (physicochemical/microbial); 10 trained sensory assessors
- **Intervention / comparator:** gamma 0/2/3.5/5 kGy; EBI 0/2/3.5/5 kGy; HPP 0/300/400/500 MPa, 30 °C, 5 min; untreated control. Selected “optimum” doses for ASLT: gamma 3.5 kGy, EBI 5 kGy, HPP 500 MPa
- **Primary endpoint:** total bacteria and yeast/mould as log CFU/g; C. perfringens as CFU/g (immediate and during ASLT); predicted shelf-life (days) at 30 °C; TBA (mmole/kg); firmness (mm); 9-point sensory scores
- **Primary result:** Untreated: total bacteria 5.15 ± 0.15 log CFU/g, yeast/mould 2.18 ± 0.20 log CFU/g, C. perfringens 8.00 ± 1.50 CFU/g. After treatment, all three selected doses brought total bacteria, yeast/mould and C. perfringens to 0.00 on day 0. Intermediate HPP was incomplete: 300 MPa left 4.15 ± 0.10 log bacteria and 5.50 ± 0.15 CFU/g C. perfringens; 400 MPa left 2.04 ± 0.77 log bacteria and 1.50 ± 0.50 CFU/g C. perfringens. During ASLT, HPP 500 MPa products showed C. perfringens by day 9 at 50 °C (1.7 ± 2.1 CFU/g) and by day 36 at 40 °C (2.5 ± 0.6), reaching 100.3 ± 2.5 CFU/g at 40 °C day 54. Gamma 3.5 kGy and EBI 5 kGy stayed 0 until late storage (gamma: 0.7 ± 0.6 CFU/g at 50 °C day 63; 1.5 ± 0.5 at 40 °C day 117). Predicted shelf-life: 217 days for gamma 3.5 kGy and EBI 5 kGy vs 146 days for HPP 500 MPa (Q10 1.857 for irradiations, 4.000 for HPP). HPP 500 MPa did not change TBA (28.4 ± 1.53 vs control 28.4 ± 1.74 mmole/kg) or firmness (97.5 ± 1.4 vs 97.4 ± 1.6 mm); gamma 5 kGy raised TBA to 35.2 ± 1.10 and firmness to 103 ± 1.0 mm and cut overall liking to 4.90 ± 0.31 vs control 6.20 ± 0.63.
- **Cannot show:** that HPP 500 MPa/5 min is a spore-sterilizing process (C. perfringens returned during storage; authors say HPP may stimulate spore germination); real 4 °C shelf-life (prediction is from 40/50 °C ASLT and Q10); pathogen challenge with L. monocytogenes or Salmonella.

## Key findings
- Measurement: HPP at 300 or 400 MPa/5 min did not zero C. perfringens; only 500 MPa did on day 0 (Table 1).
- Measurement: the same 500 MPa/5 min cycle that cleared plates on day 0 had a shorter predicted shelf-life (146 vs 217 days) than 3.5 kGy gamma or 5 kGy EBI because C. perfringens reappeared (Table 3).
- Author interpretation: HPP “may stimulate spore germination and allow for microbial growth under extended storage” (Section 4.3).
- Mechanism grade: HPP membrane/morphology/genetic interference is **cited** (Wickramanayake; Sehrawat). Spore germination after HPP is **author interpretation** of storage counts, citing Carlin 2000 and Evelyn & Silva 2019.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- ASLT temperatures (40/50 °C) may not represent retail storage.
- Single index organism (C. perfringens); other spoilers not driving the shelf-life call.
- Q10 assumed constant with temperature.
- Table 2 gamma 5 kGy texture is 4.00 ± 0.47; later prose cites 5.90 ± 0.42 — Table 2 used here.
- Product is a plant analogue, not a hen egg.

## Relevance to our research question
Clear case that a named 500 MPa/5 min cycle is not the same safety outcome as irradiation at doses that also zero day-0 counts: HPP looks equivalent immediately and fails earlier in storage, consistent with spore survival when heat is not the kill step.

## Open questions / things to verify
- C. perfringens units in Table 1 are CFU/g (not log); untreated 8.00 ± 1.50 is therefore a very low starting count, not 8 log.
- Dose rate of gamma (2.6 Gy/h) is unusually low for food irradiation; recorded as printed.
