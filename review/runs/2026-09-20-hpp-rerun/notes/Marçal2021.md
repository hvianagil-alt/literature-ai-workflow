# Macroalgae-Fortified Sausages: Nutritional and Quality Aspects Influenced by Non-Thermal High-Pressure Processing

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Marçal C, Pinto CA, Silva AMS, Monteiro C, Saraiva JA, Cardoso SM. 2021. Foods 10(2):209. DOI:10.3390/foods10020209. PMID:33498558
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Marçal2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Does adding European macroalgae flour change sausage mineral nutrition, and does 600 MPa/6 min HPP extend refrigerated shelf-life of those fortified frankfurters and Portuguese “chouriço” without major colour or fatty-acid damage?

## Methods

Industrial sausages from Irmãos Monteiro S.A.: plain vs fortified with AlgaPlus mix (Ulva spp., Gracilaria spp., Fucus vesiculosus) at 3.0% meat frankfurter (F-MFS), 2.6% chouriço (F-TPS), 7.8% vegetable frankfurter (F-VFS). Vacuum PA/PE bags. Nutrition on frozen (−20 °C) plain vs fortified (protein LECO N × 6.25; ash 550 °C; Soxhlet fat; ICP-MS minerals after microwave HNO3/H2O2). HPP only on fortified products: Hiperbaric 55 L, 600 MPa, 6 min, room temperature, tap water, vs non-pressurized fortified controls, stored 4 ± 1 °C. Sampling: frankfurters T0, T15, T30 (and T45 pressurized only); chouriço T0, T30, T60, T120, T150, T180 (max 180 days). TAM on PCA, 37 °C, 72 ± 3 h (ISO 4833:2003); threshold 6.49 log CFU/g. pH of filtrate; CIELab surface colour; FAME GC-MS with C19:0 IS. Nutrition: t-test; other data Sidak; GraphPad; p = 0.05. Means of ≥3 independent assays.

## Sample / data

Three sausage types (meat frankfurter, vegetable frankfurter, traditional Portuguese chouriço), each plain and macroalgae-fortified. HPP comparison is fortified ± pressure, not plain vs HPP.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Industrial product reformulation (plain vs seaweed) plus refrigerated storage trial of fortified sausages with vs without 600 MPa/6 min HPP.
- **Population / model:** Commercial meat frankfurters, vegetable frankfurters, and Portuguese chouriço with 2.6–7.8% mixed macroalgae flour (human RTE sausages).
- **n:** Nutritional and FA means from at least 3 independent assays. Microbiological/pH/colour time points as above; exact pack replicates per day not stated beyond that.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Seaweed vs no seaweed (nutrition); HPP 600 MPa/6 min vs non-pressurized fortified controls at 4 °C (safety/quality).
- **Primary endpoint:** TAM (log CFU/g) vs 6.49 log threshold (shelf-life); supporting pH, CIELab, FA % of total FA; minerals mg/100 g.
- **Primary result:** Immediately after HPP, F-MFS TAM <1.0 log CFU/g vs control ~2.5 log CFU/g. Time to 6.49 log: <15 days non-pressurized F-MFS vs ~45 days pressurized (authors: ~3-fold shelf-life). F-VFS day 15: 4.0 log pressurized vs 6.3 log control; day 45: 8.1 vs 8.2 log. F-TPS non-pressurized 2.2–2.7 log CFU/g over 6 months; pressurized remained below detection throughout 180 days. pH at T0: F-MFS and F-VFS 6.3–6.4 vs F-TPS <6. Non-pressurized frankfurters fell to 5.2 ± 0.1 (F-MFS) and 5.9 ± 0.0 (F-VFS) at T30; HPP prevented that drop. Colour L*a*b*: no significant HPP vs control differences (Figure 3). FA: except PUFA of F-MFS, no significant pressurized vs non-pressurized proportion changes. Minerals (Table 1, mg/100 g): F-MFS vs MFS Ca 28.6 ± 3.0 vs 12.5 ± 1.4; Mg 80.0 ± 1.3 vs 19.0 ± 0.4; F-TPS vs TPS Ca 11.4 ± 0.1 vs 4.33 ± 0.24; Mg 61.5 ± 2.7 vs 20.8 ± 0.4; Na/K 3.6→3.0 (MFS), 2.0→1.8 (VFS), 1.8→1.6 (TPS). Protein and fat ns between plain and fortified.
- **Cannot show:** Pathogen challenge (TAM only); enzyme inactivation; sensory scores; whether 600 MPa/6 min equals a thermal pasteurization F-value; HPP effect on unfortified sausages; liquid-holding capacity or texture (authors say those still needed).

## Key findings

- Measurement: 600 MPa/6 min put F-MFS TAM below 1.0 log CFU/g and delayed the 6.49 log threshold from <15 days to ~45 days (Section 3.2.1; Figure 1a).
- Measurement: Same cycle kept F-TPS TAM undetectable for 180 days while untreated fortified chouriço stayed 2.2–2.7 log (Figure 1c).
- Measurement: HPP blocked the frankfurter pH drop that tracked microbial growth (Section 3.2.2).
- Measurement: Surface colour and most FA classes were stable with or without HPP over the tested storage (Sections 3.2.3–3.2.4; Table 2).
- Measurement: Seaweed, not HPP, drove Ca/Mg gains and lower Na/K (Table 1).
- Author interpretation: HPP is suitable for seaweed-fortified charcuterie because microbial load fell without significant colour/FA detriment (Conclusions). Texture/sensory not measured.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Authors: need liquid-holding capacity, texture, and sensory work (Conclusions).
- Formulations are confidential; only approximate seaweed %.
- TAM at 37 °C (text) while figure caption says 30 °C.
- No inoculated pathogens; “safety” is mesophile load.
- Vegetable frankfurters still exceeded 6.49 log by day 45 even after HPP (8.1 log).
- HPP at “room temperature” — vessel temperature not numbered.

## Relevance to our research question

Shows 600 MPa/6 min as a nonthermal kill/shelf-life step on human sausages: the named cycle is not the same outcome as no pressure (3-fold frankfurter shelf-life; undetectable TAM in chouriço) and did not, in this sample, trade away colour or FA profile. Enzymes were not measured.
