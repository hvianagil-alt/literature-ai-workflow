# High-Pressure-Based Strategies for the Inactivation of Bacillus subtilis Endospores in Honey

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Scepankova H, Pinto CA, Estevinho LM, Saraiva JA. Molecules. 2022;27(18):5918. doi:10.3390/molecules27185918. PMC9503340.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Scepankova2022.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Can nonthermal high-pressure processing (HPP), pressure-assisted thermal processing (PATP), and hyperbaric storage (HS) inactivate *Bacillus subtilis* endospores in honey, and what water activity (aW) is required for those pressure-based methods to work?

## Methods
Laboratory inoculation study on multifloral honey. *B. subtilis* ATCC 6633 endospores (not heat-activated) were inoculated at about 10^4–10^5 cells/mL. Treatments: HPP at 600 MPa/15 min (input water 20–22 °C) on a Hiperbaric 55; PATP at 600 MPa/15 min at 70, 75, or 85 °C (adiabatic heating estimated to raise average process temperature to ~92.2, 97.2, and 107.2 °C); HS at 50–150 MPa for up to 60 days at 18–23 °C; thermal processing (TP) at 75 or 85 °C/15 min at 0.1 MPa. Endospore germination vs inactivation was distinguished by a heat shock of 80 °C/20 min. Counts on HiCrome Bacillus agar; detection/quantification limits stated as 1.30 or 2.30 log CFU/g depending on the experiment (Results 3.2–3.3).

## Sample / data
Multifloral honey (Castanea sativa pollen 53.3%; aW 0.57; 84.6 °Brix). Honey–water preparations (HWP) adjusted to aW 0.80, 0.85, 0.90, and 0.96 (63.3, 52.9, 43.4, and 33.9 °Brix). Endogenous *B. subtilis* ~10^2 cells/g; endogenous TEL <2.3 log CFU/g. After inoculation, TEL ~5 log CFU/g (5.04 ± 0.01 log CFU/g in one HS experiment; 5.65 log CFU/mL in PATP of undiluted honey).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** laboratory challenge study (inoculated endospores in honey and diluted honey)
- **Population / model:** multifloral honey; *Bacillus subtilis* ATCC 6633 endospores
- **n:** not reported as a sample-size integer; single honey botanical lot; counts expressed as log CFU/g or log CFU/mL with quantification limits 1.30 or 2.30 log CFU/g
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 600 MPa/15 min at room temperature; PATP 600 MPa/15 min at 70–85 °C; HS 50–150 MPa up to 60 days at 18–23 °C; TP 75 or 85 °C/15 min; atmospheric-pressure room-temperature storage; undiluted honey (aW 0.57) vs HWP aW 0.80–0.96
- **Primary endpoint:** total endospore load (TEL) and total microbial load (TML) as log (N/N0) of *B. subtilis* (CFU/g)
- **Primary result:** In undiluted honey, PATP 600 MPa/15 min/85 °C and TP 85 °C/15 min left inoculated TEL unchanged at 5.65 log CFU/mL immediately and after 24 h; HS at 75–125 MPa for 21 days (and 125 MPa for 60 days) did not change spore load or thermal resistance. In HWP, neither HPP 600 MPa/15 min (RT) nor TP 75 °C/15 min reduced TEL up to aW 0.90. PATP 600 MPa/15 min/75 °C reduced TEL by about 1.74 log at aW 0.80 and to the detection limit of 1.30 log CFU/g at aW 0.85 and 0.90 (≥3.70 log inactivation; Abstract also states ≥4.0 log to below detection in 52.9 °Brix honey, aW 0.85). PATP 600 MPa/15 min/70 °C reduced spores by ~2 log in diluted honey (aW 0.96) but not in whole honey. HS at 150 MPa/21 days (aW 0.80) did not reduce spores but prevented proliferation that occurred at atmospheric pressure.
- **Cannot show:** inactivation of *Clostridium botulinum* spores in honey; commercial sterility of undiluted honey; HMF, enzyme, or phenolic quality after these cycles; that a named 600 MPa/15 min cycle is a kill step when aW remains ~0.57.

## Key findings
- Mechanism (cited from earlier work, not measured here): low pressure (~150–200 MPa) is said to trigger nutrient-like germination; >200–300 MPa a non-physiological DPA-release pathway; neither pathway was triggered in this honey (Discussion 4.1).
- Measurement: HPP at 600 MPa/RT increased apparent endospore counts in HWP aW 0.96 by activating superdormant spores rather than by true growth (Results 3.3.2, Figure 6).
- Author interpretation: PATP is promising only once aW is raised to ≥0.85; HS is a preservation method against outgrowth, not a spore kill step (Conclusions).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- One honey botanical origin; one *B. subtilis* strain as a spore model, not *C. botulinum*.
- Adiabatic heating during PATP is estimated, not a fully uniform industrial HPTP vessel.
- Raw honey was not sterilized before inoculation; endogenous load was subtracted by differential medium.
- No HMF or bioactivity measurements in this paper.

## Relevance to our research question
Direct evidence that a named pressure–time cycle (600 MPa/15 min, with or without 85 °C) is **not** the same safety outcome in low-aW honey as in diluted honey: spores survive in undiluted honey and are reduced below detection only when aW is raised and heat is added (PATP). Nonthermal HPP is not a kill step for these endospores.

## Open questions / things to verify
Abstract says ≥4.0 log inactivation in 52.9 °Brix (aW 0.85) honey, while Results 3.3.1 states at least 3.70 log to the 1.30 log CFU/g detection limit at aW 0.85 and 0.90; both are from the same paper. aW of “raw” honey is given as ≈0.55 in the Abstract and 0.57 in Table 1.
