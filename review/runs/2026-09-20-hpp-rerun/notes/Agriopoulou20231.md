# Recent Advances in High Pressure Processing of Milk and Milk Products — A Review

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Agriopoulou S, Severoğlu M, Varzakas T, Özer HB. 2023. Journal of Agricultural Sciences (Tarim Bilimleri Dergisi) 29(1):1–12. DOI:10.15832/ankutbd.1150112

## Research question

What do recent HPP studies report for microbial inactivation, milk-component modification, digestion/allergenicity, and dairy-product quality when heat is not the preservation step?

## Methods

Narrative review of HPP (also HHP) applied to milk and dairy products. No new experiment. Sections: technological background; milk microbiota; milk compounds (fat, casein, whey, Ig, enzymes); pH, emulsion, viscosity; in vitro digestion and allergenicity; whey beverages; cheese; yogurt/kefir. Table 1 compiles selected milk-category studies. EU novel-food framing and commercial examples (Made by Cow® milk; Col+® colostrum; selected cheeses) are described from cited sources.

## Sample / data

Not applicable as primary data. Table 1 and in-text citations are secondary. One cited “Özer et al. 2022” TUBITAK project is labelled unpublished.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative review (no new laboratory or clinical dataset).
- **Population / model:** Cow, goat, ewe, donkey, human, UHT, reconstituted powder, colostrum, cheese, yogurt, kefir, whey beverages — as reported by cited papers.
- **n:** Not applicable (review). Individual cited n values are those of the source papers, not generated here.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP typically 300–900 MPa (practical 400–600 MPa) versus thermal pasteurization/UHT or untreated, as in the cited studies.
- **Primary endpoint:** Review endpoints are microbial log reductions, shelf-life, protein/micelle/enzyme changes, digestion/allergenicity, and dairy texture — all from prior work.
- **Primary result (as compiled, not original):** Table 1: Stratakos et al. 2019 raw milk 600 MPa, 5 min, 18 °C → 5 log E. coli, Salmonella, L. monocytogenes. Tan et al. 2020 cow and goat milk 450 MPa, 7 min, 15 °C → shelf-life up to 22 days at 8 °C with no increase in B. cereus, mesophilic spores, coliforms, yeast/mold. Yang et al. 2012 raw milk 300 MPa, 30 min, 25 °C → inactivation of Salmonella, E. coli, Shigella, S. aureus (log not numbered in Table 1). Liu et al. 2020 raw whole milk 600 MPa, 5 min, 40 °C → 3 log total bacteria and E. coli. Misiou et al. 2018 UHT whole milk 500 MPa, 10 min, 25 °C → 6.20 log L. monocytogenes. Machado et al. 2019 reconstituted powder 500–600 MPa, 3–5 min, 25 °C → inactivation of E. coli, P. fluorescens, E. aerogenes with increased microorganism-derived LPS. Rocha-Pimienta et al. 2020 human milk 593.96 MPa, 233 s, 25 °C → 6–7 log B. cereus / “Bacillus aureus”. In-text: Alpas et al. 2000 milk 345 MPa, 5 min, 50 °C → 8 log E. coli and L. monocytogenes but only 5.33 log S. aureus. Giacometti et al. 2016 donkey milk 400 MPa, 180 s → <10 CFU/ml Pseudomonas, Enterobacteriaceae, B. cereus after 30 days at 4 °C. Review statements: vegetative cells/yeasts/molds inactivated at 400–600 MPa ambient; spores need much higher pressure (authors: >1200 MPa “relative success”) and HPP “is not effective in the elimination of spores”; minimum aw 0.8; Gram-positives often more resistant than Gram-negatives. Casein: 100–200 MPa limited change; ~250 MPa ~25% micelle-size increase; >400 MPa up to 50% size decrease. Whey: β-LG irreversible unfold at >300 MPa for >30 min. Hu et al. 2017: α-casein pepsin digestibility highest at 200 MPa/5 min; digestion of α-casein 36–43% lower at 600 MPa/15 min vs 200 MPa/5 min. Bogahawaththa et al. 2018: κ-casein digestion 17% at 600 MPa vs 36% at 400 MPa. Aalaei et al. 2021: 400 MPa/15 min improved elderly-model gastric hydrolysis vs 600 MPa. Kleber et al. 2007: β-LG allergenicity increased as pressure/time rose from 200 to 600 MPa and 0 to 30 min at <25 °C. Sampedro et al. 2009 orange-milk drink: >90% PME inactivation at 650 MPa/50 °C (vs 85 °C/1 min thermal or PEF 25 kV/cm at 65 °C); HPP volatile losses 7.5–14.2% at 30 °C vs 22.9–42.3% at 50 °C. Barba et al. 2012 orange-milk 100–400 MPa, 2–9 min: ascorbic acid loss <10%.
- **Cannot show:** Any new primary log reduction, enzyme residual, or sensory score from this paper; chemical safety of HPP (authors say this is still under-studied); spore-validated dairy sterilization.

## Key findings

- Author synthesis: HPP can match pasteurization-like vegetative kill in milk at 400–600 MPa but is not a spore kill step (Introduction; Section 2.1).
- Author synthesis: The same MPa is not the same protein outcome: 250 MPa can enlarge casein micelles; >400 MPa can shrink them; 600 MPa can slow casein digestion versus 400 MPa (Sections 2.2, 4).
- Compiled measurement (Table 1): 600 MPa/5 min/18 °C → 5 log of three pathogens in raw milk (Stratakos 2019); 500 MPa/10 min → 6.20 log L. monocytogenes in UHT milk (Misiou 2018).
- Compiled measurement: 345 MPa/5 min/50 °C left S. aureus 5.33 log reduced vs 8 log for E. coli and L. monocytogenes (Alpas 2000).
- Author interpretation: 400 MPa may jointly serve microbial safety and digestion efficiency (Section 4, citing Özer 2022 unpublished).
- Author interpretation: Chemical interactions and digestion effects need more work; regulations lag industrialization (Conclusion).
- Mechanism grade: membrane/enzyme/DNA damage and protein unfolding are stated as known HPP effects (cited Chiozzi 2022; Yang 2012), not newly measured.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Authors: chemical safety, digestion, and country-level HPP rules still thin (Conclusion).
- Narrative, not systematic; no search protocol or PRISMA counts.
- Table 1 is a convenience sample of “recent studies,” not exhaustive.
- Özer et al. 2022 is an unpublished project report.
- “Bacillus aureus” in Table 1 (Rocha-Pimienta) is likely S. aureus vegetative cells per that paper’s title — do not treat as a Bacillus species without checking the source.
- Commercial brand examples are not experimental results.

## Relevance to our research question

Review-level map of dairy HPP: named cycles (e.g. 600 MPa/5 min vs 400 MPa/15 min vs 200 MPa) are not interchangeable for pathogen log kill, spore survival, casein micelle size, or gastric digestion. Use only as a pointer to primary studies; do not treat Table 1 as this team’s measurements.
