# Bacteriocin AS-48 and High Hydrostatic Pressure as Hurdles in a Vegetable Cream upon Temperature Abuse

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Rodríguez López J, Lucas López R, Grande Burgos MJ, Gálvez A, Pérez Pulido R. Microorganisms. 2026;14(4):892. doi:10.3390/microorganisms14040892. PMC13118743. PMID:42075288. Received 17 Mar 2026; accepted 14 Apr 2026; collection date Apr 2026.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun-gapfill/López2026_040892.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Do enterocin AS-48 (50 µg/g), HHP (600 MPa, 55 °C, 8 min), or both prevent aerobic mesophile proliferation and shift PMA-16S community structure in a refrigerated pumpkin–carrot vegetable cream after a 24 h room-temperature abuse on day 2?

## Methods
Commercial refrigerated RTE carrot and pumpkin puree (no added sorbic/benzoic acid), 96 polyethylene-polyamide bags, 10 g each. Four treatments: untreated (C), AS-48 50 µg/g (B; 1700 AU/mg vs *E. faecalis* S-47), HHP 600 MPa/8 min/55 °C (H; Stansted 2.5 L, come-up 75 MPa/min, water + 10% propylene glycol), sequential AS-48 then HHP (BH). Half of each arm incubated 24 h at room temperature on day 2 then returned to 4 °C (AB suffix). Storage 4 °C to 30 days; sampling days 0, 2, 3, 7, 15, 30. TSA spread plates, 37 °C, 24 h; LOD 10 CFU/g (1 log CFU/g); counts <LOD set to LOD. pH. PMA 50 µM then DNeasy PowerSoil; 16S V3–V4 Illumina MiSeq; DADA2/SILVA 138.2; two DNA extracts pooled per sampling cell. Methods text: two independent production batches, duplicate bags per point, technical triplicate plates. Table 1 footnote: mean ± SD of 3 independent biological replicates (n = 3). ANOVA + Tukey, p < 0.05. Authors selected 600 MPa/55 °C as industrially relevant HPTP because room-temperature HHP often fails on spores and pressure-tolerant enzymes.

## Sample / data
Human food: refrigerated pumpkin and carrot cream. Native microbiota (not inoculated *Listeria* or *B. cereus*). Many HHP/BH samples yielded too little PMA-treated DNA for 16S and were excluded (Table 2 lists sequenced cells only). Reads 56,674–166,157 after filtering.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** storage trial of commercial vegetable cream with a defined temperature-abuse event; culture counts plus PMA-16S on samples with enough DNA
- **Population / model:** native aerobic mesophiles and 16S community in refrigerated RTE pumpkin–carrot puree
- **n:** 96 bags, 10 g; methods describe two biological batches and duplicate bags plated in technical triplicate; Table 1 reports mean ± SD, n = 3
- **Intervention / comparator:** no treatment vs AS-48 50 µg/g vs HHP 600 MPa/8 min/55 °C vs AS-48 then HHP; each ± 24 h room-temperature abuse starting day 2; all stored at 4 °C to 30 days
- **Primary endpoint:** aerobic mesophiles on TSA at 37 °C/24 h (log CFU/g); pH; phylum/genus relative abundance after PMA-16S
- **Primary result:** Controls without abuse (C): counts stable 2.54 ± 0.16 (T0) to 2.25 ± 0.1 (T30) log CFU/g (range 2.54–2.25). Controls with abuse (CAB): 2.54 ± 0.07 (T0), 2.06 ± 0.02 (T2), then 6.47 ± 0.01 (T3), 7.73 ± 0.17 (T7), 7.59 ± 0.06 (T15), 6.96 ± 0.13 (T30); increase vs C significant from day 3 (p < 0.05). AS-48 (B): 1.65 ± 0.06 (T0) through 1.74 ± 0.06 (T30); significantly lower than C on days 3, 7, 30. AS-48 + abuse (BAB): no count increase after abuse; <1 log CFU/g on days 15 and 30. HHP (H), HHP + abuse (HAB), AS-48+HHP (BH), and AS-48+HHP + abuse (BHAB): generally <1 log CFU/g for the whole 30 days (HAB T0 listed as 1). pH of CAB fell significantly days 7–30; C pH more stable or rose at T30 (5.49 ± 0.01 to 5.96 ± 0.08). Initial 16S (abstract): Pseudomonadota 74.50%, Bacillota 21.19%, Actinobacteriota 3.69%. Without abuse, Bacillota (mainly *Bacillus*) became dominant from day 3 (87.21 to 99.48%; Results: >99%). After temperature abuse, controls kept higher Pseudomonadota, Bacteroidota, and Actinobacteriota; *Acinetobacter* associated with abuse. Treated samples that sequenced resembled early controls (Pseudomonadota-rich, less *Bacillus*). Authors: synergy of AS-48 + HHP could **not** be shown because initial load was already very low. Both AS-48 and HHP prevented the abuse-driven count rise.
- **Cannot show:** inactivation kinetics of inoculated *B. cereus* or *C. botulinum* spores; that 600 MPa at 22 °C (no 55 °C) would match this result; species-level toxin or AMR genes; unbiased 16S of HHP samples (PMA + low survivors); sensory/nutritional quality; a synergistic hurdle beyond either treatment alone.

## Key findings
- Measurement: 24 h room-temperature abuse on day 2 took untreated cream from ~2 log to 6.47 log by day 3 and 7.73 log by day 7 (Table 1).
- Measurement: 600 MPa/8 min/55 °C kept TSA <1 log CFU/g through 30 days **with or without** that abuse (Table 1).
- Measurement: AS-48 50 µg/g alone also blocked the abuse bloom; BAB fell below detection by days 15–30 (Table 1).
- Measurement: refrigerated unpressurized cream shifted to *Bacillus*-dominated Bacillota; abused untreated cream retained Pseudomonadota and rose in *Acinetobacter* (Fig. 1; Abstract).
- Author interpretation: both enterocin AS-48 and HHP are effective hurdles against proliferation under inappropriate temperature (Conclusions).
- Proposed mechanism (suggested, not imaged): HHP membrane/protein damage plus AS-48 pores; possible outer-membrane help for bacteriocin entry in Gram-negatives. Not demonstrated as synergy in this low-load puree.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Very low starting counts (~2.5 log) prevent demonstrating synergy (Discussion).
- Most HHP/BH samples dropped from 16S for lack of PMA-surviving DNA; treated community profiles are biased to rare survivors (Discussion).
- 55 °C during the 600 MPa hold is moderate heat (HPTP), not ambient HHP; authors chose it because cold HHP often fails on spores.
- n reporting differs between Methods (two batches, duplicate bags) and Table 1 (n = 3).
- No pathogen challenge, no spore inactivation curves, no quality chemistry.
- TSA at 37 °C (not 30 °C) by design, so psychrotrophs may be under-counted.

## Relevance to our research question
**Gap-fill** paper retrieved after the literature table for the HHP hurdle with bacteriocin in a vegetable cream under temperature abuse (López2026_040892). A named 600 MPa/8 min cycle is **not** the same as commercial cold HPP: this hold was at 55 °C. In this cream, that HPTP cycle (and AS-48 50 µg/g) stopped the ~5-log abuse bloom that untreated refrigeration did not. Heat is an intended co-factor here, not a classical retort kill step.
