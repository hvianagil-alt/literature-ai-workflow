# High Pressure and Pasteurization Effects on Dairy Cream

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Machado F, Duarte RV, Pinto CA, Casal S, Lopes-da-Silva JA, Saraiva JA. Foods. 2023;12(19):3640. doi:10.3390/foods12193640. PMID:37835293. PMC10572675.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Machado2023.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (HPP versus thermal pasteurization of dairy cream: microbes, color, viscosity, fatty acids, volatiles)

## Research question
Can HPP pasteurize raw dairy cream (endogenous flora and inoculated *E. coli* / *L. innocua*) with a longer refrigerated shelf life than commercial thermal pasteurization (75 °C / 15 s) without large changes in pH, color, fatty acids, viscosity, or volatiles?

## Methods
Two batches of industrially homogenized raw cream (Portugal). Packed 20 mL in PA/PE bags. HPP in a 55 L Hiperbaric unit at 7 °C (to keep adiabatic heating ≤ about 19–25 °C at 600 MPa); pressure rise 200 MPa/min; decompression nearly instantaneous. Batch 1: 450 MPa/5 min and 600 MPa/5 min versus heat (75 °C/15 s); storage 4 °C for 0, 5, 9, 18, 33, 51 days; TAP, LAB, ENT. Batch 2: 600 MPa for 5 and 15 min versus heat; storage 0, 3, 10, 28, 52 days; TAP, LAB, plus inoculated *E. coli* ATCC 25922 and *L. innocua* ATCC 33090 (~10^8 cells/mL). Detection limit 1.00 log CFU/mL; spoilage threshold 6.00 log CFU/mL. pH, CIELab ΔE*, apparent viscosity at 33 s^−1, FAME GC, HS-SPME-GC-MS. Experiments in triplicate, each analyzed in duplicate; two-way ANOVA, Tukey HSD, α = 0.05.

## Sample / data
Dairy cream, pH ≈ 6.7, a_w ≈ 0.97 (Introduction). Initial pH 6.74 ± 0.05 (batch 1) and 6.91 ± 0.14 (batch 2). Sample unit 20 mL bags; n = 3 experimental replicates.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** two-batch laboratory comparison of HPP versus commercial thermal pasteurization of dairy cream, with refrigerated storage follow-up and a pathogen-inoculation arm
- **Population / model:** industrially homogenized raw bovine dairy cream; challenge organisms *E. coli* ATCC 25922 and *L. innocua* ATCC 33090
- **n:** triplicate packs per storage day, each analyzed in duplicate; inoculum targeted ~10^8 cells/mL
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 450 MPa/5 min and 600 MPa/5 min (batch 1) and 600 MPa/5 min and 15 min (batch 2), all at 7 °C start, versus thermal pasteurization 75 °C/15 s and raw cream; all stored at 4 °C
- **Primary endpoint:** TAP, LAB, ENT and inoculated *E. coli* / *L. innocua* (log CFU/mL); pH; ΔE*; apparent viscosity (Pa·s at 33 s^−1); fatty-acid %; VOC families (µg internal-standard equivalents / 100 mL)
- **Primary result:** Thermal cream: TAP, LAB, and ENT fell below 1.00 log CFU/mL and stayed low through day 9; from day 18 TAP and LAB grew, exceeding 6.00 log CFU/mL by day 51 (Section 3.1). HPP 450 MPa/5 min: TAP immediately similar to raw cream; ENT < 1.00 log throughout; TAP and LAB > 6.00 log from day 18 (spoiled). HPP 600 MPa/5 min: slower TAP/LAB recovery; TAP increased (p ≤ 0.05) only after day 51 (Section 3.1). Batch 2: 600 MPa/15 min cut TAP and LAB about 1.4- and 1.8-fold versus raw immediately; on day 28 TAP was 4.53 ± 0.11 log CFU/mL versus thermal TAP > 6.00 log CFU/mL (Section 3.1). Abstract: inoculated *E. coli* and *L. innocua* reduced by more than 6 log to the 1.00 log CFU/mL detection limit after HPP. During storage, *E. coli* rebounded above 6.00 log by day 10 after 600 MPa/5 min then fell below 1.00 log on later days (cold intolerance suggested); *L. innocua* increased (p ≤ 0.05) from day 28 to 52 (Section 3.1). pH and ΔE* (maximum variation up to 8.43) were relatively stable (Abstract; Section 3.2). Viscosity at 33 s^−1 on day 28: HPP 600 MPa/5 min 0.028 ± 0.003 Pa·s versus heat 0.016 ± 0.002 Pa·s; on day 52, 600 MPa/15 min 0.034 ± 0.003 Pa·s (Table 2). Saturated/monounsaturated fatty acids generally unchanged (p > 0.05); polyunsaturated fatty acids decreased (p ≤ 0.05) on day 52 (Section 3.4). Total volatiles tended to increase during storage, especially acids and aliphatic hydrocarbons (Section 3.5; Table 3).
- **Cannot show:** spore inactivation (HPP is nonthermal pasteurization; Introduction); legal equivalence to U.S. milk pasteurization markers (*M. tuberculosis*, *C. burnetii*, phosphatase-negative; Section 3.1); that 5 versus 15 min at 600 MPa changes end-of-storage flora (authors say similar development by the end; Conclusions); a 30-day shelf-life extension as a regulated claim (authors’ practical inference, not a challenge study of all pathogens of concern).

## Key findings
- 450 MPa/5 min was not equivalent to 600 MPa/5 min for delaying TAP/LAB recovery (Section 3.1)—same product, different named cycle.
- Gram-negative *E. coli* was more HPP-sensitive than Gram-positive *L. innocua*, which recovered during cold storage (Section 3.1), consistent with cited barotolerance of Gram-positives.
- HPP creams were more viscous than heat-treated creams from day 3 onward (p ≤ 0.05), remaining shear-thinning emulsions (Section 3.3).
- Main fatty acids in raw cream: palmitic 24.50 ± 0.12%, oleic 20.03 ± 0.11%, myristic 11.25 ± 0.07% (Section 3.4).
- Alcohols appeared after both heat and HPP; from day 28 heat samples had more alcohol than HPP (p ≤ 0.05) (Section 3.5).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Two cream batches differed (color a* order changed; Section 3.2).
- ENT not re-tested in batch 2 because of high sensitivity in batch 1.
- Heat-treated viscosity not reported on day 52 (Table 2 dash), implying those samples were already out of the microbial acceptance window.
- Inoculated storage pattern (*E. coli* late disappearance; *Listeria* late growth) means immediate >6 log reduction is not the same as stable safety through 52 days.

## Relevance to our research question
HPP at 600 MPa can outlast 75 °C/15 s for endogenous cream flora, but 450 MPa/5 min does not; hold time at 600 MPa (5 vs 15 min) changes early counts more than end-of-shelf-life pattern; *L. innocua* recovery shows a named kill step is not a permanent safety outcome during 4 °C storage.
