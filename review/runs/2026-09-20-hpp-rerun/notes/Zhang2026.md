# Synergistic effects of Ultrasound-Assisted Ultra-High pressure on quality attributes and flavor profile of lactic acid Bacteria-Fermented fresh jujube juice

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Zhang Y, Xue Q, Yao Y, Xie J, Yan J, Yao R, Yang M, Sun X, Yu Z, Ma L (2026). Ultrasonics Sonochemistry 130:107903. doi:10.1016/j.ultsonch.2026.107903
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Zhang2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Does ultrasound-assisted ultra-high pressure (US-UHP) pretreatment, compared with pasteurization, change physicochemical quality, bioactive compounds, colloidal stability, volatile flavor, and sensory scores of mixed lactic acid bacteria–fermented Huping jujube juice?

## Methods
Laboratory factorial experiment on enzymatically hydrolyzed fresh Huping jujube juice. Five groups: uninoculated control (FJJ-C); pasteurization 85 °C, 15 min then inoculation (FJJ-P); UHP 500 MPa, 10 min then inoculation (FJJ-H); pasteurization plus ultrasound 300 W, 40 kHz, 5 s on/5 s off, 15 min, ≤35 °C applied 2 h after inoculation (FJJ-P-U); UHP plus the same ultrasound (FJJ-H-U). Mixed *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* and *Lactobacillus acidophilus* (2:1, v/v) inoculated at 1.5% (v/v) from ≥10^8 CFU/mL and fermented anaerobically at 37 °C for 40 h. Endpoints: pH, TSS, browning index, CIE Lab*, ascorbic acid, TPC, TFC, reducing sugar, ABTS/DPPH, viable LAB on MRS, zeta potential and particle size, GC-IMS, HS-SPME-GC–MS, electronic nose, trained sensory panel. One-way ANOVA and Duncan test, p < 0.05.

## Sample / data
Fresh Huping jujubes (*Ziziphus jujuba* Mill. 'Huping') from Taigu, Shanxi; juice homogenized 1:2 (w/v) with water, pectinase 0.8 g/L and cellulase 1.6 g/L at 50 °C for 4 h, then 90 °C for 10 min enzyme inactivation. All experiments in triplicate. Sensory panel n = 10 (5 male, 5 female).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Controlled laboratory factorial experiment (UHP vs pasteurization, with/without post-inoculation ultrasound) on fermented juice; not a pathogen challenge study.
- **Population / model:** Enzymatically hydrolyzed Huping jujube juice fermented with mixed *L. plantarum* and *L. acidophilus*.
- **n:** Analytical measurements in triplicate; sensory n = 10.
- **Intervention / comparator:** UHP 500 MPa, 10 min vs pasteurization 85 °C, 15 min, each with or without US (300 W, 40 kHz, 15 min pulsed, 2 h after inoculation); uninoculated control.
- **Primary endpoint:** Quality and fermentation outcomes (TPC mg/mL GAE; ABTS/DPPH %; ascorbic acid; reducing-sugar consumption; Z-average particle size d.nm; viable LAB CFU/mL; sensory scores) over 0–40 h.
- **Primary result:** At 24 h the authors report US-UHP samples with TPC 2.32 mg/mL, ABTS 87.30%, DPPH 74.86%, and higher ascorbic acid than other groups. After 40 h, FJJ-H-U reducing-sugar consumption was 54.69% (reducing sugar 33.47 mg/mL, lowest among groups, p < 0.05); TSS fell to 11.12 °Brix; terminal pH was 3.40 (FJJ-H-U) vs 3.41 (FJJ-P-U) vs 3.4–3.5 across treated groups. Particle size of treated groups stayed 33.41–53.21 d.nm at 40 h vs 1597.67 d.nm in FJJ-C. FJJ-H viable LAB was 4.77 × 10^8 CFU/mL at 40 h vs FJJ-C 1.17 × 10^5 CFU/mL (16 h FJJ-C 2.86 × 10^7 CFU/mL); other inoculated treated groups remained >1.0 × 10^8 CFU/mL. Browning index of FJJ-H and FJJ-H-U stayed 0.11–0.15 vs FJJ-C 0.26 at 40 h (p < 0.05). FJJ-P TPC peaked at 2.52 mg/mL at 16 h then fell to 1.79 mg/mL at 40 h; FJJ-H-U TFC was 0.16 mg/mL at 40 h. DPPH at 24 h: FJJ-P-U 74.97% and FJJ-H-U 74.86%. FJJ-H-U had the highest overall sensory acceptability at 24 and 40 h; TPC/TFC correlated with overall acceptance (r > 0.92).
- **Cannot show:** Pathogen kill or commercial sterile safety of UHP as a kill step (starter LAB were inoculated after UHP/pasteurization). Does not quantify indigenous spoilage/pathogen log reductions. Does not test shelf life after fermentation. Enzyme inactivation (PPO/ascorbate oxidase) is proposed, not directly assayed. Ascorbic acid units are inconsistent in the text (mg/100 g vs mg/mL).

## Key findings
- UHP (500 MPa, 10 min) is used here as a pretreatment to enable subsequent LAB fermentation, not as the sole microbial kill step. (Section 2.3)
- Measurement: UHP groups retained higher ascorbic acid at 24 and 40 h than pasteurized groups; FJJ-C retained only 0.57 mg/100 g at 40 h; FJJ-P-U 0.81 mg/mL vs FJJ-P 0.44 mg/mL at 40 h. (Section 3.5)
- Measurement: UHP limited browning (BI 0.11–0.15) versus untreated fermented juice (0.26 at 40 h). (Section 3.3)
- Author interpretation: UHP plus US cavitation releases phenolics/flavonoids and improves LAB nutrient access; TPC/TFC/reducing sugar correlated with DPPH (r > 0.94). (Section 3.16)
- Mechanism grade: cell-wall disruption and oxidase inactivation are proposed from particle-size, TPC, and color data; PPO/oxidase activity was not measured. (Sections 3.4, 3.16, Fig. 7)
- Named cycle is not interchangeable with heat: the same fermentation after 500 MPa/10 min vs 85 °C/15 min produced different color, ascorbic acid, particle size, volatiles, and sensory scores. (Sections 3.3–3.15)

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Single jujube cultivar and mixed two-strain starter; no commercial-scale HPP unit.
- Juice was heat-treated at 90 °C for 10 min during enzymatic hydrolysis before UHP, so the matrix was not raw juice.
- Sensory n = 10; no storage study after 40 h fermentation.
- Abstract TPC 2.32 mg/mL at 24 h is not restated with a group-specific table in the results narrative.

## Relevance to our research question
Directly relevant to quality when heat is not the intended kill step: 500 MPa/10 min vs 85 °C/15 min is not the same browning, ascorbic acid, colloidal, flavor, or sensory outcome in fermented jujube juice. Microbial data concern starter LAB growth after pretreatment, not pathogen inactivation.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Confirm whether abstract TPC 2.32 mg/mL at 24 h is FJJ-H-U specifically (results give FJJ-P peak 2.52 mg/mL at 16 h but no matching 24 h US-UHP table value).
- Ascorbic acid unit mismatch (mg/100 g vs mg/mL) should not be pooled.
