# Inactivation of Escherichia coli K12 in phosphate buffer saline and orange juice by high hydrostatic pressure processing combined with freezing

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Bulut S, Karatzas KAG. 2021. LWT-Food Science and Technology 136(1):110313. DOI:10.1016/j.lwt.2020.110313. CentAUR accepted manuscript.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Bulut2021.txt (accepted version)
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Does freezing PBS or orange juice before HHP at modest pressure (and 4 °C process temperature) increase E. coli K12 inactivation relative to the same pressure–time cycle on unfrozen samples, and is cell rupture the mechanism?

## Methods

E. coli K12 (University of Reading collection) grown to stationary phase (~10^9 CFU/ml), washed, resuspended in PBS pH 7.1. Double-bagged 12 ± 1 ml aliquots stored overnight at 4 °C, −24 °C, or −80 °C. HHP: Stansted Fluid Power, 16 mm ID, 55 ml; 80% alcohol / 20% castor oil PTM; jacket 4 ± 1 °C; load-to-pressurize <30 s; decompression <5 s. PBS study: rotatable CCD (α = 1.4142), pressure 159–441 MPa, time 1–11 min, 3 centre-point replicates (300 MPa, 5 min). Enumeration: spot plate on LB agar, 37 °C 24 h (controls) or 48 h (pressurized). SEM of cells; NanoDrop DNA/RNA in supernatant after 13 000 rpm, 10 min. Orange juice: freshly squeezed, inoculated ~10^9 CFU/ml, pH adjusted to 3.2, 4.5, or 5.8 (citric acid or NaOH); factorial 3 pH × 3 times (5, 10, 15 min) at 250 MPa, n = 3, frozen (−80 °C) vs unfrozen (4 °C); extra frozen juice runs at 20 °C PTM. Design-Expert RSM; p < 0.05.

## Sample / data

PBS model liquid and fresh orange juice. Overnight freeze or chill before pressure. Uninoculated juice: no growth on LB (<10 CFU/ml). After inoculation and overnight 4 °C, juice counts 2.2 × 10^8 to 5.3 × 10^8 CFU/ml; mean 8.5 ± 0.04 log CFU/ml (n = 9) across pH.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Response-surface laboratory inactivation study (PBS) plus factorial juice challenge; SEM and nucleic-acid leakage as mechanism assays.
- **Population / model:** E. coli K12 in PBS (pH 7.1) and in pH-adjusted orange juice.
- **n:** PBS CCD with 3 centre-point replicates; NanoDrop triplicate readings; juice factorial n = 3. Inoculum ~10^9 CFU/ml (PBS stock) or ~10^8–10^9 in juice.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Frozen (−24 or −80 °C) vs unfrozen (4 °C) then HHP at 4 ± 1 °C; juice also frozen vs unfrozen at 250 MPa, 5–15 min, pH 3.2/4.5/5.8; frozen juice also compared at 4 °C vs 20 °C PTM.
- **Primary endpoint:** Log reduction CFU/ml; supporting DNA/RNA (ng/µl) and SEM morphology.
- **Primary result:** Unfrozen PBS: max 1.83 log CFU/ml after 400 MPa, 9 min, 4 °C. Same cycle on frozen samples: 5.63 log (−24 °C freeze) and 6.83 log (−80 °C freeze) (Abstract). Freeze–thaw alone (no pressure): 1.73 ± 0.12 log (−24 °C) and 1.49 ± 0.07 log (−80 °C). 1 s at 300 MPa: 2.60 log (−24 °C frozen) and 3.52 log (−80 °C frozen) vs 0.01 log unfrozen. Unfrozen juice at 250 MPa: ≤0.42 log at pH 4.5 and 5.8 (all times); 3.84 ± 0.33 log at pH 3.2 after 15 min. Frozen (−80 °C) juice, 250 MPa, 15 min: Abstract 4.88, 4.15, and 4.61 log at pH 3.2, 4.5, and 5.8; Results text 4.88 (from 3.84 unfrozen), 4.22 (from 0.35), and 4.41 (from 0.42) at those pH values. Frozen juice pH 3.2, 10 min, 250 MPa: 5.0 log at 20 °C PTM vs 4.6 log at 4 °C PTM. DNA/RNA: untreated 28.43 ± 0.57 and 24.37 ± 0.06 ng/µl; freeze–thaw 181.37 ± 3.07 and 151.40 ± 1.93; pressure after freeze 537.73 ± 7.60 and 450.60 ± 6.56 ng/µl. RSM (unfrozen): log reduction = 0.022 + 1.62×10^−5 P − 0.068 t + 6.356×10^−4 P·t (r² = 0.94). Frozen −24 °C: 1.346 + 4.37×10^−3 P + 0.309 t (r² = 0.93). Frozen −80 °C: 0.861 + 8.55×10^−3 P + 0.334 t (r² = 0.93).
- **Cannot show:** Pathogenic E. coli O157:H7 (K12 used as a more pressure-resistant surrogate, citing Torres et al. 2016); enzyme inactivation in juice (authors call for that work); quality/sensory of pressurized frozen juice; spore control; industrial package size (authors flag this).

## Key findings

- Measurement: The same 400 MPa/9 min cycle at 4 °C produced 1.83 log in unfrozen PBS vs 6.83 log after −80 °C freezing (Abstract; Section 3.1.3).
- Measurement: Brief 1 s / 300 MPa already gave 2.60–3.52 log in frozen PBS vs 0.01 log unfrozen (Section 3.1.3).
- Measurement: During come-up near 315 MPa, PTM showed ~55 MPa pressure drop and ~2.5 °C (also described as 4–5 °C) temperature drop, interpreted as ice I → denser ice polymorphs (Section 3.1.2–3.1.3).
- Measurement: SEM showed wall disintegration only in frozen-then-pressurized cells, not in unfrozen pressurized cells (Section 3.1.5; Figure 3).
- Measurement: Nucleic acids in supernatant rose after freeze–thaw and further after freeze + HHP (Section 3.1.6).
- Author interpretation: Main inactivation mechanism in frozen PBS is physical cell rupture (SEM + leakage), not the same as unfrozen HHP membrane-intact inactivation (Conclusions). Proposed, supported by those measurements.
- Measurement: In juice, freezing removed most of the pH dependence seen in unfrozen HHP (pH 4.5/5.8 unfrozen ≤0.42 log vs ~4 log when frozen) (Section 3.2.3).
- Measurement: Freezing juice alone did not reduce E. coli (8.51 ± 0.03 log) unlike PBS freeze–thaw (Section 3.2.2 vs 3.1.1).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Authors: need other pathogens and enzymes, package-size effects, and cost trade-off of extra freezing (Conclusions).
- Accepted manuscript; authors advise citing the publisher version.
- Surrogate K12, not O157:H7.
- Process temperature of the vessel was 4 °C even for frozen samples (pressure-assisted thawing / ice-phase change), not subzero holding of the whole vessel.
- Abstract vs Results juice log values at pH 4.5 and 5.8 differ slightly (4.15/4.61 vs 4.22/4.41).
- Table 1 validation numbers are referenced but not fully printed in this .txt.

## Relevance to our research question

Shows that a named pressure–time cycle is not the same kill if the food is frozen first: 250–400 MPa plus freezing approached 5-log E. coli reductions that unfrozen HHP at the same settings did not. Mechanism evidence (SEM, nucleic acids) is stronger than most HPP papers in this set.
