# Effect of High-Pressure Processing Operating Parameters on Microbial Inactivation and Bioactive Protein Preservation in Bovine Milk: A Systematic Review

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Sykora R, Mark C, Biondi Ryan M, Barman B, Pitino M, Dallas DC. Compr Rev Food Sci Food Saf. 2026;25(1):e70324. doi:10.1111/1541-4337.70324. PMID:41235504. PMC12616593.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Sykora2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (systematic review of HPP in bovine milk: pathogen lethality versus bioactive protein retention)

## Research question
Which HPP pressure, time, and temperature combinations achieve FDA-style >5-log reductions of vegetative pathogens in liquid bovine milk, and do those same cycles preserve native bioactive whey proteins?

## Methods
PRISMA systematic review registered on Open Science Framework (12 Feb 2024). Searches of PubMed, EMBASE, MEDLINE, and Web of Science (20 Feb 2024; no date limit). Population: raw liquid bovine milk (whole, semi-skim, skim; colostrum included in a subset). Exposure: isostatic HPP without homogenization. Outcomes: microbial lethality (converted to log CFU/mL reduction) and native protein % retention, both requiring before/after measurements. Mixed-model ANOVA (JMP Pro 18) on pressure, time, and temperature. PlotDigitizer used for graphical data.

## Sample / data
17,198 records captured; 89 articles included after screening. 65 papers on pathogenic bacteria; 24 on protein; only 4 papers reported both (2 colostrum, 2 mature milk). Most-studied bacteria: *L. monocytogenes* (n = 34 papers), *E. coli* (n = 24), *S. aureus* (n = 19), *S. enterica* (n = 8). Proteins: β-Lg (n = 8), α-LA (n = 6), ALP (n = 5), IgG (n = 4), LF (n = 3). Pressures 50–900 MPa (most 400–600 MPa); times 1–600 min (most 5–15 min); temperatures −20 to 75 °C (most 20–30 °C).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** PRISMA systematic review with mixed-model ANOVA of published HPP trials in bovine milk
- **Population / model:** raw liquid bovine milk (fat-fractionated retail identities; some colostrum papers)
- **n:** 89 included articles (65 microbial, 24 protein; 4 with both endpoints)
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 50–900 MPa, typically 5–15 min at 20–30 °C, versus untreated milk; some cycling and antimicrobial-additive hurdles; authors compare conceptually to HTST/LTLT but do not meta-analyze thermal pasteurization head-to-head (Limitation, Section 3.9)
- **Primary endpoint:** log CFU/mL reduction relative to a >5-log pasteurization bar; percent retention of native IgG, lactoferrin (LF), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and β-lactoglobulin (β-Lg)
- **Primary result:** Averaged across heterogeneous time/temperature combinations, >5-log reductions required about 450 MPa for *L. monocytogenes*, 500 MPa for *S. enterica*, and 600 MPa for *S. aureus*; 600 MPa was also the apparent minimum for several *E. coli* strains, but strain-averaged *E. coli* did not reach >5 log (Abstract; Section 3.3). ANOVA: pressure was the strongest contributor to lethality (*L. monocytogenes* F = 171.12, P < 0.0001; *S. aureus* F = 87.73, P < 0.0001; *E. coli* F = 22.28, P < 0.0001); time was not significant for *S. aureus* (P = 0.06139) (Table 1). Pressurization/depressurization without an adequate hold (0–2 min at 20–30 °C) often failed to give 5-log *L. monocytogenes* even at >551 MPa; holds >5 min were needed at moderate temperature (Section 3.3.1). Protein: LF was most pressure-resistant, then ALP, IgG, then β-Lg; average 50% denaturation of β-Lg occurred at 200–300 MPa versus ~400 MPa (IgG), ~600 MPa (ALP), and ~800 MPa (LF) (Section 3.7). Ramos et al. as synthesized: 600 MPa/15 min/20 °C was the only listed cycle that reduced *L. monocytogenes*, *P. aeruginosa*, and *S. aureus* by >5 log in mature skim milk, with retentions IgG 57.02%, LF 39.0%, LPO 100.0%, α-LA 94.0%, β-Lg 20.1% (Section 3.7). Pressure was the strongest protein-denaturation factor (F = 52.69, P < 0.0001); time was not significant (P = 0.0589) (Table 1). Milk fat can protect *E. coli*: Garcia et al. as cited, 600 MPa/15 min/20 °C gave 1.6-log reduction in 3.6% fat milk versus 2.3 log (1.55% fat) and 3.0 log (0.05% fat skim) (Section 3.3.4).
- **Cannot show:** that HPP is FDA-approved for U.S. milk (it is not; Introduction); that any single averaged MPa guarantees 5-log *E. coli* (strain-dependent); a quantitative superiority versus HTST/LTLT (no direct comparison meta-analysis); consumer health outcomes of HPP milk (Conclusions).

## Key findings
- Only 4 of 89 papers measured microbes and proteins together, so “safe and bioactive” optimization remains incomplete (Abstract; Section 3.2).
- The same pressure can be >5 log or <5 log depending on time, temperature, strain, and assay: at 301–450 MPa, *L. monocytogenes* reductions spanned 0.39–8.87 log across papers (Section 3.3.1).
- Extreme heat (>60 °C) or extreme time (>40 min) can deliver 5-log reductions regardless of other parameters, but those conditions also substantially denature proteins and leave the nonthermal rationale (Conclusions).
- Dual cycling at 400 MPa increased *E. coli* inactivation by 1–1.5 log versus a single cycle; 1 to 10 cycles (total hold 20 min) raised reductions from 1 to 4.3 log, with diminishing returns, and still no >5 log at 400 MPa/20 °C (Section 3.4).
- Antimicrobials plus HPP added 0.8–3 log versus HPP alone in the collected papers (Section 3.5).
- Assay method changes apparent protein retention: identical 500 MPa/15 min/20 °C gave β-Lg 41.19% by ELISA versus 28.47% by HPLC (Section 3.8).
- Mechanism of HPP kill **cited**: membrane permeability, osmotic/ionic disruption, enzyme and replication arrest (Introduction). Protein unfolding **cited**: water into hydrophobic cavities above ~400 MPa (Introduction).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- High inter-study variation in equipment, come-up rates (1.3–100 MPa/s pressurization), strains, and fat level (Sections 3.2, 3.9).
- Averaging pressures across unlike time/temperature combinations blurs individual successful milder cycles.
- No head-to-head meta-analysis versus HTST/LTLT (Section 3.9).

## Relevance to our research question
The hinge paper for “a named pressure–time cycle is not the same safety or quality outcome” in milk: 600 MPa/15 min/20 °C can exceed 5 log for some Gram-positives while leaving only ~20% native β-Lg and ~39% LF, and *E. coli*/fat/strain can keep the same cycle below 5 log.
