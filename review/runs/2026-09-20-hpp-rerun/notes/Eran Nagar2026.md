# Comparative Assessment of Semi-Industrial Thermal and Non-Thermal Pasteurization Technologies for Red Globe Grape Juice

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Eran Nagar E, Romano A, Shazman A, Lesmes U, Shpigelman A (2026). Foods 15:672. doi:10.3390/foods15040672
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Eran Nagar2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
When HPP, ohmic heating (OH), and plate-heat-exchanger thermal pasteurization (TP) are matched to the same ~3-log reduction of indigenous aerobes in Red Globe grape juice, do they produce the same PPO inactivation, CIELAB color, FRAP antioxidant capacity, and monomeric anthocyanins?

## Methods
Semi-industrial Technion pilot plant. Two independent 70–75 kg grape batches, ~30 L juice each. TSS 17.0 ± 0.5 °Brix, pH 3.56 ± 0.02. Conditions pre-screened for ~3-log PCA reduction. HPP: 30 mL vacuum bags, 500 MPa, 5 min, circulating water 25 °C, product ≤40 °C (Stansted S-FL-850-09-W). OH: Raztek 50 kW, 1.1 L/min, electro-heat to 120 °C, 3 s hold, ~12 atm backpressure, 50 Hz up to 8 kV. TP: plate heat exchanger 90 °C, 1 L/min, 1 min hold, exit ~25 °C. PCA and OGYE drop plates, 30 °C, 72 h. PPO with catechol at 420 nm. CIELAB ΔE vs fresh. FRAP (µM vitamin C eq). pH-differential anthocyanins as cyanidin-3,5-diglucoside eq. ANOVA + Tukey, p < 0.05.

## Sample / data
Red Globe grapes (Dole South Africa). Independent processing twice. n = 9 for PPO (three batches in triplicate); n = 6 for microbes and FRAP (two batches in triplicate); n = 4 for anthocyanins and color (two batches in duplicate).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Semi-industrial comparative pasteurization study with microbial-equivalence matching (not a 5-log pathogen validation).
- **Population / model:** Mechanically pressed, 304-mesh-filtered Red Globe (*Vitis vinifera*) juice; indigenous aerobes, yeasts and molds.
- **n:** Two independent ~30 L batches; PPO n = 9; microbes/FRAP n = 6; color/anthocyanins n = 4.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 500 MPa, 5 min vs OH 120 °C, 3 s vs TP 90 °C, 1 min vs frozen fresh juice.
- **Primary endpoint:** Matched ~3-log total aerobic reduction (CFU/mL); PPO activity (min^−1 mL^−1); ΔE; FRAP; total monomeric anthocyanins (mg/L).
- **Primary result:** Fresh PCA 1.5 ± 0.2 × 10^5 CFU/mL reduced to 2.2–3.0 × 10^2 CFU/mL (~3.0 log) for all three processes; yeasts/molds from 1.7 ± 0.3 × 10^4 to 10^1–10^0 CFU/mL (3.0–4.0 log). PPO: fresh 58 ± 13; OH 5 ± 2; TP 4.6 ± 0.7 min^−1 mL^−1 (>90% inactivation, p < 0.05); HPP 41 ± 8 (~30% reduction, not significant vs fresh). ΔE: OH 6.5 ± 2.4, TP 6.4 ± 1.2 (“great” color difference); HPP L* a* b* not different from fresh (p > 0.05). L*: OH 28.5 ± 2.8, TP 27.9 ± 0.9 vs fresh 29.6 ± 2.2 and HPP 29.2 ± 2.3. Fresh b* 2.2 ± 0.3 became ~−3.0 after OH/TP; a* from 5.9 ± 1.1 to <2.7. Fresh anthocyanins 3.30 ± 0.17 mg/L; significantly higher only after OH vs fresh (p < 0.05). FRAP not statistically different across treatments (p > 0.05).
- **Cannot show:** 5-log pathogen reduction (FDA Juice HACCP). Shelf-life or residual PPO browning during storage (samples frozen after process). Continuous industrial HPP (HPP was 30 mL bags). Individual anthocyanin species (pH-differential total only).

## Key findings
- Named cycles matched for ~3-log aerobes are not the same quality/enzyme outcome: thermal OH/TP inactivate PPO >90% but darken juice (ΔE ~6.4–6.5); 500 MPa/5 min keeps color near fresh but leaves ~70% of PPO activity. (Sections 3.2–3.5, Conclusions)
- Measurement: yeasts/molds fell 3–4 log under all three matched processes. (Section 3.2)
- Author interpretation: HPP disrupts non-covalent protein interactions only, so PPO remains; heat breaks structure irreversibly. Apparent anthocyanin/FRAP increases after heat may reflect extractability vs degradation. (Sections 3.3–3.4)
- Mechanism grade: PPO inactivation is directly measured. Anthocyanin “release” and Maillard masking of redness are proposed, not chemically confirmed (no HMF assay).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- No refrigerated shelf-life study despite residual PPO being the HPP limitation they flag.
- HPP volume (30 mL) vs OH/TP flow (~30 L) is not equipment-matched, only microbially matched.
- Authors used Grammarly and ChatGPT for language editing (Acknowledgments).
- Low Red Globe pulp anthocyanins (3.30 mg/L) limit pigment comparisons with red-wine grapes.

## Relevance to our research question
Direct answer to “when is a named pressure–time cycle not the same safety or quality outcome?”: 500 MPa/5 min ≈ 3-log aerobes like 90 °C/1 min or OH 120 °C/3 s, but PPO and color diverge. Safety here is indigenous 3-log, not pathogen 5-log.
