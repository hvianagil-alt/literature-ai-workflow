# Continuous High-pressure Cooling-Assisted Homogenization Process for Stabilization of Apple Juice

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Szczepańska J, Skąpska S, Marszałek K. Food and Bioprocess Technology. 2021;14:1101–1117. doi:10.1007/s11947-021-02611-4. Published 24 March 2021.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun-gapfill/Szczepańska2021_26114.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
When HPH (not hydrostatic HPP) at 100–200 MPa with valve cooling (product ≤35 °C) is applied to NFC apple juice, what happens to native microbes, oxidoreductases/hydrolases, particle size, colour, vitamin C, and polyphenols—and does 150 MPa (industrial maximum cited) with extra passes equal 200 MPa?

## Methods
Laboratory HPH 2000/4-DH5 (IKA); 100, 150, 200 MPa; 150 MPa also 1–5 passes; flow 1.2 L/h, retention ~30 s; circulating cooler so product temperature did not exceed 35 °C. Untreated juice as control. Storage of 150 MPa juice at 4 ± 1 °C up to 28 days (7-day intervals). Counts: PCA 30 °C/72 h (ISO 4833-1); yeasts/moulds DRBC 25 °C/5–7 days (ISO 21527-1). Enzymes: PPO, POD, PG residual activity vs untreated; PME assayed but not detectable. PSD (Mastersizer), viscosity, turbidity, CIE L*a*b*, BI, HPLC vitamin C and polyphenols, TPC, DPPH•/ABTS•+. One-way ANOVA, Tukey, 95% CI. All analyses in triplicate. Separate colour experiment: different apple lot ± 100 mg/L vitamin C, 200 MPa, storage 21 days.

## Sample / data
Unclarified NFC juice from mixed market apple varieties; pH 3.65, density 1.045 g/cm³, acidity 3.42% malic acid, TSS 10.98 °Brix, total sugars 106.43 g/L. Human food (juice). 150 MPa chosen because it is the maximum pressure the authors state is available in industrial devices.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** laboratory HPH process study with refrigerated storage; untreated juice control
- **Population / model:** unclarified NFC apple juice (mixed varieties); native microbiota and native enzymes (not inoculated pathogens)
- **n:** triplicate analyses (mean ± SD); storage series on 150 MPa juice
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPH 100, 150, 200 MPa (1 pass) vs untreated; 150 MPa with 1–5 passes; 150 MPa stored 0–28 days at 4 °C. Product ≤35 °C (cooling-assisted). Extra colour trial: 200 MPa ± 100 mg/L vitamin C.
- **Primary endpoint:** total aerobic count, yeasts, moulds (log CFU/mL); residual PPO/POD/PG (%); vitamin C as DHAA (mg/L); TPC (mg GAE/L); ΔE and BI; PSD diameters (µm); turbidity (NTU)
- **Primary result:** Fresh juice TMC 5.36 log CFU/mL, yeasts 3.75, moulds 3.57. HPH cut TMC by 0.6–1.4 log (maximum 1.4 log at 200 MPa); yeasts 0.2–0.6 log; moulds 0.3–0.6 log. Extra passes at 150 MPa did **not** significantly change TMC, yeasts, or moulds. After 7 days at 4 °C (150 MPa), yeasts rose 1.2 log; TMC and moulds unchanged; TMC exceeded the fresh-juice initial level by day 21. 200 MPa: PPO −21.5%, POD −16.4% vs fresh; multi-pass and 28-day storage did not significantly change PPO/POD. PG did not change significantly immediately; at day 14 PG −38.5% and stayed similar. PME not detectable before or after HPH. Vitamin C: only DHAA found (no AA); fresh 128.57 ± 2.16 mg/L; 63.8% retained at 100 MPa and 38.2% at 200 MPa (200 MPa: 49.08 ± 4.43 mg/L; authors also state 61.8% decrease at 200 MPa). Passes did not change vitamin C significantly. 150 MPa storage: DHAA −34.1% by day 7 and −91.2% by day 28 (5.25 ± 0.07 mg/L). TPC fresh 648.83 ± 17.62 mg GAE/L; almost +20% at 150 MPa; −5% (authors also write 4.3% decrease) at 200 MPa. Quercetin +52% and phloridzin +47% at 150 MPa; quercetin −11% at 200 MPa. Five passes: TPC −8% max; phloridzin −21%, quercetin −17%. Day 28 TPC 471.00 ± 15.79 mg GAE/L (−39%). ΔE 3.33 (100 MPa), 4.15 (150 MPa), 7.04 (200 MPa); 200 MPa ΔE = 7.0, visible to an inexperienced observer. Turbidity 1438.3 NTU fresh vs 1272.8 at 200 MPa. Dv(50) 308 µm fresh → 15.8 µm at 100 MPa; D[3,2] and D[4,3] −73.4% and −92.7% at 100 MPa. Apparent viscosity lowest at 200 MPa. Authors estimate 7-day refrigerated shelf-life at 150 MPa for colour, TPC, and antioxidant potential.
- **Cannot show:** a 5-log pathogen kill (native counts only; max 1.4 log); that HPH equals hydrostatic HPP; that 150 MPa/5 passes equals 200 MPa; spore inactivation; that ≤35 °C means no local heating in the valve.

## Key findings
- Measurement: 200 MPa was the strongest microbial and PPO/POD reduction; still only 1.4 log TMC and ~20% oxidoreductase loss (Abstract; Conclusions; Fig. 2A, Fig. 3A).
- Measurement: industrial-relevant 150 MPa with up to 5 passes did not improve microbial kill vs one pass (Results, Microbiological Analyses).
- Measurement: a named cycle is not one quality outcome — 150 MPa raised TPC ~20% while 200 MPa slightly lowered TPC and cut vitamin C to 38.2% retained (Table 1, Fig. 6, Table 4).
- Measurement: colour and vitamin C collapsed after day 7 at 4 °C (ΔE 22.10 at day 14; vitamin C 9.07 mg/L) (Table 1).
- Author interpretation: 7-day shelf-life at 150 MPa/4 °C (Conclusions).
- Proposed mechanism: shear/cavitation in the valve disrupts cells and particles; residual PG correlates with viscosity during storage (r = 0.729, p ≤ 0.05). Vitamin C loss attributed to oxygen, shear, and possible Be-Cu seal erosion (cited), despite bulk T ≤ 35 °C.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- HPH is only for liquids (Introduction).
- Native microbiota, not inoculated *Listeria* or spores; 1.4 log is not a pasteurization claim.
- 150 MPa storage used one pressure for the shelf-life estimate.
- Table 1 lettering uses “p > 0.05” in the caption while the Statistical Analysis section states Tukey at 95% confidence; treat pairwise letters as the authors’ grouping, not a re-computed p.
- Colour + vitamin C add-on used a different raw material.

## Relevance to our research question
**Gap-fill** paper retrieved after the literature table for the primary HPH (homogenization) in apple juice gap (Szczepańska2021_26114). Heat is not the designed kill step (cooled valve, product ≤35 °C), yet HPH is **not** hydrostatic HPP: 100–200 MPa dynamic homogenization gave ≤1.4 log native kill, incomplete enzyme inactivation, and cycle-specific quality (TPC up at 150 MPa, vitamin C down at 200 MPa). Extra passes ≠ higher pressure for safety.

## Open questions / things to verify
Abstract says vitamin C decreased by 62% and TPC increased by 20%; Conclusions specify 61.8% vitamin C and 4.3% TPC loss at 200 MPa and the ~20% TPC gain at 150 MPa. Use the pressure-specific values, not the abstract blend.
