# High Hydrostatic Pressure Induced Changes in the Physicochemical and Functional Properties of Milk and Dairy Products: A Review

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Serna-Hernandez SO, Escobedo-Avellaneda Z, García-García R, Rostro-Alanis MdJ, Welti-Chanes J. Foods. 2021;10(8):1867. doi:10.3390/foods10081867. PMC8391368.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/SernaHernandez2021_081867.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
When HPP is used to pasteurise milk, what happens to lipids, casein, whey proteins, minerals, pH, colour, turbidity, emulsion stability, rheology, and sensory quality, and how do those component changes affect cheese, cream, and buttermilk?

## Methods
Narrative review of bovine (and some caprine) milk and dairy products after HPP. Opens with a microbial-inactivation table, then physicochemical/functional sections. Authors also report a preliminary optical-microscopy look at fat globules in raw vs homogenised HPP milk from their laboratory (not a full trial).

## Sample / data
Cited raw, pasteurised, UHT, and sterile whole or skim milks; micellar casein concentrates; dairy products. Table 1 compiles log reductions.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** narrative review of HPP milk and dairy (plus authors’ qualitative fat-globule microscopy)
- **Population / model:** bovine milk primarily; some caprine milk and dairy products
- **n:** not applicable for the review; Table 1 entries are individual cited challenge studies
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP typically 100–800 MPa (often 400–600 MPa, 1–10 min, ~5–40 °C) vs thermal pasteurisation (LTLT, HTST) or UHT
- **Primary endpoint:** microbial log reductions; casein micelle size (nm); whey-protein denaturation (%); fat-globule size; functional properties
- **Primary result (compiled):** Authors state 600 MPa for 5 min is adequate for 5–7 log reductions, comparable to traditional pasteurisation, but spores prevent HPP-only sterilisation (Abstract; Section 2). Table 1 examples in raw whole milk: 600 MPa/5 min/40 °C, total count and *E. coli* 3.00 log; *L. monocytogenes* 0.65 log at 350 MPa/20 min/10 °C vs 2.10 log at 400 MPa/10 min/10 °C; *E. coli* 0.85 / 5.60 / 6.80 log at 400 MPa/1 min, 600 MPa/3 min, 600 MPa/5 min (25 °C); *Salmonella* spp. 1.09, 2.36, 3.28, 6.27 log at 400 MPa/1 min, 400/5 min, 500/5 min, 600/5 min (25 °C); *L. monocytogenes* 1.42, 5.48, 5.91 log at 400/5, 500/5, 600/1 min (25 °C); TVC 3.95 log and Enterobacteriaceae, LAB, *Pseudomonas* below detection at 600 MPa/3 min/25 °C; *L. monocytogenes* 6.47 log and TVC 5.09 log at 600 MPa/10 min/25 °C. Pasteurised whole: *E. coli* K12 0.50–1.50 log at 200–300 MPa. *S. aureus* 1.90–3.40 log at 500 MPa/10 min depending on 5 vs 20 °C. UHT whole: *S. typhimurium* 5.10 log at 600 MPa/10 min/25 °C; *L. monocytogenes* 6.20 log at 500 MPa/10 min/25 °C. Whole milk 600 MPa: casein z-average 140 nm vs 222 nm LTLT and 247 nm HTST. 4% MCC: z-average 186 (control), 167 (300 MPa), 129 (450), 101 nm (600 MPa/5 min). Pressures >300 MPa cause irreversible micelle-size decrease; ~250 MPa may increase size via β-Lg–κ-casein (can be reversible). Whey (Table 3): α-LA 10% denatured at 600 MPa, 50% at 800 MPa; β-LG denaturation begins 100 MPa, 70–80% at 400 MPa, 90% at 800 MPa; immunoglobulins begin 300 MPa, 35% at 500 MPa; BSA resistant at 400 MPa. Fat globules in SDS/EDTA buffer: mean diameter 1.13 μm unchanged across 100–800 MPa/30 min; in water, apparent diameter rose up to 700 MPa because casein scattering fell. Mild 250 MPa without heat is said to promote lipoprotein lipase lipolysis. 400 MPa microscopy samples showed fat-globule agglomeration unlike 500–600 MPa.
- **Cannot show:** HPP sterilisation of milk; that 600 MPa/5 min equals heat for every pathogen (authors flag missing *S. aureus*, *Coxiella burnetii*, *Mycobacterium* coverage); original cheese yield numbers in this extraction.

## Key findings
- Mechanism (cited): inactivation via cell wall/membrane, genetic material, and cytoplasm damage; casein changes via compressed water disrupting hydrophobic bonds and solubilising micellar calcium phosphate; covalent protein bonds remain, unlike heat (Sections 2 and 4).
- Author interpretation: 300–600 MPa was once thought equivalent to pasteurisation; accumulated evidence supports 600 MPa/5 min as a pasteurisation alternative for the listed organisms, with larger functional shifts than thermal milk (smaller micelles, selective whey denaturation).
- Quality: HPP milk often has better sensory scores than heat-treated milk in the cited literature, but emulsion stability can fail if globules agglomerate (400 MPa observation).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Vegetative spores survive HPP; heat or other hurdles needed for sterilisation.
- Physicochemical literature is thinner than microbial literature.
- Authors’ fat-globule work is preliminary visual analysis, not a quantified trial.
- Resistance depends on P, t, T, milk composition, and growth phase.

## Relevance to our research question
Intake related paper from OpenAlex related-to-seeds search (not a post-table gap-fill). Milk shows that **600 MPa/5 min is not interchangeable with 400 MPa/5 min or with HTST** for either safety (*E. coli* 0.85 log at 400 MPa/1 min vs 6.80 log at 600 MPa/5 min) or structure (casein 140 nm after 600 MPa vs ~220–250 nm after heat; β-LG mostly denatured by 400 MPa while α-LA is not).

## Open questions / things to verify
Abstract “5–7 log” is a synthesis; Table 1 shows organism- and cycle-specific values from 0.50 to 6.80 log. Do not treat the authors’ microscope notes as a measured D[3,2].
