# Impact of High-Pressure Processing (HPP) on Listeria monocytogenes—An Overview of Challenges and Responses

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Wiśniewski P, Chajęcka-Wierzchowska W, Zadernowska A. Foods. 2024;13(1):14. doi:10.3390/foods13010014. PMC10778341. PMID:38201041.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun-gapfill/Wiśniewski2023_010014.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What do published HPP cycles (pressure, time, temperature, matrix) actually do to *Listeria monocytogenes* inactivation, refrigerated recovery/VBNC, virulence-gene expression, and antibiotic resistance—i.e. when is a named cycle not the same safety outcome?

## Methods
Narrative overview (not a systematic review, no PRISMA). Compiles inactivation, storage-recovery, virulence, and resistance studies in foods and broths. Tables 3–5 are literature syntheses, not new experiments.

## Sample / data
Cited matrices include TSB/TSBYE/BHI, citrate buffer, Queso Fresco, UHT and raw milk, mozzarella, smoked salmon, dry-cured ham (aw 0.88), Camembert-type cheese, cooked pork sausage, coconut water, apple cubes, donor milk, and meat simulation medium. HPP window typically 100–600 MPa (some recovery studies to 900 MPa), 1–30 min, 4–50 °C. Frozen vegetables appear as a documented *L. monocytogenes* vehicle (Introduction; CDC outbreak citations), not as an HPP matrix in Table 3.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** narrative review of HPP vs *L. monocytogenes* inactivation, injury/recovery, virulence, and antibiotic resistance
- **Population / model:** *L. monocytogenes* (single strains or cocktails of 2–14 isolates; serotypes including 1/2a, 1/2b, 1/2c, 4b) in human foods and laboratory media
- **n:** not applicable (review); individual cited studies use one strain to cocktails of 14
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP typically 100–600 MPa, a few to several minutes, room temperature or lower (examples 350–600 MPa, 1–20 min, 4–40 °C) vs untreated; some studies add bacteriocins, endolysin, coatings, sodium lactate, or freeze-drying
- **Primary endpoint:** log CFU/g or log CFU/mL reduction; presence/absence of recovery during storage; relative expression of virulence/stress genes
- **Primary result (compiled):** Commercial HPP is described as 100–600 MPa, a few–several minutes, room temperature or lower, with liquid pressure-transfer medium (Abstract). Pressures >400 MPa often reduced *L. monocytogenes* below detection in the compiled Table 3, but that is **not** a universal cycle outcome. Park et al. (2022): 500 MPa, 4 °C, raw beef cocktail of six strains — 3.90 log CFU/g in 2 min vs ≥6.50 log CFU/g in 7 min. Stratakos et al.: 400–600 MPa, 18 °C, raw milk cocktail of five — 5.65 log (3 min) vs 5.91 log (5 min); 500 MPa/5 min similar to 600 MPa/3 min. Alpas and Bozoglu (2002): five-strain cocktail, juice-type dependent reductions 0.92–3.53 (25 °C), 8.20–8.70 (40 °C), 7.78–8.04 (50 °C) CFU/mL. Perez-Baltar et al. (2021): dry-cured ham aw 0.88, strains S2 and S7-2 — 0.80 log at 450 MPa/10 min; 1.30 and 1.50 log at 600 MPa/5 min. Bruschi et al. (2017): 14 strains, viability loss 0.00–2.76 log (300 MPa), 0.06–6.31 (400 MPa), 0.75–7.23 (500 MPa); one fermented-sausage isolate <1.00 log at 500 MPa; antibiotic-resistant strains more barotolerant at 400 MPa. Misiou et al. (2018): 500 MPa/10 min/25 °C — below detection in UHT milk and mozzarella but only 1.50 log in smoked salmon. Batty et al. (2019): 450 or 550 MPa/10 min reduced *L. monocytogenes* >5 log in Camembert-type cheese but deteriorated consumer-unacceptable appearance. Jarzynka et al.: HPP alone or HPP plus freeze-drying of donor milk reduced *L. monocytogenes* below detection (7.91 log CFU/mL) even after six months of storage. Recovery: sublethal injury and refrigerated regrowth documented after 200–600 MPa, 3–30 min, 4–45 °C, storage 14–75 days at 4–37 °C (Table 4). Tomasula et al. (2014): Queso Fresco 600 MPa/3 min — regeneration within 1.00 log CFU/g by 42 days, 8.24 ± 0.41 log CFU/g at 60 days (4 °C). Bozoglu et al. (2004): no colonies immediately after ≤550 MPa, then growth after 6 days at 4 °C (or 1 day at 22–30 °C). Nakaura et al. (2019): 500 MPa, 25 °C, 10 min — most injured cells recovered during 42 days at 5, 10, or 15 °C to or above the initial count; recovery at 0 °C was limited. Koseki and Yamamoto (2007): recovery after 10–30 days at 20 °C even when none were detected immediately; no recovery at 37 °C. σB is cited as controlling acid, high hydrostatic pressure, and freeze survival (Wemekamp-Kamphuis et al., 2004, in the virulence section). Virulence-gene patterns after 450 MPa/10 min or 600 MPa/5 min in dry-cured ham were strain-dependent (overexpression in serotype 1/2b vs suppression in 1/2c; Pérez-Baltar 2020/2021).
- **Cannot show:** original freeze–thaw + HPP challenge data (frozen vegetables are a cited vehicle, not an HPP matrix here); a single 400–600 MPa cycle that equals 5-log kill in every food; that plate-count “below detection” means cells are dead rather than VBNC; that HPP always lowers virulence or antibiotic resistance.

## Key findings
- Measurement (compiled Table 3): the same nominal pressure (e.g. 500–600 MPa) yielded BD in some matrices and <2 log in low-aw ham or smoked salmon (Section 3.1.2–3.1.3).
- Measurement: hold time at the same 500 MPa/4 °C changed beef reductions from 3.90 to ≥6.50 log between 2 and 7 min (Park et al., Section 3.1.1).
- Measurement: recovery after HPP can return counts to ~8 log in cheese by day 60 despite an immediate BD result (Tomasula; Table 4).
- Author interpretation: HPP can damage cells into a VBNC-like state rather than kill them; refrigerated storage ≥42 days can restore viability (Section 3.2.2).
- Author interpretation: antibiotic-resistant strains can be more barotolerant at 400 MPa (Bruschi; Section 3.3).
- Proposed mechanism (cited, not newly measured): σB/PrfA stress–virulence axis and cell-wall repair genes (*murG*, *murC*, *pbp2A*) during recovery (Duru 2021; Section 3.4).
- Mechanism grade: gene-expression studies measured transcripts; VBNC is inferred from delayed plate-count recovery, not always live/dead staining in the cited set.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors: insufficient studies on antibiotic resistance, virulence, and gene expression in HPP survivors, and on recovery during storage (Abstract; Conclusions).
- Table 3 mixes broth and foods, inoculation 3–4 vs 6–7 log, and “BD” vs numeric logs; methods of the source papers are not re-analysed.
- No original freeze-stress HPP experiment; freeze appears via frozen-vegetable epidemiology and a donor-milk freeze-drying combination.
- Industry equipment cost EUR 500,000–3,500,000 is stated from industry data, not measured here.

## Relevance to our research question
**Gap-fill** paper retrieved after the literature table for the freeze/stress *Listeria* under HPP overview gap (Wiśniewski2023_010014). It shows that a named HPP cycle is **not** the same safety outcome: >400 MPa often looks like BD immediately, yet refrigerated recovery, low aw, smoked salmon, and strain/antibiotic-resistance differences can leave survivors. Heat is not required as the kill step in the compiled commercial window (room temperature or lower), but 4 °C storage is required and is itself a stress that can allow repair.

## Open questions / things to verify
“BD” in Table 3 is the source papers’ detection limits, not a single CFU/g threshold. Alpas and Bozoglu units are written as CFU/mL without “log” in one sentence of 3.1.1; treat as log reductions only if citing the original. Freeze survival under σB is from Wemekamp-Kamphuis 2004, not a new HPP-plus-frozen-food trial.
