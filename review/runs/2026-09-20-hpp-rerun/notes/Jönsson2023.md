# Exploration of high-pressure processing (HPP) for preservation of the Swedish grown brown macroalgae *Saccharina latissima*

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Jönsson M, Allahgholi L, Rayner M, Nordberg Karlsson E. Front. Food Sci. Technol. 2023;3:1150482. doi:10.3389/frfst.2023.1150482.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Jönsson2023.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Does HPP at 200, 400, or 600 MPa preserve Swedish-cultivated *Saccharina latissima* (composition, colour, texture, and microbial safety) better than leaving it untreated?

## Methods
Original research. *S. latissima* harvested 12 April 2021 from Kosterhavet (Nordic SeaFarm), cooled, vacuum-packed (10.0 ± 0.1 g in PA/PE) and treated 24 h after harvest in an Avure AV-10 at set 200, 400, or 600 MPa with 180 s hold (measured 202.2–204.8, 401.4–405.2, 598.1–604.6 MPa; vessel 12.9–23.6 °C; Table 1). Untreated vacuum packs as controls. Refrigerated shelf-life at 4 °C (day 1, 1 month, 6 months; n = 2 bags × duplicate plates). Accelerated shelf-life: 2 months fridge then 1 or 3 weeks at room temperature. Counts on TSA, VRBD, malt agar, MRS. Colour (n = 10 sites), texture puncture (n = 6), composition (ash, protein Dumas N×5, carbohydrates; n = 3 powders). Pre-HPP: PCR 16S V1–V3/ITS1 (n = 3) and spontaneous fermentation of 80 g fresh vs frozen seaweed (n = 3) at 37 °C/7 days.

## Sample / data
Swedish-grown brown macroalga *Saccharina latissima*; water ~90% wet weight. Microbial starting load too low for PCR amplification from biomass. Texture and colour n as above.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** laboratory HPP preservation experiment with refrigerated and accelerated shelf-life
- **Population / model:** cultivated *Saccharina latissima* (Swedish west coast)
- **n:** microbial bags n = 2 per pressure for refrigerated study (singlets for accelerated CFU); texture n = 6 sites; colour n = 10; composition n = 3
- **Intervention / comparator:** 200, 400, 600 MPa for 180 s vs untreated vacuum packs
- **Primary endpoint:** microbial CFU (TSA, VRBD, malt, MRS) and texture hardness/compression; secondary colour (L*a*b*) and composition (g/100 g DW)
- **Primary result:** Untreated hardness 312.3 ± 31.2 g and 5.5 ± 0.4 mm compression. After HPP, hardness fell to 100.8 ± 21.1 g (67.7% reduction) at 200 MPa, 81.3 ± 5.3 g (74.0%) at 400 MPa, and 38.3 ± 11.9 g (87.7%) at 600 MPa; compression 3.4, 2.8, and 2.2 mm (38.2%, 49.1%, 60.0% reductions); p < 0.05 (Table 4). L* and b* did not differ at p = 0.05. Protein rose slightly (untreated 9.9 ± 0.1 vs 11.5 ± 0.1, 11.6 ± 0.1, 11.1 ± 0.1 g/100 g DW at 200/400/600 MPa). Mannitol was lower only at 400 MPa (4.83 ± 0.1 vs 8.71 ± 0.8 g/100 g DW untreated). Fresh-biomass PCR did not amplify. Refrigerated CFU were often below LOQ or “sections of growth”; no MRS growth; VRBD appeared later. Accelerated week 3 diluted TSA counts included 6.68 and 6.66 log CFU/mL (200 MPa) and 6.54 log CFU/mL (600 MPa). Authors state they **cannot conclude** which pressure best inactivates seaweed-associated microbes.
- **Cannot show:** a validated HPP kill-step for seaweed pathogens; that 600 MPa is safer than 200 MPa in this matrix; industrial shelf-life for food use.

## Key findings
- Measurement: 6-month refrigerated samples were completely degraded after stomaching, indicating microbial activity despite often uncountable plates (Table 3).
- Measurement: freezing before spontaneous fermentation reduced OD growth (fresh 0.09 ± 0.0 to 1.29 ± 0.0 vs frozen 0.13 ± 0.0 to 0.52 ± 0.3; p < 0.05) and left mostly *Bacillus* in the one frozen library (80.3% genus *Bacillus*).
- Author interpretation: HPP mainly changes texture (protein/carbohydrate polymers) while colour and bulk nutrition are retained; microbial data are too sparse and inconsistent for a pressure ranking (Discussion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Indigenous load near or below enumeration/PCR limits; many CFU cells are “>LOQ”, “*** sections of growth”, or “—”.
- n = 2 bags for refrigerated CFU; seaweed pieces may not represent homogeneous colonisation.
- 200 and 600 MPa bags at 3 weeks RT completely dissolved, confounding OD.
- Hold time only 180 s; no thermal comparator.

## Relevance to our research question
HPP at 200–600 MPa/180 s is **not** interchangeable for quality: hardness collapses with pressure (67.7–87.7%) while colour is retained and microbial kill is not demonstrated. A named megapascal level here is a texture process more than a safety process.

## Open questions / things to verify
Table 3 is qualitative for many cells; do not treat “***” as a numeric log reduction. Protein conversion used N×5 (Angell et al. 2016).
