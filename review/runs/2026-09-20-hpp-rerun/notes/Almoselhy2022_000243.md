# High-Speed and High-Pressure Homogenization Techniques for Optimization of Food Processing, Quality, and Safety

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Almoselhy RIM. Open Access J. Microbiol. Biotechnol. 2022;7(4):000243. doi:10.23880/oajmb-16000243.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Almoselhy2022_000243.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What do high-speed homogenization (HSH) and high-pressure homogenization (HPH / UHPH) do, as alternatives to heat, for microbial load, enzymes, structure, and quality in foods?

## Methods
Narrative review of laboratory and industrial HSH/HPH applications. Distinguishes HPH (shear through a small orifice; often 20–100 MPa, UHPH 200–300 MPa) from HPP (isostatic volume reduction). No systematic search protocol or original experiment.

## Sample / data
Cited food matrices: myofibrillar-protein emulsions, tomato residue fibre, peach–carrot juice, apple juice, almond beverage, milk, donor human milk, toffee with mango seed kernel fat.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** narrative review of HSH and HPH (not a hydrostatic HPP experiment)
- **Population / model:** fluid foods and emulsions in the cited studies (juices, milk, human milk, protein emulsions, fibres)
- **n:** not applicable
- **Intervention / comparator:** HSH (rpm, no pressure change) and HPH/UHPH vs conventional blending, thermal pasteurisation (PA), HTST, or UHT in the cited papers
- **Primary endpoint:** microbial log reduction, residual oxidoreductase activity, particle size/fibre structure, bioactive extractability, sensory/physicochemical milk quality — as reported by sources
- **Primary result (compiled from cited work):** HPH >100 MPa is described as achieving microbial-load reduction and enzyme inactivation (Mesa et al. 2020 cited). Apple juice at 200 MPa: highest microbial reduction **1.4 log** and oxidoreductase activity ~20% down; hydrolases (PG, PME) did not change significantly (Szczepańska et al. 2021). HSH at 8,000–14,500 rpm improved MP emulsification; ≥20,500 rpm aggregated MPs (Zhou et al. 2019). Tomato-residue HSH broke fibres to ~60 μm particles; HPH formed a new network; soluble fibre increased by about 8% (Hua et al. 2017). HPH >200 MPa increased carotenoids and polyphenols in not-from-concentrate peach–carrot juice (Liu et al. 2022). UHPH 200 and 300 MPa increased total phytosterol extractability of almond beverage by 20–40% (Funes et al. 2014). UHPH 300 MPa produced milk the authors describe as sterile with similar or better colour, particle size, viscosity, buffer capacity, ethanol stability, and less cooked flavour vs UHT (Espejo et al. 2014, 2015). Donor human milk: 250 MPa, inlet 20 °C, reduced coliforms to undetectable levels (Irazusta et al. 2020). Emulsions with 20% oil at 100 and 200 MPa showed superior oxidative stability (Avila and Trujillo 2016).
- **Cannot show:** hydrostatic HPP of packaged solids; a 5–7 log milk pasteurisation claim generated in this paper; spore inactivation by HPH; original measurements.

## Key findings
- Mechanism (stated, not newly measured): HPH acts by shear, cavitation, and high-frequency oscillation as fluid is forced through a valve; HPP reduces volume with increasing isostatic pressure (Introduction).
- Author interpretation: HSH/HPH are efficient alternatives to thermal processes that cause nonenzymatic browning, off-flavour, or bioactive loss (Abstract).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Short review; numbers come entirely from cited papers.
- No methods for literature search, inclusion, or appraisal.
- Mixes HSH, HPH, UHPH, and mentions HPP without always keeping the unit operations separate.
- “Sterile milk” at 300 MPa is a claim attributed to Espejo et al., not independently verified here.

## Relevance to our research question
Intake related paper from OpenAlex related-to-seeds search (not a post-table gap-fill). It is the sample’s HPH counterpart to hydrostatic HPP: the same megapascal language is **not** the same process (shear vs isostatic), and even within HPH a 200 MPa apple-juice cycle (1.4 log; enzymes partly intact) is not the same outcome as 300 MPa UHPH milk or 250 MPa human-milk coliform reduction.

## Open questions / things to verify
Whether Espejo’s 300 MPa milk was truly sterile should be taken only as that source’s claim. HSH 1000 rpm/10 min toffee result (Azab et al. 2022) has no numeric quality scores in this review.
