# High Pressure Processing vs. Thermal Pasteurization of Whole Concord Grape Puree: Effect on Nutritional Value, Quality Parameters and Refrigerated Shelf Life

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Li Y, Padilla-Zakour OI. 2021. Foods 10(11):2608. DOI:10.3390/foods10112608. PMID:34828888
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Li2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Does industrial-style HPP (600 MPa, 3 min, 5 °C) preserve whole Concord grape puree (skin + seeds) as well as or better than mild heat pasteurization (63 °C, 3 min) for microbes, phenolics, enzymes, rheology, nutrition, sensory liking, and refrigerated shelf-life?

## Methods

2019 Finger Lakes Concord grapes (Welch’s), destemmed, ground 30 min, Ross sheared 9500 rpm 2 min, slurry <40 °C. HPP: 600 MPa, 3 min, 5 °C, Hiperbaric 55 L (quality/shelf-life) or Hiperbaric 525 (sensory plant). HT: 63 °C, 3 min in steam kettle (D52 = 23 min, z = 4.8 °C plus 5-fold safety factor for >5-log E. coli O157:H7, Salmonella, L. monocytogenes), ice cooled, filled into 4 oz PET. Storage 4 ± 1 °C, monthly up to 5 months. APC (PCA, 30 °C, 48–72 h) and yeast/mold (PDA pH 3.5). TSSC, pH, TA (% tartaric), Hunter L*a*b*, ΔE, browning index, Mastersizer PSD, serum separation (6000 rpm, 10 min), Brookfield viscosity. Phenolics: acidified methanol extract; Folin TP as mg GAE/g FW; pH-differential TMA as mg CGE/kg; DPPH and ABTS TEAC µmol/g. PPO/POD residual activity vs untreated. Proximate (Dairy One): control with seeds (C/W), without seeds (C/O), HPP/W, HT/W; triplicate. Sensory: 101 untrained panellists, 9-point liking, 5-point intensity/purchase, ranking; IRB exempt protocol 1405004676. Triplicate experiments; ANOVA/Tukey; Pearson correlations.

## Sample / data

Whole Concord puree including broken seeds and skin (no waste). Pathogen challenge was not performed in this puree; 5-log claim rests on juice HACCP calculation and cited Petrus et al. Concord juice HPP work. Sensory samples stored 1 week at 4 °C before testing.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Parallel preservation trial (HPP vs mild heat vs untreated) with refrigerated shelf-life, composition (seed vs no seed), and consumer sensory.
- **Population / model:** Whole Concord grape (Vitis labrusca) puree with skin and seeds; human food.
- **n:** Laboratory assays in triplicate; sensory n = 101; composition four groups × triplicate.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 600 MPa/3 min/5 °C versus HT 63 °C/3 min versus untreated control; plus seeded vs deseeded untreated puree for nutrition.
- **Primary endpoint:** APC and Y&M (log CFU/g FW) during 4–5 months at 4 °C; supporting TP, TMA, TEAC, PPO/POD residual %, colour, viscosity/SSR/PSD, proximate, hedonic scores.
- **Primary result:** Untreated day-1 APC 6.33 and Y&M 6.31 log CFU/g. Both HPP and HT achieved ~5-log reduction. HPP Y&M <1.0 log; HT Y&M 1.22 log. Month 4: HPP APC 2.0 log, Y&M still <LOD; HT APC 3.6 and Y&M 3.1 log. Month 5: HPP APC 2.7 and Y&M 2.4 log; HT >6 log with visible mould. Control day 1: aw 0.98, pH 3.35 ± 0.01, 18.0 ± 0.1 °Brix, TA 1.2 ± 0.1%. PPO residual: HPP 75.2 ± 7.4%, HT 22.6 ± 2.4%. POD: HPP 80.7 ± 6.8%, HT 10.2 ± 1.1%. TP (mg GAE/g): control 3.0 ± 0.1; HPP 3.8 ± 0.5; HT 3.6 ± 0.0; month 4 HPP 2.6 ± 0.0 vs HT 2.9 ± 0.3 (HPP vs HT ns). TMA (mg CGE/kg): control 628 ± 35; HPP 620 ± 110; HT 790 ± 120; month 4 HPP 560 ± 30 vs HT 610 ± 170 (ns). ABTS TEAC: control 34.7 ± 0.6; HPP 38.1 ± 0.2; HT 37.8 ± 0.5 µmol/g; month 4 HPP 33.9 ± 0.5 vs HT 33.6 ± 1.4. SSR day 1: HPP 44.3 ± 3.5% similar to untreated, lower than HT (exact HT % in Figure S1). Month 4 SSR HPP 65.8 ± 0.3%; HT gelled. Month 4 D90: HT 294 ± 36 µm vs HPP 218 ± 35 µm. Seeded vs seedless fresh basis: crude fibre 2.53 ± 0.05% vs 0.53 ± 0.05%; protein 0.93 ± 0.05% vs 0.70 ± 0.00%; total FA 0.29 ± 0.02% vs 0.06 ± 0.01%; linoleic acid 65% of FA with seeds vs 44% without. Sensory: HPP higher appearance/texture/flavour/overall liking and purchase intent than HT (p < 0.05); 59% ranked HPP first; aroma ns; smoothness higher for HPP.
- **Cannot show:** In-product 5-log of named pathogens (calculated/cited, not challenged in this puree); enzyme-inactivation parity with HT (HPP left >75% PPO/POD); that HPP phenolics stay higher than HT after 4 months (they equalized); wine-level alcohol effects (not a wine).

## Key findings

- Measurement: Same 3 min hold at 600 MPa/5 °C vs 63 °C is not the same microbial shelf-life: HT spoiled by month 5 (>6 log), HPP did not (Section 3.1).
- Measurement: Same cycles are not the same enzyme residual: HPP kept ~75–81% PPO/POD vs HT ~10–23% (Section 3.4; Figure 4), yet TP/TMA/TEAC after 4 months did not differ significantly between HPP and HT (Table 1).
- Measurement: Colour ΔE on day 1 noticeable for HT (>2) but not HPP (<1.5); BI higher in HT (Section 3.2.2).
- Measurement: HT raised viscosity and created lumps/syneresis; HPP stayed pourable (Section 3.2.3).
- Measurement: Including seeds (not HPP vs heat) drove fibre, protein, and linoleic acid (Table 2; Figure 5).
- Measurement: 101 consumers liked HPP more overall than HT (Section 3.6).
- Proposed mechanism: HPP kills vegetative cells by membrane rupture (cited, not imaged here). Phenolic rise after HPP attributed to mass transfer/cell permeability (cited), consistent with higher day-1 TP.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Authors: texture/flavour during storage, tannin digestibility/microbiome, and PME-related texture need more work (Conclusions).
- No pathogen inoculum in the puree; industrial HPP parameters assumed to meet juice HACCP via cited juice data.
- Sensory HPP done in a different commercial vessel than the shelf-life HPP.
- Control untreated puree was not stored for sensory (biosafety).
- Supplementary colour/SSR tables not in the .txt body.

## Relevance to our research question

Strong human-food comparison: 600 MPa/3 min is not interchangeable with 63 °C/3 min for yeast/mould shelf-life, residual PPO/POD, colour, or consistency, even when phenolic contents converge after 4 months at 4 °C.
