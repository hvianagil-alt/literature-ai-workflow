# Effects of high hydrostatic pressure on antimicrobial protein stability and the rheological and shelf-life properties of donkey milk

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Koker A, Ozturkoglu-Budak S, Alpas H (2023). Journal of Dairy Research 90:292–298. doi:10.1017/S0022029923000572
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Koker2023292.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How do HHP pressure–temperature–time combinations compare with 75 °C pasteurization for total aerobic bacteria, native lysozyme and lactoferrin, color, rheology, and refrigerated/ambient shelf life of donkey milk?

## Methods
Laboratory HHP (SITEC Type-760.0118, 100 mL vessel, distilled water as pressure medium) on fresh donkey milk in 25 mL HDPE vials. Full factorial: 200, 400, 500 MPa × 25, 35, 45 °C × 5, 10, 15 min (27 HHP treatments). Heat: 75 °C for 1 or 2 min in a water bath, then iced. Pour-plate TABC on plate count agar, 37 °C, 24 h. Lysozyme and lactoferrin native-form activity by the Billakanti et al. SPR method. Rheology (power-law), CIELAB color, pH and titratable acidity. Shelf-life sampling at days 0, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28 at 4 °C and 25 °C for untreated, HHP 400 MPa–25 °C–5 min, and heat 75 °C–2 min. Three-way ANOVA, Tukey, P < 0.05.

## Sample / data
Donkey milk from Korukoy Farm, Kirklareli, Türkiye; held at 4 °C before treatment. Initial TABC 4.04 log CFU mL^−1 (figure legend). Two replicates of HHP and heat treatments; analyses performed in triplicate. Total 29 treatments evaluated.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Controlled laboratory comparison of HHP factorial treatments vs 75 °C heat, plus 28-day storage of selected cycles.
- **Population / model:** Raw donkey milk (native microbiota; no pathogen inoculation).
- **n:** Two treatment replicates; microbial/enzyme/color assays in triplicate. Initial TABC 4.04 log CFU mL^−1.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HHP 200/400/500 MPa at 25/35/45 °C for 5/10/15 min vs 75 °C for 1 or 2 min; untreated milk. Shelf-life subset: 400 MPa, 25 °C, 5 min vs 75 °C, 2 min.
- **Primary endpoint:** TABC reduction (log CFU mL^−1); percent loss of native lysozyme and lactoferrin; storage TABC, pH, color, flow consistency index.
- **Primary result:** Results-section means: HHP at 200, 400, and 500 MPa reduced TABC by 0.85, 1.58, and 4.04 log CFU mL^−1 (all temperatures/times pooled by pressure). Heat at 75 °C for 1 and 2 min reduced TABC by 0.72 and 1.34 log CFU mL^−1. Native lysozyme losses: 16.4, 21.6, 28.1% at 200, 400, 500 MPa; lactoferrin losses 20, 36.8, 55.5% (P < 0.05); holding time 5–15 min was not significant (P > 0.05). Heat 75 °C for 1 and 2 min: lysozyme losses 71.6 and 93%; lactoferrin losses 35.6 and 53.4%. Processing at 45 °C during HHP reduced lysozyme and (at 400 MPa) lactoferrin more than 25–35 °C. Shelf-life subset: HHP 400 MPa–25 °C–5 min and heat 75 °C–2 min reduced TABC by 1.48 and 1.34 log CFU/ml (P < 0.05). Heat-treated milk at 4 °C reached 5.15 log CFU/ml on day 21; lowest end-of-storage TABC was HHP at 4 °C. Heat-treated milk had higher flow consistency index K than untreated or HHP milk (P < 0.05).
- **Cannot show:** Pathogen-specific log reductions (only TABC). Spore inactivation. Human clinical safety of HHP donkey milk. Enzyme “activity” is native-form concentration by SPR, not a kinetic activity assay in milk. Abstract numbers (1.50 log and 58%/82% enzyme losses at 75 °C/2 min; 1.80 log and 22%/37% at 400 MPa) do not match the results-section figures and should not be used.

## Key findings
- Measurement: 500 MPa inactivated the entire 4.04 log CFU mL^−1 TABC load; 400 MPa averaged 1.58 log, similar in magnitude to 75 °C/2 min (1.34 log) but with far less lysozyme loss (21.6% vs 93%). (Fig. 1; Results)
- Measurement: Lysozyme and lactoferrin native-form losses increased with pressure; time (5–15 min) did not. (Results, lysozyme and lactoferrin activity)
- Named cycle is not interchangeable: 400 MPa at 25 °C vs 45 °C changed lactoferrin loss (18% vs 25% at 400 MPa). Same 400 MPa vs 75 °C/2 min was not the same enzyme, color (lower L* after HHP), or rheology outcome. (Results; Table 1)
- Author interpretation: HHP is more suitable than heat for preserving donkey milk antimicrobial proteins under the conditions studied. (Conclusion)
- Mechanism grade: whey protein denaturation and casein-micelle size change are cited from earlier work and inferred from color/rheology; not directly measured here.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Low initial microbial load typical of donkey milk limits the observable log reduction except at 500 MPa.
- Laboratory 100 mL HHP vessel; two process replicates.
- Abstract vs results numerical mismatch for heat and 400 MPa.
- No sensory or pathogen challenge.
- Supplementary compositional and rheology tables were not in the extracted text.

## Relevance to our research question
Core evidence that a named HHP cycle is not the same safety/quality outcome as a named heat cycle in the same milk: 400 MPa can match ~1.3–1.6 log TABC reduction of 75 °C/2 min while retaining more lysozyme/lactoferrin, whereas 500 MPa equals complete TABC kill at the cost of more protein loss. Temperature during HHP (25 vs 45 °C) further changes protein retention.
