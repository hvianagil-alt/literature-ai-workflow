# Influence of high pressure processing on qualities of celery-based juices during refrigerated storage

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Le UNP, Ratphitagsanti W, Therdthai N. Agriculture and Natural Resources. 2023;57:399–408. doi:10.34044/j.anres.2023.57.3.04
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Le2023399.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Does combining natural acidification (lime or apple juice) with HPP at 400–600 MPa for 5 min keep celery-based juices microbiologically stable and limit browning enzymes over 8 weeks at 4 °C?

## Methods
Three formulations: celery-lime (90:10, target pH <3.5), celery-apple (85:15, pH <4.5), 100% celery (pH ~5–6). Packed in LLDPE, double-bagged, HPP at 400, 500 or 600 MPa with 5 min hold (HPP600MPa/5L, Bao Tou KeFA). 3×3 factorial CRD. Storage 4 ± 2 °C, sampled weeks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8. TAC (PCA, 35 °C/48 h) and yeasts/moulds (PDA, 25 °C/120 h) as log CFU/mL; pH, TSS, CIE colour/ΔE*; residual PPO and POD (% of untreated); TPC (mg GAE/100 mL); FRAP and DPPH. At least two replications; ANOVA and Tukey, p < 0.05.

## Sample / data
Fresh celery (Apium graveolens), Granny Smith apple and lime from a Bangkok supermarket. Untreated juice characterization in Table 1. Untreated juices spoiled after 1 week (gas, fermented odour).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** 3×3 factorial refrigerated storage study (juice pH × HPP pressure)
- **Population / model:** celery-based juices (low-acid vegetable juice ± fruit acidifiers); native microbiota and endogenous PPO/POD
- **n:** at least two replications (authors’ wording); untreated baselines in Table 1 are mean ± SD
- **Intervention / comparator:** 400 vs 500 vs 600 MPa, 5 min, vs untreated fresh juice; celery-lime vs celery-apple vs celery-only
- **Primary endpoint:** TAC (log CFU/mL) and YM during 8 weeks; residual PPO and POD (%); ΔE*; TPC; FRAP; DPPH % inhibition
- **Primary result:** Untreated TAC: celery-lime 3.39 ± 0.09, celery-apple 5.74 ± 0.01, celery 5.58 ± 0.03 log CFU/mL; YM 3.37 ± 0.07, 2.95 ± 0.23, 3.13 ± 0.03 respectively; pH 3.30 ± 0.08, 4.50 ± 0.08, 6.02 ± 0.06 (Table 1). HPP cut TAC to <2 log CFU/mL in all three juices and kept them there for 8 weeks; YM were not detected in any HPP juice throughout storage. Untreated spoiled after 1 week. Celery-lime at 600 MPa/5 min held POD residual activity at 30.37% (week 0) and 31.15% (week 8), lower than 400/500 MPa; abstract states PPO and POD residual activity reduced to under 36% and 50% in celery-lime and celery juice respectively. Celery-apple (pH 4.5–4.6) showed poorer enzyme control (POD often ~80% from week 3 at 500–600 MPa). After HPP, celery-lime ΔE* 3.03 ± 0.45; celery juice 1.50 ± 0.17 (below just-noticeable difference 2.3). TPC of celery-lime and celery held at 37–38 mg GAE/100 mL over 8 weeks; celery-apple fell from 46–47 to ~38 mg GAE/100 mL. FRAP fell ~14% (celery-lime), 46% (celery), 60% (celery-apple) by week 8. Authors recommend 600 MPa/5 min as the most suitable of the tested conditions.
- **Cannot show:** pathogen log reductions (no Listeria/Salmonella/STEC inoculum); spore inactivation; that 400, 500 and 600 MPa are equivalent for enzymes (they are not, especially in celery-lime); a holding time other than 5 min.

## Key findings
- Measurement: juice formulation (pH) and HPP both significantly affected TAC, YM, PPO, POD, TPC, FRAP and DPPH (Table 2 p-values); pH itself was not changed by HPP (p = 0.780).
- Measurement: same 600 MPa/5 min cycle gave stable green celery juice (ΔE* <3 through storage) but browning and TPC loss in celery-apple because residual PPO/POD stayed high (Sections on colour and enzymes).
- Author interpretation: low pH plus 600 MPa is the hurdle that inactivates browning enzymes; apple juice at pH ~4.5 sits near the enzymes’ optimum (4.5–5.5).
- Mechanism grade: HPP disruption of hydrogen bonds in enzymes at >400 MPa is **cited** (Roobab; Yoruk & Marshall); citric acid copper-chelating action in lime is **proposed**.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- “At least two replications” is less than a conventional n = 3; no exact n per cell beyond that.
- No thermal pasteurization comparator in this experiment (heat damage is cited from other papers).
- Longer holds suggested for stronger enzyme kill, not tested.
- Native counts only.

## Relevance to our research question
Matrix and pH change the meaning of a named 400–600 MPa/5 min cycle: microbes fall below 2 log TAC in all three juices, but POD/PPO and colour/phenolics do not — celery-lime is enzyme-stable at 600 MPa, celery-apple is not.

## Open questions / things to verify
