# The efficacy and safety of high-pressure processing of food (EFSA BIOHAZ scientific opinion)

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** EFSA Panel on Biological Hazards (BIOHAZ); Koutsoumanis K et al. EFSA Journal. 2022;20(3):e07128. doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2022.7128
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Koutsoumanis2022.txt (large opinion; extracted from Abstract, Summary, Introduction scope, and §4 Conclusions — not the full 10,000-line file)
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What is the microbial and chemical safety of non-thermal HPP of foods, can HPP match legal thermal pasteurization of ruminant milk/colostrum, and what pressure–time combinations reduce Listeria monocytogenes (and other pathogens) in RTE foods linked to listeriosis?

## Methods
EFSA BIOHAZ scientific opinion answering European Commission Terms of Reference. Literature searches plus industry/competent-authority questionnaires. Scope is isostatic HPP with product temperature <45 °C. High-pressure homogenization and multipulsed HPP are explicitly out of scope. HPTP/heat-assisted HPP (product >45 °C) is excluded. Vegetative-cell log10 reductions only: the opinion states non-thermal HPP does not inactivate spores. Milk P/t minima use performance criteria (5–8 log10) from international agencies and a meta-analytic Listeria model (Santillana Farakos and Zwietering) for RTE cooked meat isoreduction plots.

## Sample / data
Foods commercially HPP-treated in the EU (cooked/RTE meats, juices, guacamole, RTE meals, seafood, some dairy). Milk/colostrum hazards: Mycobacterium bovis (MAP as surrogate), Brucella, L. monocytogenes, Salmonella, STEC, Campylobacter, S. aureus, TBEV. RTE listeriosis foods: cooked meat, soft/semi-soft and fresh cheese, smoked or gravad fish. Frozen vegetables are not HPP-treated because of structural damage.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** EFSA BIOHAZ scientific opinion (evidence synthesis + models), not a primary HPP trial
- **Population / model:** vegetative pathogens in human foods, with quantitative focus on ruminant milk/colostrum and RTE foods associated with listeriosis
- **n:** not a single experimental n; milk and RTE minima come from assembled inactivation studies and a Listeria spp. meta-analysis (200–700 MPa)
- **Intervention / comparator:** industrial HPP 400–600 MPa, common hold 1.5–6 min, often with 4–8 °C transmitting water, product <45 °C, vs legal thermal pasteurization (at least 72 °C/15 s or 63 °C/30 min) and UHT (taken as 12 log10)
- **Primary endpoint:** log10 reduction of named vegetative pathogens; legal pasteurization equivalence; ALP/other milk enzymes as process indicators; chemical/FCM safety vs conventional treatments
- **Primary result:** Non-thermal HPP does not inactivate spores. Industrial HPP most often 400–600 MPa for 1.5–6 min. Low-moisture foods (<40% water) are usually not treated because inactivation is low. HPP cannot match legal thermal pasteurization of milk (>10 log10 expected for STEC, L. monocytogenes, Salmonella, S. aureus, Campylobacter). Example P/t for an 8-log PC in milk: S. aureus 600 MPa–8 min / 550 MPa–10 min / 500 MPa–15 min; STEC 600–5 min; L. monocytogenes 600–6 min; Salmonella 600–5 min; Campylobacter 600–1 min. For M. bovis (MAP surrogate), 5-log PC: 600 MPa–2.5 min, 550–4.5 min, 500–7.5 min. Most stringent industrial condition collected (600 MPa/6 min) would meet those PCs except S. aureus (only 6 log10). ALP is pressure-resistant and at best an overprocessing indicator; no suitable commercial indicator was identified for 400–600 MPa / 1.5–6 min. RTE cooked meat L. monocytogenes minima (90–95% certainty): ≥2 log10 at 600 MPa–2.3 min, 550–3.4 min, 500–5.0 min; ≥5 log10 at 600 MPa–4.7 min, 550–6.9 min, 500–10.1 min. Generic minima could not be set for cheese or smoked/gravad fish. Salmonella and pathogenic E. coli in those RTE foods are judged 66–90% certain to be inactivated similarly or more than L. monocytogenes. HPP judged 99–100% certain not to add microbial safety concerns vs routine treatments (including spore activation, virulence induction); mycotoxins/process contaminants judged >95% not increased.
- **Cannot show:** spore kill or commercial sterility by non-thermal HPP; that 600 MPa/6 min equals legal milk pasteurization; generic L. monocytogenes P/t for cheese or smoked fish; HPH or HPTP performance (out of scope); a milk enzyme that verifies industrial HPP the way ALP verifies thermal pasteurization.

## Key findings
- Measurement: legal thermal pasteurization of milk is expected to give >10 log10 of STEC, L. monocytogenes, Salmonella, S. aureus and Campylobacter; HPP cannot match that (Conclusions ToR 2a).
- Measurement: even 600 MPa/6 min gives fewer log10 than UHT (12 log10 assumed), except for Campylobacter; 450 MPa/5 min still lowers the probability of contaminated servings vs raw milk (ToR 2c).
- Measurement: for milk initially <5 log10 CFU/mL of relevant pathogens except S. aureus, 600 MPa/6 min would leave >99/100 servings uncontaminated immediately after treatment (ToR 2c).
- Author interpretation: a named P/t must be validated in the real food because aw, pH, carbohydrates, proteins and lipids change lethality (ToR 1b).
- Mechanism grade: spore survival under non-thermal HPP is stated as a **scope limitation of the treatment class**, not newly measured here.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- No generic HPP minima for smoked/gravad fish or soft cheese (data too scarce; model poorly predictive).
- No minima for Brucella melitensis or TBEV.
- Public-health meaning of few HPP survivors in milk could not be quantified (growth during storage not modelled with realistic data).
- Opinion excludes HPH, multipulse HPP, and any cycle that heats the product above 45 °C.

## Relevance to our research question
This is the legal-criteria and spore-limitation backbone: the same 600 MPa label is not pasteurization-equivalent in milk (especially vs S. aureus and vs 72 °C/15 s), is not a spore kill step, and even in RTE cooked meat a 2-log vs 5-log Listeria target changes hold time at 600 MPa from 2.3 min to 4.7 min.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Full tables of industry questionnaire P/t values live later in the opinion; conclusions above use the opinion’s own stated numbers only.
- MAP is a surrogate for M. bovis; that substitution is the Panel’s, not independently verified here.
