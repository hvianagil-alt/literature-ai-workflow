# Edible portions of precooked blue swimming crab: Chemical composition and effect of chitooligosaccharide conjugate and high-pressure processing on microbial inactivation

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Pimsannil K, Palamae S, Fan X, Zhao Q, Zhang B, Benjakul S. Food Chemistry: X. 2025;25:102070. doi:10.1016/j.fochx.2024.102070
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Pimsannil2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How do edible portions of precooked blue swimming crab (Portunus pelagicus) differ chemically and microbiologically, and do chitooligosaccharide–catechin (COSC) conjugate and HPP inactivate bacteria in lump meat and roe?

## Methods
Completely randomized design. Steam precooking of whole crab at 100–120 °C for 20 min, then hand-picking of lump, claw, backfin meat and roe. Proximate, SDS-PAGE, fatty-acid and amino-acid profiles, SEM, and 16S NGS on lump and roe. Microbial trial: 30 g lump or roe sprayed with 100 µL COSC at 0 or 200 ppm, vacuum packed, then HPP in water (Jiujiu HPP600 MPa/5 L) for 1.5 or 3 min. Results table includes 100, 300 and 500 MPa (methods text lists 0, 100 and 300 MPa; 500 MPa is reported in Table 4 and the abstract). Plate counts: APC, psychrophiles, Vibrio, H2S-producers, Pseudomonas. One-way ANOVA, Duncan; PCA/PERMANOVA on log CFU/g. Triplicate analyses.

## Sample / data
Precooked Portunus pelagicus meats and roe from All Crab Company, Nakhon Sri Thammarat, Thailand. n = 3 for composition and microbial counts (mean ± SD). NGS diversity: lump observed species 712.70 ± 7.90 vs roe 807.90 ± 10.30.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** laboratory factorial experiment (COSC × pressure × time) on precooked crab meat and roe, plus compositional characterization
- **Population / model:** precooked blue swimming crab (Portunus pelagicus) lump meat and roe (human seafood); background microbiota, not inoculated pathogens
- **n:** 3 independent analyses per treatment (explicitly “n = 3” in Tables 1–4)
- **Intervention / comparator:** COSC 0 vs 200 ppm; HPP 100, 300, 500 MPa for 1.5 or 3 min vs untreated control (C0H0). Combined COSC+HPP vs either alone
- **Primary endpoint:** microbial counts as log CFU/g (APC, PBC, Vibrio spp., H2S-producers, Pseudomonas); ND = not detected
- **Primary result:** Untreated lump APC 6.06 ± 0.10 log CFU/g (Vibrio 5.35 ± 0.12); untreated roe APC 6.33 ± 0.03 (Vibrio 5.69 ± 0.09). COSC 200 ppm alone cut APC by 0.09 (lump) and 0.10 (roe) log CFU/g. HPP 100 MPa/1.5 min cut APC by 0.27 (lump) and 0.40 (roe) log CFU/g. At 300 MPa (1.5 or 3 min), PBC, Vibrio, H2S-producers and Pseudomonas were ND in both tissues; APC remained 4.10–4.99 log CFU/g depending on time and COSC. At 500 MPa for 1.5 or 3 min, all five counts were ND in lump and roe with or without COSC. PERMANOVA: only HPP pressure significantly affected microbial counts (p < 0.05); sample type and COSC concentration did not (p > 0.05). PCA of microbial counts: two components explained 99.94% of variance.
- **Cannot show:** pathogen challenge (Listeria, Salmonella, STEC) or spore inactivation; shelf-life after HPP (authors defer quality/storage); that 500 MPa is needed for safety if a lower pressure plus another hurdle is used; enzyme inactivation (not measured).

## Key findings
- Measurement: lump protein 19.50 ± 0.66%, moisture 76.59 ± 0.75%; roe protein 20.48 ± 0.13%, fat 8.98 ± 0.67%, moisture 67.60 ± 1.18% (Table 1, n = 3).
- Measurement: meats were PUFA-rich (lump PUFA 53.21 ± 9.66 g/100 g lipid); roe SFA 53.32 ± 10.00 g/100 g (Table 2).
- Measurement: Moraxellaceae dominated both NGS profiles; roe had higher Shannon diversity (6.66 ± 0.02 vs 6.28 ± 0.02 in lump) (Section 3.6).
- Author interpretation: COSC alone is insufficient; 500 MPa “eliminated all detectable bacteria”; combining COSC with 300 MPa is proposed to avoid high-pressure structural damage (Section 3.7; Conclusion).
- Mechanism grade: membrane leakage/pore formation under HPP is **cited/proposed**, not imaged in this study.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Methods 2.5.2 lists 0, 100 and 300 MPa, while Table 4 and the abstract report 500 MPa; 500 MPa results are taken from Table 4.
- Background counts, not inoculated pathogens; ND is plate-count ND, not a validated 5-log pathogen reduction.
- COSC >200 ppm darkened lump meat (data not shown).
- No post-HPP texture, colour, or storage microbiology in this paper.

## Relevance to our research question
Shows a named cycle is not interchangeable: 100 MPa leaves ~5–6 log APC, 300 MPa clears several groups but not APC, and 500 MPa (1.5 or 3 min) makes all tested groups undetectable. COSC 200 ppm is a weak hurdle next to pressure.
