# Modelling the piezo-protection effect exerted by lactate on the high pressure resistance of *Listeria monocytogenes* in cooked ham

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Serra-Castelló C, Jofré A, Belletti N, Garriga M, Bover-Cid S (2021). Food Research International. doi:10.1016/j.foodres.2020.110003 (postprint)
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Serra-Castelló2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Do potassium lactate and sodium diacetate change HPP (400 MPa) inactivation kinetics of three *Listeria monocytogenes* strains on sliced cooked ham, and can Weibull/global models quantify a dose-dependent piezo-protective effect of lactate?

## Methods
Five cooked-ham formulations: control; 1.4% potassium lactate; 2.8% potassium lactate; 0.1% sodium diacetate; 1.4% lactate + 0.1% diacetate. Product pH 6.04 ± 0.04, aw 0.974 ± 0.003 (organic acids did not change these). Slices 12–14 g inoculated independently with CTC1011 (serotype 1/2c), CTC1034 (4b), or Scott A (4b) at ca. 10^7 CFU/g from frozen stationary-phase cultures. Vacuum packed. HPP Wave6000/120 (Hiperbaric) at 400 MPa, holding times 0, 2.5, 3.75, 5, 6.25, 7.5, 8.5, 9.5, 10 min; come-up 2.0 min, release <2 s; water, initial 13 °C. Held 2 h at room temperature then plated on Chromogenic Listeria agar (37 °C, 48 h). Presence below quantification: 1 CFU/g; absence 0.1 CFU/g. Weibull primary model for log N/N0 vs holding time; polynomial/global secondary models for lactate effect on δ. Duplicate or triplicate analyses per batch and strain; n = 25 log N/N0 points per condition for fitting.

## Sample / data
IRTA-manufactured cooked ham (pork shoulder). Three *L. monocytogenes* strains. Kinetic Table 2 reports δ and p per formulation × strain.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Inoculated kinetic challenge study with primary (Weibull) and secondary modelling of 400 MPa inactivation vs organic-acid formulation.
- **Population / model:** *L. monocytogenes* CTC1011, CTC1034, and Scott A on vacuum-packed sliced cooked ham.
- **n:** Target inoculum ca. 10^7 CFU/g; duplicate or triplicate plating per batch/strain; 25 data points per fitted condition.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 400 MPa for 0–10 min holding on ham with 0, 1.4, or 2.8% potassium lactate and/or 0.1% sodium diacetate vs control ham without organic acids.
- **Primary endpoint:** Inactivation log N/N0; Weibull δ (min for first log reduction) and p (shape); holding times for 1st and 2nd log reduction from the global lactate model.
- **Primary result:** In control ham, inactivation curve shape was strain-specific: CTC1011 convex shoulder, δ = 5.98 min, p = 3.62; CTC1034 nearly linear, δ = 3.89 min, p = 1.29; Scott A concave, δ = 0.70 min, p = 0.47, with a resistant tail after ~6 min. After >6 min, CTC1011 inactivation was 3 log higher than CTC1034 and Scott A (some CTC1011 samples not detected). Lactate increased resistance (δ rose): 1.4% lactate δ = 7.00, 4.62, 0.85 min; 2.8% lactate δ = 7.39, 7.48, 2.48 min for CTC1011, CTC1034, Scott A. At 10 min, inactivation was lower with 1.4% lactate than control by 0.5, 1.46, and 1.29 log, and with 2.8% lactate by 2.51, 1.75, and 2.35 log, respectively (differences >0.5 log for holding times >5 min). Diacetate 0.1% reduced δ by 13, 31, and 20% (δ = 5.21, 2.67, 0.56 min). Lactate 1.4% + diacetate 0.1% inactivation curves were not statistically different from control (p > 0.05). Global model predicted time for first log reduction at 0% vs 2.8% lactate: CTC1011 6.34 vs 7.17 min; CTC1034 3.52 vs 6.29 min; Scott A 0.33 vs 1.77 min. Time for second log reduction at 0% vs 2.8% lactate: CTC1011 7.51 vs 8.49 min; CTC1034 6.24 vs 11.14 min; Scott A 2.10 vs 11.22 min.
- **Cannot show:** Growth during shelf life after HPP (inactivation kinetics only). Sensory impact of acids (stated as industry-typical but not scored). Mechanism of piezo-protection (aw unchanged; gene-level hypotheses cited). Other pressures besides 400 MPa. Natural contamination levels.

## Key findings
- Named 400 MPa cycle is not one safety outcome: holding time, strain, and lactate dose change log kill by >0.5–2.5 log at 10 min. (Table 2; Section 3.2)
- Measurement: Scott A is most sensitive at short times (δ 0.70 min) but tails; CTC1011 needs ~6 min for the first log then can fall below detection. (Section 3.1)
- Measurement: 2.8% lactate roughly doubles Scott A time to 2 log reduction (2.10 to 11.22 min) in the global model. (Table 4)
- Author interpretation: lactate is piezo-protective; diacetate sensitizes; together they neutralize. Not explained by aw/pH, which did not change. (Section 3.2)
- Mechanism grade: ion-transport/membrane gene responses cited from Stasiewicz et al.; not measured in this ham experiment.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Frozen stationary-phase inoculum may not represent in-plant contaminants.
- Two-hour room-temperature hold after HPP before plating may allow repair or death not separated from process kill.
- Industrial 400 MPa only; no 600 MPa comparison.
- Modelling assumed 0.1 CFU/g for absence.

## Relevance to our research question
Direct evidence that a named 400 MPa–time cycle on cooked ham is not a fixed *L. monocytogenes* kill: adding 1.4–2.8% lactate, a common antimicrobial hurdle, protects the pathogen during HPP. Strain choice can invert which isolate looks “resistant.” Heat is not the kill step.
