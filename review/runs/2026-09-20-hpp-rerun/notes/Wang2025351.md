# Effects of Ultra-High Pressure and Thermal Treatment on Quality Characteristics of Prepared Mackerel Fish Balls

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Wang B, Hu X, Ma J, Xu Y. 2025. American Journal of Biochemistry and Biotechnology 21(3):351–361. DOI:10.3844/ajbbsp.2025.351.361
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Wang2025351.txt (PDF also present)
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Does ultra-high pressure (UHP) preserve prepared mackerel fish balls better than retort thermal treatment, and which pressure–time cycle is recommended for microbial reduction with acceptable colour, texture, and sensory quality?

## Methods

Laboratory comparison of vacuum-packed, pre-cooked mackerel fish balls. Treatments: UHP at 250 or 350 MPa for 10 min (water as pressure medium, room temperature; adiabatic heating stated as about 2–3 °C/100 MPa) versus autoclave heating at 121 °C for 13 or 15 min, versus untreated control. Samples held at 4 °C and analysed at 0 and 7 days. Endpoints: total bacteria (GB 4789.2-2022 plate count, 30 ± 1 °C, 72 h, CFU/g); CIE L*a*b* and Hunter whiteness (triplicate); TPA texture (hardness, elasticity, chewiness; P5 probe, 50% strain; triplicate); MDA/TBARS (GB 5009.181-2016); TVB-N (authors cite GB 5009.181-2016 for TVB-N; calculation as mg/100 g); sensory scores from eight trained students (colour, smell, elasticity, texture, taste; 0–25 each). SPSS; mean ± SD; p < 0.05.

## Sample / data

Prepared balls from fresh mackerel (~1 kg each) bought in Zhanjiang; white meat, scallion-ginger water (~10:1), sweet potato flour (~125:2), egg white; boiled until cooked, cooled, vacuum packed, then treated. Each experimental group analysed in triplicate. Sensory n = 8 panellists. Storage: 4 °C, 0 and 7 days.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Controlled laboratory comparison (pre-cooked surimi balls; UHP vs retort vs untreated; refrigerated storage).
- **Population / model:** Prepared mackerel (Scomberomorus) fish balls (human food; pre-cooked then treated).
- **n:** Triplicate samples per group for laboratory assays; 8 sensory panellists. Exact number of fish lots or balls per bag not reported.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 250 MPa/10 min or 350 MPa/10 min UHP versus 121 °C/13 min or 121 °C/15 min versus untreated control; all then 4 °C.
- **Primary endpoint:** Total bacterial count (lg CFU/g) at day 0 and day 7; supporting chemical (MDA mg/kg; TVB-N mg/100 g), colour, TPA, and sensory scores.
- **Primary result:** Day 0 control 2.18 lg(CFU/g); 250 and 350 MPa both <1 lg(CFU/g); 121 °C/13 min 1.74 and 121 °C/15 min 1.77 lg(CFU/g) (p < 0.05 vs control). Day 7 control 3.62 lg(CFU/g); treated groups remained significantly lower (p < 0.05). Hardness (N): control 10.81 ± 0.86; 250 MPa 6.00 ± 0.76; 350 MPa 6.95 ± 1.72; 121 °C/13 min 8.26 ± 1.54; 121 °C/15 min 10.27 ± 2.19. Elasticity (mm): control 4.87 ± 0.38; 350 MPa 5.99 ± 0.42 (p < 0.05 vs control). Chewiness (mJ): control 26.74 ± 0.70; 250 MPa 20.33 ± 0.53; 350 MPa 24.69 ± 4.65 (ns vs control); both thermal groups ~19.6–19.7 (p < 0.05). Day-0 L*: control 54.90 ± 0.39; 350 MPa 51.46 ± 0.13 (p < 0.05); 250 MPa 54.42 ± 0.04 (ns). Heat raised b* (121 °C/15 min 15.85 ± 0.29 vs control 12.23 ± 0.34); UHP did not (350 MPa 10.98 ± 0.79). Authors recommend 350 MPa/10 min. MDA and TVB-N were lower after both UHP and heat vs control (p < 0.05); exact MDA/TVB-N values are in Figures 3–4 only (not tabulated in text).
- **Cannot show:** Pathogen-specific or spore inactivation; commercial sterility; enzyme activity (not measured); whether 250 vs 350 MPa differ in log kill (both reported as <1 lg CFU/g); absolute MDA/TVB-N numbers without reading the figures; long-term shelf-life beyond 7 days; industrial scale-up.

## Key findings

- Measurement: Both UHP cycles dropped total bacteria below 1 lg CFU/g at day 0, versus ~1.7 lg after 121 °C and 2.18 lg in the untreated control (Results, total bacterial count; Figure 2).
- Measurement: After 7 days at 4 °C, untreated balls reached 3.62 lg CFU/g; UHP and thermal groups remained significantly lower (p < 0.05) (same section).
- Author interpretation: Residual colonies after both processes may include pressure-resistant Gram-positives (S. aureus, L. monocytogenes needing ≥500 MPa) or spores surviving >1000 MPa (cited Olaonipekun 2025; not measured here) (Results).
- Measurement: 350 MPa/10 min lowered L* and whiteness vs control at day 0; 250 MPa/10 min did not change L* vs control (Table 2). Thermal treatments increased b* (yellowing); UHP did not (Table 2).
- Measurement: UHP reduced hardness vs control; 350 MPa increased elasticity; thermal treatments reduced chewiness (Table 3).
- Measurement: MDA and TVB-N declined vs control after both UHP and heat; 350 MPa/10 min gave the largest TVB-N drop at day 0 (Figures 3–4; exact bar values not printed).
- Measurement: Highest composite sensory score at 350 MPa/10 min; strongest sensory detriment at 121 °C/15 min (Figure 5; numeric scores not printed). Colour and flavour after UHP ns vs control (p > 0.05) (Sensory section).
- Proposed mechanism (not directly measured): UHP sterilizes by destroying microbial cell membranes (Results). Protein-denaturation / water-retention explanations for texture are cited from Zeng et al. 2022, not measured in these balls.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Authors: process conditions “can be further optimized”; practical application with low-temperature storage needs exploration (Conclusion).
- Fish balls were boiled before UHP/heat, so this is not a raw-product kill step.
- Total plate count only; no named pathogens, no spore assay, no enzyme assay.
- Seven-day refrigerated window only.
- Sensory panel of eight students; scoring sheet in Table 1.
- Some p-value wording in the colour section is internally inconsistent (e.g. “significant decrease (p > 0.05)”).
- MDA/TVB-N and sensory totals exist only as figures, so numeric values cannot be extracted from the text without guessing.

## Relevance to our research question

Directly relevant: a named UHP cycle (250 vs 350 MPa, 10 min) is compared with a named thermal cycle on a human surimi food. The same 10 min hold is not the same outcome as 121 °C/13–15 min: UHP gave lower plate counts and better colour/texture/sensory, while 350 MPa still shifted L* and hardness relative to 250 MPa.
