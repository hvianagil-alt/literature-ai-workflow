# A Scoping Review of Thermal and Nonthermal Technologies for Listeria monocytogenes Inactivation in Ready-to-Eat Meats

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Guzel M, Okur I. Comprehensive Reviews in Food Science and Food Safety. 2026;25:e70506. doi:10.1111/1541-4337.70506
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Guzel2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What magnitude of log reduction of Listeria monocytogenes is reported for thermal and nonthermal technologies on RTE meats, and how do processing conditions change that effect?

## Methods
PRISMA-ScR scoping review. PICO-defined search of Scopus, PubMed and Web of Science. 1111 records → 688 after deduplication → 124 full texts → 74 included. Two-expert title/abstract then full-text screen. Excluded non-RTE meats, papers without inactivation data, and combinations with antimicrobials other than salt/nitrite. Designed to map evidence, not to pool a single effect size. Meta-analysis was not performed because 31/74 studies lacked SDs and some reported only D-values.

## Sample / data
74 studies of L. monocytogenes inactivation in RTE meats (deli, ham, frankfurters, salami, etc.). HPP was the most studied technology: 29/74 (39.2%). Thermal 18/74 (24.2%); ionizing radiation 9; UV 8; pulsed light 4. 27/74 (36.5%) used a single Listeria strain. Among HPP papers, 8/29 (27.6%) were single-strain.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** scoping review (PRISMA-ScR); no meta-analysis
- **Population / model:** Listeria monocytogenes in ready-to-eat meats intended for consumption without a further consumer cook
- **n:** 74 included studies; HPP subset 29/74 (39.2%)
- **Intervention / comparator:** thermal cooking/pasteurization vs HPP vs ionizing radiation, UV, pulsed light, cold plasma; untreated controls in the primary studies
- **Primary endpoint:** log reduction of L. monocytogenes (CFU/g or equivalent); some papers only D-values
- **Primary result:** Authors did not pool a single HPP effect size. Across HPP studies, reported log reductions ranged from 0 to 7 log. Most HPP used 300–600 MPa (highest 800 MPa), holds generally <15 min, temperatures typically 4–25 °C. Below 400 MPa, inactivation was typically <1 log even with long holds. At 500–600 MPa, reductions were most commonly 1–7 log CFU/g in ≤5 min. Named examples: Spanish chorizo 0.04 log at 349 MPa/6.25 min vs 2.47 log at 600 MPa/6.25 min; sliced ham 0.95 log at 400 MPa/3 min vs 4.25 log at 600 MPa/3 min (Myers et al.); Myers et al. ham/turkey at 600 MPa/3 min ≈ 3.85–4.35 log CFU/g, salt (1.8 vs 2.4%) and nitrite (0 vs 500 ppm) not significant, while 400 MPa/3 min was <1 log. Hereu et al. isoreduction: a 5-log L. monocytogenes reduction was not achievable below 500 MPa in cooked ham or mortadella; cooked ham needed ~1 min at 600 MPa or 5 min at 500 MPa; mortadella ~1 min at 600 MPa or 3 min at 500 MPa. Fat could piezoprotect at ≥650 MPa (Bover-Cid dry-cured ham, 10–50% fat). Thermal treatments were judged the most consistent class; HPP and ionizing radiation the strongest nonthermal options, but HPP performance is not uniform across products.
- **Cannot show:** a pooled mean log reduction for HPP; that any one 600 MPa cycle meets a 5-log Listeria standard in every RTE meat; spore outcomes (Listeria is vegetative); consumer cook-step comparisons.

## Key findings
- Measurement: 29 of 74 included papers were HPP — the most studied nonthermal intervention in this RTE-meat Listeria set (Abstract; screening results).
- Measurement: 400 MPa/3 min on sliced ham ≈ 0.95 log vs 600 MPa/3 min ≈ 4.25 log — same hold time, different pressure, different outcome (Section 3.3.1.1).
- Author interpretation: pressure level dominates immediate inactivation; salt/nitrite did not consistently change HPP kill under the reviewed conditions; products were most pressure-resistant at 10–30 °C.
- Mechanism grade: HPP membrane disruption and enzyme inactivation are **cited** (including EFSA/Koutsoumanis); piezoprotection by fat is **cited** from the included primary studies.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- No meta-analysis (missing SDs, D-value-only papers).
- 36.5% single-strain challenges.
- Culture-based counts may overstate lethality if injured cells fail to recover.
- Combinations with essential oils/chemical antimicrobials were excluded, so hurdle-HPP literature is out of this map.
- Quality/cost/sensory were out of scope.

## Relevance to our research question
HPP is the dominant nonthermal Listeria tool in RTE meats, but a named MPa is not a fixed log kill: <400 MPa is usually <1 log, 500–600 MPa spans 1–7 log in minutes, and fat/product type can move a 5-log target on or off the table at the same pressure.
