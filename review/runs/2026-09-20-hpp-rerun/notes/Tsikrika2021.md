# High-pressure processing and ultrasonication of minimally processed potatoes: Effect on the colour, microbial counts, and bioactive compounds

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Tsikrika K, Walsh D, Joseph A, Burgess CM, Rai DK (2021). Molecules 26:2614. doi:10.3390/molecules26092614
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Tsikrika2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Do HPP at 600 MPa and HPP followed by ultrasound change color, microbial counts during 14-day chilled storage, total phenolics, antioxidant activity, individual polyphenols, and glycoalkaloids of vacuum-packed peeled Irish potatoes (Maris Piper and Rooster)?

## Methods
Peeled tubers vacuum-packed in PE/PA pouches. HPP: Hiperbaric 55HT commercial unit, 600 MPa, 3 min, maximum temperature 10.6 °C. HPP/US: after HPP, cubes 2 × 2 × 2 cm (~150 g) sonicated 20 kHz, 200 W, 10 min (start 18 °C, end 28 ± 2 °C). Stored vacuum-packed 14 days at 4 °C. Color L*a*b* ΔE days 0, 1, 7, 10, 14. Microbes: Enterobacteriaceae (ISO 21528-2), aerobic plate count, yeasts and moulds, *Salmonella* absence (ISO 6579). TPC (Folin–Ciocalteu, µg GAE/g dw), FRAP and DPPH, LC phytochemicals. Experiments in duplicate, analyses ×3 (n = 6). ANOVA + Tukey, p < 0.05.

## Sample / data
Two Irish cultivars: Maris Piper (white) and Rooster (coloured). n = 6 for color and phytochemicals.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Controlled storage study of commercial-scale HPP ± laboratory ultrasound on fresh-cut potatoes.
- **Population / model:** Hand-peeled, vacuum-packed Maris Piper and Rooster potatoes; indigenous microbiota (not inoculated).
- **n:** Duplicate process runs, analyses in triplicate (n = 6).
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 600 MPa, 3 min, 10.6 °C vs HPP then US 20 kHz, 200 W, 10 min vs untreated vacuum-packed controls; 14 days at 4 °C.
- **Primary endpoint:** Color (L*, a*, b*, ΔE); Enterobacteriaceae, APC, yeasts/moulds (presence/delay, not always numeric in text); TPC (µg GAE/g dw); glycoalkaloids α-chaconine and α-solanine (µg/g dw).
- **Primary result:** HPP and HPP/US significantly changed a*, b*, L*, and ΔE vs controls (p < 0.05). Example ΔE at day 14: Rooster untreated 8.06 ± 0.84, HPP 21.23 ± 1.61, HPP/US 18.03 ± 1.57; Maris Piper HPP/US ΔE rose to 18.96 ± 2.43 by day 14. Enterobacteriaceae were not detectable in HPP-only samples of both cultivars throughout; HPP/US slowed Enterobacteriaceae but was not significant vs untreated by day 14. APC was delayed until day 10 (HPP/US Rooster lowest) then not different from untreated at day 14. Yeasts/moulds remained lower in treated samples at day 14. *Salmonella* absent in all samples. TPC unchanged vs controls: untreated Maris Piper 542 ± 10 and Rooster 646 ± 35 µg GAE/g dw (p > 0.05 after HPP or HPP/US). FRAP and DPPH changes were statistically insignificant. Chlorogenic acid (untreated ~81–97 µg/g dw) fell after both treatments (Maris Piper untreated 80.60 ± 6.4 vs HPP 28.38 ± 6.7 vs HPP/US 3.31 ± 0.21 µg/g dw; Rooster untreated 97.19 ± 1.7 vs HPP 15.85 ± 0.4 vs HPP/US 1.78 ± 0.21). Quinic acid increased. Glycoalkaloids: untreated α-chaconine 1.06–1.52 µg/g and α-solanine 1.10–1.73 µg/g. Maris Piper HPP cut both by 72%; HPP/US by ~97% (α-chaconine 0.05 ± 0.02, α-solanine 0.06 ± 0.005 µg/g). Rooster HPP: α-chaconine −55%, α-solanine −66%; HPP/US −95% (both 0.06 ± 0.01 µg/g).
- **Cannot show:** Pathogen challenge reductions (no inoculation; *Salmonella* never present). Complete PPO inactivation (prior work cited; browning during storage implies residual PPO). Numeric log CFU values for APC/Enterobacteriaceae (figures only in the extracted text). HPP/US is sequential after cutting, not simultaneous HPP+US.

## Key findings
- Named 600 MPa/3 min completely removed detectable Enterobacteriaceae in both cultivars; adding 10 min US after HPP did not reproduce that Enterobacteriaceae kill by day 14. (Section 2.2)
- Measurement: TPC and AOA unchanged while chlorogenic/caffeic acids fell and quinic acid rose—profile shift without total phenolic change. (Sections 2.3–2.5)
- Measurement: HPP/US reduced glycoalkaloids more than HPP alone (≈95–97% vs 55–72%). (Section 2.5 / Table 2)
- Color ΔE of treated potatoes was large by day 7–14 (often >12), i.e. HPP did not freeze fresh-cut color. (Table 1)
- Mechanism grade: residual PPO proposed as cause of storage browning (cited prior measurement, not re-assayed here). Polyphenol losses under US attributed to hydroxyl radicals (cited, not measured).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Microbial counts are discussed qualitatively relative to figures; exact log CFU are not tabulated in the text extract.
- US applied after HPP and after dicing, so HPP/US is not a single packaged process.
- Two cultivars, one HPP condition (600 MPa/3 min only).
- Authors note TPC response differed from their previous whole-potato HPP study.

## Relevance to our research question
Shows a named 600 MPa/3 min cycle is not interchangeable with 600 MPa/3 min plus US: Enterobacteriaceae outcome, ΔE trajectory, chlorogenic acid, and glycoalkaloids differ. Heat is not the kill step; residual enzyme activity still browns potatoes during 4 °C storage.
