# Enumeration Agar, Acid Exposure and Sampling Time Are Relevant Factors Accounting for the High-Pressure Inactivation of Vegetative Pathogens in Fruit Puree

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Torrents-Masoliver B, Jofré A, Ribas-Agustí A, Bover-Cid S. 2024. Foods 13(16):2600. DOI:10.3390/foods13162600. PMID:39200527
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-hpp-rerun/Torrents-Masoliver2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

How much do enumeration agar, 24 h acid exposure before HPP, sampling time after HPP, fruit matrix (apple vs banana–apple), and strain identity change the measured log reduction and sublethal injury of L. monocytogenes, Salmonella spp., and E. coli in infant-style fruit purees treated at 300 MPa for 2 min?

## Methods

Challenge tests in Golden apple puree (pH 3.32 ± 0.03, 13.33 ± 0.06 °Brix, 0.68 ± 0.01% malic acid, 2828 ± 194 mg/kg polyphenols) and banana–apple 1:1 puree (pH 4.02 ± 0.02, 15.47 ± 0.35 °Brix, 0.55 ± 0.02% malic acid, 2091 ± 294 mg/kg polyphenols). Background microbiota removed by 600 MPa/6 min, then frozen at −20 °C. Inocula ~10^7–10^8 CFU/g (1% v/w). HPP: 300 MPa, 2 min, Wave 6000 (Hiperbaric, 120 L); water at 19 °C start; come-up 2.2 ± 0.2 min; decompression almost immediate. Treatments: HPP immediately after inoculation; acid exposure (AE) 24 h at 4 °C in apple puree; AE then HPP (AEHPP). Enumeration immediately and 24 h after treatment (4 °C). Media: TSAYE (T); TSAYE + 4% NaCl (TS); chromogenic selective (S); selective + TSAYE overlay (SO). Two independent experiments; plated in duplicate. ANOVA/Tukey p < 0.05. Sublethal injury = log(T) − log(TS).

## Sample / data

Cocktails: 3 L. monocytogenes, 3 Salmonella (plus additional single Salmonella strains later), 2 E. coli. Single-strain apple-puree set: 4 L. monocytogenes, 8 Salmonella, 4 E. coli (including non-toxigenic O157:H7 CECT5947). Aliquots ~5 g in PA/PE bags.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Laboratory challenge test (cocktail then single-strain); factorial agar × matrix × acid exposure × sampling time.
- **Population / model:** L. monocytogenes, Salmonella spp., and E. coli inoculated into hygienized apple or banana–apple puree (infant-food context).
- **n:** Two independent experiments; duplicate plating. Inoculum ca. 10^7–10^8 CFU/g. Strain counts as listed above.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 300 MPa/2 min versus untreated inoculum (N0); AE (24 h, 4 °C, apple) ± HPP; immediate versus 24 h delayed enumeration; four agars.
- **Primary endpoint:** Inactivation = log(N/N0) in log CFU/g; sublethal injury as log ratio T/TS.
- **Primary result:** Banana–apple, immediate after HPP (agar ns): L. monocytogenes 0.3 ± 0.2 log; Salmonella 1.4 ± 0.3 log; E. coli 0.9 ± 0.1 log. Apple puree HPP inactivation 0.9- to 4.5-fold higher than banana–apple. Apple, L. monocytogenes cocktail: 1.8 ± 0.3 log immediately vs 3.5 ± 0.2 log at 24 h (p < 0.05). Salmonella cocktail: 4.3 ± 1.2 log immediately vs 5.5 ± 0.7 log at 24 h. E. coli cocktail immediately: 1.8 ± 0.3 log on T vs mean 2.8 ± 0.2 log on S/SO; 24 h mean 3.7 ± 0.2 log (agars ns). AE alone: Salmonella 1.7 ± 0.6 log; E. coli 1.0 ± 0.4 log; L. monocytogenes 0.5 log on TSAYE to 2.5 log on SO. AEHPP L. monocytogenes: 2.8 ± 0.5 log immediately vs 4.4 ± 0.5 log at 24 h. Single-strain apple HPP: L. monocytogenes 1.2 log (12MOB047LM) to 4.8 log (Scott A); AEHPP Scott A 6.2 ± 0.1 log. E. coli CECT5947 HPP 0.6 ± 0.1 log vs CTC1030 2.8 ± 0.7 log; AEHPP ~1 log (CECT5947) vs ~4 log (CTC1030). Abstract states CECT5947 as most piezo-resistant at 1.5 log reduction and Scott A as 6-log when acid-exposed and sampled 24 h after HPP.
- **Cannot show:** Industrial 400–600 MPa/6–10 min performance; spore or virus inactivation; nutritional or sensory quality; whether delayed 24 h counts would recover in a low-acid matrix (opposite of this acid puree); toxin production.

## Key findings

- Measurement: Same 300 MPa/2 min cycle killed more in apple (pH 3.32) than banana–apple (pH 4.02) for all three species (Section 3.1; abstract 0.9- to 4.5-fold).
- Measurement: Selective/chromogenic agars often recorded higher inactivation than nutrient-rich TSAYE (e.g. E. coli immediately after HPP or AEHPP) (Section 3.1).
- Measurement: 24 h delayed enumeration in apple puree generally increased recorded inactivation (no recovery); authors contrast this with Serra-Castelló et al. pet-food work at pH 6.8 where 24 h sampling lowered apparent inactivation (Discussion).
- Measurement: Strain range within species was large (L. monocytogenes 1.2–4.8 log HPP; E. coli 0.6–2.8 log HPP) (Section 3.2).
- Author interpretation: Some strains showed AEHPP less than additive AE + HPP (possible acid cross-protection), e.g. L. monocytogenes 12MOB089LM ~1.0 log below additive; E. coli CTC1030 likewise (Section 3.2). Direct stress-response genes were not measured.
- Measurement: Sublethal injury after HPP in apple: L. monocytogenes 0.6 ± 0.2 to 1.7 ± 0.2 log; Salmonella 0.9–4.5 log (CCUG34136T 4.5 ± 0.5; CIP106188 4.3 ± 0.1); E. coli 1.3 ± 0.6 log (strain ns) (Section 3.3).
- Author conclusion: For fruit-puree HPP validation, E. coli (especially O157:H7 CECT5947) should be the target pathogen (Conclusions).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Authors: further work needed at other pressure levels (Conclusions).
- Purees were pre-sterilized at 600 MPa/6 min then frozen; matrix is not native microbiota.
- HPP intensity (300 MPa/2 min) is below typical industrial 400–600 MPa holds.
- E. coli O157:H7 strain is stx-negative.
- Abstract 1.5 log for CECT5947 vs Results 0.6 ± 0.1 log for HPP of that strain — report both; do not collapse.
- Two independent experiments only; cocktail Salmonella list in Methods 2.3 includes CTC1754 (Rissen), which is not in the 2.1 strain list.

## Relevance to our research question

Core evidence that a named HPP cycle (300 MPa, 2 min) is not a fixed log kill: matrix pH, acid contact time before pressure, strain, agar, and 24 h sampling time change inactivation and injury of vegetative pathogens in fruit puree.
