# Synthesis rationale — 2026-09-20-hpp-rerun

## (i) Intellectual model

1. **Central question.** When heat is not the kill step, what do hydrostatic high-pressure processing (HPP / HHP) and, separately, high-pressure homogenization (HPH) do to microorganisms, enzymes, and quality in human foods, and when is a named pressure–time–temperature cycle not the same safety or quality outcome?

2. **Variables that change the outcome.** Pressure (MPa), hold time, process temperature (including adiabatic compression heating), come-up rate, frozen versus unfrozen state, water activity and moisture, pH and organic-acid hurdles, matrix (puree, juice, milk fat, meat, seafood, honey), strain identity, enumeration method (agar, acid exposure, sampling time, culture versus VBNC), and whether the cycle is ambient HPP or high-pressure thermal processing (HPTP).

3. **Mechanisms as the papers state them.** Compression of membranes and leakage of nucleic acids; PI-permeable pores that can shrink during chilled storage; protein unfolding via non-covalent disruption (hydrophobicity, allergenicity, enzyme active-site hydrogen bonds); adiabatic heating (~3 °C per 100 MPa in water-rich foods) when temperature is allowed to rise; lactate “piezo-protection” of *Listeria*; phenolic biosynthesis at mild pressure versus extraction at higher pressure. Several of these are **proposed** or modelled, not always directly assayed in the same trial.

4. **Heterogeneity.** Juice 300 MPa/2 min can be <1 log or several log depending on puree pH and strain; milk >5-log vegetative bars sit at different MPa for *Listeria*, *Salmonella*, and *S. aureus*; 600 MPa/4 min pumpkin pulp is sterile for 45 days only with a formulated mix; frozen orange juice at 400 MPa is not the same *E. coli* kill as unfrozen juice; 400 MPa on ham is slower when lactate is added; honey spores ignore 600 MPa until water activity is raised.

5. **Evidence types.** Primary laboratory and semi-industrial trials (inactivation kinetics, storage, sensory); one PRISMA systematic review with mixed-model ANOVA (bovine milk); one PRISMA-ScR scoping review (RTE-meat *Listeria*); one EFSA BIOHAZ scientific opinion (legal pasteurisation and spores); narrative reviews of plant foods, dairy, proteins, aroma, phenolics, aquatic foods, HPTP, HPH, sugarcane juice, and VBNC risk.

6. **Established (this sample).** Ambient industrial HPP (typically 400–600 MPa, minutes) inactivates many vegetative bacteria and yeasts in high-moisture foods and can extend chilled shelf-life. It does **not** inactivate bacterial spores at commercial pressures without added heat. A labelled MPa is not a pasteurisation equivalent for ruminant milk under EU thermal criteria. Enzyme and colour endpoints often move against plate-count success.

7. **Suggested / uncertain.** Whether freeze-then-HPP generalises beyond *E. coli* K12; how often culture “kill” is VBNC rather than death; whether two 1 s pulses at 600 MPa can replace a 4 min hold for pathogens (only background flora in lamb burgers); whether HPH shear is interchangeable with hydrostatic HPP for the same log claim.

8. **Unknown in this sample.** A retrieved primary paired trial of HPP versus 72 °C/15 s HTST on *S. aureus* in milk that would independently test the EFSA milk conclusion; a retrieved 5-log juice HACCP pathogen validation at industrial 600 MPa; *C. botulinum* spore kinetics under true non-thermal HPP; a primary HPH challenge trial (only a short review was OA).

9. **Consequence.** Process specifications, legal claims, and quality guarantees cannot be copied across foods or even across two cycles that share a megapascal number. The review must teach coefficients first, then show matrix evidence, then name what a 600 MPa label still cannot mean.

**Causal chain:** named HPP cycle → membrane/protein/ice/solute physics (as measured or proposed) → log kill, enzyme residual, colour, or aroma → those effects depend on matrix, injury assay, and heat assistance → therefore commercial success and a legal 5-log or milk-pasteurisation claim are different questions.

## (a) What each included study actually measured

Primary trials and models (what they did **not** measure is in the last clause of each block).

- **Nikparvar 2021.** Broth *L. monocytogenes* Scott A, 400 MPa/8 min/8 °C; PI pore diffusion and plate counts during 8 °C storage (n = 318 cells; three count replicates). Not a food matrix; one strain.
- **Machado 2023.** Raw bovine cream, HPP 450–600 MPa versus 75 °C/15 s; endogenous flora, inoculated *E. coli*/*L. innocua*, colour, storage. Two batches only.
- **Rossi Ribeiro 2026.** Pumpkin pulp ± peel preservative ± sorbate; 600 MPa/4 min versus heat; 45-day 5 °C microbiology and texture. No pathogen challenge.
- **Wang 2025.** Pre-cooked mackerel balls; 250/350 MPa 10 min versus 121 °C retort; day-0/7 counts, colour, MDA, sensory n = 8. Not a spore study.
- **Torrents-Masoliver 2024.** Pathogens in apple vs banana–apple puree; **300 MPa/2 min** only; agar × acid × sampling time. Not an industrial 600 MPa juice validation.
- **Bulut 2021.** *E. coli* K12 in PBS and orange juice; freeze then 250–400 MPa at 4 °C; SEM and nucleic-acid leak. Surrogate not STEC; one juice.
- **Marçal 2021.** Seaweed-fortified frankfurters/chouriço; 600 MPa/6 min shelf-life. No inoculated pathogens.
- **Li 2021.** Whole Concord puree; 600 MPa/3 min/5 °C vs 63 °C/3 min; APC/Y&M, phenolics, enzymes, n = 101 sensory. Indigenous flora, not 5-log named pathogens.
- **Pérez Alcalá 2023.** Sous-vide cod ± HP 300–600 MPa; TSA counts and PMA-16S. No *C. botulinum*.
- **Zhang 2026.** Jujube juice; 500 MPa/10 min ± ultrasound versus 85 °C; fermentation quality, not pathogen kill.
- **Koker 2023.** Donkey milk; 200–500 MPa vs 75 °C; TABC, lysozyme, lactoferrin, rheology. Native load only ~4 log; no *S. aureus* challenge.
- **Nilsuwan 2024.** Squid + mint extract × 400 MPa; TVC, TVB, 16S, sensory n = 50. Hurdle, not HPP alone as pasteurisation.
- **Eran Nagar 2026.** Red Globe juice; 500 MPa/5 min matched to ~3-log indigenous aerobes versus OH and plate heat; PPO residual. Not 5-log pathogens.
- **Tsikrika 2021.** Potatoes; commercial 600 MPa/3 min ± ultrasound; colour, Enterobacteriaceae, phenolics, glycoalkaloids. Counts largely qualitative.
- **Rodríguez 2024.** *E. coli* DSM682 in already-pasteurised orange juice; US then 300–400 MPa; ODE model. Re-inoculated commercial juice.
- **Serra-Castelló 2021.** Three *L. monocytogenes* strains on cooked ham ± lactate/diacetate; 400 MPa Weibull kinetics. Frozen inoculum.
- **Pimsannil 2025.** Precooked crab lump/roe; COSC × 100–500 MPa; APC and *Vibrio*. Methods/table pressure labels disagree (100/300 vs 500 MPa).
- **González-Cantillo 2026.** Lamb burgers ± lupine flour; 600 MPa 4 min vs two 1 s pulses; background flora, colour. Not a pathogen validation.
- **Puangwerakul 2024.** Plant-based eggs; 500 MPa/5 min vs gamma/EBI; *C. perfringens* and ASLT. Accelerated 40–50 °C storage.
- **Le 2023.** Celery juices at three pH values; 400–600 MPa/5 min; TAC/YM, PPO/POD, colour over 8 weeks. “At least two” replicates.
- **Scepankova 2022.** *B. subtilis* spores in honey vs diluted honey; HPP, PATP 600 MPa/85 °C, hyperbaric storage. Not *C. botulinum*.
- **Castrica 2021.** Salmon and plaice; 500 MPa/2 min/4 °C; flora, histamine, volatiles, sensory n = 5.
- **Jönsson 2023.** *Saccharina latissima*; 200–600 MPa/180 s; texture collapse vs colour; microbial n = 2 bags.

## (b) Themes the data support vs themes that would be forced

**Supported.** (1) Ambient HPP reduces many vegetative organisms in high-moisture foods, with 400–600 MPa the industrial window in the EFSA opinion and in primary storage trials. (2) The same MPa is not one kill: matrix, ice, acid, strain, agar, lactate, and hold time change log reductions inside this sample. (3) Spores and low water activity stand outside ambient HPP; HPTP is a different process because heat is part of the kill. (4) Quality (PPO, colour, texture, aroma, whey proteins, allergenicity) can improve or worsen independently of plate counts. (5) Legal 5-log juice language and legal milk pasteurisation are **performance claims**, not synonyms for “we ran 600 MPa.” (6) HPH is a shear technology; this sample has no primary HPH challenge trial.

**Forced if written.** Equal-time chapters on PEF, plasma, or ultrasound as if they were co-equal heroes; that HPP “pasteurises milk” in the EU thermal sense; that freeze-HPP is a validated STEC process; that two 1 s pulses equal a 4 min *Listeria* cycle; a pooled log reduction across juices, milk, and meat; consumer or energy claims (not measured here).

## (c) Real disagreements and why

- **300 MPa juice kill.** Torrents-Masoliver: often <2 log in banana–apple puree at 300 MPa/2 min. Rodríguez: 400 MPa/3 min can take *E. coli* to not detected in already-pasteurised orange juice. Different pressure, matrix, and whether the juice was pre-pasteurised.
- **600 MPa and enzymes vs microbes.** Li: 600 MPa/3 min ≈ 5-log APC/Y&M in Concord puree, better yeast control in storage than 63 °C/3 min. Eran Nagar: 500 MPa/5 min matched only ~3-log indigenous aerobes and left PPO active while heat killed PPO. Same “HPP juice” label, different microbial bar and enzyme fate.
- **Milk 5-log vs cream vs donkey milk.** Sykora’s mixed model puts *S. aureus* near 600 MPa for >5 log; Machado’s cream 600 MPa outlasts 75 °C/15 s for endogenous flora; Koker’s donkey milk hits the count floor at 500 MPa because starting TABC is only 4 log. Different fat, starting load, and whether the endpoint is a legal 5-log or native TABC.
- **400 MPa *Listeria*.** Nikparvar: large plate reduction in broth with repairable pores. Serra-Castelló: lactate **protects** *Listeria* on ham (longer δ). Guzel: RTE meats <400 MPa usually <1 log. Broth ≠ ham ± organic acids.
- **Spore story is consistent, not a disagreement.** Zhang 2022, Houška, Koutsoumanis, Knoerzer, Scepankova: ambient HPP does not kill spores; heat-assisted pressure or raised aW is required. Puangwerakul’s day-0 zero *C. perfringens* after 500 MPa in a plant-egg analogue is a different matrix and organism and must not be generalised to proteolytic *C. botulinum*.
