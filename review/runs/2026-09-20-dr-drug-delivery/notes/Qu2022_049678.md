# Sustained therapeutic effect of an anti-inflammatory peptide encapsulated in nanoparticles on ocular vascular leakage in diabetic retinopathy

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Qu Q, Park K, Zhou K, Wassel D, Farjo R, Criswell T, Ma J-x, Zhang Y. Front Cell Dev Biol. 2022;10:1049678. doi:10.3389/fcell.2022.1049678
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Qu2022_049678.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (original study: intravitreal PLGA nanoparticles of a PEDF 34-mer in OIR and STZ-diabetic rats)

## Research question
Does encapsulating the N-terminal 34-mer of pigment epithelium-derived factor in PLGA nanoparticles (PEDF34-NP) give a single intravitreal injection lasting anti-inflammatory and anti-leakage activity in oxygen-induced retinopathy (OIR) and streptozotocin (STZ) diabetic rats, without detectable retinal toxicity?

## Methods
Water–oil–water double emulsion: 10 mg PEDF34 in 200 µL water into 100 mg PLGA in 2 mL dichloromethane; sonication; 0.8% w/v F68 outer phase; particles collected at 3,000 rpm. Size reported 160 ± 20 nm; unencapsulated peptide washed off; blank PLGA-NP as Control-NP. In vitro: ARPE19 and primary bovine retinal capillary endothelial cells (BRCEC) under 1% O2; VEGF Western blot, ELISA of secreted VEGF, VEGF mRNA, MTT (n=3). OIR: Brown Norway rats, 75% O2 from P7–P12, then room air; IVT PEDF34-NP or Control-NP at P12; FITC-dextran angiography and pre-retinal nuclear counts at P18 (n=6); Western blot ICAM-1/VEGF/albumin at P16 (n=3); Evans blue permeability at P16 (n=6). STZ diabetes: 2-month-old Brown Norway rats ~200 g; 50 mg/kg IP STZ; diabetes if glucose >300 mg/dL; IVT at 2 weeks of diabetes; Evans blue 4 weeks after injection (n=5); Western blot albumin/VEGF/ICAM-1/TNF-α/non-phosphorylated β-catenin/CTGF (n=3); qPCR of Vegf, Icam-1, Tnf-α, Mcp-1, Ctgf (n=3). Toxicity: scotopic/photopic ERG and H&E 4 weeks post-injection (n=6). p<0.05 (Methods; figure legends).

## Sample / data
NP diameter 160 ± 20 nm. Cell assays n=3. OIR angiography/NV n=6; OIR Western n=3; OIR Evans blue n=6. STZ Evans blue n=5; STZ protein/mRNA n=3. ERG n=6.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** preclinical nanoparticle efficacy study (in vitro hypoxia + OIR + STZ diabetes)
- **Population / model:** ARPE19 and BRCEC; Brown Norway rat OIR (75% ± 2% oxygen P7–P12); STZ diabetic Brown Norway rats (glucose >300 mg/dL)
- **n:** OIR non-perfusion and pre-retinal nuclei n=6; OIR Evans blue n=6; STZ Evans blue n=5; Western/qPCR n=3; ERG n=6; in vitro n=3
- **Intervention / comparator:** PEDF34-PLGA-NP vs empty PLGA Control-NP vs untreated; Methods state 20 µg/eye IVT; figure legends for OIR and STZ state 5 µg/eye (see Open questions)
- **Primary endpoint:** retinal vascular permeability (Evans blue-albumin, normalized to retinal protein) in STZ rats 4 weeks after one IVT dose; OIR non-perfusion area (% total retina) and pre-retinal vascular nuclei at P18
- **Primary result:** PEDF34-NP reduced hypoxia-driven VEGF protein and secreted VEGF in ARPE19/BRCEC and reduced BRCEC growth without lowering ARPE19 viability (Figure 1; n=3). In OIR, PEDF34-NP reduced central non-perfusion and pre-retinal nuclei vs Control-NP and untreated (Figure 2J,K; n=6; numeric %/counts not printed in text). OIR retinas: VEGF −27% (p<0.05) and ICAM-1 −31% (p<0.01) vs Control-NP; retinal albumin −30%; Evans blue permeability lower vs Control-NP and untreated (Figure 3; Western n=3, Evans n=6). In STZ rats 4 weeks after IVT, Evans blue leakage was higher in untreated and Control-NP diabetic eyes vs non-diabetic (p<0.01) and was reduced by PEDF34-NP vs Control-NP (p<0.01) (Figure 4A; n=5). PEDF34-NP also reduced retinal albumin (p<0.05) and VEGF protein, and at 4 weeks reduced TNF-α (p<0.05), ICAM-1 (p<0.01), non-phosphorylated β-catenin, and CTGF vs Control-NP (Figure 5; n=3). mRNA of Vegf, Icam-1, Tnf-α, Mcp-1, and Ctgf decreased (p<0.01) vs Control-NP (Figure 6; n=3). No significant a- or b-wave ERG change and no H&E structural difference 4 weeks after injection (Figure 7; n=6).
- **Cannot show:** human DR/DME outcomes, comparison with an anti-VEGF antibody or steroid implant, entrapment efficiency or in vitro peptide-release curve, or which of 5 vs 20 µg was actually injected in the plotted experiments

## Key findings
- Particle size 160 ± 20 nm; EE% not reported (Methods 2.2).
- Authors state naked PEDF peptide half-life <48 h after IVT (cited Zhang et al., 2001); PEDF34-NP effects lasted at least 4 weeks in the STZ model (Discussion).
- Control-NP did not differ from untreated OIR for Evans blue leakage (Figure 3E) or from untreated diabetic rats for retinal albumin (Figure 4).
- Inhibitory MTT effect was endothelial-selective under the conditions tested (Figure 1E vs 1F).
- **Mechanism grade:** reduction of VEGF, ICAM-1, TNF-α, CTGF and albumin was **directly measured** in retina. Wnt-pathway involvement is **supported by measured non-phosphorylated β-catenin** plus **cited** prior PEDF/Wnt work; receptor occupancy of PEDF was not measured.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- OIR is an ischemia/neovascularization model, not chronic human DR (Discussion).
- No dose–response in vivo in the text; figure vs methods dose mismatch.
- No EE%, zeta potential, or release kinetics for the NP batch used in animals.
- STZ duration before injection is only 2 weeks; leakage assayed 4 weeks later (6 weeks of diabetes).
- Small n for Western/qPCR (n=3).
- No head-to-head anti-VEGF drug.

## Relevance to our research question
Direct IVT nanocarrier evidence that a sustained PEDF peptide depot can cut diabetic and OIR retinal leakage and inflammatory proteins for 4 weeks in rats, addressing duration of a non-anti-VEGF peptide. It does not test topical delivery or a marketed anti-VEGF/corticosteroid.
