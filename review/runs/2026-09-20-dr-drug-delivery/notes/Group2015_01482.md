# Dexamethasone intravitreal implant in previously treated patients with diabetic macular edema: subgroup analysis of the MEAD study

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Augustin AJ, Kuppermann BD, Lanzetta P, Loewenstein A, Li X-Y, Cui H, Hashad Y, Whitcup SM, for the Ozurdex MEAD Study Group. BMC Ophthalmol. 2015;15:150. doi:10.1186/s12886-015-0148-2
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-gapfill/Group2015_01482.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (gap-fill G1).
- **Gap-fill:** yes — retrieved after synthesis-rationale for the named gap.
- **Named gap:** G1 MEAD RCT numbers (this is a MEAD subgroup of previously treated DME, not the full 3-year Ophthalmology registration paper).

## Research question
In the MEAD phase 3 programme, does dexamethasone intravitreal implant 0.7 mg (DEX 0.7, Ozurdex) improve BCVA and central retinal thickness versus sham in the subgroup of patients whose DME had already been treated with laser, anti-VEGF, triamcinolone, or a combination, and what is the IOP/cataract safety of that marketed dose?

## Methods
Pooled subgroup analysis of two identical 3-year, randomised, multicentre, masked, sham-controlled phase 3 trials (NCT00168337, NCT00168389; 131 sites, 22 countries; February 2005–June 2012). Eligibility: adults with diabetes and fovea-involved DME; study-eye BCVA 34–68 ETDRS letters (20/200–20/50); CRT ≥300 µm on time-domain OCT (Stratus OCT2 or OCT3); prior DME treatment or laser refused/not expected to benefit. Key exclusions: HbA1c >10%; recent anti-VEGF (3 months) or steroid (6 months); glaucoma/OHT per protocol; prior vitrectomy; active NV. Randomisation 1:1:1 to DEX 0.7 mg, DEX 0.35 mg, or sham (needleless applicator). This analysis uses only marketed DEX 0.7 vs sham, split by prior DME treatment. Retreatment no more often than every 6 months if residual edema (e.g. CRT >225 µm). No concomitant macular-edema therapy; escape therapy required withdrawal. Primary endpoint (US): ≥15-letter BCVA gain at study end (LOCF). Average BCVA/CRT change (AUC) was the European primary. ITT for efficacy; Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel for binary; ANCOVA for AUC; Kaplan–Meier for time to ≥15-letter gain. Safety in treated patients. Prior-laser split was preplanned; prior steroid, anti-VEGF, and combination splits were post hoc.

## Sample / data
MEAD randomised 1048 patients overall. Previously treated subgroup: 70.4% (247/351) of DEX 0.7 and 74.6% (261/350) of sham. DEX 0.7 previously treated n=247 vs sham n=261. Mean age 63.0 years both arms; type 2 diabetes 89.1% vs 91.2%; mean DME duration 27.3 vs 31.9 months (median 20 vs 24 months). Prior laser 93.5% vs 93.1%; prior IVTA 23.5% vs 23.4%; prior anti-VEGF 10.1% vs 10.0%; ≥2 of those 3 types 24.7% vs 21.8%. Phakic 73.7% vs 68.6%. Baseline BCVA 55.2 ± 9.6 vs 56.1 ± 9.1 letters; CRT 478 ± 153 vs 472 ± 131 µm. Three-year completion 67.6% (167/247) DEX vs 43.7% (114/261) sham. Discontinuation for lack of efficacy 5.7% vs 24.5%; for AE 12.1% vs 11.1%. Mean (SD) treatments over 3 years: 4.1 (1.9) DEX vs 3.2 (2.2) sham.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** subgroup analysis of pooled 3-year randomised, sham-controlled phase 3 trials (MEAD); DEX 0.7 vs sham among previously treated DME (preplanned for any prior treatment / prior laser; post hoc for prior IVTA, anti-VEGF, combination)
- **Population / model:** adults with fovea-involved DME and prior laser, anti-VEGF, IVTA, or combination in the study eye (MEAD previously treated subset; 22 countries)
- **n:** previously treated DEX 0.7 n=247; sham n=261 (of 351 and 350 randomised to those arms overall)
- **Intervention / comparator:** biodegradable PLGA dexamethasone implant 0.7 mg (Ozurdex) via pars plana applicator, retreatment ≥6 months, vs sham needleless applicator. DEX 0.35 mg arm not analysed here. No active anti-VEGF comparator.
- **Primary endpoint:** percentage of patients with ≥15-letter ETDRS BCVA gain from baseline at year 3 or final visit (LOCF). Key secondary: average BCVA change (letters, AUC) and average CRT change (µm, AUC) over 3 years.
- **Primary result:** In previously treated patients, ≥15-letter gain at study end was 21.5% with DEX 0.7 vs 11.1% with sham (between-group difference 10.3 percentage points; P=0.002). Mean average BCVA change (AUC): +3.2 ± 8.7 vs +1.5 ± 7.5 letters (difference 1.6; P=0.024). Mean average CRT change (AUC): −126 ± 131 vs −39 ± 121 µm (abstract −126.1 vs −39.0 µm; difference −85; P<0.001). Time to 10th-percentile ≥15-letter response 41 days vs 184 days (log-rank P<0.001). Exploratory prior-therapy splits for ≥15-letter gain with DEX 0.7: prior steroid 27.6% (n=58) vs sham 8.2% (n=61); prior anti-VEGF 28.0% (n=25) vs 7.7% (n=26); prior laser 21.2% (n=231) vs 11.9% (n=243). Combination of ≥2 prior types: 26.2% vs 8.8% (difference 17.5%; 95% CI 4.2–30.7). Cataract-related AE in 70.3% of baseline phakic DEX 0.7 previously treated eyes vs 20.1% sham; IOP-related AE 38.1% vs 4.6%; serious ocular AE 6.9% vs 0.8%. (Abstract; Tables 2–5)
- **Cannot show:** the full MEAD ITT registration result for all randomised patients (that is Boyer et al. Ophthalmology 2014, cited as [21], not this paper); superiority to continued anti-VEGF (no active comparator; prior anti-VEGF n only 25 DEX / 26 sham); that ≥6-month retreatment is the optimal interval (authors state MEAD may underestimate benefit vs 4–5 month retreatment); causal effect in vitrectomised eyes (excluded).

## Key findings

- DEX implant is a biodegradable PLGA matrix releasing dexamethasone up to 6 months as the copolymer degrades to lactic/glycolic acid then CO2 and water (Introduction; PK cited from prior work, not newly measured).
- Vitreous half-life of free dexamethasone is short (<4 h), which is the rationale for the implant (Introduction; cited).
- Mean treatments 4.1 over 3 years in previously treated DEX 0.7 (Abstract; Results).
- ≥15-letter gain, AUC BCVA, and AUC CRT were better with DEX 0.7 than sham in each prior-treatment type examined (Table 3).
- Combination prior therapy (≥2 of laser, IVTA, anti-VEGF): mean AUC BCVA +4.0 vs −0.3 letters (difference 3.8; 95% CI 1.1–6.5); AUC CRT −108 vs −26 µm (difference −111; 95% CI −154 to −69) (Table 4).
- IOP-related AE in the total MEAD DEX 0.7 population was 36.0% vs 5.1% sham; cataract-related AE in phakic eyes 67.9% vs 20.4% (Table 5; total-study numbers for context, not this subgroup’s primary).
- Only 1 patient in each DEX implant dose group in the parent MEAD trials required glaucoma incisional surgery for steroid IOP (Introduction, citing Boyer 2014); this subgroup paper does not report a new incisional-surgery count for previously treated eyes.
- Author interpretation (Discussion): previously treated MEAD patients were a difficult-to-treat population (mean edema ~2.5 years; >90% prior laser); efficacy resembled the overall MEAD population; more frequent dosing than every 6 months might have given more consistent BCVA.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Number of prior laser / anti-VEGF / steroid treatments per patient was not collected (Discussion).
- Study started in 2004; few patients had prior anti-VEGF (n=25 DEX / 26 sham in that split).
- Prior vitrectomy excluded.
- Completion rates relatively low, especially sham (escape-therapy withdrawal design); LOCF for the binary primary can bias results if dropouts are not representative.
- Prior-steroid, prior-anti-VEGF, and combination splits were post hoc/exploratory.
- This paper is not the 3-year Ophthalmology MEAD primary manuscript; full-population 15-letter rates for all randomised patients are cited from Boyer 2014 rather than re-tabulated here.

## Relevance to our research question
Direct randomised evidence that a biodegradable 0.7 mg DEX implant, retreated on a ≥6-month schedule, delivers steroid to the posterior segment and improves BCVA and CRT versus sham in previously treated DME. Duration (~4–6 months per implant; mean 4.1 implants/3 years) and steroid safety (cataract, IOP) are the delivery-system limits. Does not replace the unrecovered full MEAD ITT paper for overall registration percentages.
