# Dexamethasone intravitreal implant (Ozurdex) in diabetic macular edema: real-world data versus clinical trials outcomes

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Neves P, Ornelas M, Matias I, Rodrigues J, Santos M, Dutra-Medeiros M, Martins D. Int J Ophthalmol. 2021;14(10):1571–1580. doi:10.18240/ijo.2021.10.15
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Neves2021_1015.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (real-world DEX implant DME, naïve vs switch)

## Research question
In a real-life 6-month audit, how do BCVA, central macular thickness (CMT), and IOP change after DEX implant in DME eyes that were either anti-VEGF-naïve or switched to DEX independent of prior anti-VEGF response?

## Methods
Retrospective single-center audit, Centro Hospitalar de Setúbal, Portugal. Records of DME treated with 0.7 mg Ozurdex between October 2018 and March 2019. Hospital administrative restraint on anti-VEGF forced a switch to DEX irrespective of prior response. Standard of care otherwise: anti-VEGF first-line treat-and-extend; switch after 3–6 injections if no response (EURETINA). DEX retreatment PRN, minimum visits every 2 months. BCVA, Goldmann IOP, SD-OCT (Heidelberg Spectralis) at baseline, 2-week safety visit, then monthly or every 2 months. Subgroups: naïve vs non-naïve (ranibizumab or aflibercept, no anti-VEGF switch); phakic vs pseudophakic; neurosensorial detachment (NSD) yes/no. Per-protocol analysis; p ≤ 0.05.

## Sample / data
47 patients, 57 eyes; mean age 67.6 ± 8.7 years; 25 male / 22 female; 29 phakic (61.7%), 18 pseudophakic; diabetes duration 16.9 ± 9.0 years; DME duration 10.6 ± 12.6 months. Naïve 34 eyes; non-naïve 19 eyes; 4 eyes excluded from naïve/non-naïve subanalysis because prior anti-VEGF count was uncertain. Baseline BCVA 50.56 ± 16.50 ETDRS letters; IOP 17.28 ± 3.60 mmHg; CMT 501.80 ± 141.82 µm; NSD in 13/57 eyes (22.8%). Naïve DME duration 6.2 ± 10.2 months vs non-naïve 20.2 ± 12.1 months (p = 0.001). Mean DEX implants over 6 months: 1.3. Interval first–second implant 5.52 ± 2.9 months; second–third 4.0 ± 2.5 months.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Retrospective real-world single-center audit (not an RCT).
- **Population / model:** Adults ≥18 years with center-involving DME; naïve or previously treated with ranibizumab or aflibercept.
- **n:** 47 patients (57 eyes); naïve 34 eyes, non-naïve 19 eyes (4 eyes out of naïve/non-naïve split).
- **Intervention / comparator:** Intravitreal DEX 700 µg implant, PRN; naïve first-line DEX vs switch from anti-VEGF (forced, independent of response). No concurrent randomized comparator.
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in BCVA (ETDRS letters), CMT (µm), IOP (mmHg) over 6 months.
- **Primary result:** Overall BCVA difference from baseline: month 2 +8.30 letters (p = 0.001), month 3 +8.64 (p = 0.041), month 4 +5.53 (p = 0.010), month 6 +3.43 (p = 0.147); CMT decreased at week 2 and months 1–6 (all p < 0.001). IOP mean change +4.80 mmHg at month 1 (p = 0.009) and +3.63 mmHg at month 2 (p = 0.004), managed topically. Month 2 naïve vs non-naïve BCVA 60.91 ± 11.53 vs 43.50 ± 21.06 letters (p = 0.006). Last-observation letter-gain rates naïve vs non-naïve: ≥15 letters 38.2% vs 10.5% (p = 0.055); ≥10 letters 41.2% vs 10.5% (p = 0.029); ≥5 letters 67.6% vs 21.1% (p = 0.002). Month 2 overall ≥15 / ≥10 / ≥5 letter gains 15.79% / 17.54% / 31.38%. Direct naïve vs non-naïve CMT differences not significant. After first DEX, CMT change month 1 −200.57 µm (p = 0.018), month 2 −182.94 µm (p < 0.001). No cataract progression and no serious adverse events in 6 months; 29 phakic eyes, none had cataract surgery.
- **Cannot show:** Equivalence to registration-trial (MEAD) 3-year cataract/IOP rates; causal superiority of first-line DEX (forced switch, non-randomized, naïve eyes had shorter DME duration); overall-population mean CMT in µm at each visit (only p < 0.001 stated for the overall CMT series).

## Key findings
- Peak effect 2–3 months after first implant; authors argue label 6-month reinjection should be shortened toward ~4 months (Discussion).
- NSD subgroup: CMT difference at 2 weeks −139.33 ± 127.01 µm (NSD) vs −54.44 ± 45.21 µm (no NSD), p = 0.024 (Results).
- Phakic vs pseudophakic: no relevant difference in measured outcomes (Abstract).
- Authors cite Callanan 2017 as DEX non-inferior to ranibizumab with fewer injections (2.85 vs 8.70) — that comparison is from the cited trial, not this audit (Introduction).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Retrospective, single-center, real-life (Discussion).
- Forced anti-VEGF interruption, not a protocol-defined switch-for-failure cohort.
- 6-month follow-up too short for cataract (authors compare to MEAD 59% cataract surgery / 67.9% cataract AEs over 3 years and RELDEX 47% cataract surgery over 3 years).
- Four eyes dropped from naïve/non-naïve split; some visit-level n are small (e.g. non-naïve month 1 BCVA n = 3).
- Table 3 naïve baseline–month 4 BCVA cells (60.60 then 54.80 letters, p = 0.020) conflict with the paper’s “improvement” wording and with Table 4 month-4 naïve mean 60.15 letters.

## Relevance to our research question
Original real-world duration data for a biodegradable DEX implant in DME: anatomic and visual effect peaks by month 2–3 and wanes by month 6 at mean 1.3 injections, with IOP rise confined to months 1–2. Naïve eyes gained more letters than switch eyes, but DME duration differed at baseline.
