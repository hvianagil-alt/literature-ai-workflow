# Place of intravitreal dexamethasone implant in the treatment armamentarium of diabetic macular edema

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Kartı Ö, Saatci AO. World J Diabetes. 2021;12(8):1220–. doi:10.4239/wjd.v12.i8.1220
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Kartı2021_81220.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (mini-review of DEX implant delivery, trials, and first- vs second-line place in DME)

## Research question
Where does the 0.7 mg biodegradable DEX implant (Ozurdex) sit in the DME treatment sequence versus anti-VEGF agents and other steroids, given its release kinetics, trial outcomes, and IOP/cataract limits?

## Methods
Invited mini-review (World Journal of Diabetes). Authors overview RCTs, real-life series, meta-analyses, and Delphi consensus papers on DEX implant for DME. Table 1 summarises major trials. No new patient-level analysis and no original pooled meta-analysis.

## Sample / data
Secondary literature. Hinge trials they tabulate or narrate: MEAD 1048 patients (DEX 0.7 mg n = 351; 0.35 mg n = 347; sham n = 350), 3 years, mean 4–5 injections; CHAMPLAIN vitrectomized DME (baseline CMT 403 µm, BCVA 54.5 letters); Protocol U 129 eyes of 116 patients (this paper states combination 64 eyes vs ranibizumab-only 64 eyes); ARTES 340 eyes of 287 patients; Khan et al. meta-analysis 3859 patients from 15 studies; Kodjikian et al. 63 observational studies (32 anti-VEGF, 31 DEX).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Narrative mini-review of RCTs, real-world studies, meta-analyses, and national Delphi papers.
- **Population / model:** Eyes with DME, including naïve, previously treated, vitrectomized, phakic, and pseudophakic patients.
- **n:** Not a new cohort. MEAD n = 1048; Protocol U 129 eyes / 116 patients as stated here; ARTES 340 eyes / 287 patients; Khan et al. 3859 patients (15 studies).
- **Intervention / comparator:** DEX implant 0.7 mg (23-gauge biodegradable copolymer; biphasic vitreous release labelled up to 6 months) vs sham, laser, bevacizumab, ranibizumab, FA implant (Iluvien 0.19 mg, up to 36 months), or triamcinolone acetonide.
- **Primary endpoint:** Trial endpoints they extract: ≥15-letter BCVA gain and CMT change (MEAD); CMT and BCVA at 2 and 6 months (CHAMPLAIN); BCVA and CMT at 6 months (Protocol U).
- **Primary result:** MEAD (Table 1 / text): ≥15-letter BCVA 22.2% DEX 0.7 mg vs 18.4% DEX 0.35 mg vs 12.0% sham; mean CMT −111.6 µm / −107.9 µm in DEX arms vs sham (authors state both DEX doses superior with favorable safety); FDA approved 0.7 mg on this basis. CHAMPLAIN (Table 1): from baseline CMT 403 µm, −156 µm at month 2 and −39 µm at month 6; BCVA from 54.5 letters +6.0 at month 2 and +3.0 at month 6; 30.4% gained ≥10 letters at month 2. BEVORDEX (Table 1): CMT −187 µm DEX vs bevacizumab; ≥10-letter BCVA 41% (19/46 DEX eyes). Protocol U as stated here: BCVA +2.7 vs +3 letters at 6 months (no significant difference); CMT −110 µm vs −62 µm; DME resolution 52% vs 31%. Khan et al.: recalcitrant DME after ≥6 anti-VEGF injections, mean follow-up 6 months (range 3–36); mean BCVA improvement four lines or 20 ETDRS letters. Kodjikian et al.: final BCVA 61.2 DEX vs 62 anti-VEGF letters; gain +9.6 vs +4.7 letters. Safety range they compile: IOP increase ≥10 mmHg in 6.8–27.7%; IOP ≥25 mmHg in 6.6–32%; IOP ≥35 mmHg in 0.9–6.6%; IOP-lowering medication in 6.3–41.5%; MEAD glaucoma surgery 0.9%; cataract 4–67.9%.
- **Cannot show:** That combination DEX + anti-VEGF has proven additional benefit (authors say this is still unproven); a new head-to-head n from this paper; that DEX pharmacokinetics were re-measured here (biphasic 6-month label and vitrectomy-independence are cited from earlier work).

## Key findings
- Mechanism as stated: steroids inhibit phospholipase A2/arachidonic acid, reduce VEGF and other cytokines, block leukostasis, improve permeability (Introduction). DEX is described as five times more effective than triamcinolone but with shorter free-drug vitreous half-life, motivating the implant (Introduction).
- Delivery system: biodegradable solid polymer 0.7 mg DEX; diffusion in two phases (high then low concentration); metabolized to lactic/glycolic acid then water and CO2; maximum effect ~2 months then wanes; authors state vitreous surgery does not affect implant pharmacokinetics (Introduction; Figure 2–3).
- EURETINA position they restate: anti-VEGF first-line for center-involved DME; steroids second-line after 3–6 consecutive anti-VEGF injections; first-line steroid if major cardiovascular event, pregnancy, vitrectomized or non-compliant patients (Introduction).
- FA (Iluvien) reserved for non-steroid-responders (no prior steroid IOP rise) with chronic DME unresponsive to other treatments; TA only if approved implants inaccessible (Introduction).
- He et al. meta-analysis as quoted: visual improvement comparable; better anatomy with DEX at 6 months and fewer injections; no CMT difference at 12 months; visual benefit blunted by cataract; first-line DEX only in selected cases (Meta-analyses section).
- Delphi they summarise: Spain MOMENTUM-D switch after three anti-VEGF; Italy PRN, do not wait 6 months, prefer DEX over TA/FA in pseudophakic/vitrectomized eyes; Germany switch after 3–6 monthly anti-VEGF, no same-day bilateral implant (Expert recommendations).
- Implant migration into the anterior chamber listed for aphakic, vitrectomized, sutured IOL, weak zonules, or posterior-capsule defect (Safety).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Mini-review, invited; not systematic with a reported search yield.
- Protocol U arm sizes here (64 vs 64) do not match Taloni’s 65 vs 64 recount of the same trial.
- Combination-therapy studies they cite are small (Meta-analyses / combination paragraph).
- IOP and cataract rates are ranges across heterogeneous trials, not a pooled estimate computed by Kartı.

## Relevance to our research question
Directly addresses how a biodegradable DEX implant extends corticosteroid residence in the vitreous (biphasic label 6 months, peak ~2 months), why that matters after vitrectomy versus solution anti-VEGF, and what still limits first-line translation (cataract, IOP, EURETINA second-line default).
