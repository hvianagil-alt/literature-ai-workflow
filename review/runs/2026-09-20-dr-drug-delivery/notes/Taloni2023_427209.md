# Safety and Efficacy of Dexamethasone Intravitreal Implant Given Either First-Line or Second-Line in Diabetic Macular Edema

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Taloni A, Coco G, Rastelli D, Buffon G, Scorcia V, Giannaccare G. Patient Preference and Adherence. 2023;17:3307–3329. doi:10.2147/PPA.S427209
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Taloni2023_427209.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (DEX implant first- vs second-line systematic-style review; hinge for clinical implant evidence)

## Research question
When is the 0.7 mg dexamethasone (DEX) intravitreal implant (Ozurdex) effective and safe as first-line versus second-line therapy for diabetic macular edema (DME), relative to anti-VEGF agents, laser, combination regimens, and fluocinolone acetonide (FA) implants?

## Methods
PRISMA-guided literature search of PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar (keywords: DME/DMO, DEX/Ozurdex, switching, cataract surgery, vitrectomized eyes). Included RCTs, large real-life studies, meta-analyses, and systematic reviews of intravitreal DEX implant; prospective/retrospective studies when no RCT existed (e.g. vitrectomized vs non-vitrectomized eyes). English only. Authors reached consensus on collected data. Trial results tabulated (Table 1); risk of bias summarised (Figure 2). This paper itself does **not** compute a new pooled effect size; it reports published trial and meta-analysis numbers.

## Sample / data
Secondary synthesis. Hinge source trials they quote: MEAD n = 1048 (DEX 0.7 mg 351 / 0.35 mg 347 / sham 350), 3 years; Callanan 2017 n = 363 eyes DEX vs ranibizumab, 12 months; BEVORDEX n = 88 eyes, 24 months; Protocol U n = 129 eyes (DEX + ranibizumab 65 vs sham + ranibizumab 64), 24 weeks. Cited meta-analyses: Chi et al 2409 eyes; Patil et al 14 RCTs, 827 eyes; Yuan et al 7 retrospective studies, 582 eyes (374 non-vitrectomized, 208 vitrectomized). PRISMA flowchart is Figure 1; numeric identification counts are not given as text in the extracted file.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** PRISMA-style systematic narrative review of RCTs, real-world studies, and published meta-analyses (not a new pooled meta-analysis by Taloni et al.).
- **Population / model:** Adults with center-involving DME (naïve, pretreated, anti-VEGF-resistant, vitrectomized, phakic/pseudophakic, cataract-surgery candidates).
- **n:** This review’s own pooled n is not reported. MEAD: 1048 patients. Callanan 2017: 363 eyes. Chi et al: 2409 eyes. Patil et al: 827 eyes. Yuan et al vitrectomy comparison: 582 eyes.
- **Intervention / comparator:** DEX implant 0.7 mg (and 0.35 mg in MEAD) vs sham, laser, bevacizumab, ranibizumab, aflibercept, DEX + anti-VEGF combination, or FA implant.
- **Primary endpoint:** BCVA (ETDRS letters or ≥10-/≥15-letter gain); CRT/CMT/CST (µm); IOP and cataract adverse events.
- **Primary result:** MEAD (Table 1): ≥15-letter BCVA gain 22.2% DEX 0.7 mg vs 18.4% DEX 0.35 mg vs 12.0% sham (p ≤ 0.018 vs DEX); mean CRT change −111.6 µm / −107.9 µm / −41.9 µm (p < 0.001 vs DEX). Callanan 2017: DEX met 5-letter noninferiority vs ranibizumab but mean BCVA gain was 4.3 vs 7.6 letters (p < 0.05). BEVORDEX 12-month CRT −187 µm DEX vs −122 µm bevacizumab (p = 0.015); 2-year BCVA gain not different in pseudophakic eyes. Protocol U 24 weeks: BCVA +2.7 ± 9.8 vs +3.0 ± 7.1 letters (p = 0.73); anatomy −110 µm vs −62 µm (p < 0.001); DME resolution 52% vs 31%. Chi et al: BCVA mean difference 0.00 in non-resistant eyes; in resistant DME DEX associated with greater BCVA improvement (mean difference 0.12 [95% CI 0.02–0.21]) and CRT decrease 65.37 µm [95% CI 3.62–127.13], high heterogeneity. Patil et al: BCVA gain not significant at 3, 6, 12 months (p = 0.11, 0.21, 0.24) or final follow-up (p = 0.91); retinal thickness lower with DEX at 3 months (p = 0.04), 6 months (p < 0.00001), and final follow-up (weighted mean difference 39.99 µm, p = 0.002) but not at 12 months (p = 0.18). MEAD IOP-related AEs 36.0% / 34.1% / 5.1% (DEX 0.7 / 0.35 / sham); IOP ≥ 35 mmHg 6.6% vs 0.9% sham; glaucoma surgery 0.6% in 0.7 mg; cataract-related AEs in phakic eyes 67.9% / 64.1% / 20.4%. He et al: cataract RR 2.68 (p = 0.0005); IOP-lowering therapy RR 4.14 (95% CI 1.89–8.65; p = 0.0002). Patil: no difference in cataract-related AEs. Chi: IOP change MD = −0.42 (not significant). Yuan et al: no significant BCVA/CMT/injection/AE difference by vitrectomy status; DEX duration 0.8 months shorter in vitrectomized eyes (p = 0.005).
- **Cannot show:** A new pooled estimate computed by Taloni; head-to-head ranking of brolucizumab/faricimab vs DEX (authors state those comparisons still needed); that combination DEX + anti-VEGF is approved by health authorities.

## Key findings
- Authors’ first-line DEX candidates (Abstract/Conclusion): contraindications to anti-VEGF; inflammatory OCT biomarkers (large cysts ≥250 µm, DRIL, hyperreflective foci, subretinal fluid); pseudophakia; phakic eyes planned for cataract surgery; vitrectomized eyes; switch after 3–6 anti-VEGF injections if non-response; combination for severe persistent DME (Abstract; Conclusion).
- Kodjikian observational synthesis they quote: final BCVA 62 letters anti-VEGF vs 61.2 DEX; gain from baseline +4.7 vs +9.6 letters; naïve status conferred best DEX functional response (DEX vs anti-VEGF section).
- INVICTUS real-life naïve DME: BCVA DEX +9.4, ranibizumab +7.2, aflibercept +8.5 letters; CRT −162.7 / −99.3 / −101.5 µm (DEX vs anti-VEGF section).
- Kaya simultaneous DEX + ranibizumab vs ranibizumab (68 eyes, 34 each): +21.6 vs +9.6 letters at 12 months (p < 0.001); ≥15-letter gain 65.4% vs 26.2% (p < 0.001); foveal thickness −413 vs −282 µm (p = 0.001); IOP rise ≥5 mmHg 38% vs 18% (p = 0.012); cataract progression 27% vs 12.5% (p = 0.032).
- FA implant (FAME): low-dose 0.2 µg/day +4.4 letters and −168 µm at 2 years vs sham (p = 0.02 and p = 0.005); PALADIN 202 eyes, +3.61 letters (p = 0.02) and CST −60.69 µm (p < 0.0001) at 36 months (DEX vs FA section).
- Fallico cataract-surgery meta-analysis they quote: 3-month CMT change −98.35 µm with DEX + cataract surgery vs −21.61 µm with anti-VEGF + cataract surgery (p < 0.001).
- Exclusion they state for DEX: torn/ruptured posterior capsule (anterior-chamber migration) and glaucoma/ocular hypertension (Conclusion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Not a new quantitative meta-analysis; heterogeneity of cited resistant-DME meta-analysis is high (Chi et al. as quoted).
- Cataract rates vs anti-VEGF remain conflicting across meta-analyses (He/OR 4.70 vs Patil no difference), attributed to follow-up, protocol, and baseline pseudophakia mix.
- Combination DEX + anti-VEGF studies they cite are small; simultaneous therapy not approved by health authorities (Economic considerations).
- PRISMA identification/exclusion counts are in Figure 1 only; not extractable as numbers from this text file.

## Relevance to our research question
Hinge clinical paper for how a biodegradable DEX implant delivers corticosteroid to the retina in DME, how duration (label 6 months vs real-world 3–5 months / 0.8-month shorter action after vitrectomy) and targeting (inflammatory OCT biomarkers, vitrectomized eyes) are limited, and why IOP/cataract still constrain first-line translation versus anti-VEGF.
