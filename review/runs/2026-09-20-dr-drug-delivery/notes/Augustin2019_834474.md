# Three-year results from the Retro-IDEAL study: real-world data from diabetic macular edema (DME) patients treated with ILUVIEN (0.19 mg fluocinolone acetonide implant)

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Augustin AJ, Bopp S, Fechner M, Holz F, Sandner D, Winkgen A-M, Khoramnia R, Neuhann T, Warscher M, Spitzer M, Sekundo W, Seitz B, Duncker T, Ksinsik C, Höh H, Gentsch D. Eur J Ophthalmol. 2020;30(2):382–391 (published 2019). doi:10.1177/1120672119834474
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-gapfill/Augustin2019_834474.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (gap-fill G2).
- **Gap-fill:** yes — retrieved after synthesis-rationale for the named gap.
- **Named gap:** G2 three-year FAc outcomes (Singer 2022 not retrieved); Retro-IDEAL is a German real-world 3-year FAc series, not the US PALADIN/Singer trial.

## Research question
In German routine practice, what are 3-year visual acuity, central macular thickness, supplemental-treatment, and IOP outcomes after a single 0.19 mg fluocinolone acetonide (FAc, ILUVIEN) implant in eyes with persistent or recurrent DME that had a suboptimal response to first-line (mainly anti-VEGF) therapy?

## Methods
Retrospective multicentre chart extraction (Retro-IDEAL) at 16 German sites. Data extracted September 2016–September 2017 by ANFOMED GmbH after centre and patient consent. Adults (≥18 years) who received the 0.2 µg/day FAc implant (ILUVIEN 0.19 mg) for DME; some off-licence indications were also logged then excluded from the DME analysis. A 12-month pre-implant window standardised prior DME therapy. Switch reasons: insufficient efficacy, persistent edema, no VA response, and/or ≥1 recurrence on first-line therapy. Baseline VA, CMT, IOP = last non-missing value on or before implant day. Follow-up binned every 90 days to month 36; if multiple values in a bin, the mean was used; missing bins excluded (no imputation). Analyses required both baseline and follow-up. Wilcoxon signed-rank vs baseline; P<0.05. Snellen converted to ETDRS letters (best-recorded VA). CMT from mixed OCT platforms (Topcon 3D OCT-2000, Heidelberg Spectralis, Zeiss Cirrus, Optovue RTVue 100). IOP events: change >10 mmHg from baseline and IOP above 30 mmHg.

## Sample / data
Extracted: 94 eyes / 76 patients (mean age 66.9 ± 12.2 years; 74% pseudophakic; mean follow-up 30.8 ± 11.3 months). Diagnoses: DME n=83, CME n=6, RVO n=1, uveitis n=5. Analysed DME cohort: 81 eyes from 63 patients meeting inclusion (mean age 68.0 ± 10.4 years, range 36–89). Type 2 diabetes 70.4%; type 1 27.2%. Mean diabetes duration 20.2 ± 14.07 years (n=58); mean DME duration 3.8 ± 2.9 years (n=79). Pseudophakic 75.3%; prior vitrectomy 48.2%. Prior 12-month treatment: laser 92.5%; any IVT 97.5% (ranibizumab 91.1%, mean 4.3 injections; bevacizumab 44.3%, mean 2.8; aflibercept 6.3%, mean 5.0; triamcinolone 41.8%, mean 1.1; DEX 24.1%, mean 1.5). Combination laser+anti-VEGF+steroid 39.2%; laser+anti-VEGF 43.2%. IOP treatment at baseline 22.2%. DR among n=69: mild NPDR 31.8%, moderate NPDR 34.8%, severe NPDR 17.4%, PDR 15.9%. Reason for FAc: insufficient prior efficacy 100%; persistent edema 74.7%; no VA response 32.1%; recurrences 30.9%. Mean DME follow-up 30.8 ± 11.0 months; 88.9% followed ≥30 months.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** retrospective multicentre observational (Retro-IDEAL real-world FAc)
- **Population / model:** persistent or recurrent DME in German clinical practice after suboptimal first-line (primarily anti-VEGF) therapy; ILUVIEN 0.19 mg / 0.2 µg/day FAc implant
- **n:** 81 DME eyes (63 patients); mean follow-up 30.8 ± 11.0 months (abstract also reports 30.8 ± 11.3 months for the extracted set)
- **Intervention / comparator:** single (usually) 0.19 mg FAc implant releasing 0.2 µg/day for up to 3 years. No untreated or randomised anti-VEGF control. Supplemental anti-VEGF, DEX, additional FAc, or laser allowed after implant. Pre/post IOP-medication comparison.
- **Primary endpoint:** not a single pre-registered primary; effectiveness reported as best-recorded visual acuity (ETDRS letters) and CMT (µm) vs baseline; safety as IOP (mmHg), IOP-lowering treatment, cataract, and other AE.
- **Primary result:** Baseline BRVA 49.0 letters. Peak mean change +5.5 letters at month 9 (P≤0.005, n=56); maintained through month 30 (P≤0.05, n=42). Discussion restates year-3 mean VA 52.4 letters, peak 55.6 letters at month 9, ~54.0 letters through month 30. CMT 502 µm at baseline to 338 µm at year 1 (P≤0.0001, n=43), 355 µm at year 2, 318 µm at year 3 (P≤0.0001, n=29). Mean CMT change −131 µm at 12 months (P≤0.0001), −111 µm at 24 months (P≤0.005, n=30), −158 µm at 36 months (P≤0.0001). Mean IOP 15.8 mmHg baseline, 18.2 at 12 months, 15.7 at 24 months, 15.6 at 36 months; peak mean IOP change +1.9 mmHg at year 1 (abstract); Results also give +1.9 at 12 months, −0.1 at 24 months, −0.7 at 36 months. IOP ≥30 mmHg in 12.3%; IOP >30 mmHg in 7.4%; IOP increase ≥10 mmHg in 22.2%. IOP-lowering treatment 22.2% before vs 27.2% after FAc; IOP surgery 3.7% (3 patients). Supplemental anti-VEGF or corticosteroid in 25/81 eyes (30.9%). (Abstract; Results; Table 2)
- **Cannot show:** randomised 3-year FAc vs anti-VEGF or vs sham (not FAME and not Singer 2022 PALADIN); that every eye completed 36 months (n falls to 29 for year-3 CMT and 42 for month-30 VA); device PK (release rate is labelled/cited, not measured here); that early switch after anti-VEGF failure is proven superior (all eyes were already insufficient responders).

## Key findings

- Implant described as releasing a microdose of FAc (0.2 µg/day) continuously for up to 3 years (Introduction; labelled PK, not newly assayed).
- Eyes without supplemental therapy (n=57) gained +5.8 letters at 12 months, peak +9.0 at month 18, +5.4 at month 36; the supplemental group (25 eyes) gained +5 letters at month 12 then had overall BRVA loss by month 36 (Results).
- Supplemental therapy: 16/25 anti-VEGF only, 5/25 steroid only, 4/25 both. Of 20 eyes (25%) receiving anti-VEGF after FAc, mean 4.0 injections (range 1–12); aflibercept 49 injections in 13 eyes, bevacizumab 18 in 8, ranibizumab 13 in 7. DEX implant in 6 eyes (7.4%). Additional FAc within 900 days in 3 eyes (4%); retreatment after 900 days in 4 (5%). Laser after FAc in 14/81 (17.3%), mean 1.3 treatments.
- CMT with vs without supplemental therapy: month 3 −132.0 vs −151.7 µm; month 36 −134.2 vs −173.3 µm (Results).
- Pseudophakic VA: baseline 48.5 letters; 54.0 at month 6; 53.0 at 12 months; 53.6 at 21 months; 50.7 at month 36.
- Cataract: 21.3% (17/80, 1 missing) of phakic eyes reported cataract; 10/17 already had cataract at baseline. Incident cataract (n=7) at mean 254 days; 5 removed. Baseline-cataract eyes had surgery at mean 403 days.
- One DME eye: FAc removed by PPV at day 396 after IOP 38 mmHg despite drops (VA and CMT had improved). No endophthalmitis.
- Four open-angle glaucoma eyes at baseline (contraindicated in the EU SmPC); IOP-lowering surgery in three of those four after VA or CMT worsened, not attributed by authors to IOP increases from the implant.
- Author interpretation (Discussion): first 3-year German real-world FAc report after primarily anti-VEGF (unlike FAME, which followed laser); IOP profile similar to FAME; FAc often used late after many prior injections rather than at first anti-VEGF failure.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Retrospective, no imputation, shrinking n at later visits (month-9 VA n=56; month-30 VA n=42; year-3 CMT n=29).
- Mixed OCT devices; Snellen-to-ETDRS conversion; best-recorded rather than protocol BCVA.
- Off-label glaucoma/OHT eyes included; 48% prior vitrectomy — unlike FAME exclusions.
- No randomised comparator; supplemental therapy confounds “implant-alone” durability.
- Early German ILUVIEN users: long DME duration and heavy pre-treatment, so results may not generalise to earlier switch.
- Not Singer 2022 (US prospective 0.19 mg FAc 3-year study); that paper remains not retrieved.

## Relevance to our research question
Real-world evidence that a non-biodegradable 0.19 mg FAc implant can keep CMT down and add ~5 letters for up to 3 years in previously treated DME, with about one-third still needing supplemental injections and IOP/cataract as the safety ceiling. Fills the 3-year FAc outcomes gap left by the missing Singer 2022 full text, but it is observational German practice, not PALADIN.

## Open questions / things to verify

- “Elevated IOP in 22.2% prior vs 27.2% following” in the Abstract is IOP-lowering treatment percentages (Table 2), not the 12.3% with IOP ≥30 mmHg.
- Year-3 mean VA 52.4 letters is in Discussion (from Figure 1), not a table cell; month-9 +5.5 letters (n=56) is the inferential peak.
- Do not pool Retro-IDEAL n=81 with FAME randomised n.
