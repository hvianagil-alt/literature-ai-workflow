# Intravitreal DEX Implant for the Treatment of Diabetic Macular Edema: A Review of National Consensus

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Spinetta R, Petrillo F, Reibaldi M, Tortori A, Mazzoni M, Metrangolo C, Gelormini F, Ricardi F, Giordano A. Pharmaceutics. 2023;15(10):2461. doi:10.3390/pharmaceutics15102461
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Spinetta2023_102461.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (national consensus comparison for DEX implant in DME)

## Research question
How do English-language national retina-specialist consensuses from Europe and Asia recommend using the intravitreal DEX implant in DME (first-line vs switch, retreatment interval, non-response definitions, safety), and where do they agree or diverge?

## Methods
Narrative comparison of national consensus documents written in English that fully address DEX implant for DME. Documents used Delphi voting, in-person expert meetings, or literature review. EURETINA 2017 is background, not one of the seven. Consensuses compared in Tables 1–3: Spanish 2019; French 2020; Asian 2019; Emirates Society of Ophthalmology 2019; Italian 2017; Saudi Arabia 2020; DEX-CAT Italian 2018.

## Sample / data
Seven national consensuses (no new treated eyes). Panel sizes stated in the source documents as quoted: Spain, experts from 11 hospitals, six meetings February–October 2019; Asia, 12 retinal specialists, consensus if 9/12 (75%) agreed; France, 39 retinologists, Steering Committee of six, April–September 2020, 9-point Likert (strong consensus if >75% scores ≥7 and median ≥8); Italy, Steering Committee of four plus 40 retinal specialists, consensus if ≥75% “agree/very much agree” on 30 statements; Saudi Arabia, eight experts from seven institutions; Emirates, two in-person meetings in Dubai June and December 2019. DEX-CAT Italian consensus year 2018 (Table 1).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Narrative review of seven national DME/DEX consensus documents (Delphi or expert-panel methods); not an RCT or meta-analysis.
- **Population / model:** Clinical recommendations for patients with DME (center-involving and selected non-center cases as defined by each panel).
- **n:** Not applicable (no new cohort). Expert-panel n as above.
- **Intervention / comparator:** DEX implant as first-line, switch from anti-VEGF, or combined with laser/cataract surgery, versus anti-VEGF-first algorithms.
- **Primary endpoint:** Not a trial. Compared recommendation domains: first-line DEX indications, when to treat DME, when to switch, retreatment interval, definitions of anti-VEGF and DEX non-response, combination with laser, IOP/cataract handling.
- **Primary result:** Across panels, DEX is endorsed as a first-line option in vitrectomized eyes, recent cardiovascular/arterial thromboembolic events, poor anti-VEGF compliance, planned cataract surgery or pseudophakia, pregnancy (selected trimesters in Emirates/Saudi texts), and DME with inflammatory OCT signs (SRD/SRF, large empty cysts, hyperreflective dots/foci, hard exudates, high macular volume, CMT > 500 µm in the Asian panel). Switch: Italy — persistent edema after anti-VEGF loading; Saudi — non-response after loading, switch to another anti-VEGF or steroid. Retreatment: France 3–5 months, do not wait for VA drop; Asia 4–6 months if VA ≥6/12 and CMT < 300 µm or CMT improvement >10%, else switch to anti-VEGF; Italy PRN, a fixed 6-month wait is not appropriate. France defines functional non-response as <5 ETDRS letters and anatomic non-response as CRT reduction <20% and/or no significant cyst improvement; DEX failure after two implants (good consensus). Emirates treat if BCVA ≤20/30 and/or CRT ≥300 µm, or symptomatic VA >20/25 with center-involving OCT features; they estimate 3–4 DEX implants in year 1 and ~10% IOP >25 mmHg. Saudi: 20–30% of patients classified as anti-VEGF non-responders; TA not used (quoted 44% glaucoma, 54% cataract surgery). France: 63% of experts judged cataract risk after two DEX injections on a clear lens as low (no good consensus).
- **Cannot show:** Efficacy, BCVA, or OCT effect sizes from a treated sample; that Delphi agreement equals RCT evidence; a single global protocol (authors note methods differ and several domains had no consensus).

## Key findings
- Spanish panel: prefer DEX over anti-VEGF when SRD, large empty cysts, high total volume, or HRDs are present; observe if VA 20/25 or better (OBTAIN-informed) (Spanish section; Table 2).
- Asian panel: first-line DEX also if CMT > 500 µm; routine IOP check at 6 weeks if no comorbidity, earlier if glaucoma (week 1, 2, and 4–6); stable glaucoma on monotherapy may receive DEX after risk–benefit discussion (Asian section).
- French panel: strong consensus that inflammation is present in all phases of DME and that Ozurdex is anti-inflammatory, anti-angiogenic, and BRB-stabilizing; **no** consensus that OCT inflammatory biomarkers should guide treatment choice (French section).
- Italian panel: DEX first-choice in vitrectomized eyes; alternative first-line in pseudophakia; preferred after arterial thromboembolic event in the past 4 months; did **not** agree that edema after only three ranibizumab injections defines non-response (Italian section).
- Emirates: anti-VEGF non-response estimated 25–40%; pregnancy first-line is glucose control and laser, DEX only second/third trimester in severe DME after discussion; DEX ~2 weeks before or at cataract extraction to reduce postoperative edema (Emirates section; Table 2).
- Authors’ synthesis (Abstract): DEX can be studied as first-line in inflammatory-OCT DME, vitrectomized eyes, recent cardiovascular events, pregnant women, cataract-surgery candidates, or poor compliance.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Only English national consensuses that fully address DEX; other countries omitted.
- Methods are not uniform (Delphi vs meetings vs literature).
- No new clinical measurements; quoted percentages (IOP ~10%, anti-VEGF non-response 25–40% or 20–30%) come from the source panels/literature, not from Spinetta’s own cohort.
- Several cells in Table 2 are “Not considered” (e.g. Spanish/French/Asian/Emirates switch criteria).

## Relevance to our research question
Maps how delivery choice (DEX implant vs repeated anti-VEGF) is constrained in translation: duration (3–6 month retreatment, not a 6-month fixed label), targeting (inflammatory OCT biomarkers, vitrectomized eyes), and safety (IOP, cataract, posterior-capsule integrity) as agreed—or not agreed—by national expert groups.
