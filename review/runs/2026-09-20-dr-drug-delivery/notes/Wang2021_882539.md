# Comparison of Intravitreal Dexamethasone Implant and Ranibizumab in Vitrectomized Eyes with Diabetic Macular Edema

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Wang J-K, Huang T-L, Chang P-Y, Ho W-T, Hsu Y-R, Chen F-T, Chen Y-J. J Ophthalmol. 2021;2021:8882539. doi:10.1155/2021/8882539
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Wang2021_882539.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (original DEX vs ranibizumab in pseudophakic vitrectomized DME)

## Research question
In pseudophakic, previously vitrectomized eyes with treatment-naïve center-involved DME, does a DEX implant (every 3–4 months) outperform OCT-guided treat-and-extend ranibizumab over 6 months on BCVA and central foveal thickness (CFT), and at what IOP and injection-count cost?

## Methods
Retrospective, single-center (Far Eastern Memorial Hospital, Taiwan). IRB FEMH-107160-E; NCT04089605. One eye per patient, June 2017–November 2018, 6-month monthly follow-up. Shared decision-making for DEX vs ranibizumab, then National Health Insurance reimbursement (max 8 ranibizumab 0.5 mg/0.05 mL or 5 DEX 0.7 mg in 5 years; no drug switch). Ranibizumab: 3 monthly loading then treat-and-extend modified from TREX-DME (extend 1 month if CFT <300 µm without fluid/cysts; shorten 1 month if CFT >300 µm or fluid/cysts; inject at least every 3 months). DEX: baseline then every 3–4 months (minimum 3-month interval if CFT >300 µm or fluid/cysts; mandatory by 4 months even if dry). No deferred macular laser. BCVA Snellen converted to logMAR and ETDRS letters; CFT 1 mm subfield CIRRUS HD-OCT 5000; IOP pneumotonometer. Topical hypotensives if IOP >20 mmHg. Wilcoxon signed-rank within group; Wilcoxon rank-sum and Fisher exact between groups; p < 0.05.

## Sample / data
48 pseudophakic vitrectomized eyes / 48 patients (DEX n = 22; ranibizumab n = 26). Vitrectomy indications: vitreous hemorrhage 28, tractional RD 7, active fibrovascular proliferation 5, taut posterior hyaloid 2, macular pucker 6. ILM not peeled. Persistent DME >3 months after last surgery; all had prior PRP; no silicone oil or gas. 32 eyes were phakic at vitrectomy then had cataract surgery; 16 were already pseudophakic at vitrectomy. Inclusion: age >18, HbA1c <10%, BCVA 20/400–20/40, CFT >300 µm, macular leakage on FFA; no macular traction. Baseline (Table 1), DEX vs ranibizumab: age both 59.9 years; HbA1c 8.1 ± 1.9 vs 7.9 ± 2.1%; CFT 462.9 ± 136.7 vs 444.4 ± 118.5 µm (p = 0.63); BCVA 0.68 ± 0.35 vs 0.77 ± 0.41 logMAR (p = 0.41); IOP 16.2 ± 4.8 vs 17.9 ± 5.1 mmHg (p = 0.42).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Retrospective two-arm comparative cohort in vitrectomized pseudophakic eyes; treatment chosen by shared decision-making (not randomized).
- **Population / model:** Adults with PDR after vitrectomy (no oil/gas), pseudophakic, treatment-naïve center-involved DME, HbA1c <10%.
- **n:** 48 eyes / 48 patients (DEX implant 22; ranibizumab 26).
- **Intervention / comparator:** Intravitreal DEX 0.7 mg implant every 3–4 months vs ranibizumab 0.5 mg 3 monthly + treat-and-extend; no switch, no macular laser; 6 months.
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in CFT (µm, 1 mm SD-OCT subfield) and BCVA (logMAR / ETDRS letters) at month 6; injection number and post-injection IOP.
- **Primary result:** At month 6 (Table 2), DEX vs ranibizumab: final BCVA 0.38 ± 0.38 vs 0.62 ± 0.41 logMAR (p = 0.04); BCVA change −0.30 ± 0.36 vs −0.15 ± 0.41 logMAR (p = 0.02); ETDRS letter change +17.23 ± 15.32 vs +6.62 ± 14.02 (p = 0.03); final BCVA ≥20/40 in 14/22 (63.6%) vs 6/26 (23.1%) (p = 0.008); ≥3-line gain 13/22 (59.1%) vs 7/26 (26.9%) (p = 0.03); ≥1-line loss 3/22 (13.6%) vs 6/26 (23.1%) (p = 0.02). Final CFT 310.9 ± 128.8 vs 384.2 ± 108.6 µm (p = 0.04); CFT change −150.0 ± 131.1 vs −60.1 ± 110.2 µm (p = 0.03). Injections 2.6 ± 0.5 vs 5.6 ± 0.9 (p < 0.001). IOP requiring topical therapy 6/22 (27.3%) vs 0/26 (p = 0.0002); mean peak IOP in those 6 eyes 26.3 ± 4.7 mmHg at 1–3 months (4 eyes 21–30 mmHg on brimonidine; 2 eyes 31–34 mmHg on brimonidine/timolol). Final IOP 19.7 ± 4.2 vs 18.9 ± 3.2 mmHg (p = 0.37). No glaucoma surgery, endophthalmitis, implant anterior-chamber migration, RD, or thromboembolic events. Both arms improved BCVA from month 2 through month 6 vs baseline (p < 0.05) but not at month 1.
- **Cannot show:** Randomized efficacy; non-vitrectomized or phakic-eye results; outcomes beyond 6 months; a measured intraocular cytokine or PK curve in these 48 eyes (Discussion cites prior animal/clinical PK as explanation).

## Key findings
- Authors’ PK interpretation (cited, not measured here): ranibizumab solution is diluted and cleared faster without vitreous gel (macaque aqueous half-life 2.3 vs 1.4 days non-vitrectomized vs vitrectomized); DEX NOVADUR implant PK was similar for 31 days in vitrectomized vs non-vitrectomized rabbits (Discussion).
- They contrast MAGGIORE (non-vitrectomized): DEX 4.3 vs ranibizumab 7.6 letters (pseudophakic 4.6 vs 6.6); here vitrectomized DEX 17.2 vs ranibizumab 6.6 letters (Discussion).
- DEX injections: 10 eyes × 2 implants, 12 eyes × 3. Ranibizumab: 4× in 3 eyes, 5× in 10, 6× in 8, 7× in 5 (Results).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Small sample, single center, 6-month follow-up (Discussion).
- Non-randomized allocation (patient–doctor shared decision + reimbursement cap).
- One sentence in the ranibizumab Results paragraph says CFT decreased “following IDI”; Table 2 attributes those CFT values to ranibizumab — use Table 2.
- BCVA tested as Snellen then converted.

## Relevance to our research question
Original evidence that implant (not solution) delivery matters after vitrectomy: fewer DEX injections produced larger 6-month BCVA and CFT gains than treat-and-extend ranibizumab in pseudophakic vitrectomized DME, at the cost of 27.3% medically treated IOP rise. Directly addresses duration, targeting after vitreous removal, and translation limits (IOP, short follow-up, no randomization).
