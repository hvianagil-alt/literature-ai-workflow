# Ocular Drug Delivery to the Retina: Current Innovations and Future Perspectives

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Kim HM, Woo SJ. Pharmaceutics. 2021;13(1):108. doi:10.3390/pharmaceutics13010108
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Kim2021_010108.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (narrative review of retinal delivery; DR and DME named among indications)

## Research question
How can current and experimental ocular delivery systems extend the intraocular duration of anti-VEGF biologics and other retinal drugs, and which non-intravitreal routes can reach the retina with less invasiveness?

## Methods
Narrative review (paper type: Review). The authors summarise intraocular pharmacokinetics of marketed and late-stage anti-VEGF agents, then survey dose escalation, higher molecular weight, biodegradable and non-biodegradable implants, micro- and nanoparticles, hydrogels, combined particle–hydrogel systems, the port delivery system, and periocular/topical/suprachoroidal routes (Abstract; Sections 1–5). No systematic search protocol, inclusion counts, or PRISMA flow is reported.

## Sample / data
Secondary literature of animal PK studies, human aqueous PK, and named clinical programmes (HARBOR, SAVE, READ-3, LADDER, ADAGIO/ALTISSIMO, DREAM, and listed NCT trials). The review does not state how many papers were screened or included.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** narrative review of retinal drug delivery and intraocular PK (not a systematic review).
- **Population / model:** mixed — rabbit, monkey, and human PK of anti-VEGF agents; clinical programmes in neovascular AMD, DME, DR, RVO, and geographic atrophy as quoted from cited trials.
- **n:** not reported (review). Number of included studies is not stated.
- **Intervention / comparator:** current monthly/bimonthly intravitreal anti-VEGF injections versus dose escalation, polymer-conjugated large antibodies, biodegradable vs non-biodegradable implants, nanoparticles/hydrogels/combined systems, refillable port delivery, and topical/subconjunctival/suprachoroidal/subretinal/trans-scleral routes.
- **Primary endpoint:** not applicable as original data; the review’s organising endpoints are vitreous/aqueous half-life (days), injection-interval length, and whether a technique has reached clinical trials.
- **Primary result:** Anti-VEGF biologics work but have relatively short intraocular half-lives, so monthly or bimonthly injections are required; the authors’ synthesis is that extended delivery (implants, particles, hydrogels, port systems) and less-invasive routes are under preclinical and clinical investigation, yet “despite the performance of numerous in vitro studies, only a few techniques have translated to clinical trials” (Abstract).
- **Cannot show:** a pooled half-life or a head-to-head ranking of delivery platforms in DR/DME; original clinical efficacy; which experimental nanoparticle will succeed in humans.
- **PK numbers the review quotes (animal, cited):** Dutch-belted rabbits — vitreous t½ of 0.5 mg ranibizumab 2.88 days and 1.25 mg bevacizumab 4.32 days (Bakri et al.); New Zealand white rabbits — ranibizumab 2.18 days, bevacizumab 7.56 days, aflibercept 3.92 days (Park et al.); ranibizumab vitreous t½ 2.51 days vitrectomized vs 2.75 days non-vitrectomized rabbits (Ahn et al.); monkey aqueous t½ ranibizumab 1.4 vs 2.3 days and aflibercept 1.5 vs 2.2 days (vitrectomized vs not; Niwa et al.); brolucizumab vitreous t½ 2.94 days (rabbit) and 2.08 days (cynomolgus); faricimab 2.83 days (cynomolgus); conbercept 4.24 days (Chinchilla rabbits) (Section 2; Table 1).
- **PK numbers the review quotes (human, cited):** aqueous t½ bevacizumab 9.82 days and ranibizumab 7.19 days in non-vitrectomized eyes (Krohne et al.); brolucizumab vitreous t½ 5 ± 2 days after 6 mg in trial RTH258-E003 (Section 2).
- **Duration of marketed implants (review’s own grouping):** biodegradable implants such as Ozurdex (dexamethasone in PLGA) show efficacy for 3 to 6 months with exponential decline; non-biodegradable Retisert/Illuvien (fluocinolone acetonide) last 2 to 3 years with more linear release but remain in the vitreous (Section 3.3). FDA-approved implant indications listed include DME, RVO macular edema, and posterior uveitis.
- **Mechanism (cited, not newly measured):** Hutton-Smith three-compartment model (aqueous, vitreous, retina) with ILM and RPE as barriers; Saunders et al. quoted as VEGF-A aqueous elimination t½ 3.5 days and complete VEGF-A suppression 41 days after ranibizumab in nAMD (Section 2). Anionic vitreous (collagen, hyaluronic acid, heparan sulfate) and ILM nanopore meshwork of 10–20 nm are cited as constraints on particle charge and size (Section 3.4).

## Key findings
- Molecular weights given in the review: ranibizumab Fab 49 kDa, bevacizumab IgG 148 kDa, aflibercept Fc 145 kDa (Introduction/Section 2). Table 1 also lists ranibizumab 48 kDa and bevacizumab 149 kDa in the PK table — both values are as printed.
- Review quotes HARBOR (nAMD): 2 mg PRN vs 0.5 mg PRN ranibizumab — injection interval 12.5 vs 9.9 weeks and visual gain +10.1 vs +8.2 letters. READ-3 (DME) is quoted as showing no alteration of 24-month visual improvements between 0.5 mg and 2 mg ranibizumab (Section 3.1). Mechanism of “longer/deeper VEGF suppression with higher dose” is proposed via the cited three-compartment model, not newly measured here.
- KSI-301 is described as a 950 kDa polymer-conjugated anti-VEGF antibody; a prior phase 1 study (NCT03790852) is quoted as visual improvement with 6-month injection intervals in nAMD, DME, and RVO (Section 3.2).
- GB-102 (sunitinib malate depot): review quotes ADAGIO as 88% of patients at 3 months and 68% at 6 months maintained on a single injection; a DME/RVO phase 2a (NCT04085341) is named (Section 3.4).
- Port delivery system: nondegradable refillable implant in sclera/pars plana; review quotes LADDER (NCT02510794) — similar 9-month visual outcomes for ranibizumab 100 mg/mL port vs monthly 0.5 mg injections, median time to first refill 15 months in the 100 mg/mL group; phase 3 programmes named include ARCHWAY (nAMD), PAVILION (DR), PAGODA (DME) (Section 3.7).
- Combined thermo-responsive hydrogel plus PLGA microspheres is cited (Liu et al.) as controlling ranibizumab or aflibercept release for up to 6 months (Section 3.6). Bevacizumab-loaded hydrogels are cited (Rauck et al.) as sustained release up to 9 weeks in rabbits (Section 3.5).
- Topical drops are judged inefficient for the retina because of tear turnover, blinking, and cornea/conjunctiva; DREAM (topical regorafenib, NCT02222207) is quoted as 21 of 51 subjects (41%) needing ranibizumab rescue by week 12 (Section 4).
- Subconjunctival route: review quotes that approximately 80–90% of small molecules are rapidly absorbed into systemic (blood/lymphatic) circulation (Section 4). Ayalasomayajula et al. celecoxib-PLGA microparticles for DR: cited sustained release for 7 weeks with high retinal concentration (in vitro). Misra et al. subconjunctival insulin hydrogel implant for DR is named (Section 4).
- Future systems should combine sustained release, minimally invasive administration, preserved biologic tertiary structure, RPE/photoreceptor targeting, and long-term ocular toxicity testing; brolucizumab-associated intraocular inflammation/obstructive retinal vasculitis is flagged as a regulatory caution for any new intraocular delivery system (Section 5).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Narrative review without a stated search window or study-count; many cited numbers come from AMD rather than DR/DME trials.
- Authors state that metabolic vitreous elimination is “not well explored.”
- Protein biologics can denature when loaded into PLGA implants/particles; hydrogels struggle to load a sufficient protein dose in a limited volume (Section 5).
- PMC XML text has missing figure/table callouts (“presents several ocular drug delivery routes”).

## Relevance to our research question
Core methods review for how anti-VEGF agents, steroid implants, nanoparticles, hydrogels, and the port system reach the retina, and why duration and translation remain limited. DR and DME appear as indications (implants, READ-3, KSI-301, GB-102, PAGODA/PAVILION) rather than as the sole disease focus.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Table 1 molecular weights (ranibizumab 48 vs 49 kDa; bevacizumab 149 vs 148 kDa) differ slightly from the prose; use the sentence-level values when citing MW and Table 1 when citing t½.
- Spelling “Illuvien” is the review’s own; the product is usually Iluvien.
- Whether LADDER refill-time and HARBOR letter gains should be used as DR evidence: they are nAMD programmes quoted by this review.
