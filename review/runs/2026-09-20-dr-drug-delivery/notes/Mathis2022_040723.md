# Efficacy and Safety of Intravitreal Fluocinolone Acetonide Implant for Chronic Diabetic Macular Edema Previously Treated in Real-Life Practice: The REALFAc Study

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Mathis T, Papegaey M, Ricard C, Rezkallah A, Matonti F, Sudhalkar A, Vartin C, Dot C, Kodjikian L. Pharmaceutics. 2022;14(4):723. doi:10.3390/pharmaceutics14040723
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Mathis2022_040723.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (real-world FAc implant in previously treated chronic DME).

## Research question
In routine French practice, what are the functional (BCVA) and anatomical (CMT / OCT biomarkers) outcomes, retreatment need, and IOP/cataract safety of an FAc implant in chronic DME already treated with anti-VEGF and/or dexamethasone implant (DEX-I)?

## Methods
Retrospective bicentric study (Croix-Rousse University Hospital and Desgenettes Military Hospital, Lyon). Consecutive patients receiving an FAc implant for DME December 2018–July 2021. Inclusion: type 1 or 2 DM, age ≥18, chronic DME previously treated with DEX-I or anti-VEGF, then FAc. Bilateral FAc allowed. Other CME causes excluded. BCVA (ETDRS letters), IOP, slit lamp, fundus, and SD-OCT (Cirrus or Spectralis, same device per patient) at baseline and follow-up. OCT biomarkers in the central 1 mm: SRF, IRF, HRF, DRIL, EZ/IZ alteration, hard exudates. FAc injected through pars plana with a 25-gauge injector. Additional DEX-I, anti-VEGF (ranibizumab or aflibercept), or focal laser at investigator discretion for anatomical recurrence and/or VA drop. Primary outcome: mean BCVA change from last visit before FAc to last follow-up. OHT defined as IOP >25 mmHg or IOP increase ≥10 mmHg after FAc. Ethics: French Society of Ophthalmology IRB 00008855.

## Sample / data
62 eyes of 46 patients. Mean age 71.6 ± 8.9 years; 84.8% type 2 DM; mean DM duration 25.0 ± 12.1 years; mean DME duration 60.3 ± 30.6 months. Mean follow-up after FAc 13.9 ± 7.5 months; 38/62 (61.3%) followed ≥12 months; 10/62 (16.1%) lost to follow-up. Pseudophakic 60/62 (96.8%); only 2 eyes (3.2%) phakic at baseline. Prior vitrectomy 18/62 (29.0%). Prior anti-VEGF 52/62 (83.9%); prior DEX-I 62/62 (100%). Last treatment before FAc: DEX-I in 54 eyes (87.1%), anti-VEGF in 8 (12.9%); mean delay last treatment → FAc 3.8 ± 2.6 months (DEX-I) and 1.7 ± 1.6 months (anti-VEGF). Mean prior IVT 16.0 ± 9.1. Laser (focal and/or PRP) 47/62 (75.8%). Baseline IOP-lowering therapy in 21/62 (none 41, 66.1%; monotherapy 11, 17.7%; dual 10, 16.1%); 1 prior IOP-lowering surgery; 5 prior SLT.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** retrospective bicentric observational (REALFAc)
- **Population / model:** chronic DME in adults with type 1 or 2 diabetes, previously treated with DEX-I (all eyes) and usually anti-VEGF, then given FAc
- **n:** 62 eyes (46 patients); mean follow-up 13.9 ± 7.5 months
- **Intervention / comparator:** intravitreal FAc implant delivering 0.2 µg/day (paper’s wording; commercial ILUVIEN is the 0.19 mg implant cited in referenced FAc literature). Last prior therapy DEX-I vs anti-VEGF described but not a randomised comparator. Subgroups: baseline BCVA (<50 / 50–60 / >60 letters), DME duration, CMT band, vitrectomy history, OCT biomarkers; additional treatment vs none.
- **Primary endpoint:** mean BCVA change (ETDRS letters) from pre-FAc baseline to last follow-up; secondary CMT (µm), ≥5-letter functional response, ≥20% CMT anatomical response, additional DME treatment, IOP/OHT
- **Primary result:** Baseline BCVA 64.0 ± 17.2 letters (median 67.5). Maximum mean BCVA 66.8 ± 16.7 letters at 3 months (p=0.03 vs baseline). Maximum mean BCVA gain +5.0 ± 12.7 letters at 21 months. Functional responders (≥5 letters at any time): 31/62 (50.0%); ≥10 letters 18/62 (29.0%); ≥15 letters 12/62 (19.4%). Baseline CMT 333.3 ± 112.5 (text also 112.6) µm; best mean CMT 280.5 ± 74.4 µm at 18 months (p<0.0001 vs baseline); CMT at 24 months 305.7 ± 121.4 µm. Anatomical responders (CMT decrease ≥20%): 23/62 (37.1%). Additional DME treatment in 23/62 (37.1%); mean time to additional therapy 7.2 ± 4.7 months; retreatment frequency fell from every 3.7 ± 9.9 months before FAc to every 10.3 ± 5.8 months after. Mean IOP increase about +1.3 to +1.8 mmHg at most visits (baseline IOP 13.4 ± 2.7 mmHg). OHT in 7/62 (11.3%); IOP >21 mmHg in 6/62 (9.7%); IOP-lowering procedure in 11/62 (17.7%), including 4 SLT and 1 surgery at 9.2 months. Both phakic eyes had cataract surgery 250 and 251 days after FAc. No anterior-chamber implant migration, endophthalmitis, or vasculitis. (Abstract; Results 3.2–3.6)
- **Cannot show:** randomised efficacy vs continued DEX-I or anti-VEGF; 36-month implant life (mean follow-up 13.9 months; only 4 eyes >24 months); that “keeping the macula dry” is proven superior to treating recurrent edema (this is the authors’ treatment paradigm, not a randomised comparison).

## Key findings

- Eyes with baseline BCVA <50 letters had lowest final BCVA (41.1 ± 15.0 letters, p<0.001 across BCVA bands) but highest gain (+9.7 ± 10.3 letters, p=0.02). Eyes >60 letters at baseline had final 74.0 ± 8.0 letters and mean gain −0.5 ± 6.4 letters (Table 2).
- DME duration associated with final BCVA (p=0.004) but not with BCVA gain (p=0.5). CMT band associated with BCVA gain (p=0.01): >400 µm gained +8.6 ± 8.1 letters vs −1.7 ± 7.2 for 300–400 µm (Table 2).
- History of vitrectomy: final BCVA 62.1 ± 15.4 vs 67.6 ± 16.7 letters without (p=0.1); gain +0.3 ± 9.9 vs +2.6 ± 8.7 (p=0.2) — no significant functional difference (Results 3.2; Table 2).
- OCT biomarkers: HRF and EZ/IZ alterations associated with worse final BCVA (p=0.01 and p<0.001); SRF associated with BCVA gain (p=0.048). None of HRF/DRIL/EZ-IZ significantly associated with gain except SRF (Table 2).
- Eyes without additional treatment had lower final CMT than retreated eyes (270.5 ± 83.0 vs 318.6 ± 88.3 µm, p=0.02); final BCVA 68.4 ± 16.9 vs 62.3 ± 15.2 letters (p=0.06). History of PRP associated with needing additional treatment (91.3% vs 61.5%, p=0.01) (Results 3.4; Table 3).
- Additional treatments among 23 eyes: aflibercept 11 (47.8%), DEX-I 15 (65.2%), focal laser 12 (52.2%), second FAc 2 (8.7%) at 13.1 and 19.8 months. Mean additional treatments 1.6 ± 3.0 in the whole cohort and 4.4 ± 4.2 among retreated eyes (Results 3.4).
- OHT eyes had more DEX-I injections before FAc than non-OHT eyes (12.4 ± 4.2 vs 7.6 ± 3.9, p=0.01); anti-VEGF counts did not differ (p=0.5) (Results 3.5). Authors call this a secondary analysis with small n.
- Pharmacokinetics (cited, not measured here): DEX-I described as a 7 × 0.37 mm PLGA implant with 700 µg dexamethasone, peak 1110 ng/g at 8 weeks then decline over 6 months; FAc as a nonbiodegradable 3.5 × 0.37 mm polyimide cylinder with 0.2 µg/day for 36 months and early concentration 1.26 ng/g (Discussion, citing Whitcup and Campochiaro). Author interpretation: FAc is a “basal” steroid, not a bolus.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Retrospective; no strict retreatment protocol (Discussion).
- Inhomogeneous population; modest sample; second-/third-line indication (Discussion).
- Follow-up short for a 36-month implant, though >60% followed >1 year (Discussion).
- Table 1 lists >24-month follow-up as “4 (0.6%)”; 4/62 is ~6.5% — treat 4 eyes as the count, not the printed percentage.
- Baseline CMT already relatively low (mean 333 µm, median BCVA 67.5 letters), so visual gains are smaller than series that implant during frank recurrence (authors’ own comparison).

## Relevance to our research question
Shows what a 0.2 µg/day (0.19 mg ILUVIEN-class) implant does in already-controlled chronic DME: modest letter gains, CMT reduction peaking at 18 months, fewer injections, residual ~37% rescue, and IOP/cataract as the translation limits. Vitrectomy history did not significantly change BCVA in this sample.

## Open questions / things to verify

- The paper’s own intervention wording is 0.2 µg/day, not a measured 0.19 mg mass in this cohort; 0.19 mg is the labelled ILUVIEN load used in cited studies.
- Table 2 header in the text dump is mislabelled “Baseline associated factors for additional treatment” but the table is BCVA by subgroup; Table 3 is the additional-treatment table.
- BOULEVARD-style claims are not in this paper; keep FAc PK numbers graded as cited.
- Whether CST in this note should be called CMT: the paper uses CMT throughout (SD-OCT central macular thickness).
