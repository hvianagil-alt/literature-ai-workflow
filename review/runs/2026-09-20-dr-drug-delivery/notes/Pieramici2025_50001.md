# Port Delivery System With Ranibizumab vs Monitoring in Nonproliferative Diabetic Retinopathy Without Macular Edema: The Pavilion Randomized Clinical Trial

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Pieramici DJ, Awh CC, Chang M, Emanuelli A, Holekamp NM, Hu AY, Suñer IJ, Wykoff CC, Brittain C, Howard D, Quezada-Ruiz C, Santhanakrishnan A, Latkany P. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2025;143(4):317–325. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2025.0001
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-gapfill/Pieramici2025_50001.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (gap-fill G4).
- **Gap-fill:** yes — retrieved after synthesis-rationale for the named gap.
- **Named gap:** G4 PDS in NPDR (continuous ranibizumab delivery without centre-involved DME).

## Research question
In moderately severe to severe NPDR without centre-involved DME, does the Port Delivery System with ranibizumab 100 mg/mL refilled every 36 weeks (PDS Q36W), plus monthly monitoring, improve DRSS and reduce incident CI-DME/PDR/ASNV at 52 weeks compared with monthly monitoring without PDS?

## Methods
Pavilion (NCT04503551): phase 3, multicentre, visual assessor–masked, superiority RCT at 50 US sites. Enrolment 10 August 2020–30 September 2021; primary analysis when all completed week 52; data analysis 10 August 2020–3 October 2022. Adults ≥18 years; moderately severe or severe NPDR (ETDRS-DRSS 47 or 53) from type 1 or 2 diabetes; study-eye BCVA ≥69 letters (~20/40 or better); no CI-DME (CST ≥325 µm excluded); no prior DR/DME treatment. Randomised unmasked 5:3 to PDS Q36W vs control (no PDS). Both groups monitored every 4 weeks. PDS arm: two 0.5 mg ranibizumab loading injections (day 1, week 4); implant (100 mg/mL ranibizumab) 1–14 days after the second load; refill-exchange every 36 weeks. Either arm could receive 0.5 mg ranibizumab injections if CI-DME, PDR, or anterior segment NV developed (investigator discretion). Primary endpoint: proportion with ≥2-step ETDRS-DRSS improvement from baseline at week 52 (colour fundus photographs, masked reading centre). CMH test stratified by baseline DRSS (47 vs 53) and IRF/SRF (present vs absent). Supplemental treatment, prohibited therapy, or PRP counted as nonresponders. Sample size 160 for >99% power to show ≥35% absolute improvement assuming 15% control response. Safety-evaluable PDS n=105. AESIs as in Pagoda (cataract, conjunctival events, endophthalmitis, hyphema, implant dislocation, RD, vitreous haemorrhage). Roche-funded; sponsor involved in design, analysis, and manuscript.

## Sample / data
174 randomised (PDS Q36W n=106; control n=68). Mean age 53.9 ± 11.7 years; 74 (42.5%) female. Race: White 125 (71.8%), Black 18 (10.3%), Asian 17 (9.8%); Hispanic/Latino 72 (41.4%). Mean time since DR diagnosis 12.8 ± 20.2 months. Baseline BCVA 82.1 ± 5.9 letters PDS vs 82.9 ± 4.7 control (~20/25). Seven PDS participants discontinued before implant; none after implantation. Six control discontinued. Ongoing at week 52: 99/106 (93.4%) PDS vs 62/68 (91.2%) control. Week-52 visit completed: 95/106 (89.6%) vs 56/68 (82.4%). All implanted PDS participants received a refill at ~9 months. Mean time on treatment 49.6 ± 11.0 vs 50.8 ± 5.7 weeks. Safety-evaluable PDS n=105.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** phase 3 randomised, visual assessor–masked, superiority trial of PDS vs observation (Pavilion)
- **Population / model:** moderately severe to severe NPDR (DRSS 47 or 53) without CI-DME; no prior DR/DME treatment; 50 US sites
- **n:** 174 randomised (PDS Q36W 106; control 68); PDS safety n=105; 99 PDS assessed for supplemental treatment
- **Intervention / comparator:** PDS with ranibizumab 100 mg/mL, two 0.5 mg loading injections then refill-exchange every 36 weeks, vs no PDS (monthly monitoring). Rescue 0.5 mg ranibizumab allowed in both arms for CI-DME (CST ≥325 µm), PDR, or ASNV.
- **Primary endpoint:** proportion with ≥2-step ETDRS-DRSS improvement at week 52 (percentage). Key secondary: Kaplan–Meier rates of CI-DME, PDR, or ASNV; BCVA change (ETDRS letters); CST change (µm).
- **Primary result:** ≥2-step DRSS improvement at week 52: 80.1% PDS Q36W vs 9.0% control (CMH difference 71.1%; 95% CI 61.0% to 81.2%; P<0.001). Composite CI-DME, PDR, or ASNV through week 52: 7.1% vs 47.0% (HR 0.12; 95% CI 0.05 to 0.28; P<0.001). CI-DME alone 7.1% vs 47.0% (HR 0.12; 95% CI 0.05–0.28). PDR or ASNV 1.0% vs 42.4% (HR 0.02; 95% CI <0.01–0.14; P<0.001). ≥2-step DRSS worsening 3.0% vs 46.4% (HR 0.05; 95% CI 0.02–0.16); ≥3-step worsening 3.0% vs 45.1% (HR 0.05; 95% CI 0.02–0.17). ≥3-step DRSS improvement: 16/106 (15.1%) vs 0/68 (difference 15.1%; 95% CI 8.3%–21.9%; P<0.001). Week-52 BCVA change +1.4 letters (95% CI −0.5 to 3.3) vs −2.6 (95% CI −5.0 to −0.1); difference 4.0 letters (95% CI 0.9 to 7.1; P=0.01). Transient BCVA drop 4 weeks after implant: −7.4 letters (95% CI −10.3 to −4.5; SD 14.5); resolved ~12 weeks after implantation (8 weeks after that dip per Key Points). CST week 52: −18.8 µm vs +2.5 µm (difference −21.3 µm; 95% CI −31.7 to −10.9; P<0.001). Supplemental treatment: 0/99 PDS vs 27/68 (39.7%) control (mean 2.9 ± 2.0 injections among those 27). AESIs in 17/105 (16.2%) PDS: cataract 7 (6.7%); vitreous haemorrhage 6 (5.7%); conjunctival bleb, retraction, and hyphema each 2 (1.9%); conjunctival erosion and retinal detachment each 1 (1.0%). No endophthalmitis, septum dislodgement, retinal occlusive vasculitis, or implant dislocation through week 52. (Abstract; Results; Table)
- **Cannot show:** that PDS improves long-term BCVA versus a real-world (less than monthly) observation schedule; visit-burden reduction (both arms seen every 4 weeks); DME treatment effect (this trial excluded CI-DME at baseline; CST change is small because maculas were already dry); safety beyond 52 weeks; head-to-head vs aflibercept PANORAMA/Protocol W regimens (those comparisons are discussion only).

## Key findings

- PDS Q36W is continuous ranibizumab for NPDR without edema; implant after 2 loading doses; one refill at ~9 months in this 52-week analysis (Methods; Results).
- Control event rate for CI-DME/PDR/ASNV was 47.0% by 1 year despite monthly monitoring and optional rescue, vs 7.1% with PDS (Figure 3).
- All 6 vitreous haemorrhages occurred within 28 days of PDS implantation; 1 required vitrectomy; none grade 4 (Safety).
- 2 serious AESIs (RD and VH); 1 severe VH (Safety table).
- 271 ocular AEs in 84/105 PDS (80.0%); 3 (1.1%) serious (eTables cited).
- Control AEs not fully summarised because only key events were collected until treatment started (Safety).
- Author interpretation: DRSS benefit (80.1% ≥2-step) is in the range of PANORAMA aflibercept Q8W (79.9% cited) but with one refill rather than frequent IVT; BCVA similar between arms at week 52 because monitoring caught anatomy before vision loss, as in PANORAMA/Protocol W.
- Trial population had more severe baseline DRSS than Protocol W (Pavilion PDS: 82.1% DRSS 47 and 17.9% DRSS 53 — cited in Discussion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Control monitoring every 4 weeks is more intensive than usual NPDR care.
- Participants and most staff unmasked (surgical device); only VA examiners and photograph graders masked.
- Primary analysis 1 year; longer follow-up ongoing, not in this paper.
- Seven randomised PDS patients never implanted; ITT still includes them for DRSS.
- Rescue in the control arm (39.7%) means “monitoring” is not untreated once complications appear.
- Sponsor involved in analysis and writing.

## Relevance to our research question
Shows the same refillable ranibizumab port can deliver drug to the retina in NPDR without CI-DME: 80.1% vs 9.0% ≥2-step DRSS improvement and an 88% relative reduction in CI-DME/PDR/ASNV (HR 0.12) with Q36W refill, at the cost of a transient ~7-letter postoperative dip and 16.2% PDS AESIs. Complements Pagoda (DME with Q24W) by covering delivery before centre-involved edema.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Composite 7.1% vs 47.0% and CI-DME-alone 7.1% vs 47.0% share the same HR in the text — CI-DME dominated the composite; PDR/ASNV was 1.0% vs 42.4%.
- Safety denominator is 105 implanted/treated PDS eyes, not 106 randomised.
- Key Points say the vision dip resolved “8 weeks after” the 4-week drop (i.e. ~12 weeks after implant), matching Results (“approximately 12 weeks after implantation”).
- Do not treat PANORAMA 79.9% as a Pavilion measurement; it is a cited external trial.
- PDS is FDA-approved for nAMD and DME in this paper’s introduction; Pavilion itself is the NPDR-without-DME investigation.
