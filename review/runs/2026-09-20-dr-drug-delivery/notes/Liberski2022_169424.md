# Aflibercept versus Faricimab in the Treatment of Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration and Diabetic Macular Edema: A Review

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Liberski S, Wichrowska M, Kocięcki J. Int J Mol Sci. 2022;23(16):9424. doi:10.3390/ijms23169424
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Liberski2022_169424.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (mechanism/comparison review of two intravitreal biologics; DME is an indication).

## Research question
How do aflibercept and faricimab differ in molecular target, preclinical activity, and clinical outcomes in nAMD and DME, and can dual VEGF-A/Ang-2 blockade extend injection intervals versus VEGF-family trap therapy?

## Methods
Narrative comparison review. The authors did not run a new RCT. They summarise molecular design, cited preclinical models, and published phase I–III trials in which aflibercept and faricimab were studied (sometimes head-to-head: TENAYA/LUCERNE, YOSEMITE/RHINE). Tables compile trial n, regimens, and endpoints from those publications.

## Sample / data
No new enrolled cohort. Cited DME/nAMD trial sizes compiled by this review include: aflibercept DME phase I n=5 or 6 (text vs table disagreement); DA VINCI n=221; VIVID/VISTA n=872; faricimab BOULEVARD n=229 DME (168 treatment-naïve, 61 previously treated); YOSEMITE and RHINE n=1891 DME (text also 940 and 951); TENAYA/LUCERNE n=1329 nAMD; VIEW-1/VIEW-2 n=2419 nAMD.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** narrative mechanism-and-trial comparison review (not an original interventional study)
- **Population / model:** cited human nAMD and DME trials; cited animal CNV/BRB models; cited vitreous cytokine studies
- **n:** not applicable as this paper’s experiment; DME phase III YOSEMITE+RHINE compiled as 1891 patients
- **Intervention / comparator:** aflibercept 2 mg (VEGF-A/B and PlGF-1/2 trap, 115 kDa) vs faricimab 6 mg (bispecific VEGF-A + Ang-2 CrossMAb, 150 kDa). Comparators in cited trials also include ranibizumab, laser, and sham as in the source RCTs.
- **Primary endpoint:** this review has no new primary endpoint. Cited DME phase III primary endpoint is mean BCVA change (ETDRS letters) over weeks 48/52/56 (YOSEMITE/RHINE; non-inferiority margin 4 letters) and week-52 BCVA in VIVID/VISTA.
- **Primary result (compilation of cited trials — not Liberski’s measurements):** Faricimab phase III DME (YOSEMITE/RHINE, cited): mean BCVA change YOSEMITE +10.7 and +11.6 letters (faricimab arms) vs +10.9 aflibercept; RHINE +11.8 and +11.8 vs +10.3. PTI 16-week faricimab in 52.8% (YOSEMITE) and 51.0% (RHINE). CST reduction YOSEMITE −206.6 and −196.5 µm vs aflibercept −170.3 µm; RHINE −195.8 and −187.6 vs −170.1 µm. Authors’ summary: 73.8% (YOSEMITE) and 71.1% (RHINE) achieved comparable BCVA to 8-weekly aflibercept with fewer injections. Aflibercept DME VISTA/VIVID week 52 (cited): VIVID BCVA +10.5 and +10.7 vs laser +1.2 letters; CST −195 and −192.4 vs −66.2 µm; VISTA BCVA +12.5 and +10.5 vs +0.2; CST −185.9 and −183.1 vs −73.3 µm. Aflibercept vitreous half-life cited 7.1 days; clinical activity ~2.5 months; typical extension to 8-week dosing after load. Faricimab described as first bispecific IVT antibody; FDA approval listed as 2022 in Table 1 (text also says January 2021). (Abstract; Tables 1, 3, 5; §§5–7)
- **Cannot show:** a new randomised comparison performed by these authors; that dual Ang-2/VEGF blockade is proven superior in anti-VEGF non-responders (authors say this “must be confirmed”); nanoparticle or implant PK; which molecule is better at 8 mg aflibercept (PHOTON/PULSAR cited as ongoing).

## Key findings

- Molecular comparison (Table 1, this review’s compilation): aflibercept binds VEGF-A, VEGF-B, PlGF-1, PlGF-2; faricimab binds VEGF-A and Ang-2. IVT doses 2 mg vs 6 mg. Fc of faricimab modified (Triple-A FcRn; P329G LALA vs Fcγ) to shorten systemic exposure and reduce immune stimulation (cited engineering papers).
- Mechanism grade: VEGF-A overexpression as dominant mediator — **cited**. Ang-2 antagonising Tie-2, pericyte/astrocyte apoptosis via integrins in STZ mice — **cited animal work**, not measured here. Dual blockade more effective than separate VEGF or Ang-2 inhibition in JR5558 spontaneous CNV mice — **cited preclinical**.
- Non-response estimates (cited, not measured): 8.1–15% of nAMD and 30–72% of DME patients (Introduction).
- Interval claim in Abstract (cited phase III): faricimab extends injections to 3 or 4 months in nearly 80% of nAMD and 70% of DME patients.
- BOULEVARD (cited; table vs prose conflict): treatment-naïve BCVA +13.9 (6 mg faricimab) and +11.7 (1.5 mg) vs +10.3 ranibizumab. Discussion prose also writes “+8.3 L in subjects treated with aflibercept” for previously treated patients; Table 5 lists that comparator as ranibizumab (+8.3 vs +9.6 faricimab). Prefer Table 5 / study design (ranibizumab), and flag the prose error.
- Port-delivery and other duration strategies are reviewed as alternatives (ranibizumab PDS every 6 months in PAGODA/PAVILION — cited as in development), not tested here.
- Intravitreal steroids mentioned only as background: DEX and FAc implants release over 4 and 36 months (Section 9, cited) — not this paper’s data.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Review of published trials; no new patients, no meta-analysis statistics performed by the authors.
- nAMD occupies a large fraction of the evidence; DME numbers must be kept separate.
- Internal inconsistencies: DME phase I n=5 in text vs n=6 in Table 3; faricimab FDA date January 2021 in prose vs 2022 in Table 1; BOULEVARD previously treated comparator named aflibercept in one sentence and ranibizumab in the table.
- Authors note faricimab benefit in anti-VEGF non-responders is unconfirmed (Conclusions).

## Relevance to our research question
Explains how two IVT biologics are engineered to last longer in the vitreous (trap vs bispecific Ang-2/VEGF-A) and what cited DME RCTs reported for BCVA, CST, and 12–16-week intervals. Duration still depends on repeated injections; translation limits are non-response, treatment burden, and unproven dual-pathway superiority in refractory DME.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Do not upgrade YOSEMITE/RHINE/VIVID/VISTA numbers to “Liberski measured.”
- Resolve BOULEVARD previously treated arm: source trial is faricimab vs ranibizumab, not aflibercept.
- Faricimab US approval is 2022 per this paper’s Table 1; the January 2021 sentence is likely a slip (FDA date should not be used from the wrong sentence).
