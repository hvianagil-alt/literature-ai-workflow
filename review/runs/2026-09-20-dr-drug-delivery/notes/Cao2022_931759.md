# Nanoparticles in ocular applications and their potential toxicity

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Yang C, Yang J, Lu A, Gong J, Yang Y, Lin X, Li M, Xu H. Front Mol Biosci. 2022;9:931759. doi:10.3389/fmolb.2022.931759

## Research question
How have organic and inorganic nanoparticles been used as ophthalmic drug carriers or imaging aids, and what ocular (and systemic) toxicities are reported by particle type, size, charge, and coating — including how toxicity should be assessed beyond animal models?

## Methods
Narrative review. Section 2 surveys NP classes as carriers (polymer NPs, nanomicelles, liposomes, quantum dots, nanoemulsion, hybrid NPs; silica, gold, carbon nanotubes) and diagnostic uses; later sections review organic and inorganic ocular toxicity, mechanisms, and assessment challenges including human organoids (Abstract; Sections 1–6). No systematic search or included-study count.

## Sample / data
Secondary literature of in vitro (ARPE-19, RPE, corneal cells), rabbit/mouse/zebrafish toxicity studies, and selected delivery papers. One explicitly diabetic-retina example (Pandit et al. chitosan-coated PLGA bevacizumab).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** narrative review of ocular nanoparticle applications and potential toxicity.
- **Population / model:** mixed cell lines (ARPE-19, RPE, corneal/lens epithelium), rodents, rabbits, zebrafish; one cited DR model (subconjunctival bevacizumab PLGA NPs); authors argue animal–human species differences limit current toxicity assessment.
- **n:** not reported (review). One cited clinical liposome trial is described as “only five cases.”
- **Intervention / comparator:** conventional invasive intravitreal/subretinal injection versus NP eye drops, subconjunctival or systemic NP carriers; toxicity of polymer vs lipid vs inorganic NPs, and coated vs uncoated particles.
- **Primary endpoint:** not applicable as original data; review endpoints are whether NPs reach retina/choroid, how long drug persists, and whether cornea, conjunctiva, or retina show toxicity under stated conditions.
- **Primary result:** Nano-drug delivery systems can overcome ocular barriers and are “characterized with strong biocompatibility, stability, efficiency, sustainability, controllability, and few side effects,” and have been “identified as a promising and generally safe ophthalmic drug-delivery system based on the toxicity assessment in animals”; yet “common nanoparticles can be toxic to the cornea, conjunctiva, and retina under certain conditions,” toxicity depending on exposure route, size, shape, surface charge, and coating — so more comprehensive human-relevant toxicology (including organoids) is still needed (Abstract; Introduction; Conclusion).
- **Cannot show:** a safe-by-class dose for gold, silver, or PLGA NPs in human DR; long-term clinical NP toxicity; original comparative PK.
- **DR-relevant delivery the review quotes:** Pandit et al. — chitosan-coated PLGA NPs loaded with bevacizumab injected subconjunctivally in a diabetic retina (DR) model were more permeable and maintained higher retinal concentrations than bevacizumab alone, and “the nano-drug carrier-loaded bevacizumab lasted for up to 12 weeks,” longer than the present clinically used bevacizumab solution (Section 2.1.1.1).
- **Mechanism of toxicity (cited, not newly measured):** cellular oxidative stress, mitochondrial damage, chromosome aberration, lipid peroxidation, DNA damage, and cell cycle alteration; size/shape/charge/coating associate with toxicity (Introduction, citing Gupta, Wang, Kamaleddin, Najahi-Missaoui). Example quoted: 15 nm gold NPs more toxic to liver cells than 60 nm gold NPs (Enea et al.).
- **Barrier context stated:** blood-aqueous and blood-retinal barriers “prevent most of the drugs from reaching the retina”; invasive intravitreal/subretinal injection complications include infection (Introduction).

## Key findings
- Polymer NP examples: SIR-loaded chitosan-functionalised PLGA NPs as noninvasive drops (AMD; Suri et al.); resveratrol-PLGA NPs reduced VEGF in human RPE cells (Bhatt et al.); hydroxyl-terminated PAMAM dendrimer–triamcinolone (D-TA) — a single intravenous 3 mg/kg dose “more effective than a 10-fold dose of free triamcinolone acetonide” in preventing CNV in AMD rats (Kambhampati et al.).
- Mahaling et al.: chitosan-coated poly-L-lactide dual-loaded (azithromycin + tretinoin) eye drops reached retina and choroid and released drugs continuously for more than 300 h (cited).
- Quantum dots coupled to bevacizumab: anti-vascular effect “slightly lower” than bevacizumab alone; retinal inflammation extinguished 28 days post-treatment; no retinal damage observed, but possible long-term toxicity from retinal deposition (Santana et al.).
- Mesoporous silica NPs encapsulating bevacizumab increased vitreous residence and anti-neovascular efficiency (Sun et al.); MSNAPTES up to 7% tacrolimus loading (cited).
- Polymer toxicity: chitosan NPs absorbed by conjunctiva/corneal epithelium within 2 h without surface inflammation, but exposure was short (Enríquez de Salamanca et al.). Lin et al.: PEG-PLGA not toxic in ARPE-19 and retinal vascular endothelial progenitor models, while PCL and PLGA exhibited toxic effects; PEG-PLGA detected in mitochondria, lysosomes, and ER at 24 h. HA-PLGA nontoxic to RPE (Chittasupho et al.) — surface modification “might reduce their toxicity.”
- Nanomicelles MPEG-PCL: cell activity unaffected below 2 mg/ml in corneal, lens, and RPE cells; rabbit intravitreal injection did not damage cornea/retina at less than 200 mg/ml; long-term exposure not assessed (Xu et al.).
- Liposomes: rabbit retina nontoxic after vitreal injection (Gariano 1994); ARPE-19/progenitor cells no cytotoxicity, no retinal inflammation/vitreous turbidity (Abud et al.); subacute subconjunctival and vitreal liposomes in New Zealand rabbits no significant differences vs control (Salas-Ambrosio et al.) — still need long-term assessment.
- Quantum dots (CdSe/ZnS): corneal stromal cell viability reduced by 50% after 5–20 nM for 24–48 h in vitro; can remain in mouse corneas up to 26 days (Kuo et al.).
- Fluconazole nanoemulsion in situ gel: unloaded NE >1% caused a 26% decrease in RPE viability at 24 h; 0.1% and 0.5% nontoxic; Draize score zero (Samimi et al.).
- Gold NPs: Söderstjerna et al. — 20 and 80 nm particles caused oxidative stress and apoptotic retinal-cell damage at low concentrations (0.4 μg/ml for 80 nm; 0.0065 µg/ml for 20 nm) plus photoreceptor toxicity and glial activation. Contrasting nontoxic findings: 160-nm gold nanodisks at 10 p.m. in mice (Song et al.); rabbits 67 and 670 μm/0.1 ml gold NPs nontoxic to retina/optic nerve within 1 month (Bakri et al.). Authors attribute inconsistency to model, functionalisation/charge, size, shape, and concentration.
- Diagnostic use: NPs as OCT contrast; gold NPs can cross BRB (20 nm after intravenous injection in C57BL/6 mice, Kim et al., no retinal damage in that study).
- Authors recommend advanced in vitro systems such as human organoids because of species differences (Abstract; later assessment sections). Conclusion: NPs are suitable for ophthalmic delivery but need “more comprehensive and in-depth NP toxicological assessment” of human ocular tissue.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Animal toxicity data do not fully represent humans (Abstract).
- Many cited toxicity studies are short-term; authors repeatedly note missing long-term exposure data.
- Hybrid NPs “more preclinical studies are still needed.”
- Nanoemulsion travoprost long-term safety “has not been evaluated.”
- Narrative review; DR is not the organising disease (AMD, keratitis, dry eye, and glaucoma occupy much of Section 2).
- Citekey “Cao” does not match Yang et al. authorship.

## Relevance to our research question
Defines the toxicity side of nanoparticle translation for retinal delivery: subconjunctival bevacizumab PLGA NPs can last ~12 weeks in a DR model, but particle size/charge/coating can still injure retina, cornea, and conjunctiva. Limits over-reading of nanocarrier “safety” in the other reviews.
