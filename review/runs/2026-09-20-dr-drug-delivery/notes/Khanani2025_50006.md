# Continuous Ranibizumab via Port Delivery System vs Monthly Ranibizumab for Treatment of Diabetic Macular Edema: The Pagoda Randomized Clinical Trial

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Khanani AM, Campochiaro PA, Graff JM, Marcus DM, Miller D, Mittra RA, Regillo C, Sheth VS, Bobbala A, Gune S, Lin S, Quezada-Ruiz C, Malhotra V. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2025;143(4):326–335. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2025.0006
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-gapfill/Khanani2025_50006.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (gap-fill G4; hinge PDS DME trial).
- **Gap-fill:** yes — retrieved after synthesis-rationale for the named gap.
- **Named gap:** G4 Port Delivery System original DME trial (Pagoda). HINGE paper.

## Research question
Does continuous ranibizumab 100 mg/mL delivered by a surgically implanted, refillable Port Delivery System with refill-exchange every 24 weeks (PDS Q24W) provide noninferior BCVA through 64 weeks versus monthly 0.5 mg intravitreal ranibizumab in centre-involved DME, and what are CST, DRSS, refill-interval supplemental-treatment, and ocular AESI rates?

## Methods
Pagoda (NCT04108156): phase 3, multicentre, visual assessor–masked, active-comparator noninferiority RCT at 87 US sites. Enrolment 30 September 2019–25 June 2021; data analysed to 19 September 2022. Adults ≥18 years with centre-involved DME; treatment-naïve for DR/DME in the study eye or previously treated if no study-eye treatment within 6 months. Randomised 3:2 to PDS Q24W or monthly ranibizumab. PDS arm: 4 monthly 0.5 mg ranibizumab loading doses through week 12; PDS (prefilled 100 mg/mL ranibizumab) implantation scheduled week 16 (could postpone to week 20; some later because of a COVID-19 sponsor surgery pause); refill-exchange every 24 weeks. Supplemental 0.5 mg ranibizumab allowed 16 and 20 weeks after implant or refill if prespecified criteria met. Monthly arm: 0.5 mg every 4 weeks through week 60. Primary endpoint: change in BCVA (ETDRS letters) from baseline averaged over weeks 60 and 64; mixed-effects repeated measures; noninferiority margin −4.5 letters; PDS noninferior if lower bound of two-sided 95.05% CI > −4.5. Efficacy population: all randomised who received study treatment, as randomised. mITT excluded randomisation blocks with PDS participants nonadherent to the protocol insertion schedule. Safety-evaluable: as treated before week 64. Key secondary: ≥2-step ETDRS-DRSS improvement at week 64 (NI margin 10%). AESIs: vitreous haemorrhage, endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, conjunctival retraction/erosion/bleb or filtering-bleb leak, hyphema, cataract, implant dislocation. Funded by F. Hoffmann-La Roche; sponsor participated in design, analysis, and writing.

## Sample / data
634 randomised (PDS Q24W n=381; monthly ranibizumab n=253). Mean age 60.7 ± 9.6 years (range 29–89); 363 (57.3%) male, 271 (42.7%) female. Race: White 489 (77.1%), Black/African American 93 (14.7%), Asian 30 (3.2%). Hispanic/Latino 119 (18.8%). Type 2 diabetes 610 (96.2%); mean HbA1c 7.7%. Mean time since DME diagnosis 16.7 ± 29.7 months (median 3.2). No previous IVT anti-VEGF for DME in 492 (77.6%). Baseline BCVA 65.3 ± 11.4 letters (~20/50); CST 484.4 ± 128.7 µm. Phakic 73.2%. DRSS: <47 NPDR 56.9%; 47–53 NPDR 35.8%; ≥60 PDR 5.1%. At week 64, 339/381 (89.0%) PDS and 228/253 (90.1%) monthly still on treatment. Mean ranibizumab treatments in the study eye: 6.4 ± 1.14 PDS vs 14.0 ± 3.72 monthly. Mean visits still ~every 4 weeks in both arms (15.6 vs 15.8). Safety-evaluable n=320 PDS vs n=314 monthly. Of PDS randomised, 80 (21.0%) did not undergo implantation at week 16 or 20, mainly COVID-19 pause.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** phase 3 randomised, visual assessor–masked, active-comparator noninferiority trial (Pagoda)
- **Population / model:** adults with centre-involved DME (US, 87 sites); treatment-naïve or no study-eye treatment in prior 6 months
- **n:** 634 randomised (PDS Q24W 381; monthly ranibizumab 253). Safety-evaluable 320 PDS vs 314 monthly.
- **Intervention / comparator:** surgically implanted refillable Port Delivery System with ranibizumab 100 mg/mL, refill-exchange every 24 weeks after 4 monthly 0.5 mg loading injections, vs intravitreal ranibizumab 0.5 mg every 4 weeks. Supplemental 0.5 mg ranibizumab permitted in the PDS arm per protocol.
- **Primary endpoint:** change in BCVA from baseline averaged over weeks 60 and 64 (ETDRS letters); NI margin −4.5 letters. Key anatomical endpoint: CST (µm, central 1 mm). Durability: proportion without supplemental injection during two 24-week refill intervals. Safety: ocular AESIs through week 64.
- **Primary result:** Adjusted mean BCVA change averaged weeks 60/64: +9.6 letters PDS Q24W vs +9.4 letters monthly (difference 0.2; 95% CI −1.2 to 1.6), meeting noninferiority. After implantation, mean (SD) BCVA fell 6.7 (12.0) letters at 4 weeks; mean BCVA was again similar to monthly ranibizumab 16 weeks after implant. CST change through week 64: −203.5 µm (95% CI −212.2 to −194.8) PDS vs −199.7 µm (95% CI −209.9 to −189.5) monthly (difference −3.8; 95% CI −17.2 to 9.6). Supplemental ranibizumab not given in 283/295 (95.9%) during refill interval 1 (weeks 16–40) and 298/306 (97.4%) during interval 2 (weeks 41–64). ≥2-step DRSS improvement at week 64: 39.0% vs 41.9% (difference −2.9%; 95% CI −10.7% to 4.9%) — 10% NI margin not met in the efficacy population; mITT 41.6% vs 41.6% (difference 0.1%; 95% CI −9.0% to 9.1%). AESIs in 88/320 (27.5%) PDS vs 28/314 (8.9%) monthly. No endophthalmitis and no retinal detachment in PDS through week 64 (monthly: 1 endophthalmitis, 0 RD). FDA approved PDS 100 mg/mL Q24W for DME in February 2025. (Abstract; Results; Table 2)
- **Cannot show:** reduced visit burden (both arms still seen every 4 weeks); that PDS is superior to monthly injections on BCVA or CST; DRSS noninferiority in the as-randomised efficacy population; long-term AESI rates beyond 64 weeks; outcomes with the post-recall updated implant/needle in this dataset (Pagoda used the trial-era implant; voluntary recall Oct 2022, lifted Apr 2024, updated hardware FDA-approved July 2024).

## Key findings

- PDS is a surgically placed, refillable ocular implant providing continuous ranibizumab; first approved US Oct 2021 for nAMD; class III voluntary recall Oct 2022 for implants not meeting performance standards; DME indication February 2025 (Introduction).
- Transient postoperative BCVA drop recovered by 16 weeks; authors note CST did not increase, arguing against worsening DME as the cause (Discussion).
- Ocular AEs: 925 events in 294/320 PDS (91.9%), 14 serious, most ≤37 days post-implant; 359 events in 140/314 monthly (44.6%), 5 serious (Safety).
- AESI counts (Table 2, safety population; multiple events in one person counted once per preferred term): cataract 35 (10.9%) vs 23 (7.3%); conjunctival bleb/filtering leak 25 (7.8%) vs 0; conjunctival erosion 6 (1.9%) vs 0; conjunctival retraction 4 (1.3%) vs 0; implant dislocation 1 (0.3%) vs 0; endophthalmitis 0 vs 1 (0.3%); hyphema 6 (1.9%) vs 0; retinal detachment 0 vs 0; vitreous haemorrhage 31 (9.7%) vs 5 (1.6%). Serious AESIs PDS: 5 conjunctival erosions, 4 conjunctival blebs, and 1 each cataract, conjunctival retraction, vitreous haemorrhage, implant dislocation.
- Vitreous haemorrhage timing in PDS: 2 before implant, 23 during ≤37-day postoperative window, 6 after; 29/31 resolved spontaneously.
- One PDS implant dislocated into the vitreous and was explanted; one septum dislodgement (implant retained; switched to standard IVT after 2 refill-exchanges).
- Mean (SD) time on study treatment 63.6 (3.88) weeks PDS vs 59.6 (12.92) weekly-injection arm (eTable 2 cited in text).
- Author interpretation: >95% did not need supplemental IVT in each 24-week interval; treatment frequency falls to every 6 months with BCVA/CST comparable to monthly ranibizumab after the postoperative dip.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- COVID-19 implantation pause: 21% of PDS-randomised participants missed week 16/20 implant; extra enrolment and mITT used to mitigate.
- Trial not designed to show fewer clinic visits (monthly visits in both arms).
- Visual assessor masked, but surgical assignment unmasked to other staff/patients.
- Sponsor-employed statisticians analysed data; Roche funded writing assistance.
- Primary analysis to week 64 only (two refill intervals); longer safety not in this paper.
- AESI excess is device-related (conjunctiva, VH, implant) rather than ranibizumab systemic toxicity (serious nonocular AE similar).

## Relevance to our research question
Hinge clinical evidence that a refillable scleral-port implant can keep ranibizumab in the vitreous continuously and match monthly 0.5 mg injections on 64-week BCVA (+9.6 vs +9.4 letters) and CST (−203.5 vs −199.7 µm) in DME, with refill every 24 weeks and >95% of assessed PDS eyes not needing interval rescue. Delivery trade-off is surgical AESIs (27.5% vs 8.9%) including conjunctival events and ~10% vitreous haemorrhage, without endophthalmitis or RD in this 64-week PDS cohort.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Efficacy n=381/253 vs safety n=320/314: do not use randomised n as the AESI denominator.
- 95% CI for the primary is reported as 95% rounding of 95.05% after interim-monitoring alpha spend.
- DRSS NI failed in efficacy population because delayed implants diluted PDS exposure; mITT met the 10% margin numerically but was not formally tested (fixed-sequence).
- Mean treatments 6.4 includes 4 loading injections plus implant/refills, not 6.4 refill surgeries.
- Do not import nAMD Archway endophthalmitis 1.6% as a Pagoda result (cited only as contrast).
