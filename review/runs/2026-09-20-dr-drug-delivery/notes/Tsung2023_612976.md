# Biodegradable Polymer-Based Drug-Delivery Systems for Ocular Diseases

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Tsung T-H, Tsai Y-C, Lee H-P, Chen Y-H, Lu D-W. Int J Mol Sci. 2023;24(16):12976. doi:10.3390/ijms241612976
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Tsung2023_612976.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (biodegradable polymer DDS review with a dedicated DR/DME subsection)

## Research question
How can natural and synthetic biodegradable polymers be formulated (nanoparticles, hydrogels, implants, and related systems) to give sustained, targeted ocular delivery for anterior- and posterior-segment diseases, including diabetic retinopathy, and what safety and translation barriers remain?

## Methods
Narrative review (paper type: Review). Covers polymer classes (cellulose derivatives, chitosan, HA, gelatin, alginate; PLA, PLGA, PCL, polyanhydrides, biodegradable polyurethanes; hybrids), formulation approaches (NPs, micelles, nanosuspensions, hydrogels, in situ gels, biodegradable implants, nanosheets), then disease applications including DR, AMD, and RVO, plus safety/regulatory discussion (Abstract; Sections 1–5). No systematic search protocol or included-study count.

## Sample / data
Secondary literature of polymer chemistry, cited animal/in vitro DDS papers, and named FDA products (Ozurdex, Durysta, Iluvien, Kenalog, Trivaris). DR subsection quotes Badiee, Mahaling, Xu, and Zeng studies.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** narrative review of biodegradable polymer ocular drug-delivery systems.
- **Population / model:** ocular diseases of anterior and posterior segments (glaucoma, cornea, uveitis, AMD, DR, RVO) as discussed; DR animal models (rat DR, DR mice) in cited formulation papers.
- **n:** not reported (review).
- **Intervention / comparator:** conventional topical drops/ointments versus biodegradable polymer DDS (NPs, hydrogels, implants) using collagen/chitosan/gelatin/PLGA/PLA/PCL and related polymers; biodegradable vs non-biodegradable implants.
- **Primary endpoint:** not applicable as original data; review endpoints are sustained release duration, bioavailability/targeting, and FDA product duration.
- **Primary result:** Biodegradable polymers can undergo degradation to give controlled, sustained release, improved bioavailability, and targeted therapy versus conventional drops that have inadequate bioavailability because of tear turnover and ocular barriers; further research is still needed to optimise formulations and meet regulatory requirements for clinical implementation (Abstract; Conclusions).
- **Cannot show:** original human efficacy in DR; a single polymer that is best for anti-VEGF vs corticosteroid payloads; pooled release kinetics across labs.
- **Marketed implants as stated:** FDA-authorised intraocular implants for sustained intravitreal small molecules to the retina; biodegradable polymers often PLGA and PLA; non-biodegradable PDMS, silicone, PVA, PEVA. Non-biodegradable implants give more precise and longer release but need surgical placement and later extraction. Among listed commercial products (Trivaris, Kenalog, Iluvien, Ozurdex, Durysta), “Only Ozurdex and Durysta represent biodegradable options.” Ozurdex (2009): PLGA dexamethasone implant for RVO macular edema and DME, “drug-release profile spanning up to six months.” Durysta (2020): intracameral, IOP lowering for 4 to 6 months (Section 3.6).
- **Mechanism (proposed / cited):** polymer degradation by hydrolysis or enzymes enables controlled release; chitosan cation binds negatively charged cornea/conjunctiva and can disrupt epithelial tight junctions (penetration enhancement, cited); PLGA degradation to lactic/glycolic acids can lower local pH and inflame; PLGA often shows an initial burst (Section 2.2.2). Nanoparticles defined here as 1 to 1000 nm (Section 3.1).

## Key findings
- Conventional topical methods often require frequent high-dose administration, contributing to noncompliance (Introduction).
- DR subsection (4.2.1): DR is “the majority of adult blindness between ages 20–74”; DME is “the leading cause of vision loss in DR patients” and follows BRB damage with macular thickening. Anti-VEGF and corticosteroids “have a confirmed role” in DR/DME; biodegradable formulations are being explored to augment effectiveness and bioavailability (cited).
- Review quotes Badiee et al.: bevacizumab-loaded chitosan NPs in a hyaluronic acid ocular implant sustained release for two months (in vitro; the AMD subsection later notes that analysis “lacked in vivo testing”).
- Review quotes Mahaling et al.: triamcinolone acetonide NPs (PCL core, Pluronic F68 shell) in a rat DR model — reduced NF-κB, ICAM-1, and TNFα after 20 days; decreased GFAP; reduced VEGF and microvascular tufts after 40 days.
- Review quotes Xu et al.: chitosan oligosaccharide-valylvaline-stearic acid nanomicelles of dexamethasone improved posterior-segment access via conjunctival pathways in male rats and albino rabbits, with dexamethasone levels comparable to an FDA-sanctioned NP system using hydrogenated castor oil-40/octoxynol-40.
- Review quotes Zeng et al.: PLGA/chitosan NPs of interleukin-12, encapsulation efficiency 34.7%, sustained release, inhibited VEGF-A and MMP-9 in rat endothelial cells and DR mouse retinas, increased retinal thickness and decreased neovascularization.
- Periocular injections are described as extending duration similarly to intravitreal injections with a more durable injection site and less tear-drainage outflow, but they still require trans-scleral penetration (Section 4.2.1).
- AMD supporting quotes (form, not DR outcomes): Varshochian et al. albumin-PLGA bevacizumab NPs maintained vitreous concentration “exceeding 500 g/L for approximately eight weeks in a rabbit model” (unit as printed — see Open questions); Rudeen et al. hydrogel with aflibercept microparticles plus dexamethasone NPs showed in vitro release “lasting up to 224 days.”
- RVO: FDA approval of Ozurdex June 2009, 700 μg dexamethasone; Phase III quoted as more patients gaining 15 or more letters vs sham up to 90 days, advantage diminishing to insignificance by 180 days, with a less pronounced effect after a second injection by one year. AR-1105 (340 µg dexamethasone in bioerodible PLGA, PRINT micromolding) Phase II six-month trial described as well tolerated with sustained VA and retinal-thickness improvements (cited).
- Translation challenges named: inflammatory responses from degradation products, unpredictable release kinetics, formulation/sterilization issues, in vivo toxicity studies, and regulatory harmonisation (Conclusions).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors: path to clinical translation is “fraught with numerous challenges” including inflammatory degradation products and regulatory obstacles (Conclusions).
- Broad ocular-disease scope; DR is one subsection, so many examples are glaucoma/cornea/AMD.
- No included-study count; cited NP results are single-paper.
- XML text has empty citation brackets.

## Relevance to our research question
Best source in this batch for biodegradable implant materials (Ozurdex PLGA vs non-biodegradable Iluvien) and for polymer NP/implant strategies specifically tested in DR/DME models.
