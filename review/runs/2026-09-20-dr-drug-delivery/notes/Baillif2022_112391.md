# Management of Patients with Diabetic Macular Edema Switched from Dexamethasone Intravitreal Implant to Fluocinolone Acetonide Intravitreal Implant

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Baillif S, Staccini P, Weber M, Delyfer M-N, Le Mer Y, Gualino V, Collot L, Merite P-Y, Creuzot-Garcher C, Kodjikian L, Massin P. Pharmaceutics. 2022;14(11):2391. doi:10.3390/pharmaceutics14112391
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Baillif2022_112391.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (real-world DEX-to-FAc implant switch in DME).

## Research question
What are the anatomical and functional outcomes, and the safety of the switch, when DME eyes previously treated with a short-acting dexamethasone (DEX) implant are switched to a longer-acting fluocinolone acetonide (FAc) implant, and does the timing of that switch (≤8 vs >8 weeks after last DEX) change CMT, the need for additional treatment, or IOP?

## Methods
Multicentre retrospective observational chart review (CFSR French retina sites). Charts collected April 2020–March 2021. Eligible: DME with ≥1 prior DEX implant, ≥1 FAc implant, and ≥4 months of follow-up. Other causes of macular edema, vitreomacular traction, and macular hole excluded. BCVA (ETDRS letters), CMT, and IOP recorded at baseline (immediately before FAc) and at subsequent visits (visit schedule at site discretion). Additional treatments and adverse events after FAc were recorded. Non-parametric tests (Kruskal–Wallis, Spearman, Chi-squared); multivariate general linear model / binary logistic regression. Ethics: French Ophthalmology Society IRB00008855.

## Sample / data
113 eyes from 30 sites; 27 patients received bilateral FAc. Mean follow-up 7.6 months (range 4–12). Mean age 69.8 ± 10.2 years (n=107); 51.3% women. Type 2 DM 89.3% (100/108); mean DM duration 21.9 ± 12.7 years (n=93); mean DME duration 71.8 ± 48.2 months (range 15–360; n=104). Pseudophakic 91.2% (103/113); prior vitrectomy 27.3% (30/110). All 113 eyes had prior DEX (mean 6.3 ± 4.5 injections, range 1–21); mean DEX-to-DEX interval 14.8 ± 4.5 weeks (n=69). Mean time last DEX → first FAc 11.12 ± 22 weeks (range 1–163; n=102). Timing available for 102 eyes: 41 (40.2%) at 1–4 weeks, 34 (33.3%) at 5–8 weeks, 27 (26.5%) >8 weeks; 75/102 (73.5%) received FAc ≤8 weeks after last DEX. Prior anti-VEGF in 97/113 (85.8%).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** multicentre retrospective observational (real-world switch study)
- **Population / model:** chronic DME eyes previously treated with ≥1 DEX implant, then switched to FAc implant (France)
- **n:** 113 eyes (30 sites); mean follow-up 7.6 months (range 4–12)
- **Intervention / comparator:** FAc implant after DEX (ILUVIEN described as 0.2 µg FAc/day over 3 years; Ozurdex 0.7 mg DEX over ~6 months). Timing comparator: last DEX ≤8 weeks vs >8 weeks before FAc. No untreated or anti-VEGF-only control arm.
- **Primary endpoint:** BCVA (ETDRS letters) and CMT (µm) after FAc; IOP (mmHg) and additional DME treatments as safety/management endpoints
- **Primary result:** Mean baseline BCVA 54.1 ± 17.8 letters (n=113) improved to 59.8 letters at month 4 (n=51); peak mean gain +5.3 letters at month 4 and lowest mean change −2.8 letters at month 12. Mean baseline CMT 454.7 ± 196.7 µm (n=108) fell to 320.9 µm at month 3. Additional DME treatment after FAc in 37/113 eyes (32.7%); mean time to first additional treatment 113.27 ± 94.70 days. Mean IOP remained <19 mmHg at all timepoints (baseline 19.0 ± 4.5 mmHg, n=74). IOP >21 / >25 / >30 mmHg in 18 (15.9%), 12 (10.6%), and 4 (3.5%) of 113 eyes. Risk of additional treatment associated with longer DM duration (p=0.009), longer DEX-to-FAc interval (p=0.035), and higher baseline CMT (p=0.003). Baseline CMT was higher when last DEX was >8 weeks vs ≤8 weeks before FAc (p<0.021); post-FAc mean CMT and IOP did not differ by that timing split. (Abstract; Results 3.3–3.6)
- **Cannot show:** randomised superiority of FAc vs continuing DEX or vs anti-VEGF; 3-year implant durability (follow-up 4–12 months); whether CMT at isolated visits equals thickness-fluctuation control; causal proof that ≤8-week switch is the optimal interval (observational, missing visits, COVID-disrupted follow-up).

## Key findings

- DEX implant is described as a biodegradable PLGA implant releasing 0.7 mg dexamethasone over 6 months (high release first ~2 months); FAc implant as non-biodegradable, zero-order 0.2 µg/day for 36 months (Introduction; cited PK, not newly measured here).
- ≥15-letter gain in 9%, 15%, 20%, and 20% of eyes at months 1, 3, 6, and 12 (n at those visits 67, 46, 44, 20) (Table 3).
- CMT ≤300 µm: 22/108 (20.4%) at baseline; 38/73 (52%) at month 1; 25/45 (55%) at month 3; 18/45 (40%) at month 6 (Results 3.4).
- Additional treatments among the 37 eyes: macular laser 3/37 (8.1%), anti-VEGF 18/37 (48.6%), DEX implant 15/37 (40.5%), one subconjunctival triamcinolone. Two patients received anti-VEGF on the same day as FAc (Results 3.5).
- Of 37 eyes needing one additional treatment, 25 (67.5%) received two, 21 (56.7%) three, and 9 (24.3%) four additional treatments during follow-up (Results 3.5).
- IOP-lowering medications after FAc in 29/90 eyes (32.2%); six of these were naïve to drops (mean time to start 4.8 months). One SLT and one MIGS; no filtering surgery. No endophthalmitis. Of 10 phakic eyes (8.8%), one had cataract surgery 8 months after FAc (Results 3.6).
- Four eyes with IOP ≥30 mmHg had last DEX 12, 18, 40, and 45 weeks before FAc (all >8 weeks) (Results 3.6).
- Multivariate: only DME duration correlated with change in VA (p=0.026) (Results 3.3).
- Author interpretation (Discussion): overlapping DEX burst with rising FAc levels may limit CMT fluctuation and early rescue; 73.5% of eyes were switched while DEX concentration was still high, so incremental BCVA gain from FAc was expected to be modest.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Retrospective real-world data not available at all timepoints; COVID-19 missed/delayed visits (Discussion).
- Short follow-up relative to 36-month FAc label duration.
- CMT at discrete visits may not capture retinal-thickness variability; poor CMT–VA correlation cited from prior work.
- Non-comparative; no randomisation of switch timing.
- DEX-response kinetics before the switch were not recorded, so “good vs poor DEX morphological responders” could not be analysed (Discussion).

## Relevance to our research question
Direct clinical evidence on switching from a 4–6-month biodegradable DEX implant to a 3-year FAc implant in DME: duration is extended, but ~one-third still need early rescue, and IOP/cataract remain the safety limits. Timing of the switch is a delivery-management variable, not a new carrier.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Exact FAc implant load in this cohort is described as 0.2 µg/day (ILUVIEN), not a newly measured 0.19 mg mass in the results tables.
- Month-4 BCVA n=51 vs baseline n=113: later-visit means are on shrinking samples; do not pool as if complete follow-up.
- 73.5% (75 eyes) ≤8-week switch is of 102 eyes with known timing, not of all 113.
- Table 1 IOP n=74 vs IOP-lowering-medication n=90: denominators differ by missing data.
