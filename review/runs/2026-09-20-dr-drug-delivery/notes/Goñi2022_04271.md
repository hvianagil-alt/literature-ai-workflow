# Intravitreal Corticosteroid Implantation in Diabetic Macular Edema: Updated European Consensus Guidance on Monitoring and Managing Intraocular Pressure

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Goñi FJ, Barton K, Dias JA, Diestelhorst M, Garcia-Feijoo J, Hommer A, Kodjikian L, Nicolò M. Ophthalmol Ther. 2022;11:15–34. doi:10.1007/s40123-021-00427-1
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Goñi2022_04271.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (European consensus on IOP after DME corticosteroid implants).

## Research question
How should retina specialists monitor and treat IOP after intravitreal corticosteroid implants for DME, tailoring visit frequency to baseline susceptibility and the latest IOP, and when should they refer to a glaucoma specialist? This updates Goñi et al. 2016 guidance.

## Methods
Consensus commentary. Six glaucoma specialists and two retina specialists met online in January and March 2021. They reviewed key IOP publications since 2016 (compiled as Table 1) and produced two dynamic algorithms (Figs. 1–2) plus a risk-group clarification (Fig. 3). The article “is based on previously conducted studies and does not contain any new studies performed by any of the authors.” Medical writing funded by Alimera Sciences; all authors received honoraria from Alimera.

## Sample / data
No new DME cohort. Table 1 collates published DEX and FAc IOP rates. Cited series include MEAD pooled DEX (347 implant eyes vs 350 sham, ≥36 months), Reldex/Safodex (128 eyes), IRISS (593 eyes), USER (160 eyes), PALADIN (115 eyes), Medisoft audit (256 eyes), Fallico meta-analysis (1,283 eyes), Kodjikian systematic review (1,880 eyes), and others. Overall ranges in Table 1 footer are the authors’ compilation across heterogeneous studies.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** expert consensus commentary / updated European monitoring guidance (not a new RCT or observational outcomes study)
- **Population / model:** patients receiving licensed DME corticosteroid implants in Europe — DEX 700 µg (Ozurdex, efficacy up to ~6 months) and FAc 0.2 µg/day for up to 3 years (ILUVIEN); off-label triamcinolone mentioned as a bolus suspension, not an implant
- **n:** not applicable (no new enrolled sample); guidance informed by cited studies ranging from tens to 1,880 eyes
- **Intervention / comparator:** no assigned treatment in this paper. Algorithms stratify: (A) baseline IOP <22 mmHg and no prior IOP event vs (B) baseline IOP ≥22 mmHg or prior IOP event (rise to >25 mmHg, rise ≥10 mmHg, or need for IOP-lowering treatment). Post-implant risk from latest IOP: low <22 mmHg; medium 22–25 mmHg with rise <10 mmHg; high >25 mmHg or rise ≥10 mmHg. More-susceptible patients have no low-risk arm.
- **Primary endpoint:** none measured; recommended IOP schedule and referral thresholds (mmHg)
- **Primary result:** Standard initial IOP checks at 2–7 days, 1 month, and 2 or 3 months, then by risk (low-risk every 3 months for implant life). High risk: treat with medication/laser/surgery within 1–2 weeks, recheck 1–2 weeks after starting; if still >25 mmHg after two treatments, refer to glaucoma. Medium/high risk: record visual field and optic-disc/RNFL imaging. Compiled cited IOP after implants (Table 2 summary of Table 1): IOP ≥21 mmHg in 31–60% of patients/eyes; ≥25 mmHg in 7–37%; ≥30 mmHg in 5–18%; ≥35 mmHg in 2–8%; rise ≥10 mmHg in 7–34%. IOP-lowering medication in up to 46%; trabeculoplasty 0–1.3%; trabeculectomy 0–1.2%; IOP-lowering surgery 0–3.7%. Medisoft (Bailey 2021, cited): prior IOP event vs none — IOP rise ≥10 mmHg 45.7% vs 19.1%; IOP >25 mmHg 56.4% vs 20.4%; IOP >30 mmHg 35.1% vs 8.0%; medication 50.0% vs 17.9% (all p<0.001); first IOP >25 mmHg at mean 418 ± 324 days after FAc. Europe: advanced glaucoma not controlled by medicines is a contraindication to Ozurdex; pre-existing glaucoma is a contraindication to ILUVIEN (SmPCs cited). (Abstract; Key Summary Points; Tables 1–2; Figs. 1–3)
- **Cannot show:** that the algorithms reduce glaucoma or vision loss (not prospectively tested); implant efficacy on BCVA/CMT (out of this paper’s scope); a single pooled IOP incidence (authors explicitly forbid cross-study comparison because of baseline, drug, and follow-up differences).

## Key findings

- Proposed mechanism of steroid IOP rise (cited, not measured): trabecular extracellular-lamina changes increasing outflow resistance; possibly silicone-droplet fouling from syringes/needles; gene–environment interactions for steroid glaucoma (Introduction).
- Risk factors listed as identified in cited studies: prior OHT/glaucoma (especially dual/triple therapy), prior IOP rise or IOP-lowering treatment, higher baseline IOP, myopia, axial length, type 1 diabetes, age, Latino and South Asian ethnicity, implant positioning (Introduction).
- Majority of IOP rises are managed with one or two topical agents and would generally be manageable by retina specialists (Overview of Recent Key Data, citing IRISS).
- FAc IOP can first exceed 25 mmHg late (mean 418 ± 324 days) — monitoring must last the implant’s life, not only the first weeks (Post-implantation Recommendations).
- Repeated DEX: glaucoma surgery reported after anywhere from 1 to 10 implants in uveitis/RVO series — lack of early IOP rise does not prove later safety (cited Hemarat; not DME-only).
- SLT as monotherapy is de-emphasised when IOP >25 mmHg (key change vs 2016).
- Hypothetical worked examples (John, William, Ann, Elsie) illustrate algorithm use; they are not real study patients.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- No new human or animal data (Compliance with Ethics Guidelines).
- Table 1 methods differ; authors state these preclude cross-comparisons (Table 2 note).
- Industry meeting support (Alimera) and author honoraria (Disclosures).
- Algorithms allow local adaptation; referral-from-optometrist age cut-off (Nilsson 2021) is flagged as unknown for retina-to-glaucoma referrals.
- Efficacy of the guidance itself is not an outcome.

## Relevance to our research question
Defines the main translational safety limit of long-acting corticosteroid implants used to deliver drug to the DME retina: IOP rise that can appear months to years after implantation. Duration of FAc (up to 3 years) forces equally long IOP surveillance. Does not measure retinal targeting or anti-VEGF delivery.
