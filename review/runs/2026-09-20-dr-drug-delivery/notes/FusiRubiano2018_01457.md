# Iluvien (Fluocinolone Acetonide 0.19 mg Intravitreal Implant) in the Treatment of Diabetic Macular Edema: A Review

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Fusi-Rubiano W, Blow RR, Lane M, Morjaria R, Denniston AK. Ophthalmol Ther. 2018;7:293–305. doi:10.1007/s40123-018-0145-7
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-gapfill/FusiRubiano2018_01457.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (gap-fill G2).
- **Gap-fill:** yes — retrieved after synthesis-rationale for the named gap.
- **Named gap:** G2 FAc implant pharmacology/outcomes review (secondary source for FAME PK and 3-year FAc numbers; not a new RCT).

## Research question
What is the clinical, pharmacological, real-world, vitrectomy, regulatory, and cost-effectiveness evidence for the 0.19 mg fluocinolone acetonide (FAc, Iluvien) intravitreal implant in insufficiently responsive DME?

## Methods
Narrative review (no new human or animal study by the authors; stated explicitly). Synthesises FAMOUS aqueous PK, FAME A/B sham-controlled RCTs, post hoc FAME analyses, EU/US/NICE labelling, and real-world cohorts (including the authors’ own 2018 BMC Ophthalmol series summarised in Table 1). No meta-analysis.

## Sample / data
Not a primary dataset. Pivotal cited trial: FAME A and B — high-dose FAc 0.5 µg/day n=393, low-dose 0.2 µg/day n=375, sham n=185 (2:2:1). Real-world Table 1 cohorts range from 1–2 eyes (case reports) to ICE-UK 233 eyes / 208 patients and Bailey 2017 345 eyes / 305 patients. Authors’ related 2–3 year UK series (Fusi-Rubiano 2018 BMC Ophthalmol): 29 eyes (27 patients); 22 with 24-month and 6 with 36-month data.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** narrative review of FAc 0.19 mg implant pharmacology, FAME RCTs, real-world cohorts, vitrectomised-eye series, labelling, and cost analyses (no new trial)
- **Population / model:** DME (chronic / insufficiently responsive to laser and/or anti-VEGF); FAME and real-world human eyes; FAMOUS aqueous PK; animal PK cited only for anti-VEGF in vitrectomised eyes, not newly measured for FAc here
- **n:** FAME low-dose (0.2 µg/day) n=375, high-dose (0.5 µg/day) n=393, sham n=185 (cited). Review authors performed no new enrolment.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Iluvien FAc 0.19 mg implant, labelled slow release 0.2 µg/day for up to 36 months, vs FAME sham; real-world papers mostly have no concurrent control. High-dose 0.5 µg/day FAc was a FAME arm, not the marketed implant.
- **Primary endpoint:** as defined in cited FAME: percentage with ≥15-letter ETDRS BCVA gain at 24 months (US); 36-month visual and foveal thickness outcomes; FAMOUS aqueous FAc concentration (ng/ml).
- **Primary result (cited, not newly generated):** FAMOUS: aqueous FAc slightly more than 2 ng/ml for ~first 3 months, then 0.5–1.0 ng/ml through 36 months. FAME ≥15-letter gain at month 24: 28.7% low-dose, 28.6% high-dose, 16.2% sham (p=0.002); at month 36: 28.7%, 27.8%, 18.9% (p=0.018); among those remaining at month 36: 33.0%, 31.9%, 21.4% (p=0.030). Mean letter gain month 24: 4.4 (low), 5.4 (high), 1.7 (sham); month 36: 5.3 both FAc groups vs 2.0 sham (p≤0.018). Chronic-DME preplanned subgroup ≥15-letter gain at month 36: 34.0% low-dose (p<0.001), 28.8% high-dose (p=0.002), 13.4% sham. Cataract by month 36: 81.7% low-dose (80.0% surgery), 88.7% high-dose (87.2% surgery), 50.7% sham (27.3% surgery). IOP-lowering drops ≥7 days by month 36: 38.4% low, 47.3% high, 14.1% sham. IOP ≥30 mmHg: 16.3% of treated participants by month 24 and 18.4% by month 36. Incisional IOP surgery: 1.6% and 4.8% low-dose at 24 and 36 months; 6.4% and 8.1% high-dose; 0.5% sham. Endophthalmitis 0.2% (2 eyes) FAc vs none sham. (FAME/FAMOUS sections)
- **Cannot show:** new randomised 3-year outcomes (this is a review); head-to-head FAc vs monthly anti-VEGF; that real-world Table 1 rows can be pooled (different n, follow-up, and missing data); Singer 2022 PALADIN (not cited; post-dates or outside this 2018 review).

## Key findings

- Implant: 0.19 mg FAc (C24H30F2O6) injected intravitreally; 0.2 µg/day for up to 36 months (Brief overview).
- Mechanism (cited, not measured here): corticosteroids block leukotriene/prostaglandin synthesis, inhibit VEGF-α, IL-6, ICAM-1, and increase vasoconstriction via nitric oxide inhibition (Introduction).
- Benefit seen from week 3 in FAME at both FAc doses (Efficacy).
- Post hoc: among prior-corticosteroid “non-responders” for IOP (n=72), none needed incisional IOP surgery vs 6.1% (n=18) of steroid-naïve FAME patients (Adverse Effects).
- NICE TA301 (UK): FAc restricted to insufficiently responsive DME in a pseudophakic eye with a patient-access-scheme discount (Regulatory).
- US label: DME after prior corticosteroid without clinically significant IOP rise; contraindicated if infection, glaucoma CDR ≥0.8, or hypersensitivity (Regulatory).
- ICE-UK (Holden/Currie 2017): 233 eyes / 208 patients, 12 months; ≥5 / ≥10 / ≥15 letter gain in 44% / 30% / 18%; median CRT reduction 153 µm; median VA gain 4.3 letters; IOP drops 29/233; cataract surgery 6/26 phakic eyes (Table 1).
- Bailey 2017 UK EMR: 345 eyes; mean BCVA gain 4.5 letters at 18 months (n=120) and 5.3 at 24 months (n=53); CRT 451.2 to 355.5 µm last observation; IOP drops 48/345; trabeculectomy 1/345 (Table 1).
- Authors’ BMC Ophthalmol 2018 series in Table 1: 29 eyes; mean CRT reduction 114 µm (103 at 24 months / 65 at 36 months); BCVA +6.5 letters at 24 months / +11 letters at 36 months; IOP drops 2/29; 0 endophthalmitis; 3-year n only 6 eyes.
- Massin 2016 phase IV: group 1 (laser only, 7 eyes) CRT −299 µm and +5.6 letters at 12 months vs group 2 (laser+anti-VEGF, 10 eyes) −251 µm and +0.9 letters (Table 1).
- Vitrectomised eyes: Meireles 2017, 26 eyes, mean follow-up 255 days, +11.7 letters, CRT −233.5 µm, mean IOP rise 1.4 mmHg (Vitrectomized eyes). Pessoa 2018: ≥15-letter gain 37.5% of 24 vitrectomised vs 36.8% of 19 non-vitrectomised eyes; CRT −217.7 vs −155.6 µm; mean follow-up 8.1 months; no statistical difference between groups.
- Migration into AC reported in two vitrectomised eyes with posterior capsule defect (El-Ghrably); “floater” on visual axis (Moisseiev); dislodgement into infusion cannula during vitrectomy (Andreatta) — case reports, not incidence rates from a trial.
- Cost (Quhill & Beiderbeck, cited): estimated saving ~£6068 in pseudophakic eyes and £5341 in phakic eyes vs ranibizumab as-needed (average 14 Lucentis treatments over 3 years from RESTORE extension).
- EURETINA guidance cited: consider FAc if lacking response to first-line therapy; retreatment after 1 year may be considered (Regimens).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Review, not a trial; FAME and real-world numbers are cited from other papers and must be attributed as such.
- Early real-world studies have small n and short follow-up; 36-month real-world n in the authors’ own series is 6 eyes.
- ICE-UK and Bailey EMR: incomplete records, retrospective missing data.
- Vitrectomy comparisons underpowered and short follow-up.
- Cost models depend on assumed injection counts, not a randomised economic trial in this paper.
- Manufacturer was offered peer-review comment (Acknowledgements); interpret promotional-adjacent cost claims cautiously.

## Relevance to our research question
Supplies the labelled FAc delivery rate (0.19 mg implant; 0.2 µg/day; aqueous 0.5–1.0 ng/ml through 36 months) and the FAME 24-/36-month visual, CRT-context, cataract, and IOP surgery percentages needed for the G2 pharmacology/outcomes gap. Real-world rows support that rescue injections fall but do not disappear. Not a substitute for unrecovered Singer 2022.

## Open questions / things to verify

- All FAME percentages in Claim-ready facts are as reported by this review, not re-read from Campochiaro 2011/2012 PDFs in this run.
- Table 1 “Fusi-Rubiano 2018” is the authors’ separate BMC Ophthalmol cohort (29 eyes), not this review’s sample.
- Low-dose FAME arm is 0.2 µg/day, matching marketed Iluvien; do not cite the 0.5 µg/day arm as current standard.
- NICE pseudophakic restriction is UK reimbursement, not the EU-wide licence (17 countries: insufficiently responsive DME).
