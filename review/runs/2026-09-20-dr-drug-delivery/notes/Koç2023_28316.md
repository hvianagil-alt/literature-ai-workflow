# Comparison of the efficacy of intravitreal Anti-VEGF versus intravitreal dexamethasone implant in treatment resistant diabetic macular edema

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Koc H, Alpay A, Ugurbas SH. BMC Ophthalmol. 2023;23:97. doi:10.1186/s12886-023-02831-6
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Koç2023_28316.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (original four-arm switch vs continue anti-VEGF in persistent DME)

## Research question
After three monthly anti-VEGF injections for DME with CMT still >300 µm, is continuing the same anti-VEGF for three more doses superior, equivalent, or inferior to switching to a DEX implant for BCVA, CMT, and IOP at 6 months?

## Methods
Retrospective study, January 2014–January 2019. Ethics: Bülent Ecevit University 2018-144-23/05. Four groups after three monthly anti-VEGF injections: (1) AFL+AFL — three further aflibercept; (2) RAN+RAN — three further ranibizumab; (3) AFL+DEX — switch to intravitreal DEX implant; (4) RAN+DEX — switch to DEX implant. Monthly follow-up to 6 months. BCVA Snellen (analysed as logMAR), Goldmann IOP, CMT on Heidelberg OCT. FFA used to exclude macular ischemia. Injection: 4 mm from limbus if phakic, 3.5 mm if pseudophakic. Kruskal–Wallis between groups; Wilcoxon within group; p < 0.05 significant.

## Sample / data
94 eyes of 94 patients (right eye only if both eligible). Mean age 64.64 ± 7 years; 58 men (61.7%), 36 women (38.3%). Type 2 DM, ≥18 years; NPDR or early PDR; CMT >300 µm after three anti-VEGF (aflibercept or ranibizumab). Group n from Table 1 NPDR/PDR counts: AFL+AFL 25 (20/5), RAN+RAN 26 (22/4), AFL+DEX 22 (17/5), RAN+DEX 21 (15/6). No significant between-group difference in age, retinopathy stage, laterality, or lens status (p > 0.05). Exclusions: high-risk PDR, pregnancy, uncontrolled hypertension, other retinal vascular disease, AMD, ERM on OCT, uveitis, glaucoma, prior PPV, severe cataract, YAG capsulotomy within 6 months, prior laser photocoagulation, missed monthly visits.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Retrospective four-arm comparative cohort (not randomized).
- **Population / model:** Adults with type 2 DM and NPDR or early PDR; persistent center-involved DME (CMT >300 µm) after three monthly aflibercept or ranibizumab injections.
- **n:** 94 eyes / 94 patients (AFL+AFL 25, RAN+RAN 26, AFL+DEX 22, RAN+DEX 21).
- **Intervention / comparator:** Continue three more doses of the same anti-VEGF vs switch to one DEX implant after the first three anti-VEGF doses; 6-month follow-up.
- **Primary endpoint:** BCVA (logMAR), CMT (µm), IOP (mmHg) at 6 months (also vs month-3 post-loading values).
- **Primary result:** After 3 anti-VEGF (month 3, Table 2), groups did not differ (visual acuity p = 0.260; CMT p = 0.964; IOP p = 0.392). Month-3 vs month-6 within groups (Table 3): BCVA logMAR 0.59 ± 0.26 → 0.49 ± 0.22 (AFL+AFL, p = 0.132); 0.64 ± 0.32 → 0.57 ± 0.38 (RAN+RAN, p = 0.284); 0.80 ± 0.4 → 0.62 ± 0.38 (AFL+DEX, p = 0.064); 0.85 ± 0.43 → 0.80 ± 0.43 (RAN+DEX, p = 0.704) — no group had a significant BCVA change. CMT (µm) 403.6 ± 70.4 → 291.6 ± 54.4 (AFL+AFL, p = 0.000); 411.5 ± 84 → 293.1 ± 79.5 (RAN+RAN, p = 0.000); 413.6 ± 88.1 → 320 ± 103.8 (AFL+DEX, p = 0.001); 418.1 ± 88 → 332.5 ± 96.8 (RAN+DEX, p = 0.000) — CMT fell in all four groups. IOP (mmHg) 15.3 ± 2.7 → 15.9 ± 3.5 (AFL+AFL, p = 0.542); 15.4 ± 2.9 → 15.59 ± 4 (RAN+RAN, p = 0.834); 14.7 ± 2.9 → 15.9 ± 3 (AFL+DEX, p = 0.013); 16.2 ± 3.2 → 18.1 ± 3.8 (RAN+DEX, p = 0.027) — IOP rose only in DEX-switch groups; no patient needed IOP medication or surgery. Between groups at month 6 (Table 4): BCVA p = 0.159, CMT p = 0.295, IOP p = 0.109 (no significant difference). Authors conclude no superiority of continuing anti-VEGF vs switching to DEX after three loading doses.
- **Cannot show:** Randomized treatment-assignment effect; outcomes beyond 6 months; inflammatory-cytokine mechanism (not measured); results in high-risk PDR, glaucoma, or vitrectomized eyes (excluded).

## Key findings
- Authors’ interpretation: choose switch vs continue by glaucoma history, adherence, and cost; DEX needs fewer injections and they state its cost is cheaper than three further anti-VEGF doses (Conclusion).
- They contrast Protocol U (Maturi): more CMT decrease and IOP rise after DEX add-on at 24 weeks but no VA difference — similar VA pattern to this cohort (Discussion).
- Persistent DME after ≥6 months of anti-VEGF is cited as 32–66% from DRCR.net/other literature, not measured in this sample (Introduction).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Retrospective; small n per arm; no aqueous inflammatory markers; mean follow-up only 6 months (Strengths and limitations).
- Snellen BCVA converted to logMAR; not an ETDRS-protocol trial.
- Intergroup Results sentence says “no statistically significant difference … (p < 0.05) Table 4”; Table 4 p-values are 0.159 / 0.295 / 0.109 (all > 0.05). Use the table p-values.
- DEX groups had numerically worse month-3 BCVA (0.8 and 0.85 vs 0.59 and 0.64 logMAR) even though Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.260; residual confounding possible.

## Relevance to our research question
Original clinical comparison of continuing solution anti-VEGF versus switching to a sustained DEX implant after incomplete loading response. Anatomy improved in all arms by month 6; vision did not; DEX added IOP rise without medication need. Directly addresses duration (one implant vs three more injections) and translation (no VA superiority of switch in this 6-month resistant-DME sample).
