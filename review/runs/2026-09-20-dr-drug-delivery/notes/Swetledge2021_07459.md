# Distribution of polymeric nanoparticles in the eye: implications in ocular disease therapy

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Swetledge S, Jung JP, Carter R, Sabliov C. J Nanobiotechnology. 2021;19:10. doi:10.1186/s12951-020-00745-9
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-dr-drug-delivery-found/Swetledge2021_07459.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Screening:** Included (narrative review of polymeric NP ocular biodistribution; DR listed among posterior targets and in Table 2)

## Research question
How do polymer type, size, charge, coating, suspension medium, and administration route determine where polymeric nanoparticles go in the eye, and which designs are therefore plausible for diseases of cornea, lens, choroid, and retina (including diabetic retinopathy)?

## Methods
Narrative review organized by ocular tissue (cornea, iris/ciliary body, lens, choroid, retina). Table 1 compiles distribution site, route, polymer, coating, size (nm) and zeta (mV), and suggested clinical use. Table 2 compiles disease, animal model, polymer/drug, size/zeta, route, efficacy, clearance, toxicity. No new experiment.

## Sample / data
Secondary literature. Table 1 DR-relevant rows include topical PCL/PF68 165 nm, −20 mV; topical PLGA/chitosan 170 nm, +35 mV; suprachoroidal polystyrene 20–10,000 nm; subconjunctival pRNA 10 nm in PLGA-PEG-PLGA hydrogel; IVT PLGA/PVA 227 ± 15 nm, −2 ± 1 mV; IVT hyaluronic acid 213.4 ± 10.3 nm, −26.2 ± 4.0 mV; IVT HSA 326.3 ± 9.7 nm, −20.9 ± 2.0 mV (asterisked zetas extracted from bar graphs). Table 2 DR row: mouse CK30PEG10K/miR200b, size NA, IVT, decreased VEGFR-2 and angiogenesis for 3 months (ref 59).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** narrative review of polymeric nanoparticle ocular biodistribution
- **Population / model:** not applicable (cited rabbit, rat, mouse, bovine vitreous, and cell studies)
- **n:** not applicable for the review; cited primary n values are generally not restated in the prose
- **Intervention / comparator:** polymeric NPs (PLGA, PCL, HA, HSA, chitosan, CK30PEG DNA polyplexes, polystyrene beads) by topical, subconjunctival, suprachoroidal, intravitreal, or systemic routes vs free drug or uncoated particles, as reported by the source papers
- **Primary endpoint:** not applicable (distribution and cited efficacy, not a single trial endpoint)
- **Primary result:** Corneal epithelial pores ~2 nm, so most NPs need transcellular permeation; corneal surface area ~17× smaller than conjunctiva (Cornea). Inner limiting membrane pores 10–25 nm, a strict filter from vitreous to inner retina unless Müller-cell transcytosis is used (Retina). In bovine vitreous, PEG-coated polystyrene beads: neutral particles up to 750 nm diffuse, with higher diffusion at 100–500 nm than 750 nm; carboxylated (negative) 500 nm beads did not effectively diffuse (Xu et al. cited as [67]). Positive PEI NPs do not diffuse after IVT; glycosylated chitosan 200–500 nm can reach retina but not penetrate ILM; HA NPs 213.4 ± 10.3 nm, −26.2 ± 4.0 mV and HSA NPs 326.3 ± 9.7 nm, −20.9 ± 2.0 mV reach retina and penetrate ILM via Müller cells after IVT (Table 1; [63]). Systemic gold NPs crossed inner BRB at 20 nm but not 100 nm ([68]). Topical chitosan-coated PLGA (170 nm, +35 mV) showed the greatest retinal fluorescence among compared PLGA/PCL coatings; PF68-PCL also reached choroid/retina (Table 1; [14,26]). Subconjunctival DSP-PLGA (~200 ± 8 nm, −8 ± 1.4 mV) sustained ocular drug ~7 d after one dose (release ~16 d) and prevented rat corneal graft rejection when given weekly for 9 weeks vs free DSP failure (Table 2; [23]). IVT PLGA microparticles (6000 nm) of a serpin peptide kept anti-angiogenic effect 14 weeks vs 4 weeks for free peptide ([57]). CK30PEG10K/miR200b IVT in diabetic mice suppressed angiogenesis and VEGFR-2 up to 3 months after one injection (Table 2; [59]). Authors conclude IVT is the most direct retinal route but cationic particles and particles >500 nm are poorly suited; anionic/neutral ~100–500 nm designs are preferred for AMD, retinal detachment, and diabetic retinopathy (Conclusions).
- **Cannot show:** original DR/DME efficacy, human translation, or a head-to-head ranking generated by this paper’s own measurements

## Key findings
- Tear film, cornea/sclera, and blood–ocular barriers plus continuous clearance into systemic circulation limit topical and systemic delivery (Background).
- Mahaling et al.: two fluorescence gradients after topical NPs — anterior→posterior and outer→inner — supporting periocular/scleral rather than straight trans-vitreal transit to the macula (Retina; [26]).
- Higher topical or subconjunctival doses may be needed because much of the dose never reaches inner retina (Retina).
- Pathologic CNV may enhance BRB penetration via an EPR-like effect; BRB modulation is untested with NPs and is flagged as a safety risk (Retina).
- NPs >200 nm are subject to Kupffer/spleen scavenging after systemic dosing; PEG “stealth” is cited as a countermeasure (Retina).
- **Mechanism grade:** size/charge sieving in vitreous and ILM is **synthesized from cited measurements** (especially [63], [67], [68]); EPR in diabetic retina is **proposed**. This review did not newly track NPs.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors note many zeta values in Table 1 were read from bar graphs (*).
- Polystyrene rows are proof-of-concept, not intended drug carriers (†).
- Review is not restricted to DR; many efficacy rows are cornea, CNV, uveitis, cataract.
- Animal-to-human gap: they note rats are used more than pigs despite pigs being closer to human physiology (Discussion).
- No quantitative meta-analysis of retinal AUC.

## Relevance to our research question
Supplies the physical rules (size, charge, ILM pores, IVT vs topical gradients) that explain why anti-VEGF, corticosteroid, or gene cargo in polymeric NPs may or may not reach diabetic retina, and cites one 3-month IVT miR200b diabetic-mouse result. It does not itself formulate an anti-VEGF protein or a DME implant.
