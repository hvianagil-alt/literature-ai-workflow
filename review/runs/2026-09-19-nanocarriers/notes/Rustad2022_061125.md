# The pH-Responsive Liposomes — The Effect of PEGylation on Release Kinetics and Cellular Uptake in Glioblastoma Cells

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Rustad E, et al. Pharmaceutics. 2022;14:1125. doi:10.3390/pharmaceutics14061125
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-19-nanocarriers-found/Rustad2022_061125.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Included (experimental PEG length vs pH-release in GL261)

## Research question
How do DSPE-PEG750 vs DSPE-PEG2000 change calcein release and GL261 uptake of DOPE:CHEMS pH-responsive liposomes?

## Methods
DOPE/CHEMS ± PEG750 or PEG2000; calcein leak vs pH; confocal/flow uptake in mouse GL261 cells (Abstract; Methods). Size <200 nm (Abstract).

## Sample / data
GL261 cell line; in vitro only.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** in vitro formulation + release + cell uptake
- **Population / model:** GL261 murine glioblastoma cells
- **n:** not stated as a single N in the Abstract
- **Intervention / comparator:** non-PEGylated pH-Lip vs pH-Lip-PEG750 vs pH-Lip-PEG2000 vs non-pH-responsive Lip
- **Primary endpoint:** pH-dependent calcein release; cellular uptake
- **Primary result:** No calcein release at pH 7.4 from any tested formulation. PEG2000 liposomes released significantly less calcein at acidic pH than pH-Lip or pH-Lip-PEG750 (Results, Figure 1). PEG2000 reduced uptake in GL261 vs non-PEGylated; PEG length differences in uptake described as negligible in the Abstract.
- **Cannot show:** in vivo GBM efficacy

## Key findings
- Entrapment of calcein was low (0.2–0.5% range) (Results).
- Longer PEG compromised pH-responsiveness (Results).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Calcein, not a drug; 2D GL261 cultures.

## Relevance to our research question
Experimental confirmation of Mahmoudzadeh’s PEG vs trigger trade-off, with PEG *length* as a variable.
