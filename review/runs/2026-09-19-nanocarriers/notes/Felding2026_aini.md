# A design of experiments study of nasal spray deposition in the Alberta idealized nasal inlet

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Felding MS, Cubbin C, Moser C, Ajalloueian F, Nielsen LH. Drug Deliv Transl Res. 2026;16:3015-3025. doi:10.1007/s13346-026-02147-0
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-19-nanocarriers/Felding2026_aini.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Included (device/inlet constraint for nasal delivery)

## Research question
How do actuation angle, inspiratory flow, and temperature — and their interactions — change regional deposition of Otrivin® in the Alberta Idealized Nasal Inlet?

## Methods
DoE in AINI; cone angles 30/45/60°; flows 0, 7.5, 15, 29 L/min; 21 vs 35 °C; Brij coating considered (Methods; Abstract).

## Sample / data
In vitro anatomical inlet; commercial oxymetazoline spray (Otrivin®). Not a nanocarrier.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** in vitro DoE deposition study
- **Population / model:** Alberta Idealized Nasal Inlet; Otrivin®
- **n:** not reported as a single N of human subjects (in vitro repeats; figures report mean ± SEM)
- **Intervention / comparator:** angle, flow, and temperature combinations
- **Primary endpoint:** regional deposition (turbinate vs vestibule vs nasopharynx)
- **Primary result:** administration angle and temperature had significant main effects; airflow×angle and temperature×airflow interactions were significant; 30° gave the highest turbinate deposition among angles tested (Abstract; Results).
- **Cannot show:** liposome or polymersome deposition; human PK

## Key findings
- Authors argue physiologically relevant temperature should be used in AINI testing (Abstract).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Single commercial product; idealized geometry.

## Relevance to our research question
Places a hard device constraint on any nasal nanocarrier spray (including CRS polymersome concepts in the seed set).
