# Stimuli-responsive lipid vesicles and antibiotic–metal combinations: a narrative review of release triggers, stealth coatings, and mucosal constraints

## Abstract

A liposome that reaches a tumour or an infected mucosa still has to empty its cargo. Long-circulating vesicles exploit leaky vasculature, yet they can remain intact in the wrong compartment, trapped in endosomes or stuck in extracellular polymer. Stimulus-sensitive bilayers try to solve that emptying problem with local acid, enzymes, redox chemistry, heat, light, or ultrasound. The same polymer that hides vesicles from clearance, polyethylene glycol (PEG), can also pin a pH-sensitive membrane in the lamellar state and cut cellular uptake. Outside oncology, silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) restore or sharpen antibiotic activity in vitro, including against vancomycin-resistant enterococci and carbapenem-resistant Pseudomonas, but those particles are not liposomes and their human dose window is not established here. Nasal sprays add a third constraint: deposition in an idealized inlet depends on actuation angle, airflow, and temperature even for a simple commercial mist. This review reads original formulation papers and recent open-access reviews together. It treats PEG length, pectin coats, and AgNP–antibiotic checkerboards as design facts, not as a single clinical platform. A PEGylated thermosensitive doxorubicin liposome that reached phase III did not meet its primary progression-free survival endpoint in hepatocellular carcinoma when paired with ablation. Biofilm nanocarrier reviews still describe matrix barriers of orders of magnitude; a 2026 polymersome paper named in the seed set could not be read as a full text.

## Introduction

A phospholipid bilayer in water closes on itself. The resulting vesicle, a liposome, can trap water-soluble drugs in its interior and lipophilic drugs in the membrane. That geometry is why liposomes entered cancer chemotherapy and anti-infective practice decades ago: they change distribution without changing the covalent structure of the active molecule. They do not, by themselves, decide when the molecule should leave.

Tumours and infected tissues are not well-stirred tanks. Solid tumours often have irregular endothelium and poor lymphatic drainage, so macromolecules and nanoparticles can accumulate by the enhanced permeability and retention effect. The same vesicles that benefit from that leakiness are frequently PEGylated so that they survive long enough in blood to use it. The cost of that stealth layer is a vesicle that may sit in the interstitium or in an endosome without releasing enough drug to matter. Endosomes acidify as they mature. Many tumours have a mildly acidic extracellular space because of glycolytic metabolism. Those two acidities are not the same compartment, but both have been used as chemical cues to crack a bilayer.

A bilayer made largely of phosphatidylcholine is relatively cylindrical in molecular shape and prefers a flat lamellar phase. Dioleoylphosphatidylethanolamine (DOPE) is more cone-shaped. Alone, it prefers an inverted hexagonal phase that does not hold an aqueous core. Mixing DOPE with a titratable lipid such as cholesteryl hemisuccinate keeps the membrane lamellar at plasma pH, when the succinate is ionized and hydrated, and allows hexagonal-leaning disruption when the headgroup is protonated. That is the physical idea behind a large family of pH-sensitive liposomes. Heat-sensitive designs use a different trick: a bilayer whose gel-to-fluid transition sits a few degrees above 37 °C, sometimes with lysolipids that stabilize pores at the transition. Light, ultrasound, and magnetic fields add energy from outside the body. Enzymes and glutathione-rich cytosol supply chemical scissors from inside it.

PEG is a flexible, well-hydrated polymer. When a small mole fraction of PEG-lipid is mixed into a vesicle, the chains extend into water and reduce protein adsorption and macrophage uptake. The same chains can hydrogen-bond into a DOPE-rich membrane, increase area per lipid, and oppose the compaction that acid-triggered hexagonal transition wants. Whether that opposition is a fatal design error or a tunable length effect is an experimental question, not a slogan. Short PEG and long PEG are not equivalent steric objects.

Infection adds a matrix that tumours do not always have. Bacteria in a biofilm secrete polysaccharides, proteins, and DNA that slow antibiotic diffusion and change metabolism so that minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values measured on planktonic cultures understate the dose needed at the site. Silver ions and AgNPs damage membranes and DNA with little target specificity. Combining them with a conventional antibiotic is therefore a bet on synergy or restored susceptibility, not on a single receptor. Colistin and vancomycin are chemically unlike aminoglycosides; a fractional inhibitory concentration that is synergistic for one pair does not transfer to another without measurement.

Mucosal delivery, especially the nose, is a different geography again. The respiratory epithelium of the turbinates is the usual target for local uptake. An in vitro inlet that copies average adult geometry can rank sprays, but only if the experiment admits that angle, sniff, and temperature interact. A nanocarrier that is perfect in a vial can still miss the turbinate if the plume hits the vestibule.

The papers in this sample were assembled from user-supplied seeds on liposomes, AgNP–antibiotic combinations, pectin coats, and nasal deposition, then expanded with 2021–2026 peer-reviewed open-access journal articles on those same constructs. The aim is to state what those full texts actually measured about release triggers, stealth coatings, polymer coats, metal–antibiotic combinations, and inlet deposition, and to separate that from what they cannot decide about clinical use.

## Methods

This is a narrative review of retrieved full texts, not a meta-analysis. Seed titles named liposomes (stimuli-responsive, pectin-coated, pH-sensitive, sertraline-loaded), AgNP–colistin and AgNP–vancomycin work, a nasal-inlet spray study, and a polymersome biofilm paper. Related search used OpenAlex records limited to open-access journal articles from 2021 through 2026, ranked by relevance, then title-screened for liposomes, polymersomes, AgNPs, pectin, or nasal inlets. Inclusion required a retrieved full text. The polymersome seed was not retrieved as a PDF and is not used as a results source. An Asian Journal of Pharmaceutics 2020 paper on anionic sertraline liposomes was used where the 2020 Journal of Drug Delivery Science and Technology PDF was not public. After the first table, extra retrieval targeted clinical thermosensitive doxorubicin and biofilm nanocarrier context.

### Stimulus-responsive liposomes as a class

Inefficient release at the target and endosomal trapping of long-circulating vesicles are the problems that stimulus-responsive liposomes were invented to meet [3], [8]. External cues in the 2017 map are hyperthermia, magnetic fields, light, and ultrasound; internal cues are pH, reduction, and enzymes [3]. A 2017 methods review covering 2008–2016 makes the same mechanical claim in different words: release is local bilayer defect and destabilization, not a new covalent prodrug in most designs [8]. By 2025 the endogenous list had lengthened to include reactive oxygen species and hypoxia, and dual- or multi-responsive vesicles were offered as a way to raise precision [9]. A colorectal-focused review applies that catalogue to 5-fluorouracil, whose short half-life and resistance profile motivate carriers even though the review reports no new trial [10]. A 2025 lipid-nanocarrier review puts liposomes beside lipid nanoparticles for nucleic acids and states that active targeting remains extremely hard to translate even when the chemistry looks specific [11].

None of those reviews replaces a measurement of leak, uptake, or tumour volume. They do justify reading later PEG and coating papers as tests of a known trade-off rather than as isolated recipes.

### PEG versus acid-triggered DOPE membranes

Calcein-loaded DOPE–cholesteryl hemisuccinate vesicles have been reported to release 55% of dye at pH 5.5 when they are not PEGylated, 24% with 0.6 mol% DSPE-PEG2000, and 10% with 3 mol% [5]. Those percentages are experimental literature cited inside a molecular dynamics paper, not new wet-lab curves from that paper. The simulations themselves ask why the polymer hurts the trigger. Anionized cholesteryl hemisuccinate hydrates the interface and supports a lamellar DOPE membrane at pH 7.4. Protonation reduces hydrophilicity and area per lipid, a change that, on molecular-shape arguments, favours leaving the lamellar state. PEG chains penetrate the DOPE bilayer, raise area per lipid, and hydrogen-bond in ways that phosphatidylcholine membranes are said not to support as readily, because phosphatidylcholine lacks hydrogen-bond donors [5].

Cell experiments with the same lipid idea show that PEG length is not a binary switch. DOPE:CHEMS vesicles below 200 nm held calcein at pH 7.4 in every formulation tested [14]. Insertion of PEG2000 cut acid-triggered leak relative to PEG750 or unPEGylated pH-responsive vesicles, which matches the lamellar-stabilization story [14]. Uptake of calcein in GL261 glioblastoma cells fell when PEG2000 was present; differences between PEG lengths were described as negligible for uptake in the paper’s abstract [14]. Entrapment of calcein was low (about 0.2–0.5%), so the study is a mechanistic dye study, not a glioblastoma drug product [14].

A doxorubicin version of pH-sensitive vesicles was taken into 4T1 tumour-bearing mice. Non-PEGylated and PEGylated particles were similar in size (on the order of 140 nm), polydispersity, and encapsulation (above 90%) [15]. Cellular uptake favoured the non-PEGylated doxorubicin liposomes. In mice (n=7 per group), those non-PEGylated vesicles inhibited tumour growth by 60.4% and left no histologic lung metastases in that arm, which the authors read as a reason not to add PEG by default [15]. That in vivo result does not contradict the need for PEG in long-circulating commercial products; it says that in this pH-sensitive doxorubicin design, stealth cost uptake and efficacy in 4T1.

Heat-triggered vesicles that use lysolipids and a sharp melting transition are a different membrane. A 2023 review of liposomal doxorubicin records that lyso-thermosensitive liposomal doxorubicin (DPPC:MSPC:DSPE-PEG2000 86:10:4), given at 50 mg/m2 fifteen minutes before radiofrequency ablation, failed the primary progression-free survival endpoint of the HEAT phase III trial versus ablation alone in 3–7 cm hepatocellular carcinoma; overall survival was similar in the main analysis [18]. A subgroup with ablation lasting at least 45 minutes had better overall survival [18]. Phase I work in 24 patients had shown a dose–response on time to treatment failure and allowed a jump to phase III [18]. The clinical message is therefore not that thermosensitive liposomes cannot be made. It is that a PEGylated heat-triggered doxorubicin product plus ablation did not win its pre-specified progression-free survival comparison.

### Polymer coats, charge, and the gastrointestinal story

Pectin is an anionic polysaccharide used as a coat, not as a bilayer lipid. Cationic CAF04 liposomes (DDA:MMG) were coated in a microfluidic chip with low-methoxyl, amidated, or high-methoxyl pectin [1]. A pectin/liposome weight ratio of 0.7 and a 2:1 pectin:liposome flow-rate ratio were selected as optimal [1]. Uncoated particles remained near 220 nm through the chip; coated particles were at least threefold larger, with strongly negative zeta potentials for low-methoxyl types (−38 to −40 mV) and a less negative value for high-methoxyl pectin (−18 ± 3.68 mV) [1]. Microscopy was interpreted as a pectin network with liposomes inside or attached, and the coat was reported as stable on storage [1]. The paper does not measure intestinal survival; it measures process control.

A 2023 coating review places pectin with chitosan and alginate among materials used to protect liposomes in the gastrointestinal tract and to alter absorption [12]. That review mixes food and pharmaceutical examples and does not replace Lutta’s microfluidic numbers [12]. Together they support a narrow claim: polymer coats are a standard answer to fragile phospholipid membranes, and microfluidics can impose a reproducible pectin coat on a cationic adjuvant vesicle.

Anionic sertraline hydrochloride liposomes made by film hydration are a charge example outside infection and cancer. Mean size was 154.10 nm and entrapment 69.46% ± 1, with Higuchi kinetics [7]. Rats received 5 mg/kg in a chronic unpredictable mild stress protocol; the authors report behavioural differences versus a marketed tablet [7]. Group n is not stated in the extracted methods, and the direction of the immobility readout should be read against the paper’s scoring rules before anyone treats it as a definitive antidepressant assay [7]. Human pharmacokinetics from a related 2020 journal article were not in the public file used here.

### Silver nanoparticles with antibiotics

Colistin conjugated to AgNPs (optimized formulation F3) had 11.55 ± 0.93% drug loading and a strongly negative zeta potential [2]. Against E. coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa indicator strains, Col-AgNP MIC/MBC values sat in a 4–16 µg/mL band, with F3 highlighted at 4 µg/mL, while unconjugated AgNPs were much less potent (64 µg/mL bacteriostatic and 128 µg/mL bactericidal in the comparison given) [2]. Free colistin remained more potent on MIC (1 µg/mL) [2]. Compatibility with red cells and renal cells held to 16 µg/mL; at 32 µg/mL the conjugate showed higher cell survival than AgNPs or colistin in the authors’ cytotoxicity readout [2]. Mechanism claims include membrane disruption and DNA damage [2]. The honest comparison is therefore conjugate versus AgNPs plus a possible toxicity offset, not a claim that conjugation beats colistin on MIC.

Ten clinical carbapenem-resistant P. aeruginosa isolates, all strong biofilm formers, were tested with green-synthesized AgNPs plus colistin [16]. Colistin MICs ranged from 8 to 128 µg/mL; AgNP MICs from 0.07 to 37.5 µg/mL [16]. Checkerboards were synergistic or additive for every isolate [16]. Quorum-sensing gene expression fell in ATCC 27853 and isolate 354 under synergistic conditions, and scanning electron microscopy was used to support biofilm inhibition [16]. Murine L929 fibroblasts had an AgNP IC50 of 75 µg/mL and still more than 50% viability at 37.5 µg/mL [16]. These particles are not the same as Muenraya’s chemically conjugated Col-AgNPs, so the two papers agree on the usefulness of combining silver with colistin, not on a shared pharmaceutical identity.

Vancomycin plus 10 nm AgNPs is a Gram-positive, cell-wall story. Against vancomycin-resistant E. faecium, AgNPs or rhodamine-labelled AgNPs alone had MIC 13.5 mg L−1 and vancomycin alone 512 mg L−1; the combination dropped to 3.38 plus 2 mg L−1, FIC 0.25 [4]. Imaging was interpreted as oxidative weakening of the wall plus intracellular particles deforming DNA [4]. That FIC is strong synergy on the usual ≤0.5 rule, in vitro.

Aminoglycosides plus AgNPs close a loop toward tobramycin without reading the missing polymersome paper. Across MDR strains, AgNPs cut the amikacin MIC about 22-fold [17]. In E. coli DH5α the AgNP MIC was about 3 µg/mL with an MBC/MIC of 2 in the reported experiment [17]. Host assays in that paper (mammalian cells, C. elegans lifespan, proteotoxicity) were negative in the authors’ hands [17]. Fractional inhibitory concentration arithmetic is defined in the methods and should be applied per pair; it is not a licence to assume colistin-level synergy for amikacin or the reverse.

A 2021 review of antibiotic liposomes reminds the reader that some lipid antibiotic products already exist in practice and that encapsulation can raise bioavailability and cut peripheral degradation [13]. Liposomes and AgNPs are parallel tactics against resistance, not interchangeable objects [13].

### Nasal inlet physics and biofilm geography

Turbinate deposition of Otrivin in the Alberta Idealized Nasal Inlet depended on actuation angle and temperature, with interactions between airflow and angle and between temperature and airflow [6]. Flows of 0, 7.5, 15, and 29 L/min were used to span breath-hold to heavy breathing [6]. Among 30°, 45°, and 60°, 30° favoured the turbinate region [6]. The authors argue that physiologically relevant temperature belongs in the in vitro protocol [6]. The product is a conventional nasal spray, not a liposome. The result still constrains any future nasal nanocarrier: if angle and heat change where a simple mist lands, a 100 nm vesicle spray cannot be assumed to match a beaker experiment.

A lung-biofilm nanocarrier review states that the biofilm phenotype can increase antibiotic tolerance by as much as about 1000-fold, and that reaching the organ is not the same as penetrating extracellular polymer [19]. Inhalation is discussed as a local route [19]. That is the interpretation gap left by a 2026 polymersome paper on tobramycin and doripenem in chronic rhinosinusitis that could not be opened as a PDF: the problem is real, the specific vesicle result is unread.

### How to read a pH number on a liposome paper

Plasma pH is near 7.4. Early endosomes are often near 6.5 and late endosomes and lysosomes lower, commonly discussed around 5–5.5 in liposome papers. Extracellular tumour pH is often only mildly acidic, sometimes near 6.5–6.8, and is heterogeneous. A vesicle tuned to dump dye at pH 5.5 in a cuvette is being asked to mimic endosomal escape more than extracellular tumour acid, unless the authors say otherwise. DOPE/CHEMS systems in this sample are discussed in that endosomal register [5], [14]. Heat-sensitive lysolipid systems in the doxorubicin review are discussed in a locoregional hyperthermia or ablation register [18]. Mixing those two pH and temperature stories into one “smart liposome” efficacy number would be a category error.

Area per lipid is not a clinical endpoint. It is a simulation or monolayer observable that predicts whether a cone-shaped lipid will stay in a bilayer. When PEG increases area per lipid in DOPE, the simulation is offering a reason that hexagonal transition is delayed [5]. When PEG2000 reduces calcein leak at acid pH, the cell-free fluorescence experiment is the corresponding wet observation [14]. When PEG reduces doxorubicin uptake and 4T1 efficacy, the mouse experiment is a third, still not human, observation [15]. The chain of inference is mechanistic, not regulatory.

### What a fractional inhibitory concentration is

Checkerboard assays dilute two agents together. For each, the MIC in combination is divided by the MIC alone; the sum is the FIC index. Values at or below 0.5 are commonly called synergy, values above 4 antagonism, and the middle band additive or indifferent depending on the paper’s cut-offs [16], [17]. Valkova’s 0.25 against VRE is synergy on that rule [4]. Azimzadeh’s “all synergistic or additive” means the panel was not antagonistic, not that every isolate was 0.25 [16]. Dove’s 22-fold amikacin shift is a fold-drop in MIC, which is related to but not identical to an FIC index [17]. Reporting all three as one “AgNP synergy effect size” would hide those definitions.

Silver is not inert at high concentration. L929 viability falling to 50% at 75 µg/mL AgNPs is a cell-line IC50, not a human no-effect level [16]. Muenraya’s hemolysis rising at 32 µg/mL is a blood-compatibility warning at a concentration above the 16 µg/mL “safe” band they emphasize [2]. Those numbers matter because any nasal or wound use would have to sit below host damage while still exceeding biofilm-effective levels, which this sample does not measure in humans.

### Microfluidics as a manufacturing claim

Batch coating of liposomes with polymer can be sensitive to mixing order and local concentration. A chip with defined flow-rate ratios converts those accidents into parameters [1]. FRR 2:1 producing a 0.7 weight ratio is a process identity, not a biological dose [1]. PDI remaining near 0.2–0.3 while diameter jumps from 220 nm to about 1 µm means the object in suspension is no longer a small unilamellar vesicle even if liposomes still exist inside a pectin mesh [1]. Downstream filtration, spraying, or injection would see a different aerodynamic or pharmacokinetic object than uncoated CAF04. The paper is honest in being a technical note.

### Why nasal temperature is not a luxury variable

Nasal air is heated and humidified in the turbinates. A spray droplet’s viscosity, evaporation, and bounce depend on temperature. A design of experiments that crosses 21 °C and 35 °C and finds a temperature main effect plus a temperature–airflow interaction is saying that room-temperature inlets can rank products wrongly [6]. Brij coating to reduce bounce is a second in vitro choice that tries to mimic mucus without being mucus [6]. None of that validates a liposomal nasal spray; it sets a test standard that such a spray should meet.

### Lipid nanoparticles are not liposomes

Cheng’s review is included because cancer papers often blur lipid vesicles. Liposomes have an aqueous core bounded by one or more bilayers. Lipid nanoparticles used for nucleic acids are typically ionizable-lipid cores without that same interior water space [11]. Stimuli-responsive release language does not transfer unchanged from a DOPE/CHEMS liposome to an mRNA lipid nanoparticle. The shared lesson is only that clinical translation of targeting ligands has been slower than formulation papers imply [11].


### Endosomal escape is a timing problem

A vesicle taken up by endocytosis has minutes to hours before lysosomal hydrolases degrade a peptide cargo. pH-sensitive DOPE membranes are an attempt to use that clock. If PEG delays hexagonal transition until after the vesicle is destroyed, the design fails even if plasma half-life looks excellent. Rustad’s lack of leak at pH 7.4 is the safety half of that clock; reduced leak at acid pH with PEG2000 is the failure half [14].

### Calcein is not doxorubicin

Calcein is a fluorescent marker used because self-quenching reports release. Doxorubicin is a topoisomerase-targeting anthracycline with its own membrane interactions and fluorescence. Nunes’s 60.4% tumour-growth inhibition is a doxorubicin result [15]. Mahmoudzadeh’s 55/24/10% figures are calcein results cited from earlier work [5]. They support the same PEG-versus-trigger hypothesis at different distances from a patient.

### Indicator strains versus clinical isolates

TISTR collection strains in the colistin-conjugate paper are reproducible tools [2]. Burn-unit P. aeruginosa with colistin MIC 128 µg/mL are a different distribution [16]. CDC AR Bank organisms with named resistance genes are a third [17]. Synergy that appears in all three settings is more convincing than synergy in one, but the AgNP cores still differ (chemically conjugated, green-synthesized, commercial antimicrobial AgNPs).

### Charge signs

CAF04 starts cationic so that pectin can adsorb [1]. Sertraline liposomes are made anionic [7]. AgNPs in several papers are negatively charged at the measured zeta [2], [16]. Cationic polymersomes, if that is what the unread CRS paper used, would be a fourth charge story. Zeta potential is not a therapeutic class.

### Approved lipid products are a low bar and a high bar

Ferreira’s table of lipid anti-infectives and vaccines shows that liposomes can be products [13]. HEAT shows that a sophisticated thermosensitive doxorubicin liposome can still miss a primary endpoint [18]. Both belong in the same discussion: manufacturing success is not the same as a positive phase III on the endpoint that was written down.

### What dual-responsive means

Torres describes vesicles that wait for more than one cue [9]. Dual-responsive designs can reduce premature leak if both cues are required, or they can add manufacturing failure modes. This sample contains no original dual-responsive experiment, so the claim stays at review level [9].

### 5-fluorouracil as a hydrophilic cargo

Alrbyawi’s review exists because 5-fluorouracil is water-soluble, short-lived in plasma, and still a backbone of colorectal regimens [10]. Hydrophilic cargo is the interior-core use case for liposomes. It is also the use case that most needs a trigger, because a stable bilayer will otherwise hold the drug through the wrong tissues. No 5-fluorouracil mouse number from that review is treated as a new finding here [10].

### Storage stability is not in vivo stability

Lutta reports that pectin coats stayed put on storage [1]. Pasarin reviews hydrolysis, oxidation, and gastrointestinal insult as separate threats [12]. A coat that survives a shelf can still peel in bile. The next experiment, already implied by related pectin–liposome work outside this table, is simulated intestinal fluid plus a mucin binding assay, which this particular microfluidic paper does not run [1].

### Hemolysis percentages

Muenraya gives 11.61 ± 0.84% hemolysis for Col-AgNPs at 32 µg/mL versus a higher value for AgNPs in the same sentence structure [2]. Percent hemolysis is a continuous safety metric. It does not replace renal histopathology. The paper’s own motivation was colistin nephrotoxicity; the conjugate’s renal-cell viability advantage at 32 µg/mL is a cell-line hint, not a kidney study [2].

### Imaging artefacts

Rhodamine-labelled AgNPs and fluorescent vancomycin are necessary for Valkova’s microscopy and might change MIC slightly; the paper reports labelled and unlabelled silver MICs as matched at 13.5 mg L−1 against VRE [4]. That matching is the reason the imaging particles can be discussed as relevant to the synergy number.

### Radiofrequency duration as a hidden dose

Ablation that lasts 45 minutes is not the same energy deposition as a shorter burn. The HEAT subgroup that favoured lyso-thermosensitive doxorubicin used that duration cut [18]. Future thermosensitive trials that ignore time-at-temperature will repeat an endpoint that mixes drug release physics with surgical technique. The 24-patient phase I study that justified a phase III jump measured time until treatment failure against dose, which is a different endpoint from phase III progression-free survival [18]. Changing endpoint and widening tumour-size eligibility at the same time makes a missed primary result hard to invert in a narrative.

### What this sample still cannot pool

There is no shared primary endpoint across Table 1. Size in nanometres, percent calcein leak, percent tumour-growth inhibition, FIC, and turbinate deposition fraction do not go into one forest plot. The correct synthesis is a set of design rules: if the membrane is DOPE/CHEMS and the cue is endosomal acid, test PEG as a continuous factor [5], [14], [15]; if the coat is pectin, report weight ratio and flow ratio [1]; if the partner is an antibiotic, report FIC and a host IC50 [2], [4], [16]; if the route is nasal, fix inlet temperature [6]; if the cue is heat plus ablation, pre-specify duration [18].

The seed set also asked for a polymersome that releases tobramycin under acid and holds doripenem in an inert partner vesicle. University abstracts describe ~100 nm imidazole-functionalized particles, alginate-binding in quartz-crystal experiments, nasal-epithelial tolerance, and strong empty-particle effects on Pseudomonas biofilms under flow. None of those sentences is a substitute for a methods section, a figure of live-dead biomass, or a dose. Until a PDF that starts with the PDF header is in hand, the only biofilm-delivery measurements in this review are Guo’s secondary account of lung matrix barriers and Azimzadeh’s clinical-isolate plates [16], [19]. Empty charged nanoparticles that look antimicrobial are a known confounder; any later polymersome read must keep unloaded controls.

### Cryo-electron microscopy and the 140 nm object

Nunes reports similar morphology for PEGylated and non-PEGylated doxorubicin vesicles on cryo-electron microscopy, with dynamic light scattering diameters near 140 nm and polydispersity below 0.2 [15]. That similarity is useful: efficacy differences in 4T1 mice are then more likely to come from surface PEG and uptake than from a size-bin change [15]. Lutta’s pectin objects, by contrast, leave the 200 nm bin entirely [1]. Comparing those two “liposome” diameters as if they were the same pharmaceutical species would confuse a polymer mesh with a unilamellar vesicle.

## Discussion

Charge, polymer length, and inlet temperature are three orthogonal knobs. Papers that turn only one knob should not be averaged with papers that turn another. The retrieved experimental papers are enough to name those knobs and to show that a phase III thermosensitive doxorubicin study can still fail its primary endpoint [18]. They are not enough to pick a single winning vesicle for cancer and infection together. A fair next experiment names the knob it turns, reports n, and keeps an empty-carrier control when the particle itself can kill bacteria. Those controls belong in the same figure as the loaded vesicle. Empty AgNPs, empty pectin meshes, and empty polymersomes are not interchangeable blanks; each can change MIC or biomass on its own.

The sample agrees on a negative statement: accumulation is not efficacy. Heidarli and Lee wrote that statement for cancer liposomes [3], [8]. Torres and Cheng repeat it with newer dual-trigger and lipid-nanoparticle vocabulary [9], [11]. The original papers then disagree productively about the next move.

For pH-sensitive DOPE membranes, PEG is a tax on the trigger and on uptake [5], [14], [15]. For lyso-thermosensitive doxorubicin, PEG is part of a product that was tested in phase III and did not meet progression-free survival against ablation alone [18]. Those facts can both be true because the membranes, the drugs, and the clinical add-on (ablation duration) differ. A review that concluded “never PEG” or “always PEG” would be ignoring Table 1 and Table 2 at once.

Pectin coating is not a stimulus. It is a change of surface charge and hydrodynamic size aimed at mucosa and the gut [1], [12]. Whether a 1 µm pectin–liposome object still behaves as a liposome in mucus is an open physical question that Lutta’s AFM images pose without answering in animals [1]. Sertraline liposomes show that anionic vesicles are also being explored for central-nervous-system-adjacent drugs, but the OA paper here is behavioural, not a human exposure study [7].

AgNP–antibiotic work is the most quantitative synergy block in the sample, and it is still preclinical microbiology [2], [4], [16], [17]. FIC 0.25 against VRE is a strong in vitro signal [4]. It is not a liposome, not a nasal spray, and not a licensed combination. Colistin conjugates that lose to free colistin on MIC but beat AgNPs and look gentler on cells at 32 µg/mL occupy a different niche: a silver formulation that carries some peptide, not a peptide that needs silver to work at 1 µg/mL [2]. Biofilm gene and MBIC readouts in clinical P. aeruginosa sit closer to the therapeutic problem of chronic infection [16], [19]. Without the Andresen polymersome full text, this review cannot say whether a pH-disassembling polymer vesicle plus tobramycin/doripenem outperforms those AgNP combinations in a sinus-like flow system.

Felding’s inlet study is easy to dismiss as off-topic if the only allowed objects are vesicles. It is on-topic if the research question includes getting a particle to a mucosa [6]. Angle and temperature effects are large enough that a formulation group could “improve” a nanocarrier in a dish and lose the comparison in the nose by holding the bottle wrong or running the inlet cold [6].

Limitations of this review follow the files. Several seeds older than 2021 were kept because they were the starting set. Related expansion used journal open-access records from 2021–2026 and therefore missed paywalled clinical papers that may exist. HTML was used where a publisher PDF was blocked for two seed reviews and the molecular dynamics paper; claims were restricted to passages that survived that conversion. One misidentified journal PDF was discarded and replaced. A fetch that returned an unrelated susceptibility-table file instead of the vancomycin–AgNP paper was discarded. This is a narrative reading of the retrieved full texts, not a systematic review of every smart-liposome or AgNP paper published in that window.

What to measure next is concrete. First, retrieve and extract the polymersome CRS paper or an equivalent pH-responsive vesicle in a sinus biofilm flow model, with empty-particle controls, because blank charged vesicles can look active [19]. Second, put a PEG titration (0, 0.6, 3 mol%, and a short PEG) on the same DOPE/CHEMS doxorubicin recipe in one mouse model so Mahmoudzadeh, Rustad, and Nunes share an endpoint [5], [14], [15]. Third, run AINI deposition on an actual liposomal or polymersomal spray at 35 °C, not only on Otrivin [6]. Fourth, for AgNP–antibiotic pairs, report host IC50 beside FIC on the same page and include a biofilm MBIC, not only planktonic MIC [2], [4], [16]. Fifth, any thermosensitive liposome claim should pre-specify ablation or hyperthermia duration, because HEAT’s subgroup hinged on that clock [18].

## Conclusions

Stimulus-responsive liposomes exist to empty a vesicle where long circulation left it intact. In DOPE membranes that use acid as the cue, PEG can stabilize the lamellar state, reduce dye leak, reduce uptake, and, in one 4T1 doxorubicin study, reduce antitumour effect when it is added [5], [14], [15]. Polymer coats such as pectin change size and charge in a controllable microfluidic process but have not been shown in this sample to improve a clinical endpoint [1]. AgNPs can synergize with vancomycin, colistin, or aminoglycosides in vitro, with FIC 0.25 as the strongest single index here, yet they remain a different platform from liposomes [2], [4], [16], [17]. Nasal deposition of a simple spray already depends on angle and temperature [6]. A PEGylated thermosensitive doxorubicin liposome plus ablation did not meet phase III progression-free survival in the HEAT trial as reviewed [18]. The unread polymersome full text leaves chronic mucosal biofilm delivery as an open measurement, not as a completed story [19].
