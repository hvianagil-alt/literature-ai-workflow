# Subcutaneous weekly semaglutide with automated insulin delivery in type 1 diabetes: a double-blind, randomized, crossover trial

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Pasqua, Melissa-Rosina and Tsoukas, Michael A. and Kobayati, Alessandra and Aboznadah, Wedyan and Jafar, Adnan and Haidar, Ahmad, 2025, Nature Medicine. doi:10.1038/s41591-024-03463-z
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa-full/Pasqua20251239.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** included after full text.

## Research question
Does weekly SC semaglutide as adjunct to AID improve time in range vs placebo in adults with T1D?

## Methods
Double-blind randomised crossover; titration 11 weeks then AID 4 weeks; primary TIR 3.9–10.0 mmol·L⁻¹ in last 4 weeks (NCT05205928).

## Sample / data
Adults with T1D using AID; 28 randomised, 24 completers.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** double-blind crossover RCT
- **Population / model:** adults with type 1 diabetes on automated insulin delivery
- **n:** 28 randomised, 24 completers
- **Intervention / comparator:** weekly SC semaglutide (to 1 mg or maximum tolerated) vs placebo
- **Primary endpoint:** time in 3.9–10.0 mmol·L⁻¹ (last 4 weeks of each AID period)
- **Primary result:** +4.8 (SD 7.6) percentage points (74.2% vs 69.4%; P=0.006); TBR not increased; HbA1c −0.5%; two euglycaemic ketosis episodes without acidosis; no DKA or severe hypoglycaemia
- **Cannot show:** oral/pulmonary/microneedle equivalence; CV outcomes; large AID populations

## Key findings
- TIR +4.8 pp vs placebo (P=0.006) without more time <3.9 mmol·L⁻¹.
- Two recurrent euglycaemic ketosis episodes on semaglutide; no DKA or severe hypoglycaemia.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Small n; 4-week AID window after titration; single centre class of evidence.

## Relevance to our research question
Only blinded human AID adjunct trial in the sample.
