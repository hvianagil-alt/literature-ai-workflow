Semaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist, improves glycemic control and weight but is limited by gastrointestinal (GI) side effects, notably nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea, which impair adherence. Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS), a non-invasive therapy, shows potential in alleviating gastrointestinal symptoms, yet its role in mitigating semaglutide- related side effects and enhancing drug efficacy remains unexplored. Methods: A total of 60 T2DM participants experiencing semaglutide-induced GI side effects will be randomly assigned to a control or intervention group. The control group will receive semaglutide (0.25 mg weekly) plus sham-taVNS, with electrodes attached to the bilateral auricular scapha at 4/20 Hz for 30 min, twice daily.
