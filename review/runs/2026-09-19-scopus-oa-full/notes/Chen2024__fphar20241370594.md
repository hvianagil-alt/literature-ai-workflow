# Real-world effectiveness of GLP-1 receptor agonist-based treatment strategies on “time in range” in patients with type 2 diabetes

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Chen, Yongru and Chen, Jingxian and Zhang, Shuo and Zhu, Dan and Deng, Feiying and Zuo, Rui and Hu, Yufei and Zhao, Yue and Duan, Yale and Lin, Benwei and Chen, Fengwu and Liang, Yun and Zheng, Jiaxiong and Khan, Barkat Ali and Hou, Kaijian, 2024, Frontiers in Pharmacology. doi:10.3389/fphar.2024.1370594
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa-full/Chen2024__fphar20241370594.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** included after full text.

## Research question
Is GLP-1 RA-based therapy associated with higher CGM time in range than other oral agents in T2D?

## Methods
Multicentre retrospective CGM study; 1:1 propensity matching.

## Sample / data
People with T2D; 202 propensity-matched patients.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** retrospective observational, propensity-matched
- **Population / model:** type 2 diabetes with CGM
- **n:** 202 matched
- **Intervention / comparator:** GLP-1 RA-based therapy vs other oral antidiabetic drugs
- **Primary endpoint:** time in range at 3–6 months
- **Primary result:** TIR 76.0% vs 65.7% (P<0.001); lower TAR and mean glucose
- **Cannot show:** causal effect; pooling with the AID RCT

## Key findings
- Matched TIR 76.0% vs 65.7% at 3–6 months (P<0.001).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Not randomised; mixed molecules; residual confounding.

## Relevance to our research question
Human TIR in T2D, weaker design than Pasqua.
