# Development of an orally delivered GLP-1 receptor agonist through peptide engineering and drug delivery to treat chronic disease

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Pechenov S, Revell J, Will S, et al. Sci Rep. 2021;11:22521. doi:10.1038/s41598-021-01750-0
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa-full-gapfill/Niu2021_oralglp1.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Gap-fill (G3: oral GLP-1 bioavailability comparator for Jeong). Included after full text.

## Research question
Can a GLP-1 RA be engineered and formulated for oral tablet delivery with measurable bioavailability?

## Methods
Peptide engineering (protease-site substitution; bis-lipidation) plus sodium chenodeoxycholate/propyl gallate permeation enhancement in enteric-coated tablets (MEDI7219).

## Sample / data
Dogs receiving oral tablets; dog model of obesity and insulin resistance. Exact n **not stated** in the extracted abstract.

## Key findings
- Authors report oral-tablet bioavailability of about 6% in dogs.
- They contrast typical oral peptide bioavailability often limited to about 0.4–1% in cited literature.
- Oral tablets were studied in a dog obesity/insulin-resistance model (efficacy numbers not restated from the lead).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Preclinical (dog), not human PK of marketed oral semaglutide.
- Not from the original Scopus export; gap-fill only.

## Relevance to our research question
Independent OA full text on oral GLP-1 RA engineering/bioavailability, used only to contextualize Jeong’s product-literature contrast with oral semaglutide.
