# A Bioequivalence Study of Two Formulations of Oral Semaglutide in Healthy Participants

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Nielsen, Mette Søndergaard and Brøndsted, Lise and Kankam, Martin and Morelli, Gaetano and Nguyen, David and Skjøth, Trine Vang and Patted, Usha Rani and van Hout, Marloes, 2025, Diabetes Therapy. doi:10.1007/s13300-024-01674-8
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa-full/Nielsen2025269.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** included after full text.

## Research question
Are second-generation oral semaglutide tablets bioequivalent to first-generation tablets?

## Methods
Randomised, multicentre, open-label, full-replicate crossover at steady state (NCT05227196); 20 weeks, four 5-week periods.

## Sample / data
Healthy participants; 222 / 201 / 123 in high, middle, and low dose pairs.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** bioequivalence crossover RCT
- **Population / model:** healthy participants
- **n:** 222, 201, and 123 in the three dose pairs
- **Intervention / comparator:** oral semaglutide 2G 1.5/4/9 mg vs 1G 3/7/14 mg
- **Primary endpoint:** AUC0–24h,SS and Cmax at steady state
- **Primary result:** EMA, FDA, and PMDA bioequivalence met at all three dose levels
- **Cannot show:** that oral tablets reproduce AID adjunct TIR

## Key findings
- Bioequivalence criteria met at all three dose levels.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Healthy volunteers; interchangeability not glycaemic superiority vs injection.

## Relevance to our research question
Human oral tablet PK/BE.
