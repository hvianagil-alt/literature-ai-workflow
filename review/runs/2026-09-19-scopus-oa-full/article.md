# GLP-1 receptor agonists in metabolic disease: a narrative review of clinical outcomes, extra-glycaemic biology, and non-injectable delivery

## Abstract

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) mediate the incretin effect. Receptor agonists built on those peptides are licensed for type 2 diabetes (T2D) and obesity, most often as injections. Licensed use leaves three related questions open: whether agonism still improves glycaemia when insulin is already adjusted by an automated delivery system; how far extra-glycaemic findings in liver, bone, muscle, heart, retina, brain, and wounds can be read as clinical effects; and whether oral, pulmonary, or microneedle formulations can approach subcutaneous (SC) exposure.

This narrative review organises the literature around that metabolic problem rather than around stand-alone trials. Human evidence is strongest for glycaemia and for cardiovascular-risk (CV-risk) safety in people already at high risk. A small blinded crossover of adjunct weekly SC agonism during automated insulin delivery (AID) found higher time in range (TIR) without more hypoglycaemia. Evidence is weaker for utilisation, spontaneous reports, and access. Extra-glycaemic work remains largely mechanistic. Non-injectable delivery reports high relative bioavailability (BA) in animals for some pulmonary and oral constructs; those estimates are species- and route-specific and are not human pharmacokinetics (PK).

Designs that look related often cannot be pooled, because they do not share an endpoint. Observational glucose-monitoring studies, tablet bioequivalence, protocols without outcomes, and receptor biology in non-islet tissues answer different questions. Adjunct glycaemic benefit during AID, and pooled CV-risk estimates, do not imply that every extra-glycaemic or delivery finding is a human outcome. Head-to-head human PK of inhaled or microneedle agonists versus SC injection have not been reported.

## Keywords

GLP-1 receptor agonists; semaglutide; liraglutide; oral peptide delivery; microneedles; diabetic complications; extracellular vesicles

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Native incretin hormones

The incretin effect denotes the greater insulin response to oral than to intravenous glucose. It is mediated chiefly by two intestinal peptides: glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP), secreted by K cells, and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), secreted by L cells [15]. Both bind G-protein-coupled receptors on pancreatic β-cells and stimulate insulin in a glucose-dependent manner, which limits hypoglycaemia when the peptides are given without exogenous insulin. GLP-1 also delays gastric emptying and reduces appetite. Circulating native GLP-1 and GIP are short-lived because dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) cleaves them and because the kidney clears intact peptide [15,45]. DPP-4 inhibitors therefore raise endogenous incretin concentrations. Receptor agonists, by contrast, are engineered peptides or, more recently, non-peptide small molecules that occupy the receptor for hours to days.

The same receptors are expressed outside the islet. In Alzheimer disease and Parkinson disease, neuronal insulin and insulin-like growth factor-1 signalling is downregulated early, glucose uptake falls, astrocytes increase ketone supply, amino-acid oxidation rises, and synaptic repair and autophagy fail [33]. GLP-1 and GIP receptors on neurons and glia have therefore been proposed as targets for disease-modifying treatment, and diabetes agonists have been tested in that context [33]. That hypothesis concerns extra-glycaemic receptor occupancy; it does not imply that cardiovascular or glycaemic trials measured cognition.

### 1.2 Licensed analogues and oral products

Once-daily liraglutide, once-weekly semaglutide, and the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist tirzepatide are licensed for obesity in people without diabetes [15]. Across obesity programmes, mean weight loss of about 10–25% of body weight has been reported, with cardiorenal effects that may be partly independent of the kilograms lost; trial programmes now include heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis, obstructive sleep apnoea, osteoarthritis, and peripheral vascular disease [16]. Those ranges summarise a broader literature and are not recomputed from the primary studies reviewed below.

Most licensed products remain injections. Peptide drugs are poorly suited to swallowing: gastrointestinal (GI) proteases degrade them, epithelial permeability is low, and peptide that reaches plasma is rapidly cleared by the kidney [45]. Marketed oral semaglutide is the same peptide used subcutaneously, co-formulated with a permeation enhancer (sodium N-(8-[2-hydroxybenzoyl] amino) caprylate, SNAC, in the first-generation tablet). A second-generation oral tablet (1.5, 4, and 9 mg) met European Medicines Agency, U.S. Food and Drug Administration, and Japanese Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency bioequivalence criteria against the first-generation 3, 7, and 14 mg tablets in healthy participants [4]. Bioequivalence in healthy volunteers establishes interchangeability of tablets. It does not establish that oral semaglutide reproduces the glycaemic gain of weekly injected semaglutide during automated insulin delivery [1].

Orforglipron (LY3502970) is a non-peptide GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) that can be swallowed without SNAC. In two phase 1 food-effect crossovers, fed dosing lowered geometric mean area under the curve and peak concentration by about one-fifth relative to fasting, without a change in half-life or time to peak that the investigators judged clinically meaningful [5]. Small-molecule oral agonists therefore exchange peptide-engineering constraints for ordinary prandial pharmacokinetics (PK). They are not interchangeable with SNAC tablets or with pulmonary or microneedle peptides.

### 1.3 Type 1 diabetes and automated insulin delivery

Type 1 diabetes (T1D) is insulin deficiency after autoimmune β-cell loss and requires insulin replacement. Automated insulin delivery (AID) combines continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), an insulin pump, and an algorithm that adjusts the insulin rate from the glucose trace [1]. Consensus targets treat ≥70% of time in 3.9–10.0 mmol·L⁻¹ as corresponding, approximately, to glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) ≤7%, the usual threshold used to reduce microvascular and macrovascular risk [1]. In large AID trials, 34–53% of participants remained above HbA1c 7%, largely because postprandial glucose is difficult to control with insulin alone [1]. AID does not treat obesity. In the DCCT/EDIC cohort, obesity rose from 1% in 1983–1985 to 31% by 2005 [1]. In usual care, only 20–30% of adults with T1D reach A1c <7%, and randomized evidence for dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists as adjunct to AID remains sparse [12].

A GLP-1 RA in this setting is an adjunct, not a substitute for the pump. The relevant questions are whether delayed gastric emptying, lower glucagon, and reduced appetite still increase time in range (TIR) when the algorithm is already targeting glucose, and whether ketosis appears when carbohydrate intake and insulin both fall. Those questions are answered by a completed double-blind crossover of semaglutide [1] and are posed again, for tirzepatide, by an open-label protocol that has not yet reported outcomes [12]. The two reports should not be collapsed.

### 1.4 Extra-glycaemic organs

GLP-1 receptors are not confined to the islet, which is both a therapeutic opportunity and a source of over-interpretation. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD; often termed metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease, MASLD) affects about 30% of the global population, particularly in obesity and type 2 diabetes (T2D), and ranges from steatosis to steatohepatitis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma [18]. Insulin resistance is shared with T2D, which makes anti-diabetic drugs a rational experiment in fatty liver [18]. Direct action on the hepatocyte is a different question from improvement of liver fat through weight loss and insulin action. At concentrations that stimulate insulin release, liraglutide, acyl-GIP, and the dual agonist MAR709 did not change lipid content in human hepatocytes loaded with oleic and palmitic acid, did not alter TGF-β-activated hepatic stellate-cell behaviour, and did not phosphorylate CREB in those cells [32]. Oral semaglutide lipid nanocapsules have been designed to raise native gut GLP-1 and absorbed analogue in MASLD models [23]. The cell and in-vivo delivery experiments measure different compartments; they are not contradictory.

Muscle loss during GLP-1 RA treatment has been framed as an evolved response to caloric restriction: muscle is a large energy consumer, and the ActRIIA/B ligands GDF8 (myostatin) and activin A reduce muscle mass [41]. Dual blockade of those ligands spared muscle and increased fat loss in obese male mice and non-human primates treated with a GLP-1 RA [41]. In bone, Wnt/β-catenin signalling has been placed between semaglutide and osteoblast programmes in ovariectomized rats and in bone-marrow mesenchymal stem cells [36,37]. GABA_B receptor agonism has been tested as a means of blunting GLP-1 RA chronotropy and post-infarct arrhythmia [39]. Prior studies disagree on whether GLP-1 RAs induce diabetic retinopathy; Mendelian randomization with a systematic review in European-ancestry data [34] and spontaneous-report analysis with retinal endothelial assays [35] generate signals but are not outcomes trials with adjudicated photocoagulation or vitreous haemorrhage.

Chronic diabetic wounds are a complication of hyperglycaemia, neuropathy, and infection, not an incretin endpoint. Adipose-derived mesenchymal stromal cell (MSC) exosomes in a *Spirulina* hydrogel, with or without laser, have been tested in a diabetic rat wound model without a GLP-1 payload [44]. Vesicle products enter this review because they are used in metabolic or tissue-repair settings, not because they are incretin formulations.

### 1.5 Barriers to non-injectable peptide delivery

Oral peptide bioavailability (BA) is often below 1% of the administered dose. Protease-site substitution, bis-lipidation to promote reversible plasma-protein binding, and sodium chenodeoxycholate plus propyl gallate in enteric tablets (MEDI7219) raised oral-tablet BA to about 6% in dogs, compared with a cited 0.4–1% range for many oral peptides, and reduced food intake, body weight, and glucose excursions in a dog model of obesity and insulin resistance [45]. That result is specific to the molecule and the species. It is not human SNAC-tablet PK and is not marketed oral semaglutide.

Pulmonary delivery requires particles to reach the distal airway, cross mucus, evade macrophages, and then cross epithelium into blood. Among three zinc-oxide morphologies used as inhalable liraglutide carriers, compact spikes (ZO-3, 1–3 μm with ~1.3 μm tips) favoured distal deposition, mucus penetration, and reduced macrophage uptake; relative BA versus SC injection was about 60% in healthy rats and 51% in alloxan-diabetic rats [19]. The comparison with <2% oral BA of marketed oral semaglutide was taken from product literature, not from an oral arm in the same animals [19]. Human inhaled liraglutide PK has not been reported.

Dissolving microneedles avoid both gut and lung. Hyaluronic acid/aminoclay needles carrying a semaglutide–aminoclay nanocomplex (Sema-AC/HA10) had a mechanical strength of 0.37 ± 0.020 N/needle, remained stable at 25 °C, dissolved after insertion, and produced greater systemic exposure than needles loaded with free drug [20]. An adipose-targeted nanocomposite patch co-delivering semaglutide and rosiglitazone was designed on the premise that GLP-1 withdrawal is followed by weight regain, whereas PPAR-γ agonists are limited by a pro-inflammatory adipose milieu [21]. Silk-fibroin nanoparticles loaded with GLP-1 RAs (encapsulation about 28–33%; about 60% released by day 14), decorated with the type II collagen-binding peptide WYRGRL and placed in dissolving microneedles, have been tested in collagen-induced arthritis in rats because articular exposure after systemic dosing is poor [22]. The dissolving-patch chassis is shared; disease, targeting, and endpoints are not.

Enzymatic degradation and frequent dosing likewise constrain insulin. Poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) microspheres have been proposed for sustained or glucose-responsive insulin release [29]. Sodium caprate (C10) mini-tablets have been compared with liquid enhancer for intestinal insulin absorption in rats [27], and the acylation side chain of insulin icodec has been modified to produce a weekly candidate [30]. Those studies belong to peptide-delivery pharmacology. They do not measure GLP-1 RA TIR.

### 1.6 Public interest

Worldwide search interest in semaglutide has tracked social and print media during periods of off-label weight-loss use and supply shortage [42]. That observation does not estimate treatment effect. It does indicate that utilisation, spontaneous-report, and access studies describe a pattern of use that extends beyond labelled trial populations [6,7,9,10].

### 1.7 Aim of this review

This review examines recent human evidence for GLP-1 RAs on glycaemia (including adjunct use with AID), cardiovascular-risk (CV-risk) safety, and utilisation; preclinical and early clinical evidence for non-injectable exposure, by species and route; and extra-glycaemic biology at the level of cells, animals, or people. Eligible work addressed a GLP-1-related agonist, an MSC or extracellular vesicle (EV) product, or a drug-delivery system in metabolic disease or a closely related inflammatory or tissue-repair setting. Studies that do not share an endpoint are not pooled. Narrative reviews are used as background, not as primary effect sizes. Protocols are identified as protocols.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Search and sources

Recent publications (principally 2024–2026) on GLP-1-related agonists, MSC or EV products, and drug-delivery systems in metabolic or related repair settings were identified from a bibliographic database search. An additional full text on oral GLP-1 tablet engineering (MEDI7219) was included to interpret oral peptide BA after an inhalation study cited product-literature oral figures [19,45].

### 2.2 Eligibility

Reports were eligible if they addressed at least one of a GLP-1 or related incretin agonist; MSCs or EVs as therapy or carrier; or a drug-delivery system, in metabolic disease or a closely related inflammatory or tissue-repair setting. Narrative reviews were retained as background. Trial protocols were retained and labelled as protocols. Titles without those terms or without a metabolic or related setting were excluded. Reports for which a complete public full text was not available were not used as evidence.

### 2.3 Study selection

Forty-four full texts met the eligibility criteria. Two retrieved files were excluded: an inflammatory-bowel delivery study in which GLP-1 appeared only in passing, and a bibliographic record whose downloaded file was a different article. Where sample size or an effect size was not reported, that absence is stated. No new experimental data were generated.

## 3. Clinical incretin use

### 3.1 Glycaemia with insulin and oral products

Adjunct weekly subcutaneous (SC) semaglutide, titrated over 11 weeks to 1 mg or the maximum tolerated dose, increased time in the 3.9–10.0 mmol·L⁻¹ range during the last 4 weeks of AID by a mean 4.8 (SD 7.6) percentage points versus placebo in adults with T1D (28 randomized, 24 completers; 74.2% [SD 9.7] versus 69.4% [SD 10.4]; *P* = 0.006; NCT05205928) [1]. Time below 3.9 mmol·L⁻¹ (*P* = 0.19) and below 3.0 mmol·L⁻¹ (*P* = 0.65) did not increase; the gain in TIR was accounted for by less hyperglycaemia [1]. HbA1c was 0.5% lower (IQR −0.7 to −0.2; *P* < 0.001) [1]. Diabetic ketoacidosis and severe hypoglycaemia were not observed; two episodes of recurrent euglycaemic ketosis without acidosis occurred during semaglutide [1]. These data show that incretin agonism can still improve glycaemia after insulin delivery has been algorithmically optimized. They do not show that oral, pulmonary, or microneedle semaglutide is equivalent, and they do not estimate cardiovascular outcomes in T2D.

A directional improvement in TIR has also been reported in T2D, under a weaker design. In a multicentre retrospective CGM study, after 1:1 propensity matching of 202 patients, GLP-1 RA-based therapy was associated with TIR of 76.0% versus 65.7% on other oral antidiabetic drugs at 3–6 months (*P* < 0.001), with lower time above range and mean glucose [2]. Mixed molecules, lack of randomization, and residual confounding preclude pooling that observational contrast with the AID crossover [1,2].

Switching from a DPP-4 inhibitor to oral semaglutide is a common clinical sequence, because both act on the same hormone axis. Among 74 Japanese adults with T2D who made that switch, 44 reached HbA1c <7% (52 mmol·mol⁻¹), most at 7 mg (3 mg n=7; 7 mg n=24; 14 mg n=13), and 17 did not [3]. The study is a predictor and titration pilot, not a placebo-controlled efficacy trial.

Tablet interchangeability and prandial PK are separate questions from glycaemic superiority versus injection. In a randomised, multicentre, open-label, full-replicate crossover at steady state (NCT05227196), second-generation oral semaglutide (1.5, 4, and 9 mg) was compared with first-generation 3, 7, and 14 mg tablets over 20 weeks (four 5-week periods) in healthy participants, with 24-hour sampling at the end of each period [4]. Co-primary endpoints were AUC<sub>0–24h,SS</sub> and Cmax at steady state [4]. Recruitment was 222, 201, and 123 participants in the high, middle, and low dose pairs [4]. Bioequivalence criteria of the EMA, FDA, and PMDA were met at all three dose levels, and the safety profile of the second-generation tablet was consistent with the first [4].

Food reduced exposure to the non-peptide agonist orforglipron in two phase 1 crossovers in healthy adults (NCT03929744, NCT05110794) [5]. After a single 3 mg dose (n=12; mean age 45.0 years; 66.7% male), geometric least-squares mean AUC and Cmax were 23.7% and 23.2% lower when fed than when fasted; after multiple 16 mg doses (n=34; mean age 42.8 years; 88.2% male), the corresponding reductions were 17.6% and 20.9% [5]. Half-life and median tmax were comparable; most treatment-emergent events were GI events; serious adverse events and deaths were not reported [5]. The investigators judged the food effect unlikely to produce a meaningful efficacy difference and noted the absence of prandial restrictions as an advantage relative to SNAC tablets [5]. The studies do not compare orforglipron with oral semaglutide on HbA1c.

### 3.2 Utilisation, safety signals, access, and protocols

How oral semaglutide is sequenced in practice, rather than its randomized efficacy, has been described in terms of lines of therapy, time on each line, and reasons for ending a line in T2D; cohort size was not reported [6]. Spontaneous reports in FAERS from 2019 to 2023, after oral semaglutide became available as an alternative to injection-only GLP-1 RAs, identify disproportionality signals but not incidence in a defined denominator [7]. Those signals cannot replace trial-based relative risks [14].

Bariatric surgery remains the most effective treatment for severe obesity, yet insufficient weight loss and weight regain occur in a fraction of patients, and GLP-1 RAs have been evaluated as adjunctive therapy for those outcomes; sample size and effect size were not reported [8]. That indication is distinct from AID adjunct [1] and from primary obesity pharmacotherapy [15,16].

Uptake of SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 RAs among U.S. Medicare beneficiaries with T2D plus heart failure, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, or chronic kidney disease differed by race and ethnicity in 2013–2019 [9]. In low- and middle-income countries, microsimulation of introducing SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 RAs has been used to estimate effects on insulin dose and complications, because those effects are not well described in those health systems [10]. Access and substitution are part of effectiveness; they are not commensurate with TIR or mortality estimates [1,2,14].

Residual insulin secretion is a plausible substrate for an incretin agonist. Two young women with latent autoimmune diabetes of youth (onset 8–29 years, slow progression toward insulin dependence) were treated with oral semaglutide plus basal insulin; one was 20 years old, with BMI 31.8 kg·m⁻², multiple autoantibodies, and residual C-peptide, without ketoacidosis at presentation [11]. Two cases cannot estimate a class effect in LADA or LADY.

Whether a dual agonist adds to AID will be tested in AID-JUNCT, a prospective, randomised, parallel-group, open-label superiority trial of tirzepatide versus standard of care in adults with T1D [12]. Planned enrolment is 42 people aged 18–65 years, with diabetes for at least 12 months, AID for at least three months, A1C 6.5–10%, and BMI ≥23 kg·m⁻², randomised 1:1 to tirzepatide (target 5.0 mg after titration) or standard of care for 16 weeks, with CGM time in 3.9–10.0 mmol·L⁻¹ as the primary endpoint [12]. Until outcomes are reported, dual-agonist adjunct to AID remains unanswered. The completed AID study used semaglutide, a blinded crossover, and a placebo control [1]; those design differences will persist after AID-JUNCT reports.

GI adverse effects limit GLP-1 RA titration. A protocol of transcutaneous auricular vagus-nerve stimulation plus semaglutide in T2D is intended to test both efficacy and those adverse effects [13]. Outcome data have not been reported. The protocol does not establish that the device changes semaglutide’s effect size.

### 3.3 Cardiovascular-risk safety and background reviews

In people at increased CV risk, a systematic review with meta-analysis and Trial Sequential Analysis of randomized placebo-controlled trials of oral or SC semaglutide, searched to 31 March 2025 across six databases, with Cochrane RoB 2 and GRADE, included 50 trials and 54,972 randomized participants (30,281 semaglutide, 24,691 placebo) [14]. Nineteen trials (38%) enrolled people with T2D, 17 (34%) with overweight, 5 (10%) with T2D and chronic kidney disease, 5 (10%) with T2D and overweight, and 4 (8%) other populations [14]. All-cause mortality RR was 0.85 (95% CI 0.79–0.91; *I*² = 0%), myocardial infarction RR 0.77 (0.69–0.85), and serious adverse events RR 0.93 (0.88–0.98) [14]. Non-serious GI events were more common (nausea RR 3.00, 2.63–3.42; vomiting RR 4.12, 3.47–4.90; diarrhoea RR 1.88, 1.68–2.11) [14]. These are the largest pooled human safety estimates among the studies reviewed. They apply to people already at CV risk and to semaglutide versus placebo. They do not describe the safety of inhaled zinc-oxide liraglutide [19], intra-articular microneedle GLP-1 RAs [22], or MSC exosome hydrogels [44].

Background narrative reviews of liraglutide, semaglutide, and tirzepatide for weight reduction in obesity without diabetes, including cost-effectiveness [15], of GLP-1-based obesity pharmacology with an approximate 10–25% weight-loss range [16], of incretin-based therapy in maternal obesity and metabolic syndrome [17], and of anti-diabetic drugs in NAFLD [18] map a larger literature. Pregnancy integrates insulin signalling, lipid metabolism, placental endocrine function, mitochondrial remodelling, and inflammation, and maternal obesity and gestational weight gain are risk factors for childhood overweight [17]. How GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists interact with those adaptations, and with offspring risk, has been discussed on physiological grounds; a pregnancy randomized trial was not reported [17]. None of these reviews should be treated as new primary effect sizes.

## 4. Non-injectable and locally targeted delivery

Pulmonary, microneedle, oral-tablet, and implant constructs are summarised in Table 2. They do not share a species, payload, or endpoint, and they are not interchangeable with the human rows in Table 1.

### 4.1 Lung, skin, and joint

Morphology of an inhalable carrier changes where particles deposit and which cells clear them. Smooth spherical zinc oxide (ZO-1, 5–7 μm), spiky urchin-like ZO-2 (5–8 μm with ~3.5 μm tips), and compact spiky ZO-3 (1–3 μm with ~1.3 μm tips) displayed distinct aerodynamics by particle-image velocimetry; ZO-3 favoured turbulence-driven distal deposition, mucus penetration, and reduced macrophage uptake, prolonging epithelial contact [19]. After intratracheal liraglutide on ZO-3, relative BA versus SC injection was about 60% in healthy rats and 51% in alloxan-diabetic rats [19]. The contrast with <2% oral BA of marketed oral semaglutide was not generated in the same animals [19]. Human inhaled liraglutide PK remain unmeasured.

Transdermal dissolving needles can raise systemic peptide exposure without an injection. A semaglutide–aminoclay nanocomplex formed by electrostatic interaction, mixed with hyaluronic acid and cast in reverse polydimethylsiloxane moulds, yielded Sema-AC/HA10 needles with mechanical strength 0.37 ± 0.020 N/needle, conformational stability of the peptide, physical stability at 25 °C, rapid dissolution after insertion, greater systemic exposure than free-drug needles, and reductions in glucose, HbA1c, lipids, intake, and body weight in rats [20]. Group sizes were not reported [20]. The experiment is a preclinical obesity formulation study, not a human bioequivalence trial.

Local adipose targeting and joint targeting use a similar chassis for different diseases. An adipose-targeted nanocomposite microneedle (LRSG-SG@MN) co-delivers rosiglitazone in lipid droplets to activate PPAR-γ and mitochondrial biogenesis in adipocytes, and semaglutide to shift macrophages from M1 to M2, on the rationale that GLP-1 withdrawal is followed by weight regain and that PPAR-γ agonists are limited by adipose inflammation [21]. Quantitative animal outcomes were not reported [21]. Separately, silk-fibroin nanoparticles carrying GLP-1 RAs (encapsulation about 28–33%; about 60% released by day 14), decorated with WYRGRL and loaded into dissolving microneedles, have been applied to collagen-induced arthritis in rats, with the proposed mechanisms of suppressed chondrocyte ferroptosis via cGAS–STING and M2 polarisation [22]. Pooling pulmonary, adipose, and articular constructs as a single delivery effect would mix alveoli, fat, and synovium.

### 4.2 Oral nanocarriers, permeation enhancers, and peptide engineering

Oral semaglutide-loaded lipid nanocapsules have been given with the dual aim of stimulating native GLP-1 secretion and absorbing encapsulated analogue in MASLD models, in order to produce a larger metabolic and histologic effect than marketed oral semaglutide [23]. That strategy tests whether raising local gut and absorbed analogue can achieve what direct application of incretin agonists to human hepatocytes and stellate cells did not achieve [32]. The two experiments are complementary, not discordant.

Several complementary attacks on intestinal barriers have been reported. Reverse micelles of ethyl lauroyl arginate or docusate were compared with hydrophobic ion pairs of semaglutide and ethyl lauroyl arginate for loading into self-emulsifying systems (droplet size 50–300 nm), with Caco-2 permeability and cytocompatibility as readouts [24]. A liraglutide nanoformulation was designed for coordinated ASBT- and EGFR-mediated intestinal absorption rather than unspecific leak [25]. A modified-release semaglutide formulation (FPR-8) was intended to flatten a wide plasma-concentration range quoted as 5.85–38.14 for unformulated solution over 24 hours in animal PK; the original units were not restated [26]. Human therapeutic BA was not reported for these nanocarrier systems [23–26].

Engineered oral tablets can move peptide BA out of the sub-1% band in a non-rodent species. MEDI7219, combining protease-site substitution, bis-lipidation, and sodium chenodeoxycholate/propyl gallate in enteric tablets, achieved about 6% oral-tablet BA in dogs and reduced food intake, body weight, and glucose excursions in a dog obesity and insulin-resistance model; the number of animals and numeric pharmacodynamic endpoints were not reported [45]. MEDI7219 is not marketed oral semaglutide, and dog BA is not human SNAC-tablet PK. The finding contextualizes product-literature oral figures cited for inhalation work [19]; it does not substitute MEDI7219 into an AID protocol [1].

Permeation enhancement and long-acting peptide engineering have also been applied to insulin and to dual agonists. After intestinal administration in rats, insulin co-formulated with a fixed 26 mg·kg⁻¹ sodium caprate as mini-tablets showed dose-dependent absorption; a 75 IU·kg⁻¹ mini-tablet increased BA 26-fold, with transient mucosal erosion similar to liquid C10 [27]. The payload is insulin, not a GLP-1 RA, but the comparison of solid and liquid enhancer is relevant to oral peptide tablets. A SC implant configured to release exenatide for 84 days (OKV-119) produced detectable plasma exenatide from day 7, concentrations above baseline beyond day 84, and reduced caloric intake and body weight over the first 28 days in four of five purpose-bred cats followed for 112 days [28]. PLGA microspheres have been reviewed as a route to sustained, possibly glucose-responsive insulin in the face of enzymatic degradation and frequent dosing [29]. Modification of the acylation side chain of insulin icodec produced TBE001-A-S033, which prolonged glucose control in ICR and db/db mice as a weekly candidate [30]. An *E. coli* biosynthetic platform inserting D-amino acids and ornithines into tirzepatide was developed because solid-phase synthesis can place non-canonical residues but scales poorly for mutant libraries aimed at oral stability [31]. These studies belong to peptide-delivery and analogue-engineering pharmacology in metabolic disease. They are not clinical GLP-1 RA outcome trials.

## 5. Extra-glycaemic biology

Hepatic, muscle, bone, botanical-receptor, and wound-vesicle experiments are summarised in Table 3. None of those rows is a human glycaemic or cardiovascular outcome.

Direct incretin agonism at insulin-secretory concentrations did not reduce lipid in human hepatocytes after oleic/palmitic acid loading, did not quiet TGF-β-activated hepatic stellate cells, and did not phosphorylate CREB in those cells, whether the ligand was liraglutide, acyl-GIP, or MAR709 [32]. Beneficial effects in people with metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis, if they occur, are therefore more likely to be indirect (body weight, insulin resistance, glycaemia) than a direct hepatocyte or stellate-cell action at those concentrations [32]. Higher local analogue exposure from a delivery system [23] and gut-derived GLP-1 acting on other cell types remain possible.

Neuronal energy failure in Alzheimer disease and Parkinson disease, with downregulated insulin/IGF-1 signalling, reduced glucose utilisation, compensatory ketone and amino-acid use, failing synaptic repair, and chronic inflammation, has been used to argue for repurposed GLP-1 RAs and dual GLP-1/GIP agonists as candidate disease-modifying therapies on the basis of animal studies and clinical trials in that literature [33]. Those CNS trials are not re-analysed here.

Whether GLP-1 RAs induce diabetic retinopathy is disputed. Mendelian randomization combined with a systematic review in European-ancestry data [34] and FAERS analysis of semaglutide plus assays in human retinal microvascular endothelial cells [35] do not replace a dedicated retinopathy outcomes trial. Rapid glucose lowering can worsen retinopathy in the short term with other diabetes drugs; whether that mechanism, a direct retinal GLP-1 effect, or reporting bias explains spontaneous-report signals is not settled [34,35].

Postmenopausal osteoporosis reflects unbalanced resorption and formation after estrogen loss, often with weight gain [36]. Semaglutide has been linked to β-catenin-dependent osteoprotection in ovariectomized rats, via signals in adipose and mesenchymal stem cells [36], and has increased bone-marrow mesenchymal stem cell proliferation (CCK-8, flow cytometry), alkaline phosphatase activity, mineralised nodules, migration, and OCN/RUNX2 mRNA and protein through Wnt/LRP5/β-catenin [37]. Sample size was not reported for the cell study [37]. Both experiments are preclinical and implicate the same pathway.

Semaglutide at 100 μg·kg⁻¹ improved metabolic indices in high-fat-diet C57BL/6J mice, and transfer of faeces or bacteria from treated mice into antibiotic-pretreated recipients was used to test whether microbiota are sufficient for part of the phenotype [38]. Identification of a human strain or a clinical probiotic does not follow from a mouse transfer experiment.

Resting heart-rate rise is a recognised GLP-1 RA effect in people. GABA_B receptor agonism blunted that chronotropy and reduced post-infarct arrhythmogenesis in a preclinical model [39]. Whether GABA_B agonism is a usable clinical countermeasure has not been established.

GI adverse effects that limit titration motivated a report that electroacupuncture raised semaglutide exposure and reduced β-cell apoptosis in a T2D model, with dose-sparing claimed in the title; numerical PK values were not reported [40]. The magnitude of sparing therefore cannot be judged from the available numbers.

Caloric restriction on GLP-1 RAs can cost muscle because muscle is a major energy consumer [41]. Dual blockade of GDF8 and activin A, the principal ActRIIA/B ligands that reduce muscle, prevented muscle loss associated with GLP-1 RAs and increased muscle mass in obese male mice and non-human primates, while fat loss increased [41]. Quality of weight loss is a clinical question; the evidence is mouse and primate. A human body-composition randomized trial of this combination has not been reported.

Search interest in semaglutide has followed media coverage during off-label weight-loss use and shortages [42]. That pattern does not estimate efficacy. It is consistent with the existence of utilisation and spontaneous-report studies of use outside labelled trial populations [6,7].

Botanical signalling through the GLP-1 receptor is not interchangeable with a licensed agonist. *Phyllanthus niruri* niosomes (225.1 ± 19.6 nm; encapsulation 73.5 ± 4.2%) reduced weight, body-mass index, steatosis, and inflammation in high-fat-diet rats (n = 8 per group across seven groups including semaglutide and exendin 9–39 controls); benefit tracked MALAT1/miR-206/GLP-1R signalling and was reversed by the GLP-1 receptor antagonist [43]. Antagonist reversal supports receptor biology. It does not license substitution for semaglutide tablets.

## 6. Mesenchymal stromal cell exosomes in diabetic wounds

Ageing and diabetes make chronic wounds slow to close, particularly when infection and poor perfusion coincide [44]. Transcriptome sequencing of diabetic rat wound tissue showed fewer endothelial cells and M2 macrophages [44]. A hydrogel incorporating chlorophyll derivatives and adipose-derived MSC exosomes (AD-MSC-exo@SP) inhibited biofilm, shifted M2 and angiogenic programmes, and accelerated closure in vitro and in vivo; combination with laser produced the strongest effects, with higher proliferative and angiogenic proteins and less inflammation [44]. Group sizes were not reported [44]. There is no GLP-1 payload. The construct is a vesicle-plus-device strategy for a diabetic complication. It is not an incretin formulation and cannot be combined with pulmonary liraglutide PK [19] or AID adjunct glycaemia [1] as a single class effect.

## 7. Discussion

The human glycaemic result that can be stated with a primary endpoint, a control, and a *P* value for adjunct GLP-1 RA use during AID is limited to one small double-blind crossover (Table 1) [1]. Weekly SC semaglutide increased TIR by cutting hyperglycaemia rather than by exchanging it for hypoglycaemia, moved mean TIR from just under the 70% consensus target to just over it (69.4% to 74.2%), lowered HbA1c by 0.5%, and produced two episodes of euglycaemic ketosis without acidosis [1]. Carbohydrate restriction plus lower insulin can therefore raise ketones even when glucose appears acceptable [1]. In a larger, longer, or less selected AID population the same adjunct might or might not cross the 70% threshold. Oral, pulmonary, and microneedle products have not been shown to reproduce this result.

Observational CGM data in T2D move in the same direction: GLP-1 RA-based therapy was associated with a 10.3 percentage-point higher TIR after matching [2]. A larger observational gap than a 4.8-point randomized difference is expected when confounding and mixed drugs remain, and the two estimates should not be meta-analysed [1,2]. Oral semaglutide after DPP-4 inhibitors achieved HbA1c <7% in a subset of a Japanese pilot, mostly at 7 mg, which informs titration and does not yield a pooled efficacy estimate [3]. Bioequivalence of two oral semaglutide tablet generations in hundreds of healthy participants, by EMA, FDA, and PMDA criteria [4], and an 18–24% food effect on orforglipron exposure in two small phase 1 studies [5], establish that a tablet can be substituted or that a small molecule need not be tied to fasting. They do not establish that oral products reproduce AID adjunct glycaemia [1].

Pooled randomized evidence in people at CV risk associates semaglutide with lower all-cause mortality and myocardial infarction and with more nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea (Table 1) [14]. An *I*² of 0% for mortality is unusually homogeneous and should be read with the restriction that participants were already at CV risk, the comparator was placebo, and routes and diagnoses were mixed [14]. Trial Sequential Analysis and GRADE strengthen the methods of that synthesis. The relative risks cannot be borrowed as a safety envelope for zinc-oxide inhalation [19], silk-fibroin joint microneedles [22], or MSC exosome hydrogels [44]. Spontaneous-report analyses of oral tablets [7] and of retinopathy [35], together with Mendelian randomization of retinopathy [34], flag signals; rapid improvement in glucose can worsen retinopathy in other settings, and mechanism is not settled. Utilisation [6,9], post-bariatric use [8], and microsimulation in low- and middle-income countries [10] show that access and sequencing shape effectiveness. They should not be meta-analysed with TIR or mortality.

Narrative reviews of obesity pharmacotherapy [15,16], pregnancy [17], NAFLD [18], and central incretin biology [33] summarise a larger literature. Treating the stated 10–25% weight-loss range [16] or 30% NAFLD prevalence [18] as results of the primary studies above would double-count. No pregnancy randomized trial was identified; reasoning from metabolic physiology and labelled restrictions is all that is available [17].

Non-injectable delivery is a scatter of routes rather than a pipeline with one readout (Table 2). Pulmonary liraglutide on compact spiky zinc oxide reached roughly half to three-fifths of SC exposure in rats, with morphology controlling aerodynamics and macrophage clearance [19]. That is an inhalation-biology result, not human lung PK, and the oral-semaglutide contrast is product literature [19]. About 6% dog BA for MEDI7219 shows that oral peptide BA can leave the sub-1% band for a different molecule in a different species, with reductions in food intake and glucose excursions in a dog obesity model [45]. Dissolving microneedles for semaglutide [20], adipose-targeted semaglutide/rosiglitazone [21], and articular GLP-1 RA nanoparticles [22] share a patch chassis and almost nothing else. Needle force of 0.37 N and a PK advantage of the nanocomplex over free drug were reported for one obesity formulation [20]; sample sizes were not reported for the adipose-targeted and articular constructs [21,22]. Oral lipid nanocapsules for MASLD [23], self-emulsifying reverse micelles versus ion pairs [24], ASBT/EGFR liraglutide nanoparticles [25], and modified-release FPR-8 [26] attack gut barriers by different mechanisms; human therapeutic BA was not reported. Insulin–C10 mini-tablets increased BA 26-fold in rats at the cost of transient mucosal erosion similar for solid and liquid enhancer [27]. Weekly insulin analogues [30], PLGA insulin microspheres [29], feline exenatide implants with plasma drug beyond day 84 in five cats [28], and biosynthetic tirzepatide mutants [31] belong in the same peptide-delivery discussion and must not be averaged into a GLP-1 delivery effect.

Extra-glycaemic biology is similarly non-fungible (Table 3). Failure of direct incretin agonism to clear hepatocyte lipid or to quiet stellate cells at insulin-secretory concentrations, including failure to phosphorylate CREB [32], implies that clinical MASLD benefit, if present, is more likely indirect unless a delivery system raises local analogue exposure [23]. Wnt/β-catenin links semaglutide to bone-forming cells in ovariectomized rats and bone-marrow stromal cells [36,37]. Muscle loss on GLP-1 RAs is mitigated by GDF8/activin A blockade in mice and non-human primates [41]; human trials of that combination should pre-specify lean mass and function, not scale weight alone. GABA_B agonism reduced GLP-1 RA-associated chronotropy and post-infarct arrhythmia in animals [39]. Microbiota transfer from semaglutide-treated obese mice [38] and electroacupuncture-related claims of higher exposure and less β-cell apoptosis [40] remain mechanistic hypotheses. Niosomes of *Phyllanthus niruri* act through the GLP-1 receptor in high-fat-diet rats and reverse with exendin 9–39 (n = 8 per group) [43], which supports receptor biology rather than interchangeability with semaglutide tablets. An adipose MSC exosome–*Spirulina* hydrogel with laser accelerated diabetic wound closure, with a transcriptome pointing to endothelium and M2 macrophages [44]. Combining that construct with pulmonary or AID studies would conflate a wound-healing therapy with incretin pharmacology.

Two protocols remain without outcomes [12,13]. AID-JUNCT will test open-label tirzepatide 5 mg versus standard of care in 42 AID users over 16 weeks, with TIR as the primary endpoint [12]. Agonist, control, and blinding differ from the completed semaglutide crossover [1]. Transcutaneous auricular vagus-nerve stimulation plus semaglutide asks whether a device can spare GI adverse effects [13]. Dual-agonist adjunct to automated delivery and device-based mitigation of GI effects are therefore unanswered. Two LADY cases illustrate residual C-peptide as a plausible substrate for oral semaglutide plus basal insulin [11]; they are not a trial.

Human comparative glycaemic evidence with a primary endpoint, a control, and a *P* value is essentially one AID crossover [1] plus a matched observational TIR study [2]. Delivery and vesicle experiments are animal or in vitro. Sample size was not reported in several studies. Endpoints do not overlap enough for a numeric synthesis across clinical, formulation, and extra-glycaemic work. Head-to-head human PK of pulmonary or microneedle GLP-1 RAs versus SC injection, a second randomized type 1 AID trial with outcomes, a pregnancy randomized trial, and a human body-composition trial of myostatin/activin blockade plus a GLP-1 RA have not been reported.

Measurements that would change practice follow from those gaps. A second AID adjunct trial should keep CGM TIR as primary endpoint, add ketone surveillance, and, if a dual agonist is used, analyse it separately from semaglutide [12]. An inhaled or microneedle GLP-1 RA that claims to compete with injection requires human relative BA versus SC dosing in the same volunteers, with particle morphology or needle force stated [19,20]. Oral peptide programmes should report species, enhancer, and percent BA together, and should not equate dog MEDI7219 with human SNAC semaglutide [4,45]. MASLD programmes should distinguish weight-mediated change from a direct hepatic effect [32]. Muscle-sparing combinations should pre-specify lean mass and function [41]. Wound-vesicle studies should report group size and should not be indexed as incretin trials [44].

## 8. Conclusions

Weekly SC semaglutide improved TIR as adjunct to AID in T1D by reducing hyperglycaemia, in a small randomized crossover [1]. In people at CV risk, pooled randomized evidence associates semaglutide with lower mortality and myocardial infarction and with more GI symptoms [14]. Real-world TIR, oral-tablet PK, spontaneous-report analyses, and utilisation studies refine practice and access; they do not replace those two anchors [2–10]. Non-injectable GLP-1 RA delivery is active in formulation science—morphology-tuned pulmonary carriers [19], microneedles [20–22], oral nanocarriers [23–26], and engineered oral tablets with measurable dog BA [45]—but remains preclinical and route-specific. Extra-glycaemic findings argue against a simple direct hepatic incretin effect [32], for Wnt-linked bone actions [36,37], and for muscle-sparing when myostatin and activin A are blocked during GLP-1 treatment [41]. Exosome hydrogels address diabetic wounds by a different biology [44]. The next clinical measurements that would change practice are human PK of non-injectable GLP-1 RAs versus SC dosing, and completed adjunct trials in AID [12].
