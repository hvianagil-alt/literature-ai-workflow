# Phyllanthus niruri niosomes ameliorate obesity-induced hepatic steatosis in rats via modulating MALAT1/miR-206/GLP-1R signaling and hepatic lipid metabolism

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Khater SI, Hussein MMA, Abdel-Magied SS, Lotfy MM, khamis T, Abdelaziz S, Mostafa M, El-Shaer NO, El-Emam MMA. Biol Res. 2026;59:27. doi:10.1186/s40659-026-00682-1
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa/Khater2026.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Included (niosomal delivery; obesity/MAFLD; GLP-1R mechanism vs semaglutide)

## Research question
Does *Phyllanthus niruri* extract in niosomes (PHYLN-NIO) protect against high-fat-diet hepatic steatosis, and is the effect GLP-1 receptor–dependent, analogous to semaglutide?

## Methods
Seven rat groups (n=8): control, PHYLN-NIO, HFD, HFD+semaglutide, HFD+semaglutide+exendin 9–39, HFD+PHYLN-NIO, HFD+PHYLN-NIO+exendin 9–39. Niosomes characterized (size, PDI, zeta, EE, SEM, release). Molecular readouts: MALAT1/miR-206, GLP-1/GLP-1R, lipogenic and FAO genes (Abstract; Methods).

## Sample / data
Rats, n=8 per group (Methods). HFD for 12 weeks to induce NAFLD (Methods). PHYLN-NIO 100 mg/kg orally (Methods).

## Key findings
- Niosomes: size 225.1 ± 19.6 nm; PDI 0.372; zeta −11.4 ± 0.92 mV; encapsulation efficiency 73.5 ± 4.2% (Results).
- PHYLN-NIO reduced body weight/BMI, steatosis, and inflammation and improved antioxidant status, insulin sensitivity, and lipids versus HFD (Abstract).
- Effects were reversed by the GLP-1R antagonist exendin 9–39, which the authors interpret as GLP-1R dependence (Abstract).
- Semaglutide was the clinical GLP-1 RA comparator arm (Abstract; Introduction).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Rat HFD model only.
- PHYLN is a botanical extract (multi-component), not a GLP-1 RA drug product.
- One extracted sentence describes faster niosome release than free drug (78% vs 40% at 6 h); that direction is unusual for “sustained release” and should be read against Fig. 3D.

## Relevance to our research question
On-topic for **delivery** (niosomes) and **GLP-1R signaling** in obesity-related liver disease, with a semaglutide control.

## Open questions / things to verify
Confirm the in-vitro release comparison in Fig. 3D; the prose description of free vs niosomal release may be inverted or poorly phrased.
