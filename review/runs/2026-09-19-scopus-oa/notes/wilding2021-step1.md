# Once-Weekly Semaglutide in Adults with Overweight or Obesity

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Wilding JPH, Batterham RL, Calanna S, Davies M, Van Gaal LF, Lingvay I, McGowan BM, Rosenstock J, Tran MTD, Wadden TA, Wharton S, et al. N Engl J Med. 2021;384:989-1002. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2032183
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa-gapfill/Wilding2021_032183.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Gap-fill **G1** (contextualize Wu 2026 semaglutide 2.4 mg weight MD). Included after OA PDF retrieved (UCL green OA copy).

## Research question
Can adults with overweight or obesity without diabetes achieve weight loss with once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg plus lifestyle intervention?

## Methods
Double-blind RCT (STEP 1); 2:1 randomization to semaglutide 2.4 mg SC weekly vs placebo, 68 weeks, plus lifestyle intervention. Coprimary: percent body-weight change and ≥5% weight reduction. NCT03548935. Funded by Novo Nordisk (Abstract).

## Sample / data
1961 adults; BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with ≥1 weight-related comorbidity; **no diabetes**. Semaglutide n=1306, placebo n=655 (Table 1). 43.7% had investigator-classified **prediabetes** at baseline (Results). Mean baseline weight 105.3 kg; BMI 37.9 (Results).

## Key findings
- Mean body-weight change at week 68: −14.9% semaglutide vs −2.4% placebo; estimated treatment difference −12.4 percentage points (95% CI −13.4 to −11.5; P<0.001) (Abstract).
- Absolute weight: −15.3 kg vs −2.6 kg; ETD −12.7 kg (95% CI −13.7 to −11.7) (Abstract).
- ≥5% / ≥10% / ≥15% weight-loss rates higher with semaglutide (Abstract).
- GI events (nausea, diarrhea) most common AEs; discontinuation for GI events 4.5% vs 0.8% (Abstract).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Population is overweight/obesity **without diabetes**, not a dedicated prediabetes NMA like Wu 2026.
- Industry-funded; lifestyle co-intervention.
- Prediabetes was a baseline characteristic/exploratory subset, not the primary analysis population.

## Relevance to our research question
Puts Wu’s NMA weight MD for semaglutide 2.4 mg SC (−13.59 kg vs placebo in **prediabetes**) next to a large RCT of the same dose in **obesity without diabetes** (ETD −12.7 kg). Same molecule/dose, different indication and design — not a replication of Wu’s network.
