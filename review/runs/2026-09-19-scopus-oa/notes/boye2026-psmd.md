# Development of the Patient Satisfaction with Medication for Diabetes (PSMD) questionnaire

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Boye KS, Stewart KD, Matza LS, Soucier-Ernst D, Houle C, Goetz I, Patel H, Kanu C. J Patient-Rep Outcomes. 2026;10:73. doi:10.1186/s41687-026-01037-w
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa/Boye2026.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Included (GLP-1 RAs appear as treatments patients were taking; this is a PRO instrument paper, not a delivery or efficacy trial)

## Research question
Can a new patient-reported outcome measure capture treatment satisfaction for people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes on current regimens, including newer medicines such as GLP-1 receptor agonists?

## Methods
Qualitative concept-elicitation interviews, item generation, then cognitive interviews to evaluate a draft questionnaire (Patient Satisfaction with Medication for Diabetes, PSMD). No psychometric validation results are reported in this paper beyond content-validity interviews.

## Sample / data
Concept elicitation: T1D n=10 (mean age 41.7 years; 70% female) and T2D n=20 (mean age 61.2 years; 45% female). Cognitive interviews: T1D n=5 (mean age 40.8 years; 60% female) and T2D n=10 (mean age 60.8 years; 60% female). Among cognitive-interview T2D participants, 30% were on oral medication plus an injectable GLP-1 RA and 10% on oral medication plus an injectable GIP/GLP-1 RA (Table 1 / Results).

## Key findings
- Patients named medication effectiveness, administration, and effects on energy, weight, and eating behavior as contributors to satisfaction (Abstract).
- Authors state the final instrument was clear, comprehensible, and relevant in cognitive interviews (Abstract; Conclusions).
- The paper argues existing satisfaction measures may not be sensitive to attributes of newer diabetes medicines, including GLP-1 RAs (Introduction; Conclusions).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Content validity only; no quantitative psychometric (reliability/validity) results in this article.
- Small qualitative samples recruited for interviews, not a clinical-outcome trial.
- Authors are affiliated with Eli Lilly and a contract research organization (title page).

## Relevance to our research question
Documents that GLP-1 and GIP/GLP-1 users were part of the development sample for a diabetes medication-satisfaction tool. It does not test efficacy or a delivery system.

## Open questions / things to verify
Whether later papers report PSMD measurement properties; not in this PDF.
