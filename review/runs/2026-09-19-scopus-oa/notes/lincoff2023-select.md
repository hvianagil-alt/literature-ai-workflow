# Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Obesity without Diabetes

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Lincoff AM, Brown-Frandsen K, Colhoun HM, Deanfield J, Emerson SS, Esbjerg S, Hardt-Lindberg S, Hovingh GK, Kahn SE, Kushner RF, Lingvay I, et al. N Engl J Med. 2023;389:2221-2232. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2307563
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa-gapfill/Lincoff2023_307563.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Gap-fill **G5** (clinical CV outcomes of semaglutide vs Guo’s PCSK5 mechanism). Included after OA PDF retrieved (UCL green OA copy).

## Research question
Does once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg reduce major adverse cardiovascular events in people with preexisting CVD and overweight/obesity **without** diabetes?

## Methods
SELECT: multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, event-driven superiority trial. 1:1 semaglutide 2.4 mg SC weekly vs placebo. Primary endpoint: composite of CV death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke (time-to-first-event). NCT03574597. Funded by Novo Nordisk (Abstract).

## Sample / data
17,604 patients ≥45 years; BMI ≥27; preexisting CVD; **no history of diabetes**. Semaglutide n=8803; placebo n=8801. Mean follow-up 39.8±9.4 months (Abstract).

## Key findings
- Primary endpoint: 569/8803 (6.5%) semaglutide vs 701/8801 (8.0%) placebo; HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.72–0.90; P<0.001) (Abstract).
- Permanent discontinuation for adverse events: 16.6% vs 8.2% (Abstract).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Population is obesity + CVD **without diabetes**, not a post-MI angiogenesis mechanism study.
- Does not measure PCSK5, endothelial gene programs, or Guo’s mouse endpoints.
- Industry-funded.

## Relevance to our research question
Supplies **clinical MACE reduction** for semaglutide 2.4 mg SC. It does **not** confirm that PCSK5 mediates that effect. Guo 2026 remains a mechanistic paper; SELECT remains an outcomes trial. Complementary, not confirmatory of the same claim.
