# Liraglutide, a glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonist, exerts analgesic, anti-inflammatory and anti-degradative actions in osteoarthritis

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Meurot C, Martin C, Sudre L, Breton J, Bougault C, Rattenbach R, Bismuth K, Jacques C, Berenbaum F. Sci Rep. 2022;12:1567. doi:10.1038/s41598-022-05323-7
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa-gapfill/Meurot2022_53237.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Gap-fill **G6** (other GLP-1 work in joint disease to contextualize Zhang 2026 RA microneedles). Included after OA PDF retrieved.

## Research question
Does liraglutide have analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and anti-degradative effects in osteoarthritis models, and is GLP-1R the target?

## Methods
In vitro chondrocytes and macrophages; in vivo sodium monoiodoacetate (MIA) **osteoarthritis** mouse model with **intra-articular** liraglutide. GLP-1R antagonist exendin 9–39 used to compete anti-inflammatory effects (Abstract; Results).

## Sample / data
Short-term MIA: von Frey n=15–19 per group; synovitis score n=8–9 per group. Long-term MIA: n=9–10 per group (figure legends). Human chondrocytes from explants n=18 mentioned for GLP-1R expression (Results).

## Key findings
- Intra-articular liraglutide reduced pain-related behaviour in MIA mice (Abstract; Fig. 2).
- Decreased IL-6, PGE2, and nitric oxide secretion and inflammatory gene expression in chondrocytes and macrophages, dose-dependent (Abstract).
- Shifted macrophages from M1 toward M2 (Abstract).
- Exendin 9–39 competed away liraglutide’s anti-inflammatory effects; authors interpret GLP-1R as the primary target (Results).
- Anti-catabolic activity on metalloproteinases in vitro (Abstract).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- **Osteoarthritis**, not rheumatoid arthritis (Zhang’s CIA model).
- Intra-articular injection, not microneedle nanoparticles.
- Named payload is **liraglutide**; Zhang’s extract did not name the GLP-1 RA.
- Preclinical.

## Relevance to our research question
Shows a second preclinical GLP-1 RA (liraglutide) acting in **joint** inflammation with M2 polarization and GLP-1R dependence. Supports “GLP-1R signalling in joints” as a construct, **not** a replication of Zhang’s RA microneedle device.
