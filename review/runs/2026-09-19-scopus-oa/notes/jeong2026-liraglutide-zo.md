# Morphology-driven zinc oxide biointeractive carriers with biological barrier modulating effects for pulmonary delivery of liraglutide

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Jeong J-H, Han C-S, Kang J-H, Kim D-W, Park C-W. J Nanobiotechnology. 2026;24:446. doi:10.1186/s12951-026-04085-y
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa/Jeong2026.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Included (pulmonary delivery of the GLP-1 RA liraglutide)

## Research question
Can zinc-oxide particle morphology be engineered so that inhalable liraglutide carriers deposit distally, cross mucus, evade macrophages, and raise pulmonary bioavailability versus injection?

## Methods
Three ZO morphologies: smooth spherical ZO-1 (5–7 μm), spiky urchin-like ZO-2 (5–8 μm, ~3.5 μm tips), compact spiky ZO-3 (1–3 μm, ~1.3 μm tips). PIV aerodynamics; cell mucus/macrophage assays; intratracheal PK in healthy and diabetic rats vs subcutaneous liraglutide (Abstract; PK section).

## Sample / data
PK: n=4 per group in healthy-rat and alloxan diabetic-rat arms (figure legends). Some formulation characterization n=3 or n=15 (figure legends). Diabetic model: alloxan-induced (PK figure legend).

## Key findings
- ZO-3 showed turbulence-driven dispersion favoring distal deposition, better mucus penetration, and lower macrophage uptake (Abstract).
- After intratracheal LG@ZO-3, bioavailability was ~60% relative to subcutaneous injection in healthy rats and ~51% in diabetic rats (Abstract; Discussion). Authors contrast this with oral semaglutide (Rybelsus) absolute bioavailability below 2% (Discussion; they cite the marketed product, not a head-to-head arm in this study).
- Authors report ZO particles were largely cleared from tissues within 48 h (Discussion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Small PK n (n=4).
- Zinc oxide pulmonary safety/long-term clearance “warrant further study” (Discussion).
- Oral semaglutide <2% is a literature/product comparison, not an oral arm in these rats.

## Relevance to our research question
Core **GLP-1 RA delivery** paper (liraglutide, pulmonary/ZnO carriers) in healthy and diabetic rats.
