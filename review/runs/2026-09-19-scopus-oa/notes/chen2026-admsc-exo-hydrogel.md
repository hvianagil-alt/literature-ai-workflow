# Novel approach for diabetic wound healing: adipose-derived mesenchymal stromal cells Exo@SPHydrogel combined with laser therapy

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Chen W, Hong J, Wei Y, Ye J, Wu Q. npj Regen Med. 2026;11:15. doi:10.1038/s41536-026-00459-w
- **Source file:** papers/scopus-oa/Chen2026.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-19
- **Screening:** Included (AD-MSC exosomes + hydrogel delivery in diabetic wounds)

## Research question
Does a hydrogel incorporating *Spirulina platensis*/chlorophyll derivatives and adipose-derived MSC exosomes (AD-MSC-exo@SP), with or without laser, improve diabetic skin-wound healing in rats?

## Methods
Diabetic rat dorsal wound model; single-cell/transcriptome analysis of wound vs healthy skin; in vitro antimicrobial, biofilm, angiogenesis, and M2 polarization assays; in vivo hydrogel ± laser (Abstract; Introduction).

## Sample / data
Diabetic rat wound model. Exact n per in-vivo group is **not stated** in the extractable text (PDF text extraction did not yield a clear “n = …” for the animal efficacy arms).

## Key findings
- Wound tissue transcriptome showed decreased endothelial cells and M2 macrophages versus healthy skin (Abstract).
- AD-MSC-exo@SP inhibited biofilm, was antimicrobial, and stimulated angiogenesis and M2 polarization in vitro (Abstract).
- In animals, combined with laser, authors report increased proliferative/angiogenic proteins, more M2 polarization, less inflammation, antimicrobial effect, and faster wound closure (Abstract).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Preclinical rat only.
- Group sizes not recoverable from extracted text — do not invent n.
- Multi-component device (exosomes + spirulina/chlorophyll + laser); contribution of each component is not isolated in the abstract.

## Relevance to our research question
On-topic for **stem-cell/exosome products** and **hydrogel delivery** in a diabetic complication (wounds). No GLP-1 RA.
