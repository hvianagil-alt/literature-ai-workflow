# GLP-1 receptor agonists, vesicle therapeutics, and delivery systems in metabolic disease: a mini-review of a heterogeneous 2026 open-access set

## Abstract

Injectable glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) and dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists are established in type 2 diabetes and obesity. Whether a 2026 gold open-access Scopus export on those drugs, cell-derived vesicles, and delivery systems actually measures comparable constructs is a different question. This mini-review includes seven full texts from a 10-record export plus five public open-access papers retrieved only to put named interpretation gaps in perspective. The only pooled human comparative evidence *in the export* is a Bayesian network meta-analysis in prediabetes: subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg was associated with a mean weight difference of −13.59 kg versus placebo (95% CI −17.30 to −9.91). A large obesity trial of the same dose (without diabetes) reported an estimated treatment difference of −12.7 kg (95% CI −13.7 to −11.7) at 68 weeks; that is a related magnitude, not a replication of the prediabetes network. Three animal papers in the export attempt non-injectable or local GLP-1–related exposure and do not share a payload, disease, route, or endpoint. Extra-glycaemic work remains unpooled: a PCSK5-dependent angiogenesis mechanism for semaglutide after infarct, set beside a clinical trial in which 2.4 mg semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events (hazard ratio 0.80) in people with cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity without diabetes — outcomes that do not demonstrate that mechanism. Adipose MSC-exosome hydrogels for diabetic wounds appear in two preclinical papers; neither is a human trial, and one still lacks recoverable group sizes. A patient-reported satisfaction instrument included GLP-1 users but did not estimate drug effect. These papers should not be synthesized as a single delivery, vesicle, or cardiovascular-efficacy result.

## Keywords

GLP-1 receptor agonists; semaglutide; liraglutide; drug delivery; extracellular vesicles; prediabetes; diabetic complications

## 1. Introduction

GLP-1 RAs improve glycaemia and body weight in routine metabolic practice, and dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists extend that pharmacology. That clinical background is not a finding of the 2026 Scopus export reviewed here. What the export can test is narrower: in one open-access slice tagged to GLP-1 RAs, stem-cell or exosome products, and drug-delivery systems, which of those constructs were actually measured, in whom, and with which endpoints.

If those threads were empirically aligned, a short review could report a delivery effect, a vesicle effect, and a repair effect. They are not aligned in this set. The argument below is therefore about *incommensurability*, with targeted extra open-access full texts used only where an included result could not be put in perspective from the seven export papers alone.

This article is a mini-review of seven readable full texts from the export plus five gap-fill open-access papers, not a comprehensive survey of GLP-1 RAs or extracellular vesicles. Search, eligibility, extra retrieval, and selection are in Section 2.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Search and sources

Records were those in a Scopus BibTeX export dated 19 September 2026 (10 articles, all 2026, all tagged gold open access by Scopus). After notes and a literature table existed, interpretation gaps were listed and OpenAlex was queried for public full texts that might put those specific results in perspective. Additional PDFs were kept only if a public PDF file was obtained from OpenAlex, Unpaywall, Europe PMC, or publisher open-access URLs. Paywalls were not bypassed.

### 2.2 Eligibility

A paper was included if the full text addressed GLP-1 or a related incretin drug, a stem-cell or exosome product, and/or a drug-delivery system, in metabolic disease or a closely related inflammatory or tissue-repair setting. Papers with no such link, unreadable files, and files that could not be retrieved were excluded from the evidence base. Gap-fill papers had to address a named interpretation gap, not merely share keywords.

### 2.3 Study selection

Of 10 export records, one was excluded on topic (yeast plasmid engineering). Nine full texts were sought; one gold open-access Elsevier article (exosome-mimetic nanovesicles in diabetic cerebral infarction) could not be downloaded then or on retry and is not cited as evidence. Eight full texts were assessed; one mixed-methods evaluation of a digital behavioural weight programme was excluded because GLP-1 drugs were not the intervention. **Seven export studies were included.**

For eight interpretation gaps, eight additional DOIs were selected from OpenAlex hits (off-topic highly cited papers were not fetched). **Five public PDFs were retrieved and extracted**; three selected DOIs yielded no public PDF (a 2020 molecular-metabolism review, the 2016 semaglutide cardiovascular-outcomes trial in type 2 diabetes, and the original Huang 2026 record).

## 3. The only pooled human comparative evidence in the export is in prediabetes

If the question is what incretin drugs *do in people in this Scopus slice*, the export contains one paper. Wu et al. performed a Bayesian network meta-analysis of randomized trials of at least 12 weeks in adults with prediabetes, searching to 29 March 2025 [1]. Fifty-five trials (16,610 participants; 37 interventions) contributed to the network [1]. Versus placebo, most included drugs reduced HbA1c (mean difference −0.94 to −0.27%), fasting plasma glucose (−26.42 to −0.15 mg/dL), weight (−13.59 to −5.99 kg), and BMI (−4.50 to −0.08 kg/m²) [1]. Subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg had the largest highlighted weight effect (−13.59 kg; 95% CI −17.30 to −9.91) and an HbA1c difference of −0.39% (−0.55 to −0.25) [1]. Tirzepatide 15 mg reduced fasting glucose by −9.58 mg/dL (−12.00 to −7.15) [1]. The authors judged GLP-1 RAs, GIP/GLP-1 RAs, and thiazolidinediones favourably [1]. Stated limitations include few trials per class, heterogeneity, follow-up from 12 to 120 weeks, and incomplete safety data [1].

That −13.59 kg figure cannot be compared with another trial *inside the export*. A gap-fill retrieval of STEP 1 (Wilding et al.) supplies a same-dose subcutaneous comparator in a different population: 1961 adults with overweight or obesity **without diabetes**, 68 weeks, estimated treatment difference −12.7 kg (95% CI −13.7 to −11.7) and −14.9% versus −2.4% body weight versus placebo [8]. About 43.7% of STEP 1 participants had investigator-classified prediabetes at baseline, but prediabetes was not the primary analysis population [8]. The magnitudes are in the same range; the designs are not the same (single RCT in obesity versus a prediabetes network of many interventions). This is perspective, not confirmation that Wu’s ranking of 37 nodes is reproduced.

Three limits follow for the rest of this review. First, Wu’s population is prediabetes [1]. Second, Wu does not evaluate pulmonary, transdermal, or niosomal delivery [1]. Third, Wu’s safety statement cannot underwrite zinc-oxide inhalation or an exosome hydrogel.

## 4. Non-injectable GLP-1-related exposure is an engineering problem without a shared endpoint

The remaining GLP-1-related experimental papers in the export do not test whether incretin drugs work. They test whether a GLP-1-related signal can be presented to tissue by a route other than conventional subcutaneous injection—or, in one case, whether a botanical nanocarrier acts through GLP-1R.

Jeong et al. asked a pharmacokinetic question in rats: can zinc-oxide morphology be chosen so that inhalable liraglutide approaches subcutaneous bioavailability [2]. After intratracheal liraglutide-loaded ZO-3, relative bioavailability versus subcutaneous injection was about 60% in healthy rats and 51% in alloxan-diabetic rats (pharmacokinetic n = 4 per group) [2]. The authors contrast this with the <2% absolute bioavailability of marketed oral semaglutide, from product literature, not an oral arm in these rats [2]. An independent open-access review of liraglutide and semaglutide development reports phase 2 data in which oral semaglutide co-formulated with SNAC had bioavailability of about 1% yet similar efficacy to subcutaneous semaglutide in the cited programme [9]. That supports Jeong’s *direction* (oral peptide exposure is low) and still does not compare inhalable liraglutide with oral or subcutaneous semaglutide in one study. Long-term pulmonary zinc-oxide safety was not established [2]. No clinical head-to-head of pulmonary or microneedle GLP-1 RA delivery versus subcutaneous dosing was retrieved.

Zhang et al. asked a different exposure question in collagen-induced arthritis, not diabetes [3]. Silk-fibroin nanoparticles with a type II collagen-binding peptide were loaded with a payload named only as “GLP-1RAs” in the extracted abstract and placed in dissolving microneedles [3]. Encapsulation efficiency was about 28–33%; about 60% of payload was released by day 14 [3]. In CIA rats the authors report joint accumulation, suppression of chondrocyte ferroptosis via cGAS-STING, macrophage M2 polarization, and attenuated disease [3]. A gap-fill osteoarthritis study found that *intra-articular liraglutide* reduced pain-related behaviour in monoiodoacetate mice, decreased inflammatory mediators, shifted macrophages toward M2, and lost anti-inflammatory effects in the presence of exendin 9–39 [11]. That supports GLP-1R pharmacology in *joints*; it is osteoarthritis rather than rheumatoid arthritis, injection rather than microneedles, and a named liraglutide payload rather than Zhang’s unnamed “GLP-1RAs” [3,11]. It is not a replication of Zhang’s device.

Khater et al. sit next to these papers because the phenotype was GLP-1R-dependent, not because the product is a GLP-1 RA [4]. *Phyllanthus niruri* niosomes improved metabolic and histologic readouts in high-fat-diet rats (n = 8 per group); benefit was reversed by exendin 9–39 [4]. Semaglutide was the licensed-agonist comparator, not the formulated payload [4]. Reading Jeong’s oral-semaglutide remark and Khater’s oral niosomes as a disagreement about oral GLP-1 bioavailability would confuse payloads.

What these papers jointly support is modest: in this set, non-injectable or local GLP-1-related exposure remains preclinical, and the experiments are not replicates of one another.

## 5. Extra-glycaemic and regenerative work here is mechanistic, not a pooled outcomes class

Guo et al. identified PCSK5 as up-regulated after myocardial infarction and reported that semaglutide promoted angiogenesis and tissue repair in a partly PCSK5-dependent manner [5]. Human plasma panels are in the tens [5]. This is mechanistic cardiology, not an outcomes trial.

A gap-fill cardiovascular-outcomes trial (SELECT) in 17,604 people aged 45 years or older with preexisting cardiovascular disease and BMI ≥27 **without diabetes** found that weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced a composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke versus placebo (6.5% versus 8.0%; hazard ratio 0.80; 95% CI 0.72–0.90) over a mean 39.8 months of follow-up [10]. SELECT does not measure PCSK5 [10]. Guo does not estimate event rates [5]. The clinical result shows that semaglutide 2.4 mg can reduce major adverse cardiovascular events in a defined obesity-plus-cardiovascular-disease population; it does not show that PCSK5 is the pathway. The 2016 semaglutide cardiovascular-outcomes trial in type 2 diabetes was sought for the same gap and was not retrieved as a public PDF.

Chen et al. combined adipose-derived MSC exosomes with a *Spirulina platensis*/chlorophyll-derivative hydrogel, with or without laser, in a diabetic rat skin-wound model [6]. Authors report antimicrobial, angiogenic, M2-polarizing, and faster-closure effects [6]. Group sizes were not stated in the extracted text [6]. A 2019 adipose-MSC-exosome hydrogel study in diabetic mouse full-thickness wounds reported faster closure, angiogenesis, and less scarring than exosomes or hydrogel alone [12]. That places Chen in a recognizable preclinical design family. It does not supply Chen’s missing n, and neither paper is a human wound trial [6,12].

The missing Huang paper would have sat nearer Chen than Guo on product type (exosome-mimetic nanovesicles in a diabetic cerebral complication). It was not retrieved on either attempt, so vesicle conclusions still rest on preclinical wound hydrogels, not on a read diabetic-stroke nanovesicle study.

## 6. Satisfaction with diabetes medicines is a different endpoint

Boye et al. developed the Patient Satisfaction with Medication for Diabetes (PSMD) questionnaire [7]. Concept elicitation included 10 adults with type 1 diabetes and 20 with type 2; cognitive interviews included 5 and 10 respectively [7]. In the type 2 cognitive sample, 30% used oral medication plus an injectable GLP-1 RA and 10% used a dual GIP/GLP-1 RA [7]. The paper supports content validity only [7]. A search for later psychometric papers returned the same 2026 article; no additional full text was retrieved.

Wu’s network does not tell us whether people prefer 2.4 mg subcutaneous semaglutide [1]; Boye’s interviews do not tell us whether that dose reduces weight [7].

## 7. Discussion

Four messages are stable.

First, **incretin pharmacology in people, in the export, is a single prediabetes network** [1]. STEP 1 places the same 2.4 mg subcutaneous dose in obesity without diabetes at a similar kilogram-scale weight difference [8]. That is the available perspective; it is not Wu’s network re-run.

Second, **non-injectable GLP-1-related exposure remains unlike itself** [2–4]. Oral semaglutide bioavailability near 1% in a development review [9] explains why Jeong invoked a low oral figure, and still leaves pulmonary liraglutide in rats without a clinical comparator. No retrieved paper was a human head-to-head of inhalable or microneedle GLP-1 RAs versus subcutaneous dosing.

Third, **semaglutide’s roles do not validate one another**: NMA node [1], niosome control [4], PCSK5 probe [5], and — from gap-fill — obesity weight-loss trial [8] and MACE trial [10]. Complementary uses.

Fourth, **vesicle-hydrogel diabetic-wound work is a preclinical class, not a clinical result** [6,12]. Huang 2026 remains unread.

Disagreements in the export are almost entirely differences of methods, population, and endpoint. Meurot 2022 does not contradict Zhang 2026; they are different joint diseases and devices [3,11]. SELECT does not contradict Guo; one measures events, the other a pathway [5,10].

Gaps that remain after extra retrieval: Huang 2026 still not retrieved; Chen’s animal n still not in the extract [6]; SUSTAIN-6 not retrieved; no clinical non-injectable versus subcutaneous GLP-1 RA delivery trial; no PSMD measurement-properties paper. Those gaps are not filled from memory.

## 8. Conclusions

In this open-access 2026 slice, pooled human evidence that incretin drugs improve weight and glycaemia *in prediabetes* comes from Wu et al. [1]. A same-dose obesity trial reports a similar kilogram-scale weight effect in a different population [8]. Work on pulmonary liraglutide, microneedle GLP-1 RA nanoparticles, niosomal GLP-1R agonism by a botanical extract, and MSC-exosome hydrogels remains preclinical and unpooled [2–4,6,12]. Semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events in SELECT [10] and promoted PCSK5-linked angiogenesis in Guo et al. [5]; those are different claims. Patient satisfaction with diabetes medicines, including GLP-1 RAs, was explored only as content validity [7]. Claims in this review are limited to the retrieved full texts listed below.
