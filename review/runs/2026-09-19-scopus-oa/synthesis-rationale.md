# Synthesis rationale — 2026-09-19-scopus-oa

**Research question:** In this Scopus open-access export, what is reported about GLP-1 receptor agonists, stem-cell/exosome products, and drug-delivery systems in metabolic disease (diabetes, obesity, and closely related inflammatory or repair settings)?

**Included studies:** 7 full texts from the export — Jeong 2026; Zhang 2026; Khater 2026; Wu 2026; Chen 2026; Guo 2026; Boye 2026 — plus 5 gap-fill OA full texts retrieved after this rationale’s first pass (Wilding 2021; Knudsen 2019; Lincoff 2023; Meurot 2022; Wang 2019). Gap-fill papers are labeled as such; they were not in the original 10-record identification set.

**Not used as evidence:**
- Li 2026 — yeast plasmid copy-number control; excluded on topic.
- Barrett 2026 — NHS digital weight-management mixed-methods evaluation; GLP-1 not the intervention.
- Huang 2026 — title suggests exosome-mimetic nanovesicles in diabetic cerebral infarction, but no public PDF was retrieved; no findings used.

This file is the interpretation step. It is not a token or phase meter.

## (a) What each included study actually measured

**Wu 2026 (BMC Medicine).** Bayesian network meta-analysis of randomized trials (≥12 weeks) in *adults with prediabetes*. Search to 29 March 2025; 55 RCTs, 16,610 participants, 37 interventions. Endpoints: HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, body weight, BMI, and adverse events versus placebo and one another. This is the only paper in the set that supplies pooled *human* effect sizes for incretin (and other anti-prediabetic) drugs. It does not measure a delivery system, an exosome product, extra-glycaemic tissue repair, or patient-reported satisfaction. Population is prediabetes, not type 2 diabetes.

**Jeong 2026 (J Nanobiotechnology).** Preclinical formulation/PK study. Three zinc-oxide particle morphologies as inhalable carriers for *liraglutide*. Measurements: particle aerodynamics (PIV), mucus penetration, macrophage uptake, and *relative bioavailability after intratracheal versus subcutaneous dosing* in healthy and alloxan-diabetic rats (PK n = 4 per group). The ~60% / ~51% relative-bioavailability figures are versus SC liraglutide in the same models. The authors’ contrast with oral semaglutide (<2% absolute bioavailability) is product literature, not an oral arm in these rats. Long-term pulmonary ZnO safety was not established. No human PK, no glycaemic outcomes trial.

**Zhang 2026 (J Nanobiotechnology).** Preclinical device/pharmacology study in *collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) rats*, not diabetes. Silk-fibroin nanoparticles with a type II collagen-binding peptide (WYRGRL), loaded with a payload named only as “GLP-1RAs” in the extracted abstract, placed in dissolving microneedles. Measurements: encapsulation efficiency (~28% and ~33% at silk:drug 5:1 and 10:1), ~60% release by day 14, joint accumulation (some biodistribution arms n = 6), chondrocyte ferroptosis / cGAS-STING, macrophage M2 polarization, and RA disease attenuation. It does not measure metabolic efficacy, human articular exposure, or a named licensed GLP-1 RA dose.

**Khater 2026 (Biol Res).** Rat high-fat-diet hepatic steatosis (12 weeks; n = 8 per group, seven groups). Intervention is *Phyllanthus niruri* extract in niosomes (PHYLN-NIO: 225.1 ± 19.6 nm; encapsulation 73.5 ± 4.2%; 100 mg/kg oral), with semaglutide as a comparator arm and exendin 9–39 as a GLP-1R antagonist. Measurements: niosome physicochemical characterization; body weight/BMI, steatosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, insulin sensitivity, lipids; MALAT1/miR-206 and GLP-1R pathway readouts. Benefit reversed by exendin 9–39 is the authors’ evidence of GLP-1R dependence. This is a botanical nanocarrier, not a licensed GLP-1 RA product, and not a human MASLD trial.

**Chen 2026 (npj Regen Med).** Diabetic *rat skin-wound* model. Adipose-derived MSC exosomes in a *Spirulina platensis*/chlorophyll-derivative hydrogel, with or without laser. Measurements: wound vs healthy-skin transcriptome (fewer endothelial cells and M2 macrophages in wounds); in vitro biofilm/antimicrobial, angiogenesis, and M2 assays; in vivo proliferative/angiogenic proteins, M2 shift, inflammation, and wound closure. Group sizes for the animal efficacy arms were **not stated** in the extracted text. No GLP-1 RA. Multi-component device; contribution of each component is not isolated in the extract.

**Guo 2026 (Nature Communications).** Mechanistic cardiology: does endothelial PCSK5 promote post-MI angiogenesis, and does *semaglutide* act partly through that pathway? Human MI tissue/plasma (e.g. ELISA n = 30 vs 28 AMI; some correlation panels n = 42), mouse MI including endothelial *Pcsk5* knockout (several arms n = 6), HUVECs, GEO GSE132143 / GSE184986. Semaglutide is a mechanistic probe (ETS1-linked PCSK5 induction; angiogenesis partly PCSK5-dependent per the authors). Not a diabetes outcomes trial; human n is in the tens.

**Boye 2026 (J Patient-Reported Outcomes).** Qualitative instrument development (concept elicitation then cognitive interviews) of the Patient Satisfaction with Medication for Diabetes (PSMD) questionnaire. Samples: CE T1D n = 10 / T2D n = 20; cognitive T1D n = 5 / T2D n = 10. In the T2D cognitive sample, 30% used oral medication plus an injectable GLP-1 RA and 10% a dual GIP/GLP-1 RA. Endpoint is *content validity* (clarity/relevance of items). It does not estimate glycaemic or weight efficacy, does not validate measurement properties quantitatively, and does not test a delivery system.

## (b) Themes the data support vs themes that would be forced

**Supported — as constructs, not as pooled effects.**

1. **Human comparative pharmacology of incretin drugs in this set is a single-study observation, confined to prediabetes.** Only Wu 2026 measures HbA1c, FPG, weight, and BMI in people. Semaglutide 2.4 mg SC and tirzepatide 15 mg are the authors’ highlighted options among included interventions. This can anchor a section; it cannot be generalized from this set to type 2 diabetes or to delivery devices.

2. **Non-injectable or locally targeted exposure of GLP-1–related agents is being engineered in animals, by unlike routes.** Jeong (intratracheal liraglutide/ZnO), Zhang (microneedle NP into RA joints), and Khater (oral botanical niosomes with a GLP-1R-dependent phenotype) all address *how a GLP-1-related signal is presented to tissue*. They support a *translational gap* narrative (no shared clinical PK), not a claim that “delivery systems work.”

3. **Tissue-repair biology appears in three papers with different products and diseases.** Zhang (GLP-1 RA payload, chondrocyte ferroptosis, RA), Guo (semaglutide–PCSK5 angiogenesis after MI), Chen (MSC-exosome hydrogel ± laser in diabetic wounds). The shared idea is extra-metabolic repair; the products and endpoints are not the same class.

4. **Patient-reported satisfaction is a separate construct** that only Boye 2026 measures, and only as content validity. GLP-1 users are in the development sample; drug effect is not.

**Would be forced — do not write these as findings of the set.**

- **“GLP-1 delivery systems improve metabolic outcomes.”** No included paper reports human bioavailability or a clinical endpoint for pulmonary, microneedle, or niosomal GLP-1 RA delivery. Khater’s payload is a plant extract.
- **“Exosomes and GLP-1 RAs are one therapeutic class.”** Chen has no GLP-1; Guo and Wu have no vesicles; Huang (the missing stroke nanovesicle paper) was not read.
- **Pooling Jeong, Zhang, and Khater as one delivery platform.** Different payload (named liraglutide vs unnamed “GLP-1RAs” vs *P. niruri*), species/disease (healthy/diabetic rat PK vs CIA vs HFD steatosis), route (lung vs skin vs oral), and endpoint (relative BA vs joint pathology vs steatosis/GLP-1R antagonism).
- **Using Guo as evidence that semaglutide improves cardiovascular outcomes in diabetes.** Mechanistic post-MI angiogenesis in mixed human/mouse assays; not an outcomes trial.
- **Using Boye as evidence of GLP-1 effectiveness or tolerability.** Interview content validity only.
- **Treating Wu’s AE finding (“no overall difference”) as safety of ZnO inhalation or of exosome hydrogels.** Those safety questions were not in the NMA.

## (c) Real disagreements and why

There is **no head-to-head conflict on a shared clinical endpoint** in this set. Apparent tension is almost entirely *incommensurability*:

- **Population.** Wu: adults with prediabetes. Jeong/Khater/Chen: rat metabolic or diabetic models. Zhang: CIA rats (inflammatory joint disease). Guo: human/mouse MI. Boye: mixed T1D/T2D qualitative interviews. These populations cannot adjudicate one another’s primary claims.
- **Endpoint.** Relative pulmonary bioavailability (Jeong) is not articular ferroptosis (Zhang), is not HFD steatosis reversed by exendin 9–39 (Khater), is not NMA weight MD (Wu), is not wound closure (Chen), is not PCSK5-dependent angiogenesis (Guo), is not item clarity (Boye).
- **Payload.** Jeong uses liraglutide. Wu’s highlighted node is semaglutide 2.4 mg SC (and tirzepatide). Khater uses a botanical extract with semaglutide as control. Zhang does not name the GLP-1 RA molecule in the extracted abstract. Chen uses MSC exosomes, not an incretin.
- **Oral bioavailability language.** Jeong cites marketed oral semaglutide absolute bioavailability <2% from product literature. Khater reports oral niosomes of a plant extract. These sentences are not a disagreement about oral GLP-1 RA bioavailability; they measure different things.
- **Semaglutide’s three roles do not confirm one another** (NMA node in Wu; positive-control GLP-1 RA in Khater; mechanistic probe in Guo). Complementary uses, not replication.
- **Safety.** Wu reports no overall difference in AE incidence among NMA interventions, with higher SAEs for sitagliptin 100 mg in their analysis. Jeong states ZnO long-term pulmonary safety warrants further study. These are not contradictory; they are different safety questions.

A disagreement that *is* real but modest: **Khater is on-protocol for GLP-1R mechanism and delivery, yet it is not a GLP-1 RA formulation study.** Treating it as evidence for “niosomal GLP-1 RA” would misread the payload. The review should keep that distinction explicit rather than split the paper out of the delivery discussion entirely.

## (d) What this sample cannot answer

Questions a reader of a “GLP-1 / vesicles / delivery in metabolic disease” review might expect, which **these seven full texts cannot settle**:

- Clinical (human) bioavailability or efficacy of pulmonary liraglutide, microneedle GLP-1 RAs, or niosomal GLP-1R agonists.
- Comparative effectiveness of incretin drugs in *type 2 diabetes or obesity* (Wu is prediabetes only).
- Whether MSC-exosome hydrogels heal diabetic wounds in patients (Chen: rat; n not in extract).
- Whether semaglutide’s PCSK5-linked angiogenesis is clinically relevant in diabetes or after MI in a trial (Guo).
- Quantitative measurement properties of PSMD, or satisfaction differences between GLP-1 and other drugs (Boye).
- The contribution of Huang 2026 (exosome-mimetic nanovesicles, diabetic cerebral infarction) to the vesicle story — PDF not retrieved.
- Head-to-head comparison of delivery routes.
- Long-term pulmonary safety of zinc-oxide carriers.
- Identity and dose of Zhang’s GLP-1 RA payload, from the extracted text.

This is a 10-record gold-OA Scopus slice from 2026, not a field survey. Wider clinical GLP-1 evidence exists outside this set; this review must not write as if it had searched that field.

## (e) Outline of the review

The article is a mini-review of *this set*. Argument: the only human comparative pharmacology here is a prediabetes NMA; everything else is preclinical or qualitative and does not share endpoints, so the set documents a translational scatter rather than a single delivery or vesicle effect.

1. **Title / Abstract / Keywords** — State n = 7 / 10, heterogeneity, and that papers must not be pooled as one “delivery effect.” Include Wu’s semaglutide 2.4 mg weight MD as the only pooled human effect size highlighted from this set.
2. **Introduction** — Clinical success of injectable GLP-1 RAs and dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists is the background, not a finding of this set. The question for these papers is whether 2026 OA work on delivery, vesicles, and extra-glycaemic biology actually measures those constructs in a comparable way.
3. **Methods** — Scopus OA export; eligibility; PRISMA counts (10 identified; 1 title exclude; 9 sought; 1 not retrieved; 8 full-text; 1 full-text exclude; 7 included). No token estimates.
4. **The only human comparative evidence is in prediabetes (Wu).** Job: establish the pharmacological baseline and its limits (prediabetes; not delivery; heterogeneity 12–120 weeks).
5. **Non-injectable GLP-1-related exposure is an engineering problem without a shared endpoint (Jeong, Zhang, Khater).** Job: compare route, payload, disease, and endpoint; refuse pooling; keep Khater’s botanical payload visible; keep Zhang’s RA setting visible.
6. **Extra-glycaemic and regenerative work in this set is mechanistic, not metabolic-outcomes evidence (Guo, Chen; Zhang already placed as local joint delivery).** Job: separate cardiac PCSK5/semaglutide from MSC-exosome wounds; do not treat them as one “regenerative GLP-1” class.
7. **Satisfaction with diabetes medicines, including GLP-1 RAs, is a different endpoint (Boye).** Job: content validity only; GLP-1 users in the sample ≠ drug-effect estimate.
8. **Discussion** — Three stable messages from (b); incommensurability from (c); cannot-answer list from (d); Huang gap.
9. **Conclusions** — Claims limited to retrieved full texts (export + gap-fill); open gaps stay open.
10. **References** — Included full texts only (excluded/not-retrieved named in methods, not used as evidence).

Every included paper appears: Wu in §4; Jeong, Zhang, Khater in §5; Guo and Chen in §6 (Zhang cross-referenced); Boye in §7. If extra OA full texts are retrieved for the gaps in (f), they are added as rows in the table and cited in the matching section; they are not silently omitted and they are not invented.

## (f) Interpretation gaps (must attempt retrieval)

These are obstacles to putting an included result in perspective. Each must be searched; failure to retrieve is allowed if logged.

1. **G1 — Wu 2026 weight MD for semaglutide 2.4 mg SC (−13.59 kg vs placebo in prediabetes).** This is the only pooled human effect size in the sample. No source RCT or independent NMA is in the seven full texts, so the magnitude cannot be compared with obesity or type 2 diabetes trials from this set.
2. **G2 — Jeong 2026 relative pulmonary bioavailability (~60% / ~51% vs SC) contrasted with oral semaglutide <2%.** The oral figure is product literature, not a paper in the sample. Need an independent OA full text on oral semaglutide bioavailability, or another inhaled-peptide PK study.
3. **G3 — Huang 2026 not retrieved** (exosome-mimetic nanovesicles, diabetic cerebral infarction). The vesicle/CNS arm of the question is missing. Retry that DOI and search for other OA EV/stroke/diabetic-CNS papers.
4. **G4 — Chen 2026 diabetic-wound MSC-exosome hydrogel.** Animal group n was not recovered from the extract; no other diabetic-wound EV paper is in the sample to judge typical design or effect.
5. **G5 — Guo 2026 semaglutide–PCSK5 post-MI angiogenesis.** Mechanistic only. No clinical cardiovascular-outcome trial of semaglutide is in the sample.
6. **G6 — Zhang 2026 GLP-1 RA microneedles in CIA rats.** No other GLP-1 / rheumatoid-arthritis full text is in the sample.
7. **G7 — No clinical head-to-head of non-injectable vs subcutaneous GLP-1 RA delivery** among the seven papers.
8. **G8 — Boye 2026 PSMD.** Content validity only; no later psychometric paper in the sample.
