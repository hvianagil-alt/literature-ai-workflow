# Comparative Assessment of Semi-Industrial Thermal and Non-Thermal Pasteurization Technologies for Red Globe Grape Juice

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Eran Nagar, Eden; Romano, Alon; Shazman, Asher; Lesmes, Uri; Shpigelman, Avi. 2026. Foods 15(4): 672. doi:10.3390/foods15040672. PMC12939781
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Eran Nagar2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
When HPP, ohmic heating (OH), and conventional thermal pasteurization (TP) are matched to the same ~3-log reduction of indigenous aerobic microbes in Red Globe grape juice, how do they differ in PPO inactivation, CIELAB color, FRAP antioxidant capacity, and monomeric anthocyanins?

## Methods
Semi-industrial Technion pilot. Red Globe grapes (Dole South Africa), 70–75 kg per independent batch, twice (~30 L juice each). TSS 17.0 ± 0.5 °Brix; pH 3.56 ± 0.02. HPP: 30 mL vacuum PE bags, Stansted S-FL-850-09-W, 500 MPa / 5 min, circulating water 25 °C, product ≤40 °C. OH: Raztek 50 kW, 1.1 L/min, electro-heat to 120 °C, 3 s hold, ~12 atm backpressure, then cool. TP: plate heat exchanger, 90 °C / 1 min hold, 1 L/min, exit ~25 °C. Microbes: drop plate PCA and OGYE, triplicate, 30 °C / 72 h. PPO with catechol, 420 nm. Color Minolta CR-300. FRAP as µM vitamin C eq. TMAC pH-differential as cyanidin-3,5-diglucoside eq. Replicates: PPO n = 9; microbes and FRAP n = 6; anthocyanins and color n = 4. ANOVA, Tukey p < 0.05. Fresh juice frozen at −40 °C as control; treated juice stored −20 °C until assays (no refrigerated shelf-life).

## Sample / data
Mechanically pressed, 304-mesh filtered juice (not solvent-extracted pomace). Fresh TMAC 3.30 ± 0.17 mg/L. Fresh PPO 58 ± 13 min−1 mL−1. Fresh aerobic load 1.5 ± 0.2 × 10^5 CFU/mL; yeasts/molds 1.7 ± 0.3 × 10^4 CFU/mL.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Semi-industrial three-technology comparison matched to equal indigenous microbial reduction (not a storage trial)
- **Population / model:** Red Globe (*Vitis vinifera*) grape juice
- **n:** two independent 70–75 kg process batches; PPO n = 9; microbes/FRAP n = 6; color/anthocyanins n = 4
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 500 MPa / 5 min (~25–40 °C) vs OH 120 °C / 3 s vs TP 90 °C / 1 min vs fresh juice; conditions set for ~3-log aerobic reduction
- **Primary endpoint:** Matched ~3-log aerobic count reduction; PPO activity (min−1 mL−1); ΔE and L*a*b*; FRAP; TMAC (mg/L)
- **Primary result:** Aerobic counts fell ~3.0 log (to 2.2–3.0 × 10^2 CFU/mL); Y&M 3.0–4.0 log (to 10^1–10^0). OH and TP cut PPO to 5 ± 2 and 4.6 ± 0.7 (>90% inactivation); HPP 41 ± 8 (~30%, ns vs fresh 58 ± 13). ΔE OH 6.5 ± 2.4 and TP 6.4 ± 1.2 (“great” color difference); HPP color ns vs fresh (L* 29.2 ± 2.3 vs fresh 29.6 ± 2.2). FRAP not statistically different across treatments. TMAC significantly higher only after OH vs fresh. HPP best preserved native color; OH/TP best inactivated PPO.
- **Cannot show:** Refrigerated shelf-life or browning during storage; 5-log pathogen HACCP validation (authors used 3-log indigenous flora as a benchmarking basis, not FDA 5-log); PEF or other nonthermals.

## Key findings
- OH/TP L* 28.5 ± 2.8 and 27.9 ± 0.9; b* shifted from +2.2 ± 0.3 (fresh) to about −3 (thermal); a* from 5.9 ± 1.1 to <2.7 (Section 3.5).
- Authors hypothesize thermal cell disruption can raise extractable anthocyanins enough to offset degradation (Conclusions).
- Residual PPO after HPP is flagged as a likely shelf-life risk needing cold chain (Section 3.3; Conclusions).
- Process intensities were optimized in preliminary runs to hit 3-log, not to maximize quality (Methods 2.3).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- No storage study (explicit).
- 3-log indigenous reduction ≠ commercial 5-log pathogen process.
- Low starting anthocyanin of pressed Red Globe juice (3.30 mg/L) makes color sensitive.
- HPP in 30 mL bags vs OH/TP as 30 L continuous flow—scale differs.
- English-editing tools disclosed (Acknowledgments).

## Relevance to our research question
Juice HPP vs thermal (and OH) under equal microbial kill: HPP (500 MPa/5 min) keeps color and FRAP but leaves ~70% PPO; OH/TP brown the juice but knock PPO down >90%. Directly useful for quality–enzyme trade-offs; does not itself measure shelf-life days.
