# High Pressure Processing Applications in Plant Foods

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Houška M, Silva FVM, Evelyn, Buckow R, Terefe NS, Tonello C. Foods 2022, 11, 223. doi: 10.3390/foods11020223
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp-gapfill/Huang2022plant.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Note:** gap-fill OA retrieval after synthesis-rationale gap 2 (spore/HPTP kinetics in plant foods).

## Research question
How do HPP and heat-assisted HPP (HPTP) affect spoilage and pathogenic microorganisms (including spores), endogenous enzymes, quality, and industrial equipment for fruit, vegetable, and herb products?

## Methods
Narrative industrial-and-kinetics review (partly upgraded from a Houska and Silva book). Compiles commercial product examples, D/z and Weibull parameters, PME/PG enzyme studies, and Hiperbaric/equipment notes.

## Sample / data
Cited juice, puree, soup, and herb HPP/HPTP studies; commercial SKUs listed in tables. Number of studies not reported as a PRISMA n.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Narrative review with kinetic parameter compilation (gap-fill).
- **Population / model:** Fruit, vegetable, and fresh-herb homogenates; vegetative pathogens, yeast, bacterial and mould spores; PME/PG.
- **n:** not reported.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Cold HPP 300–600 MPa vs HPTP (pressure plus heat, often >60 °C for spores) vs thermal cloud-stabilisation of orange juice (90–99 °C / 15–30 s for PME).
- **Primary endpoint:** Microbial D-values / Weibull parameters; enzyme residual activity; refrigerated distribution need.
- **Primary result:** HPP alone does not inactivate most microbial spores and many enzymes; HPTP is required for bacterial/mould spores. Example: *Alicyclobacillus acidoterrestris* spores in apple juice D_45°C at 600 MPa = 8.6 min, rising to 20 min in 20 °Brix lime and 46 min in 30 °Brix blackcurrant concentrate (Uchida and Silva as cited). Vegetative D-values at 350 MPa ~0.6–2 min in juices. Authors state food authorities recommend about six log pasteurisation of the relevant pathogen (range 4–8). Commercial machines usually max 600 MPa without supplied heat, so products are distributed <7 °C.
- **Cannot show:** A new 5-log *E. coli* O157:H7 juice validation; bovine-milk HTST pairing.

## Key findings
- Vegetative inactivation in fruit often first-order; spore curves concave-up (Weibull or biphasic) with tails (Section 3.1).
- Heat-labile orange PME is also pressure-labile at ≥500 MPa in some studies, but residual thermostable PME and study-to-study variation remain (enzyme section).
- Low-acid vegetable juices (pH > 4.6) still need control of *C. botulinum* spores via cold chain after HPP (Section 3).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Narrative; D-values drawn from heterogeneous published fits; commercial tables are illustrative.

## Relevance to our research question
Fills the spore-kinetics gap left by Zhang2022 with quoted D-values and states the industrial 600 MPa / refrigerated-distribution constraint that matches EFSA’s milk caution.

## Open questions / things to verify
Cited D-values should be attributed to Uchida/Silva/Parish, not treated as new experiments in this review.
