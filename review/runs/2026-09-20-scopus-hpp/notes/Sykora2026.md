# Effect of High-Pressure Processing Operating Parameters on Microbial Inactivation and Bioactive Protein Preservation in Bovine Milk: A Systematic Review

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Sykora RM, Mark C, Biondi RM, Barman B, Pitino M, Dallas DC. Compr Rev Food Sci Food Saf. 2025;25:e70324. doi: 10.1111/1541-4337.70324 (Scopus citekey Sykora2026)
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Sykora2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Which HPP operating parameters in raw liquid bovine milk both inactivate pathogens and preserve bioactive proteins, given that HPP milk is not yet approved in the U.S.?

## Methods
PRISMA systematic review registered OSF 12 Feb 2024. PEO: raw liquid bovine milk; isostatic HPP (not homogenisation); microbial safety and native protein concentration/activity measured before and after pressure. Dual independent screening.

## Sample / data
Pathogen outcomes in 65 articles; protein analysis in 24; only 4 articles reported both bacteria and proteins. Pressures 50–900 MPa (most 400–600 MPa); times 1–600 min (most 5–15 min); temperatures −20 to 75 °C (most 20–30 °C).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Systematic review (PRISMA).
- **Population / model:** Raw liquid bovine milk (fat-fractionated whole/semi-skim/skim allowed; powders excluded).
- **n:** 65 pathogen papers + 24 protein papers; 4 overlap.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP parameter space versus the implicit HTST/LTLT standard (no paired HTST meta-comparison).
- **Primary endpoint:** Log CFU/mL reductions (L. monocytogenes, E. coli, S. aureus, S. enterica) and native protein (IgG, lactoferrin, LPO, α-LA, β-Lg, ALP).
- **Primary result:** Pressure was the strongest contributor to log reductions for L. monocytogenes, E. coli, and S. aureus (mixed-methods p < 0.05). For L. monocytogenes, >551 MPa with >5 min at 20–30 °C was needed for >5-log in the papers that reached that target; pressurisation/depressurisation alone was not enough. S. aureus often needed longer holds at 401–600 MPa; some >5-log S. aureus kills used 60 °C and are no longer non-thermal. Only four papers measured bacteria and proteins together.
- **Cannot show:** An FDA-ready milk HPP schedule that simultaneously meets 5-log pathogen kill and preserves all bioactive proteins; no direct HTST comparison.

## Key findings
- Cocktails were more resistant than single strains because HPP selects the most barotolerant member.
- ALP denaturation at >600 MPa ranged 42% to >99.2% depending on intensity (three articles).
- Authors conclude nutritional/sensory papers must be paired with bacterial data.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- No direct HTST comparison; heterogeneous minor parameters (ramps, packaging); single extractor for data.

## Relevance to our research question
Best included systematic map of the milk HPP safety–bioactivity trade-off, complementary to the EFSA opinion.
