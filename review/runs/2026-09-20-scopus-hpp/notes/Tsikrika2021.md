# High-pressure processing and ultrasonication of minimally processed potatoes: effect on the colour, microbial counts, and bioactive compounds

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Tsikrika, Konstantina; Walsh, Des; Joseph, Ashik; Burgess, Catherine M.; Rai, Dilip K. 2021. Molecules 26(9):2614. doi:10.3390/molecules26092614
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Tsikrika2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Do HPP at 600 MPa and HPP followed by 20 kHz ultrasound change colour, native microbial counts, total phenolics, antioxidant activity, individual phenolics, and glycoalkaloids of vacuum-packed minimally processed Maris Piper and Rooster potatoes during 14 days at 4 °C?

## Methods
Washed, peeled tubers vacuum-sealed in PE/PA pouches; HPP 600 MPa, 3 min, max 10.6 °C (Hiperbaric 55HT). Then cubes 2 × 2 × 2 cm (~150 g) sonicated 20 kHz, 200 W, 10 min (start 18 °C; max 28 ± 2 °C). Controls untreated. Colour L*a*b*/ΔE days 0, 1, 7, 10, 14 (two sides × three points, n = 6). Microbes days 1, 2, 7, 10, 14: Enterobacteriaceae (ISO 21528-2), yeasts/moulds (ISO 21527-1), APC (ISO 4833-1), Salmonella (ISO 6579-1), as log10 CFU/g. TPC (Folin), FRAP, DPPH; UPLC-TQD phenolics and α-chaconine/α-solanine. Duplicate experiments, analyses in triplicate (n = 6). ANOVA + Tukey, p < 0.05.

## Sample / data
Solanum tuberosum cvs. Maris Piper (white) and Rooster (coloured), Dublin retail. TPC untreated: Maris Piper 542 ± 10 µg GAE/g dw; Rooster 646 ± 35 µg GAE/g dw. Phytochemicals Table 2, n = 6.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Controlled laboratory storage study (two cultivars × untreated / HPP / HPP+US)
- **Population / model:** Minimally processed Maris Piper and Rooster potatoes (native flora; not inoculated)
- **n:** Colour and composition n = 6; microbial sampling ~10 g per time point (replicate structure as n = 6 overall)
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 600 MPa / 3 min / 10.6 °C vs HPP then US 20 kHz, 200 W, 10 min vs untreated, stored 14 days at 4 °C
- **Primary endpoint:** Colour (L*, a*, b*, ΔE) and cultural counts (Enterobacteriaceae, APC, yeasts/moulds, Salmonella) during storage; TPC/AOA as co-primary quality
- **Primary result:** HPP and HPP/US lowered L* and b* and raised ΔE vs controls (p < 0.05). Enterobacteriaceae not detectable after HPP in both cultivars; HPP/US slowed them but was not significantly different from untreated by day 14. APC delayed through day 10 (HPP/US Rooster lowest) but not different from untreated at day 14. Yeasts/moulds remained lower in treated samples at the end of storage. Salmonella absent in all samples. TPC and FRAP/DPPH unchanged (p > 0.05).
- **Cannot show:** Pathogen D-values or 5-log pasteurization (no inoculum); US-only effects (no US-without-HPP arm); that browning enzymes were fully inactivated (authors link residual PPO from prior work)

## Key findings
- Day-14 ΔE examples (Table 1): Maris Piper untreated 4.39 ± 0.83 vs HPP 10.10 ± 1.48 vs HPP/US 18.96 ± 2.43; Rooster untreated 8.06 ± 0.84 vs HPP 21.23 ± 1.61 vs HPP/US 18.03 ± 1.57.
- Chlorogenic acid fell sharply (Maris Piper 80.60 ± 6.4 → 28.38 ± 6.7 HPP → 3.31 ± 0.21 HPP/US µg/g dw); quinic acid rose (24.12 → 28.14 → 35.07) (Table 2).
- Glycoalkaloids: Maris Piper HPP ~72% decrease in α-chaconine and α-solanine; HPP/US ~97%. Rooster HPP: α-chaconine −55%, α-solanine −66%; HPP/US ~95% (Section 2.5).
- Absolute APC/yeast log CFU/g are plotted (Fig. 1) and not tabulated in the extract.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Colour deterioration during storage despite delayed microbes; residual PPO suspected.
- HPP/US cubes were cut after HPP, so US acted on cut surfaces only.
- No US-only or thermal blanching comparator in this paper.
- Native counts only; Salmonella absence is not a challenge-test result.
- Glycoalkaloid drop may reflect peeling/cutting plus US, not HPP of whole tubers (authors contrast their 2019 whole-potato study).

## Relevance to our research question
Direct HPP ± US on a starch staple: 600 MPa eliminated detectable Enterobacteriaceae and delayed APC/yeasts while holding TPC and AOA, but increased browning (ΔE) and degraded chlorogenic acid. Illustrates the microbial-vs-colour trade-off for HPP on fresh-cut potatoes.
