# Effects Of Ultra-High Pressure and Thermal Treatment on Quality Characteristics of Prepared Mackerel Fish Balls

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Wang, Biaoshi; Hu, Xiaojun; Ma, Jingqiu; Xu, Ying. 2025. American Journal of Biochemistry and Biotechnology 21 (3): 351–361. DOI:10.3844/ajbbsp.2025.351.361
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Wang2025351.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How do ultra-high pressure (UHP) and thermal sterilization change microbiological, chemical, color, texture, and sensory quality of prepared mackerel fish balls, and which process better preserves quality while reducing spoilage indicators?

## Methods
Laboratory comparison of vacuum-packed, pre-cooked mackerel surimi balls. UHP at 250 or 350 MPa for 10 min (water as pressure medium; adiabatic heating stated as about 2–3 °C/100 MPa) versus autoclave heating at 121 °C for 13 or 15 min, plus an untreated control. Endpoints: total bacteria (GB 4789.2-2022; 30 ± 1 °C, 72 h), color (L*, a*, b*, whiteness), TPA texture, MDA (GB 5009.181-2016), TVB-N, and trained sensory scoring (eight students; color, smell, elasticity, tissue, texture, taste). Samples held at 4 °C and measured at day 0 and day 7.

## Sample / data
Fresh mackerel (~1 kg each) from Zhanjiang fish market, made into fish balls (white meat, scallion-ginger water ~10:1, sweet potato flour ~125:2, egg white), boiled, cooled, vacuum packed. Each experimental group analyzed in triplicate; results as mean ± SD.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Controlled laboratory processing trial (UHP vs thermal vs untreated) with refrigerated follow-up
- **Population / model:** Prepared mackerel (*Scomberomorus*) surimi fish balls
- **n:** triplicate analyses per group
- **Intervention / comparator:** UHP 250 or 350 MPa / 10 min vs thermal 121 °C / 13 or 15 min vs untreated control; storage 4 °C, 0 and 7 d
- **Primary endpoint:** Total bacterial count (lg CFU/g) plus TVB-N, MDA, color, texture, sensory score
- **Primary result:** Day 0 control 2.18 lg CFU/g; UHP 250 and 350 MPa both <1 lg CFU/g; 121 °C/13 min 1.74 lg CFU/g and 121 °C/15 min 1.77 lg CFU/g (p<0.05 vs control). After 7 d at 4 °C, control rose to 3.62 lg CFU/g; UHP and heat remained significantly lower than control. 350 MPa/10 min gave the largest TVB-N reduction at day 0 and day 7, did not significantly change chewiness vs control, significantly increased elasticity (p<0.05), and had the highest comprehensive sensory score; 121 °C/15 min had the worst sensory effects.
- **Cannot show:** Commercial-length shelf-life, pathogen challenge (only total bacteria), nutritional composition (vitamins/fatty acids), or industrial-scale UHP validation. Storage was only 7 days.

## Key findings
- Both UHP and heat reduced total bacteria, TVB-N, and MDA versus untreated balls (Abstract; Results; Figs. 2–4).
- UHP color changes were described as remaining within acceptable bounds; heat adversely affected color, texture, and sensory quality (Abstract).
- Day 0 L* for 350 MPa/10 min was 51.46 ± 0.13 (Table 2 excerpt in text).
- Authors recommend 350 MPa / 10 min over thermal processing for prepared mackerel fish balls (Abstract; Conclusion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors note no prior UHP studies on prepared mackerel fish balls and limited use in prepared surimi products (Introduction).
- Conclusion calls for further optimization of sensory attributes and other possibilities (not specified as a completed industrial process).
- Only 7-day refrigeration; no named pathogens; sensory panel of eight students.

## Relevance to our research question
Direct seafood matrix evidence that UHP (350 MPa, 10 min) can cut total bacteria below 1 lg CFU/g and slow TVB-N/MDA rise versus untreated balls, while heat at 121 °C damages sensory/texture more. Supports HPP for fish-ball quality and short refrigerated holding, not a nutrition-profile study.
