# Investigating the microbial inactivation effect of low temperature high pressure carbon dioxide and its application in frozen prawn (*Penaeus vannamei*)

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Lian, Zimeng; Yang, Dong; Wang, Yongtao; Zhao, Liang; Rao, Lei; Liao, Xiaojun. 2023. Food Control 145: 109401. doi:10.1016/j.foodcont.2022.109401. PMC9512252
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Lian2023.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Can low-temperature high-pressure carbon dioxide (LT-HPCD) inactivate bacteria and coronaviruses in suspension and on frozen prawn/package surfaces, and does 6.5 MPa / 10 °C / 15 min keep prawn quality during chilled and frozen storage?

## Methods
HPCD vessel 3000 mL; CO2 99.00%. Bacteria: *E. coli* 8099, *S. aureus* ATCC 6538 in 0.85% NaCl. Viruses: SARS-CoV-2 Spike pseudovirus (luciferase) and hCoV-229E (TCID50 on Huh-7). Inocula on 1 cm prawn cubes and PET, or natural flora on whole brine-frozen *Penaeus vannamei* (stored < −18 °C ~3 months). Plate counts: three replications; texture/color: six replications; TVB-N and salt-soluble protein: three replications. Sensory: 11 evaluators, 0–10 scales. Storage: 6.5 MPa / 10 °C / 15 min vs untreated; 4 °C for 8 d (three bags/treatment) or −18 °C for 12 weeks. ANOVA, SNK p < 0.05.

## Sample / data
Seawater-cultured prawns from Zhangzhou, China. Optimal LT-HPCD chosen because >10 °C melted frozen prawn.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Laboratory LT-HPCD inactivation study (suspension, inoculated surfaces, natural flora) plus chilled and frozen storage quality
- **Population / model:** Frozen *Penaeus vannamei*; *E. coli*, *S. aureus*, SARS-CoV-2 Spike pseudovirus, hCoV-229E
- **n:** microbial/TVB-N/protein three replications; texture/color six; sensory 11 evaluators; storage three replicate bags
- **Intervention / comparator:** LT-HPCD 6.5 MPa (or 10 MPa) at 5–10 °C for up to 30 min vs untreated; storage 4 °C / 8 d or −18 °C / 12 wk
- **Primary endpoint:** Percent or log inactivation of bacteria/virus and total aerobic count on prawn; quality (pH, hardness, color, sensory, TVB-N)
- **Primary result:** 6.5 MPa / 10 °C / 15 min inactivated 99.45% *E. coli*, 94.6% *S. aureus*, >99% (body: >99.9%) Spike pseudovirus in suspension; hCoV-229E 10^4.8 to 10^3.74 TCID50/mL (>1-log). On prawn surfaces: 93% *E. coli*, 67% *S. aureus*, 95% Spike; on PET 91%, 59%, 92%; hCoV-229E <1-log on surfaces. Natural flora: ~60% inactivation at 10 °C / 15 min (6.5 or 10 MPa); none at 5 °C. Prawn stayed frozen. pH 8.09 ± 0.04 → 7.21 ± 0.05. Hardness 274.4 ± 18.98 vs 268.1 ± 43.47 g (ns). Total sensory 8.15 ± 0.24 vs 7.52 ± 0.17 (ns). During 4 °C storage, treated TAC rose ~10-fold in 8 d vs ~10,000-fold in controls; no melanosis vs controls from day 4.
- **Cannot show:** True SARS-CoV-2 (only pseudovirus + hCoV-229E surrogate); hydrostatic HPP (this is HPCD, ~6.5 MPa); 5-log food-safety process for pathogens on prawn; nutrition (vitamins).

## Key findings
- 30 min did not significantly beat 15 min in suspension (Section 3.1).
- 6.5 and 10 MPa similar at 10 °C (Section 3.3).
- Abdominal somite L, a, b increased (brighter/redder/yellower) but sensory color still >6 (Table 1; Section 3.4).
- Shucking-effect acceptance 8.33 ± 0.29 after LT-HPCD (no control score).
- Frozen storage 12 weeks: no significant sensory or texture difference vs untreated frozen prawn (Section 3.6).
- TVB-N during chill did not differ significantly between treated and control despite slower plate counts (Section 3.5).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Surface inactivation weaker than in liquid (food protection, low surface water) (Section 3.2).
- No compulsory microbial limit for frozen food cited, so “60%” natural-flora reduction is not a regulatory endpoint.
- COVID-era virus rationale; foodborne SARS-CoV-2 infection not established (Introduction).
- HPCD ≠ commercial HPP (600 MPa class).

## Relevance to our research question
Seafood nonthermal process, but **high-pressure CO2 at 6.5 MPa**, not hydrostatic HPP. Useful as a related nonthermal hurdle for frozen prawn quality and microbes; do not pool its MPa values with HPP 400–600 MPa trials.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Abstract “>99%” Spike vs Results “>99.9%”.
- Absolute TAC (log CFU/g) on prawn is in figures, not a starting log in the extract—only % inactivation and fold-change during storage.
