# Potential impact of ultrasound, pulsed electric field, high-pressure processing and microfluidization against thermal treatments regarding sugarcane juice (Saccharum officinarum)

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Mukhtar, Kinza; Nabi, Brera Ghulam; Arshad, Rai Naveed; Roobab, Ume; Yaseen, Bilal; Ranjha, Muhammad Modassar Ali Nawaz; Aadil, Rana Muhammad; Ibrahim, Salam A. 2022. Ultrasonics Sonochemistry 90:106194. doi:10.1016/j.ultsonch.2022.106194
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Mukhtar2022.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How do novel thermal (ohmic heating, microwave, pasteurization) and non-thermal (HHP, ultrasound, PEF, UV, ozone, membranes, microfluidization) processes, alone or as hurdles, affect microbial load, enzymes (PPO/POD), and quality of sugarcane juice compared with conventional heat?

## Methods
Narrative review of published sugarcane-juice studies. No new experiments, no stated PRISMA protocol, no search-date window beyond “literature.” Authors tabulate process conditions and reported physicochemical, enzymatic, and microbial outcomes (Tables 1–3) and discuss limitations of each technology.

## Sample / data
Secondary data from cited SCJ trials. Matrices are sugarcane juice (sometimes with lemon/ginger, whey, or preservatives). Primary n from the underlying papers is not re-extracted uniformly. This review itself has n = not applicable.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative literature review (not a meta-analysis)
- **Population / model:** Sugarcane juice (Saccharum officinarum) as reported in cited studies
- **n:** not applicable (review); cited trials keep their own n
- **Intervention / comparator:** HHP, US, PEF, UV, ozone, microfluidization, membranes vs thermal pasteurization, ohmic heating, microwave, and hurdle combinations
- **Primary endpoint:** Authors’ synthesis target is microbial reduction plus retention of nutrients/enzymes/shelf life (mixed endpoints across studies)
- **Primary result:** Authors conclude HHP is the most used/efficient non-thermal option for SCJ and ohmic heating is the preferred novel thermal option because of smaller nutritional loss (Highlights/Abstract). Example HHP rows they report: 300 MPa, 2–5 min, 6.32-log plate-count reduction with PPO still ~92.77 residual (Table 2); 523 MPa, 50 °C, 11 min, 5-log microbes, 62% PPO and 59% POD inactivation. They state thermal methods reduce microbes but degrade nutrients.
- **Cannot show:** A pooled effect size or ranking that is independent of the heterogeneous cited protocols; spore kill; industrial cost-effectiveness (authors say most processes remain lab-scale)

## Key findings
- SCJ spoils via PPO/POD, pH ~5.0–5.5, sugars, and microbes; fresh juice browns and ferments quickly (Introduction).
- Ohmic heating examples: 32 V/cm, 1 min — TPC reduced 4.2-fold, no yeast & mold detected, PPO 10.07 ± 0.32%; 70 °C, 3 min, 48 V/cm — TPC plate count 4.70 ± 0.009 log CFU/mL, 10-day refrigerated shelf life (Section 2.1).
- PEF example: 30 kV cm−1, 150 pulses — 2.71 log CFU/mL aerobic mesophiles; another report >1.42-log including E. coli at 110 V, 30 s (Table 2).
- US example: 40 kHz, 240 W, 40 min — 1.95 log mesophiles and 0.42 log yeast & mold; TPC +18%, TFC +16%, vitamin C −13% (Table 2).
- Ozone 4.23 g/L, 20 min: PPO −67.8%, POD −75.3%, TPC −13.5%, ascorbic acid −81.46%, 3.72-log TPC count (Table 2).
- Hurdles: ozone 1.2 g/h + 0.5% lactic acid — 4.3-log TBC, ~60–72% PPO/POD loss, ~1 month quality; thermosonication 50 °C — 5-log E. coli, B. cereus, and aerobic plate count (Table 3).
- Authors list cost, scale, fouling, ozone toxicity, PEF spore gap, and irradiation perception as limits (Section 5).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Narrative, not systematic; cited numerical rows can mix different juices and analytics.
- Many cited processes are laboratory or test scale with higher unit cost than industrial heat (Conclusion).
- Some table cells are sparse or “–”; several microbial units look inconsistent across source papers (e.g., CFU/10 mL vs log CFU/mL).
- Microfluidization in one row is described as increasing PPO/POD, i.e. not always a kill/inactivation tool.

## Relevance to our research question
Useful SCJ-focused map of HPP, US, PEF, UV, ozone, and hurdles versus heat. Supports the review’s interest in nonthermal kill vs quality, but every number must be traced to the original trial before use as a claim.
