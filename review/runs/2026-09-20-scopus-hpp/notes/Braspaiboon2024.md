# High Hydrostatic Pressure: Influences on Allergenicity, Bioactivities, and Structural and Functional Properties of Proteins from Diverse Food Sources

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Braspaiboon S, Laokuldilok T. Foods 2024, 13, 922. doi: 10.3390/foods13060922
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Braspaiboon2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How does HHP change protein structure, allergenicity, bioactivity, and functional properties across food protein sources?

## Methods
Narrative review consolidating HHP effects on proteins from cereals, legumes, nuts, meat, poultry, milk, eggs, seafood, algae, insects, seeds, and vegetables.

## Sample / data
No new experiments; literature across the listed protein classes. Study n not reported.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Narrative review of protein-level HHP effects.
- **Population / model:** Food proteins from the listed sources.
- **n:** not reported.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HHP (non-covalent disruption) versus untreated proteins; microbial/enzyme inactivation mentioned as industrial context.
- **Primary endpoint:** Allergenicity, bioactivity, digestibility, hydrophobicity, foaming/gelling.
- **Primary result:** HHP unfolds interior protein regions by disrupting non-covalent bonds. Allergenicity of cereals, legumes, and nuts decreases while bioactivities and digestibility increase (abstract). Surface hydrophobicity rises especially for seed and vegetable proteins. Dairy proteins show weaker allergenicity and higher foaming; gelling and antioxidant activity also increase (abstract).
- **Cannot show:** A single pressure that detoxifies all allergens; clinical allergy trials.

## Key findings
- Mechanism: non-covalent bond disruption and exposure of hydrophobic interiors (abstract/conclusion).
- Source-dependent functional outcomes (foaming, gelling, antioxidant activity) rather than a uniform quality benefit.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- No new data; trends are qualitative across heterogeneous methods.

## Relevance to our research question
Shows HPP is not only a microbial kill step: protein unfolding can lower allergenicity and change function, which matters when interpreting “quality” of pressurised foods.
