# Recent Progress in the Synergistic Bactericidal Effect of High Pressure and Temperature Processing in Fruits and Vegetables and Related Kinetics

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Zhang S, Meenu M, Hu L, Ren J, Ramaswamy HS, Yu Y. Foods 2022, 11, 3698. doi: 10.3390/foods11223698
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Zhang2022.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How do HPP and high-pressure thermal processing (HPTP) inactivate microbes in fruit and vegetable systems, which kinetic models fit, and which factors (species, growth stage, P/T/time, matrix) change lethality?

## Methods
Narrative review of primary kinetic models (first-order; Weibull), secondary zT/zP models, and polynomial/RSM models, plus factor analysis for fruit/vegetable HPP and HPTP.

## Sample / data
Cited fruit and vegetable juices, purees, and related systems. Number of studies not reported.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Narrative review of HPP/HPTP bactericidal kinetics.
- **Population / model:** Harmful microorganisms in fruit and vegetable products.
- **n:** not reported.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP at low/ambient temperature versus HPTP (moderate 40–90 °C or low-temperature/freezing synergy) versus thermal-only.
- **Primary endpoint:** Log reductions and kinetic parameters (k, D, b, n, zT, zP).
- **Primary result:** Spores are the main HPP failure mode at industrial pressures (<600 MPa, ambient); HPTP is required for many spores. Example: HPP-thermal 600 MPa / 75 °C / 10 min gave 3.3 log cfu/mL reduction of Neosartorya fischeri ascospores in apple juice versus no reduction by 75 °C thermal-only (Evelyn et al. as cited). Alicyclobacillus acidoterrestris spores: 2.7 log at 600 MPa / 65 °C / 10 min versus none from T or P alone (Filipa et al. as cited). Pasteurization target stated as at least 5–6 log cfu/g or ml.
- **Cannot show:** One kinetic model for all matrices; ambient HPP as a sterilant for bacterial spores (authors note B. subtilis spores surviving 1200 MPa at ambient temperature in cited work).

## Key findings
- Resistance order generally spores > Gram-positive > fungi > Gram-negative, but matrix can reorder this (Section 3.1).
- Stationary-phase cells are more pressure-tolerant than exponential-phase cells.
- Weibull often fits HPTP spore curves with tailing; first-order can fit some linear cases (Section 2.1).
- Multi-pulse HPP can outperform single pulses for some yeasts/spores (Aleman, Donsì as cited).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Accurate in-situ temperature under pressure is required or kinetic models mislead.
- Low-temperature + high-pressure literature is thinner than moderate-temperature HPTP.

## Relevance to our research question
Core statement of the HPP spore gap and why pressure–temperature synergy and kinetics matter for fruit/vegetable safety.

## Open questions / things to verify
