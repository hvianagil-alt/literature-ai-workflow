# The efficacy and safety of high-pressure processing of food

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** EFSA BIOHAZ Panel (Koutsoumanis K et al.). EFSA Journal 2022;20(3):e07128. doi: 10.2903/j.efsa.2022.7128
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Koutsoumanis2022.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What minimum HPP pressure/time (and other factors) are needed for pathogens in raw milk/colostrum relative to legal thermal pasteurisation, what indicators can verify HPP, how does risk compare among HPP vs raw vs pasteurised vs UHT milk, and what HPP reduces Listeria monocytogenes in selected RTE foods?

## Methods
EFSA scientific opinion: literature review, modelling of log10 reductions, isoreduction plots using the Santillana Farakos and Zwietering meta-analysis model for Listeria spp. (200–700 MPa), and a serving-level contamination model (250 mL milk). Terms of reference include milk pasteurisation equivalence and RTE L. monocytogenes control.

## Sample / data
Published HPP inactivation data for milk/colostrum pathogens (L. monocytogenes, Salmonella, STEC, S. aureus, Campylobacter, plus data gaps for Brucella, M. bovis/MAP, TBEV) and RTE cooked meat among other RTE categories.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Scientific opinion / evidence synthesis with quantitative risk comparison, not a new challenge trial.
- **Population / model:** Raw milk/colostrum for direct consumption; selected RTE foods; pathogens listed in ToRs.
- **n:** Multiple cited studies (e.g. 15 studies on L. monocytogenes in milks); not a single experimental n.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Industrial HPP (including 600 MPa / 6 min as the most stringent industrial condition discussed) versus EU thermal pasteurisation minima (at least 72 °C / 15 s or 63 °C / 30 min) versus raw and UHT milk.
- **Primary endpoint:** log10 pathogen reductions; probability a 250 mL serving is contaminated; ALP and other enzyme/protein indicators; L. monocytogenes log reductions in RTE meats.
- **Primary result:** Even 600 MPa for 6 min would lead to less log10 reduction than thermal pasteurisation for the milk pathogens that could be compared, so HPP milk is not equivalent to legal pasteurisation on that metric (ToR comparative assessment). ALP is relatively pressure-resistant and would be at best an overprocessing indicator. For RTE cooked meat, the Listeria model supports >2 log10 reduction examples at 600 MPa with short times (isoreduction section). Thermal pasteurisation PC of 5 log10 is the milk reference.
- **Cannot show:** Equivalence of HPP milk to HTST for all hazards (data scarce for Brucella, M. bovis, TBEV). Does not experimentally re-challenge milk.

## Key findings
- Vegetative resistance in HPP milk literature: S. aureus often more resistant than E. coli, then L. monocytogenes and Salmonella (reviews cited in the opinion).
- L. monocytogenes in raw milk: 200–250 MPa / 5–15 min gave <0.5 log10; 300/350/400 MPa / 5 min gave about 1 / 4.4 / 6.3 log10 in cited work.
- Indicator enzymes used for heat pasteurisation do not map cleanly onto HPP lethality.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Sparse or old data for some milk pathogens; strain and matrix variability; model assumptions of contaminated batches at 1–8 log10 CFU/mL scenarios.

## Relevance to our research question
Highest-authority included text on whether industrial HPP matches pasteurisation in milk and how Listeria reductions in RTE meat should be specified as P/t pairs.

## Open questions / things to verify
