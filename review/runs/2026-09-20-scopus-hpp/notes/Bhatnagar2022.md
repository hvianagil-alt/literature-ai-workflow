# Impact of irradiation on physico-chemical and nutritional properties of fruits and vegetables: A mini review

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Bhatnagar, Pooja; Gururani, Prateek; Bisht, Bhawna; Kumar, Vinod; Kumar, Navin; Joshi, Raja; Vlaskin, Mikhail S. 2022. Heliyon 8(10):e10918. doi:10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e10918
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Bhatnagar2022.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How do non-ionizing (mainly UV-C) and ionizing (gamma, X-ray, e-beam) irradiations change microbial load and physico-chemical/nutritional quality of fruits and vegetables, and which dose/matrix factors govern efficacy?

## Methods
Mini narrative review. Summarizes mechanisms (UV-C DNA dimers vs ionizing direct DNA damage plus water radiolysis), process factors (dose, organism, food optics/composition, O2, moisture, temperature), then tabulated case studies of UV, gamma, X-ray, and electron beam on produce. No new experiment and no systematic search protocol.

## Sample / data
Cited produce includes tomato, strawberry, banana, pineapple, mango, leafy greens, pomegranate, litchi, apple, green onion, pear, kiwi, spinach, parsley, garlic, dates, and others. Review n = not applicable.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Mini narrative review
- **Population / model:** Fresh and some dried fruits and vegetables in cited irradiation trials
- **n:** not applicable
- **Intervention / comparator:** UV-A/B/C and ionizing gamma (typically 60Co), X-ray (≤5 MeV), electron beam (≤10 MeV) vs untreated stored produce
- **Primary endpoint:** Mixed: microbial logs, shelf life, phenolics, vitamins, color, firmness
- **Primary result:** Authors state FAO/IAEA/WHO found no toxicological, microbiological, or nutritional problem at doses up to 10 kGy, and FDA allows up to 4.5 kGy on refrigerated meat (not produce-specific). They summarize typical lethal ranges: 0.5–10 kGy yeast/mold/vegetative bacteria; 10–50 kGy bacterial spores; 10–200 kGy viruses. Example they report: X-ray 2.0 kGy reduced E. coli O157:H7, S. flexneri, Salmonella enterica, and L. monocytogenes by 5 log CFU per spinach leaf; parsley at 1 kGy reduced those pathogens by 5.7, 5.8, 5.2, and 3.1 log CFU, undetectable at 1.5 kGy. Gamma 2.8 kGy: 1.3-log MuNoV reduction on strawberries. UV-C tomato 1.0 kJ/m2, 1 h: lycopene 59.91 ± 1.47 during 8-day room storage.
- **Cannot show:** A single “safe quality-preserving dose” for all produce; spore inactivation at produce-typical kGy; consumer-acceptance data generated by this paper; HPP/PEF/plasma effects (mentioned only as competing technologies)

## Key findings
- UV-C (200–280 nm; peak germicidal 260–265 nm) forms CPDs and 6-4 photoproducts; ionizing kill is mostly indirect in high-water fresh produce (Section 2).
- Sensitivity order stated: molds > yeasts > bacteria > viruses; Gram-negatives more UV-sensitive than Gram-positives; spores more resistant than vegetative cells (Section 3.2).
- Gamma examples (Table 2): strawberries 1–1.5 kGy extended shelf life with limited TSS/pH/TA change; pears 1.5–1.7 kGy delayed decay 16 days; pomegranate juice TPC fell as dose rose 0.4–2 kGy; green onion 1.2 kGy raised polyphenols (163–188 µM Trolox eq.) but ascorbic acid 364 → 213 µM.
- Authors argue irradiation is more versatile than HPP/PEF (no spore kill; liquid/semi-moist limits) or cold plasma (lipid oxidation, firmness/color loss) (Introduction), while still facing consumer-acceptance barriers (Conclusion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Mini-review; in-text citations are often stripped in the extracted file, so many “reported that” clauses lack recoverable source IDs.
- Produce vs meat regulatory doses are mixed in the same paragraphs.
- Quality outcomes are dose- and commodity-specific (vitamin C and firmness often fall at higher kGy).
- No original microbial statistics.

## Relevance to our research question
Irradiation arm of nonthermal produce decontamination: documents kGy-scale pathogen logs and frequent nutrient/texture trade-offs. Useful contrast to HPP/US/plasma, not a substitute for those technologies’ food-specific trials.
