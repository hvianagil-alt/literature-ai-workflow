# Enhancing shelf life of pumpkin pulp with natural-based preservative and high pressure processing

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Rossi Ribeiro, Luma; Leichtweis, Maria G.; Landgräber, Eva; Silva, Beatriz; Azevedo, Miguel; Pereira, Carla; Carocho, Márcio; Barros, Lillian; Schlüter, Oliver. 2026. Frontiers in Food Science and Technology 6: 1817509. doi:10.3389/frfst.2026.1817509
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Rossi Ribeiro2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Can HPP (600 MPa/4 min) plus a pumpkin-peel natural-based preservative (NBP) keep formulated pumpkin pulp safe and high-quality for 45 days at 5 °C while cutting or replacing potassium sorbate, compared with thermal pasteurization?

## Methods
*Cucurbita moschata* butternut pulp (67% yield after peel/seed removal). Five arms: A1 pulp + industrial mix (no sorbate) + HPP; A2 mix with sorbate + pasteurization; A3 mix with sorbate + HPP; A4 mix without sorbate + NBP + HPP; A5 mix with 50% sorbate + NBP + HPP. Mix: natural β-carotene, starch, citric acid, citrate. HPP: Unipress U4000, 600 MPa / 4 min / ambient, vacuum Whirl-Pak. Heat: retort 90 °C / 3 min hold, 15 min including come-up, glass jars. Storage 5 °C, dark, 45 d; analyses days 0, 21, 45. Microbes: TAM (PCA 37 °C 48 h), Enterobacteriaceae (Endo), molds/yeasts (DRBC 30 °C 72 h), plated in duplicate. Color (Nikon + Octave L*a*b*), pH, TPA texture, β-carotene, lutein, γ-tocopherol, TPC, FRAP.

## Sample / data
Pumpkins from a Berlin market, processed at ATB Potsdam; NBP from pumpkin peel. TPC example at day 0 (mg GAE/100 g dw): A1 48.86 ± 1.13; A2 42.52 ± 2.02; A3 38.01 ± 2.61; A4 52.49 ± 4.20; A5 52.55 ± 1.69 (Table 8).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Five-arm refrigerated shelf-life trial (HPP ± NBP ± sorbate vs thermal pasteurization)
- **Population / model:** Formulated pumpkin (*Cucurbita moschata*) pulp
- **n:** microbiology plated in duplicate; TPC table means ± SD (independent n not uniformly stated); one p-value line uses n = 15
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 600 MPa / 4 min vs retort 90 °C / 3 min (15 min total); NBP from peel; potassium sorbate at 100%, 50%, or 0%
- **Primary endpoint:** Microbial counts (TAM, Enterobacteriaceae, molds/yeasts) over 45 d at 5 °C; color, TPA, TPC/antioxidants/carotenoids
- **Primary result:** No microbial growth detected in any formulation for 45 days. NBP samples better kept color and firmness and had higher TPC (A4/A5 ~52.5 mg GAE/100 g dw at day 0 vs A3 38.01). Thermal A2 showed larger texture (firmness, stickiness, chewiness) and bioactive (β-carotene, lutein, γ-tocopherol) changes. Authors conclude HPP + NBP can cut sorbate 50% or replace it without compromising safety or quality under these conditions.
- **Cannot show:** Open retail abuse temperatures; spore-forming pathogens; consumer sensory; that HPP alone without mix/NBP would be sterile for 45 d (no untreated pulp arm).

## Key findings
- Authors attribute zero counts to combined processing, industrial mix, and NBP (Abstract; Results).
- Pasteurized A2 was darker than HPP pulps (Results).
- TPC did not deteriorate in a way they judged nutritionally important over 45 d at 5 °C (Discussion).
- HPP is described as cold pasteurization that does not inactivate spores or all enzymes (Introduction).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- No raw unprocessed control in the five coded arms.
- Exact sorbate and NBP concentrations in the mix are not fully numeric in the extract.
- Duplicate plating; product is a formulated pulp (starch, acid, carotene), not plain puree.

## Relevance to our research question
Juice/pulp-adjacent HPP: 600 MPa/4 min plus peel extract matched 45-day microbial stability of sorbate+heat while better holding color, firmness, and phenolics. Nutrition endpoints are carotenoids, tocopherol, TPC, FRAP—not protein.

## Open questions / things to verify
