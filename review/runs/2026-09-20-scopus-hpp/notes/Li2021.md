# High Pressure Processing vs. Thermal Pasteurization of Whole Concord Grape Puree: Effect on Nutritional Value, Quality Parameters and Refrigerated Shelf Life

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Li, Yuanyuan; Padilla-Zakour, Olga I. 2021. Foods 10(11): 2608. doi:10.3390/foods10112608. PMC8620349
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Li2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How do industrial HPP (600 MPa, 3 min, 5 °C) and mild heat pasteurization (63 °C, 3 min) compare for microbial stability, physicochemical quality, phenolics/antioxidants, enzymes, composition (with vs without seeds), and consumer liking of whole Concord grape puree (skin and seeds, no waste) during refrigerated storage?

## Methods
Same-batch Finger Lakes Concord grapes (Oct 2019, Welch’s), destemmed, ground, high-shear homogenized (<40 °C). HPP: Hiperbaric 55 (quality/shelf-life) or Hiperbaric 525 (sensory), 600 MPa / 3 min / 5 °C in 4 oz PET. Heat: steam kettle 63 °C / 3 min (designed for >5-log of *E. coli* O157:H7, *Salmonella*, *L. monocytogenes* from D52 = 23 min, z = 4.8 °C plus 5-fold safety factor), ice-cooled. Storage 4 ± 1 °C, sampled monthly up to 5 months. Microbial APC (PCA, 30 °C, 48–72 h) and Y&M (PDA pH 3.5). Physicochemical: TSS, pH, TA, color, PSD, SSR, viscosity. TP, TMA, DPPH/ABTS, PPO/POD residual activity. Proximate on C/W, C/O, HPP/W, HT/W (triplicate; Dairy One). Sensory: 101 untrained panelists, 9-point hedonic plus intensity/JAR/ranking (IRB-approved). Experiments in triplicate; Tukey after ANOVA.

## Sample / data
Whole Concord grape puree including broken seeds and skin. Control day 1: aw 0.98, pH 3.35 ± 0.01, TSSC 18.0 ± 0.1 °Brix, TA 1.2 ± 0.1% tartaric acid. Fresh APC 6.33 log CFU/g FW; Y&M 6.31 log CFU/g FW. Sensory samples refrigerated 1 week before tasting.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Parallel processing comparison (HPP vs mild heat vs untreated) with refrigerated shelf-life and consumer sensory
- **Population / model:** Whole Concord grape (*Vitis labrusca*) puree with skin and seeds
- **n:** quality assays in triplicate; sensory n = 101 untrained panelists; composition four groups in triplicate
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 600 MPa / 3 min / 5 °C vs heat 63 °C / 3 min vs untreated; 4 °C storage up to 5 months
- **Primary endpoint:** APC and yeast/mold (log CFU/g FW) during storage; also phenolics, enzymes, color, texture, consumer liking
- **Primary result:** Both HPP and HT caused a 5-log reduction in fresh APC; HPP Y&M <1.0 log CFU/g FW vs HT 1.22. After 4 months, HPP APC 2.0 log CFU/g FW and Y&M still undetectable; HT APC 3.6 and Y&M 3.1. At 5 months HPP APC 2.7 and Y&M 2.4; HT spoiled (>6 log CFU/g FW) with visible fungi. PPO residual 75.2 ± 7.4% (HPP) vs 22.6 ± 2.4% (HT); POD 80.7 ± 6.8% vs 10.2 ± 1.1%. After 4 months TP fell from 3.8 to 2.6 mg GAE/g (HPP) and 3.6 to 2.9 (HT); TMA unchanged. HPP overall liking, purchase intent, and first-rank preference (59% of panelists) exceeded HT (p < 0.05).
- **Cannot show:** Pathogen challenge in this puree (safety inferred from juice HACCP literature and process design); ambient shelf-life; long-term aroma chemistry.

## Key findings
- Day-1 HPP: pH 3.35 ± 0.01, TSSC 18.3 ± 0.3 °Brix, TA 1.1 ± 0.1%; HT similar; parameters mostly stable except HT pH rose to 3.42 ± 0.05 at 4 months with microbial growth (Section 3.2.1).
- HT ΔE >2 (noticeable) on day 1; HPP ΔE <1.5. HT b* and browning index higher than HPP (Section 3.2.2).
- HPP SSR 44.3 ± 3.5% (similar to untreated), lower than HT; after 4 months HPP SSR 65.8 ± 0.3% while HT gelled. D90 at 4 months: HT 294 ± 36 µm vs HPP 218 ± 35 µm (Section 3.2.3).
- Untreated TP 3.0 ± 0.1 mg/g GAE; TMA 628 ± 35 mg/kg CGE. HPP raised TP vs control on day 1 (p < 0.05) (Section 3.3).
- Seeds ~5× crude fiber vs seedless puree; PUFA 68% of FA with seeds vs ~2× SFA in seedless; linoleic acid 65% with seeds vs 44% without (Section 3.5).
- HPP higher smoothness; no difference in sweetness/sourness; modest but positive liking as a stand-alone product (Section 3.6).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors call for more work on tannin digestibility/gut microbiome, PME-related texture, and aroma compounds (Conclusions).
- HPP sensory processed at a different plant than the shelf-life HPP unit.
- Enzyme residual activity remains high after HPP; quality held at 4 °C despite that.

## Relevance to our research question
Strong juice/puree evidence: industrial 600 MPa/3 min matches or beats mild heat on microbes and refrigerated shelf-life (≥4–5 months), keeps phenolics/antioxidants similar, and scores better with consumers, while leaving most PPO/POD active. Seed inclusion is a nutrition finding, not an HPP effect.

## Open questions / things to verify
- “At least 4 months” (abstract) vs “at least 5 months” microbial stability (conclusions) — 5-month HPP counts were still <3 log but rising.
- Composition tables (minerals, FA mg/g) live in paper tables not fully numeric in the extract.
