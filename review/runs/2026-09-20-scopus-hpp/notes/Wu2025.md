# The Effect of Non-Thermal Processing on the Fate of Pathogenic Bacteria and Hidden Hazardous Risks

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Wu Y, Li X, Ma X, Ren Q, Sun Z, Pan H. 2025. Foods 14(13):2374. doi:10.3390/foods14132374. PMC12248489
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Wu2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

How do non-thermal technologies (HPP, PEF, CAP/PAW, HPCD, ultrasound) inactivate foodborne bacteria, by what mechanisms, and when do they instead induce a viable but nonculturable (VBNC) state that culture methods miss and that may retain virulence?

## Methods

Open-access narrative review (MDPI Foods). Compares thermal vs non-thermal (Table 1), then per technology: inactivation tables (log N0/N and conditions), bactericidal mechanisms, VBNC evidence, and shared vs treatment-specific VBNC pathways (ATP depletion emphasized). No new data (Data Availability: none created). Funded by NSFC 32302245.

## Sample / data

Compilation of published inactivation and VBNC studies in juices, seafood, meat, produce, dairy, broths, and model systems. Review N not reported. Table 1 quality contrasts include aronia juice phenolics 1.21 ± 0.01 mg/mL thermal vs 1.55 ± 0.02 mg/mL HPP; orange juice ethyl butyrate loss 22.4% thermal vs 5.1% PEF.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative review of non-thermal inactivation plus VBNC food-safety risk
- **Population / model:** Foodborne bacteria and viruses in foods and model media (E. coli, Salmonella, Listeria, Vibrio, L. plantarum, norovirus, etc.) as in the cited trials
- **n:** not reported (narrative review; no new samples)
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP (stated 100–800 MPa), PEF (typically 20–80 kV/cm, µs–ms), CAP/PAW, HPCD, US, often plus hurdles (freezing, nisin, mild heat, CO2, antimicrobials) vs thermal pasteurization/UHT and vs culture-only kill estimates
- **Primary endpoint:** Culture-based log inactivation (log N0/N) versus viability assays (epifluorescence, flow cytometry) indicating VBNC/sublethal survivors; mechanistic endpoints (membrane, ribosomes, ROS/RNS, ATP)
- **Primary result:** Non-thermal methods can give large culture reductions (HPP examples in Table 2: pitaya juice L. innocua and S. cerevisiae >5 log at 550 MPa/16 min and 600 MPa/12 min; V. parahaemolyticus 9 log in TSB+2.5% NaCl at 300 MPa/10 min; cantaloupe puree Salmonella and L. monocytogenes >6.7 log at 500 MPa/8 °C/5 min; E. coli O157:H7 6.4–8.34 log in carrot/grapefruit juices at 615 MPa/15 °C/2 min). Sublethal HPP can leave VBNC cells: Ritz et al. 400–600 MPa / 2 °C / 10 min gave 8-log drop in culturable L. monocytogenes and S. Typhimurium but ~4-log still viable by direct viable count, resuscitable at 4 °C and 20 °C; Karamova et al. 300 MPa / 23 °C / 15 min, >7-log culture reduction of S. Typhimurium vs 0.72-log by PI-excluding FCM. Berlin et al. 200–300 MPa / 25 °C / 5–15 min inactivated pathogenic Vibrio without VBNC. HPP + CO2 or nisin reduced resuscitable VBNC while increasing kill (Yang et al., as cited).
- **Cannot show:** Cannot quantify a universal VBNC fraction after commercial HPP, prove that all VBNC cells cause foodborne disease, or replace challenge-study validation with this review.

## Key findings

- Thermal methods degrade vitamin C, polyphenols, carotenoids and can form acrylamide; HPP acts on non-covalent bonds at ambient T, avoiding Maillard/caramelization. (Introduction; HPP section)
- Table 1: PEF natural-gas savings estimated 100%, electricity up to 18%; HPP specific energy for can sterilization reduced from 300 to 270 kJ/kg (as cited). Lettuce juice Vc 1.25 ± 0.02 vs 0.22 ± 0.01 µg/100 g and chlorophyll 51.91 ± 0.60 vs 61.18 ± 1.61 µg/mL (thermal vs non-thermal as printed). (Table 1)
- HPP mechanisms: SEM membrane rupture and permeability; lipid-bilayer phase transition; ribosomal subunit dissociation; higher-order protein aggregation without primary-structure cleavage. Hurdles: nisin access after outer-membrane damage; freezing gels lipids and increases pressure susceptibility. (Section 2.2)
- VBNC: culture-negative but metabolically active; may keep toxins/invasiveness (Vibrio, Legionella, C. jejuni, L. monocytogenes cited); Japanese salted salmon roe outbreak linked to VBNC EHEC. (Introduction)
- HPP VBNC: present throughout treatment; pressure resistance correlates with resuscitable VBNC (Yang et al.). L. plantarum VBNC at 400 and 500 MPa / 600 s; resuscitation from day 6 delayed post-acidification ≥24 days; riboflavin metabolism and NADH drop. (Section 2.3)
- PEF: 20–80 kV/cm; industrial scale limited by field uniformity. Rowan reported VBNC in B. cereus and L. monocytogenes; Yaqub et al. found PEF did not induce VBNC in E. coli, B. cereus, L. monocytogenes; P. putida VBNC resuscitated in BHI. (PEF)
- Shared VBNC theme: ATP depletion; CAP adds oxidative-stress responses; HPCD adds acidification/asr-driven aggregation. (Section before conclusions)
- Authors: pathogenicity of VBNC still inconclusive but some retain virulence; detection and predictive models are needed. (Conclusions)

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- VBNC literature is uneven across technologies; mechanisms still limited; mostly theoretical vs plant control.
- Culture methods overestimate kill if VBNC/sublethal injury is ignored.
- Extracted HTML dropped many in-text citation numbers and some Table 1 column alignments (lettuce Vc assignment thermal vs non-thermal looks internally inconsistent with the chlorophyll/browning rows).
- HPP cost/maintenance cited as limiting commercial spread despite use.

## Relevance to our research question

Highly relevant caveat for HPP/hurdle claims: large plate-count reductions can hide VBNC pathogens, while HPP+nisin/CO2/mild heat is presented as reducing that reservoir.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Table 1 lettuce juice vitamin C (thermal 1.25 vs non-thermal 0.22 µg/100 g) contradicts the paper’s narrative that non-thermal better preserves nutrients; likely a swapped column — check the PDF table before citing.
- Confirm Ritz 2006 “about 4-log surviving” interpretation (absolute survivors vs log remaining).
- PEF VBNC findings conflict between Rowan and Yaqub; review does not resolve which conditions differ.
