# A review of recent advances in the decontamination of mycotoxin and inactivation of fungi by ultrasound

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Hashemi Moosavi, Motahareh; Mousavi Khaneghah, Amin; Javanmardi, Fardin; Hadidi, Milad; Hadian, Zahra; Jafarzadeh, Shima; Huseyn, Elcin; Sant'Ana, Anderson S. 2021. Ultrasonics Sonochemistry 79:105755. doi:10.1016/j.ultsonch.2021.105755
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Hashemi Moosavi2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What do studies from 2018–2021 report about ultrasound (alone or combined with heat, ozone, electrolyzed water, or other hurdles) for inactivating fungi and degrading mycotoxins in foods, and which process parameters and matrix factors change efficacy?

## Methods
Narrative review. Authors searched PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and Google Scholar (2018–2021) with terms mycotoxin, inactivation, ultrasound, decontamination. They summarize cavitation mechanisms, factors (frequency, intensity, time, temperature, pressure, viscosity, pH, fat/lactose), food-quality side effects, and a table of recent food trials (Table 1). No meta-analysis or pooled statistics.

## Sample / data
Cited foods include strawberry, tomato juice, turmeric, wheat, green asparagus, Chinese cabbage, cucumber, orange juice, lettuce, alfalfa/mung sprouts, plus mycotoxin model solutions (AFB1, DON, ZEN, OTA). Review n = not applicable.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative review of 2018–2021 ultrasound–fungi/mycotoxin literature
- **Population / model:** Fungi, yeasts, and mycotoxins in foods and model liquids as reported by cited authors
- **n:** not applicable
- **Intervention / comparator:** Power ultrasound (typically 20–40 kHz; bath or probe) ± hot water, ozone, electrolyzed water, essential oil, electric current vs untreated or water dip
- **Primary endpoint:** Heterogeneous: log CFU reductions, fungal decay delay, % mycotoxin degradation
- **Primary result:** No single pooled estimate. Illustrative cited results: Liu et al., 20 kHz, 6.6 W/cm2, 80 min, 85.1% AFB1 degradation; same group, 25% duty cycle, >95% degradation of AFB1, DON, ZEN, OTA. Starek et al. tomato juice, 40 W/cm2, 10 min: mesophilic aerobes reduced 2.8–5.4 (authors’ CFU/g wording). Chinese cabbage sweep US: mesophiles 0.24–1.83 log CFU/g; yeast/mold 0.15–2.26 log CFU/g. Orange juice thermo-US 24 kHz, 50 °C: ~1.5 log S. cerevisiae; with cinnamon leaf oil 2.52 vs 1.32 log without the oil. DON degradation fell from 50% to 37% when concentration rose from 2000 to 4000 µg/L (20 kHz, 40 min).
- **Cannot show:** That US reliably meets 5-log bacterial pasteurization in all matrices; that combined US is always synergistic (lettuce electro-sonication sometimes antagonistic); that mycotoxin degradation products are non-toxic (not demonstrated here)

## Key findings
- Mechanism: transient cavitation (~5000 K / 5000 atm in the review’s wording), membrane thinning, and radicals (·OH, H2O2) attacking cells and toxin molecules (Sections 2–3).
- Efficacy rises with amplitude/time and generally with temperature >50 °C; fruits/vegetables often 20–40 kHz, 1–60 min, 20–40 °C (Section 4).
- High viscosity and fat/lactose can protect microbes; acidic, low-viscosity media improve kill.
- Quality is condition-dependent: some trials retain ascorbic acid/lycopene/chlorophyll better than heat; others lose vitamin C or alter protein structure at long/high power (Section 5).
- Combined US + ozone on strawberry: ~2 log mesophiles; fungal decay delayed 4 days and cold shelf life +6 days (Maryam et al., cited).
- Rudik et al. wheat: 24–26 kHz, 1 W/cm2, up to 98% microorganisms inactivated (authors’ report).
- Conclusion: US can be effective at low temperature if optimized per product; pairing with heat or chemicals can help or hurt and needs more work.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Four-year window only; not a systematic review with inclusion counts.
- Table 1 mixes bacteria, yeasts, and molds; several rows are not mycotoxins.
- Units in some cited tomato-juice results (CFU/g vs log10 CFU/g) are internally inconsistent.
- Industrial scale, sensory panels, and toxin-breakdown safety are largely unaddressed.

## Relevance to our research question
Ultrasound evidence for fungal/mycotoxin control and quality side effects, including hurdle combinations. Complements juice/produce HPP–US papers but is not HPP- or plasma-primary.
