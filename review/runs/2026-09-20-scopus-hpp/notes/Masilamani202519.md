# Recent advances in nonthermal hurdle approach in the food sector

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Masilamani S, Natarajan V, Radhakrishnan M. 2025. Quality Assurance and Safety of Crops & Foods 17(2):19–36. doi:10.15586/qas.v17i2.1515
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Masilamani202519.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

What are the principles, applications, and synergistic effects of combining nonthermal technologies as hurdles (ultrasound, HHP, PEF, plasma, UV, ozone, pulsed light, irradiation/MEF) for microbial safety, shelf life, nutrition/sensory retention, residue reduction, and extraction in foods?

## Methods

Open-access narrative review article. Sections cover why nonthermal hurdles, microbial inactivation, disinfectant/chemical-residue reduction, solid vs liquid foods, extraction, most-used combinations (especially ultrasound + plasma-activated/functionalized water, HHP, or PEF), future challenges, and SDG alignment. Tables 1–2 compile cited treatment parameters and effects. No systematic search, inclusion criteria, or pooled statistics.

## Sample / data

Secondary compilation of published food studies (juices, produce, meat, fish, milk, powders, oils, wastewater). Number of papers screened/included is not reported.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Open-access narrative review of nonthermal hurdle combinations
- **Population / model:** Solid and liquid foods (juices, produce, meat, seafood, dairy, powders, extracts) as reported in the cited studies
- **n:** not reported (narrative review; no study-count)
- **Intervention / comparator:** Paired nonthermal hurdles (e.g. thermosonication + HHP; US + UV; PEF + US; PAW + US; ozone + UV-C; IPL + plasma + UV) vs single technologies or untreated/thermal comparators in the source papers
- **Primary endpoint:** Microbial log reductions (CFU/mL or CFU/g), enzyme inactivation, residue degradation, extraction yield, and quality/sensory retention as reported by cited authors
- **Primary result:** Authors conclude combinations outperform single methods by allowing milder individual intensities. Recited examples include: blueberry juice thermosonication (240 W, 40 kHz, 25 or 45 °C, 15 min) + 400 or 600 MPa / 5 min reduced microbes below 1 log CFU/mL and inactivated PPO (Chen et al. 2021); pineapple juice US 33 kHz / 22.95 min + UV 1.577 J/cm² / 10 min achieved 5-log reduction of bacteria and yeast (Anjaly et al. 2022); PAW 10 min + US 15 min degraded chlorothalonil on tomato by 89.29% (Ali et al.); sweet orange juice pulsed light 2.4 kV / 180 s + US 80 W / 360 s gave 98.3% PME inactivation, >5-log native flora reduction, 99.4% phenolic retention, 5% vitamin C loss (Sahoo & Chakraborty 2023).
- **Cannot show:** Cannot establish a single optimal hurdle recipe, industrial energy savings, or SDG impact from this paper’s own data; cited log reductions are not re-analyzed.

## Key findings

- Hurdle aim: multi-target, moderate preservation so no one treatment is severe enough to ruin quality. (Introduction; “Why a Nonthermal as a Hurdle?”)
- Chen et al. 2021 blueberry juice: 400/600 MPa + thermosonication 25/45 °C → microbial load <1 log CFU/mL plus PPO inactivation via pressure, cavitation, and free-radical oxidation. (Microbial inactivation)
- Anjaly et al. 2022 pineapple juice: US + UV-C 254 nm; 5-log bacteria and yeast; organoleptic quality nearly like fresh juice. (same section)
- Hwang et al. 2023 IPL (24.4 J/cm², 30 V, 30 min) + atmospheric plasma (1 kV, 30 Hz) + UV 253.7 nm: max 1.33 ± 0.24 log on sesame seeds; black pepper 0.41 ± 0.04; red pepper 0.52 ± 0.05. (same)
- Lara et al. 2022 beef: aqueous ozone 0.9 ppm spray + UV-C 69 mJ/cm² / 30 s; 1.7 log E. coli after 10 cycles with 1 h gaps. Authors note combined antimicrobial effect can be lower than individuals. (same)
- Table 1 examples: PAW + US on cherry tomato chlorothalonil up to 89.29% (97.25% during storage); US + ozone pesticide removal 79.1–92.2% on vegetables; PEF + US potato chips acrylamide reduction up to 66%; PEF + US milk fat globule 0.22 ± 0.02 µm at 1 kJ/mL US. (Table 1)
- Table 2: black pepper CP + UV-C; catfish ozone water 13.28 mg/L / 10 min + UHP 200 MPa / 10 min; rice germ plasma jet + pulsed light + UV-C reduced natural bacteria, mold, and yeast from 3.7 and 5.7 log CFU/g to 2.0 and 1.0 log CFU/g. Crayfish US + PAW: 1.17 log CFU/g TVC at 40 min. Sonozone wastewater: 5-log within 5 min; energy down up to 67% vs individual. (Table 2)
- Extraction: US 600 W / 22 kHz + PEF 8 kV / 30 µs / 15 Hz raised virgin olive oil yield from 16.3% to 18.1% (US alone 17.8%) and tocopherols/tocotrienols +15.9% (Grillo et al. 2022). PEF 0.75 kV/cm, 600 µs on potato peels: steroidal alkaloids 1856.2 µg/g, 99.9% increase vs untreated (Hossain et al. 2014). (Extraction)
- Most-followed combinations: plasma-activated/functionalized water + US; US + HHP; PEF pretreatment + US. (Most followed combinations)
- Challenges: scale-up machinery, standards, consumer misconceptions, high capital and skilled labor. (Future prospects)

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- High initial investment and skilled operators.
- Need to optimize combinations by food type; wrong pairings can be antagonistic (ozone + UV-C on beef).
- Narrative review; SDG claims (SDG 2, 3, 6, 7, 12, 13, 15) are interpretive, not measured here.
- Some Table 1/2 numbers are from heterogeneous labs; parameters (Hz vs kHz) are as printed.

## Relevance to our research question

Core hurdle paper: documents HHP with thermosonication and other nonthermal pairs for inactivation, quality, residue removal, and extraction — the exact combination lens of this review.

- Ali 2022 vs Ali 2023 chlorothalonil studies are both cited; 89.29% is tied to PAW 10 min + US 15 min in the narrative.
