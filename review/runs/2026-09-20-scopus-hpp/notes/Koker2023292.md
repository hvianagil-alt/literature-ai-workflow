# Effects of high hydrostatic pressure on antimicrobial protein stability and the rheological and shelf-life properties of donkey milk

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Koker, Alperen; Ozturkoglu-Budak, Sebnem; Alpas, Hami. 2023. Journal of Dairy Research 90: 292–298. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022029923000572
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Koker2023292.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How do HHP (pressure × temperature × time) and mild heat affect total aerobic bacteria, native lysozyme and lactoferrin, color, rheology, and refrigerated vs room-temperature shelf-life of donkey milk compared with untreated milk?

## Methods
Laboratory SITEC HHP (100 mL vessel, distilled water). Full factorial: 200, 400, 500 MPa × 25, 35, 45 °C × 5, 10, 15 min (27 HHP treatments). Heat: 75 °C for 1 or 2 min in a water bath, then ice-water cool. Two replicates; 29 treatments total. TABC on PCA, 37 °C, 24 h. Lysozyme and lactoferrin by Billakanti et al. SPR-style method (supplement). Power-law rheology (Kinexus). CIELab. Shelf-life at 4 °C and 25 °C on days 0, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28 for untreated, selected HHP (400 MPa–25 °C–5 min), and heat (75 °C–2 min). Three-way ANOVA, Tukey p < 0.05. Microbial and enzyme assays in triplicate.

## Sample / data
Donkey milk from Korukoy Farm, Kırklareli, Türkiye; held at 4 °C; 25 mL HDPE vials. Initial TABC 4.04 log CFU mL−1 (figure caption); abstract/body also say initial count <5 log CFU mL−1. Composition (protein, fat, dry matter) in Supplementary Table S1. Lactoferrin in this milk 0.2 mg/mL (authors contrast with 0.097 mg/mL in a prior study).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Factorial HHP vs heat laboratory trial plus 28-day shelf-life at 4 °C and 25 °C
- **Population / model:** Fresh donkey milk
- **n:** two treatment replicates; microbial/enzyme experiments in triplicate
- **Intervention / comparator:** HHP 200–500 MPa at 25–45 °C for 5–15 min vs heat 75 °C / 1–2 min vs untreated
- **Primary endpoint:** TABC reduction (log CFU mL−1) and native lysozyme/lactoferrin activity loss (%); shelf-life TABC, pH, acidity, color, flow indices
- **Primary result (body text):** HHP 200, 400, 500 MPa reduced TABC by 0.85, 1.58, and 4.04 log CFU mL−1. Heat 75 °C / 1 and 2 min: 0.72 and 1.34 log reduction. Lysozyme activity losses 16.4, 21.6, 28.1% at 200, 400, 500 MPa; lactoferrin 20, 36.8, 55.5%. Heat lysozyme losses 71.6% (1 min) and 93% (2 min); lactoferrin 35.6% and 53.4%. Shelf-life selected conditions: HHP 1.48 log and heat 1.34 log TABC reduction (p < 0.05). Heat-treated milk at 4 °C reached 5.15 log CFU/mL on day 21; lowest TABC at day 28 was HHP stored at 4 °C.
- **Cannot show:** Pathogen challenge; vitamin or full amino-acid nutrition; sensory; commercial HHP of donkey milk. Abstract numbers for 75 °C/2 min and 400 MPa do not match the Results section (see Open questions).

## Key findings
- Holding time (5–15 min) did not change lysozyme/lactoferrin losses (P > 0.05); pressure did (Results).
- 25 and 35 °C during HHP did not significantly change microbial load or those proteins; 45 °C reduced them (Conclusion).
- Heat-treated milk had the highest flow consistency index K and lower flow behavior index n vs untreated and HHP (Conclusion; rheology).
- L* fell during storage in all samples; HHP milk was darker (lower L*) than untreated/heat at day 0 (Table 1: HHP L* 54.84 ± 0.87 vs untreated 63.92 ± 0.91 at 4 °C day 0).
- pH of HHP and heat samples at 4 °C stayed largely unchanged for 28 d; untreated pH dropped after 14 d (Results).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Heat at usual pasteurization temperatures is described as unsuitable for donkey milk’s heat-labile proteins (Introduction).
- Abstract vs Results numerical mismatch for inactivation and enzyme % (must not mix them).
- Small vessel (100 mL); two process replicates.
- TABC only (no Enterobacteriaceae/pathogens in the extract).

## Relevance to our research question
Dairy HHP vs heat with a nutrition-adjacent endpoint (antimicrobial whey proteins). 400 MPa retains more lysozyme/lactoferrin than 75 °C while giving similar or slightly better TABC reduction than 2 min heat; 500 MPa gives ~4 log TABC cut but ~28–56% protein activity loss.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Abstract: heat 75 °C/2 min = 1.50 log inactivation, lysozyme 58%, lactoferrin 82%; HHP 400 MPa = 1.80 log, 22% and 37% enzyme losses. Results: 1.34 log / 93% lysozyme / 53.4% lactoferrin for 75 °C/2 min, and 1.58 log / 21.6% / 36.8% at 400 MPa. Prefer Results/figure caption (initial 4.04 log) over the abstract.
