# Application of High Hydrostatic Pressure (Long Holding Time vs. Two Consecutive Short Cycles) for the Preservation of Lamb Burgers Enriched with Lupinus albus Flour

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** González-Cantillo, N., Martín-Mateos, M.J., Sánchez-Ordóñez, M., López-Parra, M.M., García-Parra, J.J., Matías, J., Ramírez-Bernabé, M.R. (2026). Foods 15(4):789. doi: 10.3390/foods15040789
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/González-Cantillo2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Do two 1 s cycles at 600 MPa match a single 4 min cycle (same equipment time) for microbial control of lamb burgers, and does 1% *Lupinus albus* flour (conventional vs compression-milled) help colour and oxidation during 14 days at 6 °C?

## Methods

Lamb burgers (~100 g; 40% flank / 60% leg; salt and spices) as control or with 1% w/w lupine flour from *L. albus* var. Orden Dorado: Flour 1 hammer-milled (1 mm), Flour 2 cold compression-milled. Vacuum packed. HHP1: two consecutive 600 MPa / 1 s cycles; HHP2: one 600 MPa / 4 min cycle; Hiperbaric Wave 6000/55, water 15 °C, come-up 3.5 min, instantaneous decompression. Stored 6 ± 1 °C, dark, 14 days. Day-1 and day-14: mesophiles, psychrophiles, moulds/yeasts, *S. aureus*, sulphite-reducing anaerobes/*C. perfringens*, coliforms and *E. coli* (detection 10 CFU g^-1 except *S. aureus* 100 CFU g^-1). CIE L*a*b*, TBARS (mg MDA kg^-1), protein carbonyls (nmol mg protein^-1), phenolics. Proximate and FAME on day-0 characterisation burgers. Sensory: 8 trained panellists, grilled to 72 °C internal, day 1 only (0–10 scales). n = 5 burgers per treatment for storage chemistry/microbiology; untreated day-14 burgers discarded for high load. Two-way ANOVA treatment × formulation; Tukey; p ≤ 0.05 / 0.01 / 0.001.

## Sample / data

22 kg lamb across assays; 90 burgers in the HHP storage grid (5 × 3 formulations × 3 pressure treatments × 2 times) plus 15 characterisation burgers; 54 grilled burgers for sensory. Flour characterisation n = 3.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Factorial formulation × HHP schedule × storage; uninoculated burgers (natural microbiota).
- **Population / model:** Refrigerated lamb burgers ± 1% lupine flour.
- **n:** 5 burgers per cell for micro/colour/oxidation; sensory 8 panellists, 7 sessions; flour chemistry n = 3.
- **Intervention / comparator:** No HHP vs two 600 MPa / 1 s cycles vs one 600 MPa / 4 min; Flour 1 vs Flour 2 vs meat-only; day 1 vs day 14 at 6 °C.
- **Primary endpoint:** Log10 CFU g^-1 of indicator/pathogen groups; TBARS; carbonyls; CIE colour; grilled sensory scores.
- **Primary result:** HHP cut mesophiles from >7 log CFU g^-1 (no HHP, day 1) to 3.7–4.5 log CFU g^-1. Psychrophiles fell from 7.6–7.8 to about 1 log CFU g^-1. Coliforms (2.8–3.2 log) and *E. coli* (1.2–1.4 log) went below 1 log CFU g^-1 and stayed there to day 14. *S. aureus* fell from 2.2–2.6 log to <2 log. Formulation did not drive day-1 microbial counts. HHP1 and HHP2 were not different for those taxa at day 14.
- **Cannot show:** A ≥5-log challenge of *L. monocytogenes*, *Salmonella* or STEC (authors explicitly say this is not a pathogen validation). Consumer purchase of *raw* HHP burgers (sensory was grilled).
- **Lipid oxidation:** Day-1 TBARS 0.1–0.3 mg MDA kg^-1. At day 14, Flour 2 + HHP1 had 0.1 mg kg^-1 vs control 0.5 and Flour 1 0.4; under HHP2, Flour 2 0.3 vs Flour 1 0.6 vs control 0.9 mg kg^-1. TBARS rose in storage except in Flour 2 burgers.
- **Colour:** HHP increased L* (no-HHP ~46.7–47.3; HHP samples paler). After 14 days HHP1 L* 55.4–55.9; HHP2 57.0–58.2. Redness fell more after the long cycle than after two short cycles.
- **Flour phenolics:** Flour 2 74.0 mg 100 g^-1 vs Flour 1 36.8 mg 100 g^-1; ABTS antioxidant activity not significantly different. 1% flour did not change burger moisture, protein, fat or SFA/MUFA/PUFA vs control.
- **Sensory (grilled, day 1):** Appearance, odour, taste, texture, overall 6.8–7.9; no significant treatment or formulation effects; unpleasant taste minimal.
- **Recovery in storage:** Mesophiles in some HHP2 samples reached 6.6 log CFU g^-1 by day 14; psychrophiles 2.6–4.6 log. Moulds/yeasts were better held by the long cycle. Coliforms/*E. coli* did not rebound.

## Key findings

- Two 1 s pulses at 600 MPa matched 4 min at 600 MPa for coliform/*E. coli* control, with less bleaching and less TBARS (abstract; Sections 3.5, 4).
- Compression-milled lupine flour (higher phenolics) was the best lipid-oxidation hurdle, especially with short cycles (Section 3.4).
- Protein carbonyls did not track TBARS (Flour 2 + HHP1 had the *highest* carbonyls at day 14, 5.9 nmol mg protein^-1).
- Instrumental raw-colour damage from HHP disappeared from the grilled sensory scores (Section 3.6).
- Authors refuse to treat 2–5 log indicator reductions as a substitute for pathogen challenge (conclusions).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- No inoculated pathogen validation; FDA 5-log language is discussed as a gap.
- Untreated day-14 burgers were thrown out, so storage contrasts are HHP-only.
- 6 °C storage is warmer than 4 °C.
- 1% flour is a sensory compromise; higher levels were disliked in pretests.
- Phenolic *profile* of flours was not identified; ABTS did not rise with Folin phenolics.
- *C. perfringens* stayed <1 log in all samples, so spore kill cannot be judged.
- Protein-oxidation chemistry is flagged as poorly resolved.
- Abstract repeats the “two short cycles are better” sentence twice (editorial, not data).

## Relevance to our research question

HPP at 600 MPa inactivates vegetative Gram-negatives in a raw comminuted red-meat product and changes quality (whiter, more oxidised). A multi-pulse schedule can hold microbial effect while sparing colour and lipids, and a plant-flour hurdle further slows TBARS. Pathogen kinetics are not provided.
