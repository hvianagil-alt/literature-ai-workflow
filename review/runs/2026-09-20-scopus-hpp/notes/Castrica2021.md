# Effect of High-Pressure Processing on Physico-Chemical, Microbiological and Sensory Traits in Fresh Fish Fillets (Salmo salar and Pleuronectes platessa)

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Castrica, M., Pavlovic, R., Balzaretti, C.M., Curone, G., Brecchia, G., Copelotti, E., Panseri, S., Pessina, D., Arnoldi, C., Chiesa, L.M. (2021). Foods 10(8):1775. doi: 10.3390/foods10081775
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Castrica2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

What does 500 MPa for 2 min at 4 °C do to spoilage microbes, volatiles, histamine, colour, pH, metabolome and sensory scores of vacuum-skin-packed salmon and plaice fillets during refrigerated storage?

## Methods

Fresh *Salmo salar* (~250 g) and *Pleuronectes platessa* (~100 g) fillets were split into untreated vacuum-skin packs (CTRL) vs the same packs HPP-treated at 500 MPa / 2 min / 4 °C, then stored at 4 °C. Sampling: day 0, 3, 6, 8 (both groups) and day 12 (HPP only, because CTRL failed hygiene). n = 3 fillets per group per day. Microbiology (duplicate): Enterobacteriaceae (Petrifilm, 37 °C / 24 h), mesophiles (Petrifilm AC, 30 °C / 48 h), psychrotrophs (4 °C / 10 d), *Pseudomonas* on CFC (25 °C / 48 h). Histamine and untargeted polar metabolome: HPLC-Q-Exactive-Orbitrap (metabolome at D0 only). VOCs: HS-SPME-GC-MS with 4-methyl-2-pentanone IS, reported as ppb IS equivalents. Colour CIE L*a*b* (5 readings, duplicate); pH in triplicate. Sensory: five laboratory panellists, 9-point appearance/odour/texture scales. Nonparametric Mann–Whitney / Wilcoxon, p ≤ 0.05.

## Sample / data

Commercial fresh salmon and plaice fillets, not inoculated. Three biological replicates per group × time. Metabolomics: 72 identified metabolites; volcano plots vs fresh. Sensory n = 5 judges (COVID staff limit).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Split-plot storage study of HPP vs untreated vacuum-skin fillets; destructive sampling over 12 days.
- **Population / model:** Uninoculated fresh salmon and plaice muscle (natural microbiota).
- **n:** 3 fillets per group per day; microbiology in duplicate; colour 5 readings; pH triplicate; sensory 5 judges.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 500 MPa / 2 min / 4 °C vs untreated VSP; 4 °C storage to day 8 (CTRL) or 12 (HPP).
- **Primary endpoint:** Log CFU/g of four bacterial groups; histamine; sulphur VOCs/ketones; L*a*b*; sensory scores; D0 metabolome.
- **Primary result:** HPP significantly reduced Enterobacteriaceae, mesophiles, psychrotrophs and *Pseudomonas* at every sampling day (p ≤ 0.05) and kept treated fillets microbiologically acceptable to day 12. Histamine in HPP fish stayed low until day 12 and remained far below the 100 µg g^-1 legal limit (Reg. EC 1441/2007).
- **Cannot show:** Inactivation kinetics of inoculated *Listeria* or *Vibrio*; a consumer sensory panel; that HPP fillets still look like raw fish (they did not).
- **Colour:** HPP raised L* at every time vs CTRL (p = 0.01). Salmon D0 L* 44.91 ± 2.57 (CTRL) vs 70.25 ± 5.06 (HPP); plaice D0 L* 62.14 ± 3.73 vs 79.50 ± 3.06. a* and b* were not described as consistently shifted in the same way; salmon a* was higher in HPP at D0 (14.10 ± 3.09 vs 10.75 ± 0.98).
- **pH:** No overall HPP effect matching Rode and Hovda; salmon CTRL pH rose from 5.89 ± 0.18 (D0) to 6.58 ± 0.15 (D6).
- **VOCs:** HPP prevented formation of sulphur compounds (methanethiol, dimethyl sulfide/disulfide/trisulfide, mercaptoacetone) and ketones (2,3-butanedione, 3-hydroxy-2-butanone) vs CTRL (p ≤ 0.05 to < 0.01).
- **Sensory (Table 3):** Appearance and texture of HPP salmon at D0 already “not satisfactory” (appearance 3.18 ± 0.06; texture 2.49 ± 0.04) vs CTRL 8.31 ± 0.08 and 8.11 ± 0.11. Odour of HPP salmon stayed high through D6 (8.38 ± 0.06) vs CTRL 5.64 ± 0.74.
- **Metabolome:** Alanyl-tyrosine up-regulated after HPP in both species (proposed marker). Plaice: AMP up, IMP down, with more hypoxanthine/xanthine. Salmon: cysteinyl-phenylalanine down. Free amino-acid pool largely stable.

## Key findings

- 500 MPa / 2 min delayed spoilage microbiota and histamine enough to extend analytical shelf-life from 8 to 12 days at 4 °C (Section 3.3).
- The same treatment cooked-looking the muscle (high L*, opaque, hard) so appearance/texture scores collapsed immediately (Section 3.4; Table 3).
- Odour was the sensory attribute HPP helped, in line with blocked sulphur VOCs and amines (Section 3.1, 3.4).
- Untargeted MS points to protein/nucleotide shifts (Ala-Tyr; AMP/IMP in plaice) rather than a wholesale free-amino-acid scramble (Section 3.2).
- CTRL histamine rose at days 6–8 without a matching microbiological jump; authors suggest proteolysis freeing histidine (Section 3.3).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Sensory panel of five in-house judges during COVID; not a consumer test.
- CTRL not sampled at day 12, so 12-day comparison is one-sided.
- Exact log reductions are in figures, not fully numeric in the extracted text (Yagiz et al. 4–6 log at 450–600 MPa is cited, not this dataset).
- Metabolomics only at D0.
- Vacuum-skin packaging is part of both arms; HPP effect is on top of VSP.
- Species chosen for Italian market volume, not as universal fish models.
- Nonparametric tests; normality failed.

## Relevance to our research question

HPP at a commercial-like 500 MPa / 2 min clearly suppresses spoilage organisms and histamine in two human seafoods, and changes quality: lighter, firmer, more opaque fillets with better odour stability. It is a joint inactivation-and-quality paper, not a pathogen-kinetic study.
