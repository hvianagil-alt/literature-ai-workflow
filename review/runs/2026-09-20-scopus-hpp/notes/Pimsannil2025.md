# Edible portions of precooked blue swimming crab: Chemical composition and effect of chitooligosaccharide conjugate and high-pressure processing on microbial inactivation

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Pimsannil, K., Palamae, S., Fan, X., Zhao, Q., Zhang, B., Benjakul, S. (2025). Food Chemistry: X 25:102070. doi: 10.1016/j.fochx.2024.102070
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Pimsannil2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

How do lump, claw, backfin meat and roe of steam-precooked blue swimming crab (*Portunus pelagicus*) differ chemically and microbiologically, and can chitooligosaccharide–catechin (COSC) conjugate and HPP inactivate residual bacteria in lump meat and roe?

## Methods

Whole crabs were steam-cooked 100–120 °C / 20 min; meats and roe were hand-picked (All Crab Company, Thailand). Proximate (AOAC), SDS-PAGE, FAME-GC, amino acids (lump and roe), SEM, and 16S NGS (ZymoBIOMICS) on lump and roe. COSC: chitosan COS grafted with catechin (10% w/w COS), 200 ppm sprayed on 30 g sample, vacuum packed. HPP: commercial 5 L unit, water medium; 0, 100, 300 and (in results) 500 MPa for 1.5 or 3 min, with or without 200 ppm COSC. Counts (n = 3): APC 37 °C / 3 d; psychrophiles 4 °C / 10 d; *Pseudomonas* isolation agar; H2S-producers on TSI; *Vibrio* on TCBS 37 °C / 18 h. PCA/PERMANOVA on log CFU/g. Colour darkening above 200 ppm COSC noted as unpublished.

## Sample / data

Precooked, not raw, crab portions. Microbiology and NGS focused on lump meat and roe. All chemical and microbial tests in triplicate.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Composition survey of four edible portions plus factorial COSC × HPP challenge of native (not inoculated) microbiota on lump meat and roe.
- **Population / model:** Steam-precooked *P. pelagicus* lump/claw/backfin meat and roe; natural post-picking flora.
- **n:** Triplicate analyses; 30 g packs for HPP; 10 g for stomaching.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 0 vs 200 ppm COSC; HPP 100 / 300 / 500 MPa for 1.5 or 3 min vs untreated control.
- **Primary endpoint:** APC, psychrophiles, *Vibrio* spp., H2S-producers, *Pseudomonas* (log CFU/g); plus proximate, FA, AA, NGS.
- **Primary result:** Control lump APC 6.06 ± 0.10 log CFU/g; roe 6.33 ± 0.03. 200 ppm COSC without HPP cut APC by 0.09 (lump) and 0.10 (roe) log. 100 MPa / 1.5 min cut APC 0.27 (lump) and 0.40 (roe) log. At 300 MPa, PBC, *Vibrio*, H2S-producers and *Pseudomonas* were not detected; lump APC 4.52 ± 0.07 (1.5 min) and 4.26 ± 0.24 (3 min). At 500 MPa (1.5 or 3 min), all five counts were ND in lump meat (and authors state the same for roe), with or without COSC.
- **Cannot show:** Inoculated pathogen D-values; shelf-life after 500 MPa; that COSC adds kill at 500 MPa (authors say it does not); texture after 500 MPa (they warn of structure damage).
- **Proximate (Table 1):** Moisture meats ~76.6–77.5% vs roe 67.60 ± 1.18%; protein highest in roe 20.48 ± 0.13% and lump 19.50 ± 0.66%; fat highest in roe 8.98 ± 0.67% vs lump 1.46 ± 0.56%.
- **Flora:** Moraxellaceae dominated both NGS samples; roe had more Vibrionaceae / *Vibrio*; lump genera *Pseudoalteromonas*, *Psychrobacter*, *Planococcus*, *Vibrio*.
- **Control *Vibrio*:** Lump 5.35 ± 0.12 log CFU/g; roe 5.69 ± 0.09. COSC 200 ppm without HPP: lump *Vibrio* 5.22 ± 0.10.
- **PERMANOVA:** Only HPP pressure significantly structured the microbial-count PCA (p < 0.05); sample type and COSC concentration did not.
- **Proteins:** Meats: myosin heavy chain, actin, tropomyosin. Roe: 144 and 109 kDa bands, no MHC/actin.
- **Amino acids (narrative):** Glu/Gln dominant in lump; leucine dominant in roe.

## Key findings

- Hand-picked precooked crab still carries ~6 log APC and ~5 log *Vibrio*; roe is slightly dirtier and more diverse (Section 3.7; NGS).
- COSC at a colour-acceptable 200 ppm is a weak hurdle (~0.1 log APC).
- 300 MPa knocks *Vibrio*, *Pseudomonas*, psychrophiles and H2S-producers below detection but leaves ~4.3–4.5 log APC on lump meat.
- 500 MPa for 1.5 min is enough for ND on all plated groups; extra time or COSC is unnecessary for that endpoint (Section 3.7).
- Authors recommend COSC + *lower* pressure if 500 MPa wrecks meat/roe structure (not quantified here).
- Meats are PUFA/EPA-DHA richer; roe is SFA/MUFA and fat richer (Tables 1–2).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Methods Section 2.5.2 lists 0, 100 and 300 MPa only; 500 MPa appears in results/table footnotes — protocol write-up is incomplete.
- Native flora, not a defined cocktail; ND is limit-of-detection, not sterility.
- Steam precooking already selected heat-resistant survivors; HPP is a post-picking hygiene step, not a raw-crab process.
- No water activity, pH, or texture numbers after HPP in the extracted results.
- COSC >200 ppm darkened lump meat (data not shown).
- 16S NGS on two sample types, not on HPP-treated packs.
- TCBS *Vibrio* counts are genus-level, not *V. parahaemolyticus* confirmation.

## Relevance to our research question

HPP at 300–500 MPa inactivates residual vegetative bacteria — including *Vibrio* — on a ready-to-eat crab meat/roe, with 500 MPa giving complete plate-count clearance in this design. Quality here is mostly compositional (protein, fat, FA), not HPP-induced quality change. COSC is a minor extra hurdle.

## Open questions / things to verify

- APC “32.0% and 30.0%” reductions at 300 MPa + COSC / 3 min are percent of log counts, not log reductions — do not treat as 32 log.
