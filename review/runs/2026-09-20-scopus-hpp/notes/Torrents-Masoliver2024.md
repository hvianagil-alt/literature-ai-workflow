# Enumeration Agar, Acid Exposure and Sampling Time Are Relevant Factors Accounting for the High-Pressure Inactivation of Vegetative Pathogens in Fruit Puree

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Torrents-Masoliver, B., Jofré, A., Ribas-Agustí, A., Bover-Cid, S. (2024). Foods 13(16):2600. doi: 10.3390/foods13162600
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Torrents-Masoliver2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

How much of the apparent HPP lethality against *L. monocytogenes*, *Salmonella* and *E. coli* in infant-style banana–apple and apple purees is an artefact of enumeration agar, 24 h acid contact before pressure, and sampling 24 h after pressure?

## Methods

Golden apple puree (pH 3.32 ± 0.03, 13.33 ± 0.06 °Brix, 0.68 ± 0.01% malic acid) and banana–apple 1:1 puree (pH 4.02 ± 0.02, 15.47 ± 0.35 °Brix) were hygienised at 600 MPa / 6 min, frozen, then inoculated at about 10^7–10^8 CFU/g. Cocktail experiment: *L. monocytogenes* (CTC1034, Scott A, 12MOB047LM), *Salmonella* (GN085, CTC1003, CTC1754), *E. coli* (LMG 2092T, CTC1029). Single-strain experiment in apple only: 4 *Listeria*, 8 *Salmonella*, 4 *E. coli* including non-toxigenic O157:H7 CECT5947. Treatments: HPP 300 MPa / 2 min (Hiperbaric Wave 6000, water 19 °C, come-up 2.2 ± 0.2 min); acid exposure (AE) 24 h at 4 °C in apple puree; AE then HPP (AEHPP). Counts immediately and after 24 h at 4 °C on TSAYE (T), TSAYE + 4% NaCl (TS), chromogenic selective agar (S), and selective agar with TSAYE overlay (SO). Two independent experiments, plated in duplicate. Sublethal injury = log(counts_T) − log(counts_TS). ANOVA/Tukey, p < 0.05.

## Sample / data

Laboratory fruit purees intended as infant-food analogues (not commercial jars). Strain panels listed in Section 2.1. Cocktail and single-strain designs.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Inoculated challenge; factorial of matrix, agar, acid pre-exposure, and sampling time at one mild HPP condition.
- **Population / model:** Cocktails and single strains of *L. monocytogenes*, *Salmonella* spp. and *E. coli* in apple and banana–apple puree.
- **n:** Two independent experiments; microbiological analysis in duplicate; inoculum ca. 10^7–10^8 CFU/g.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 300 MPa / 2 min vs untreated inoculum; ± 24 h acid exposure in apple puree; immediate vs 24 h delayed count; four agars.
- **Primary endpoint:** Inactivation log(N/N0); sublethal injury log-ratio T vs TS.
- **Primary result:** HPP inactivation was 0.9- to 4.5-fold higher in apple than banana–apple puree. In banana–apple, immediate HPP reductions on non-selective counting were 0.3 ± 0.2 (*Listeria*), 1.4 ± 0.3 (*Salmonella*) and 0.9 ± 0.1 (*E. coli*) log. In apple, *Listeria* cocktail inactivation rose from 1.8 ± 0.3 log immediately to 3.5 ± 0.2 log at 24 h; *Salmonella* 4.3 ± 1.2 then 5.5 ± 0.7 log; *E. coli* 1.8 ± 0.3 log on TSAYE immediately vs 2.8 ± 0.2 log on chromogenic agars.
- **Cannot show:** A 5-log commercial juice process (pressure/time were deliberately mild); thermal vs HPP quality of infant puree; toxigenic STEC (CECT5947 is stx-negative).
- **Most resistant / sensitive in apple (single strains):** *E. coli* O157:H7 CECT5947 was most piezo-resistant (0.6 ± 0.1 log HPP reduction; AEHPP about 1 log). *L. monocytogenes* Scott A was most piezo-sensitive (HPP up to 4.8 log; AEHPP 6.2 ± 0.1 log, and about 6 log when acid-exposed and sampled at 24 h — abstract).
- **AE alone:** *Listeria* 0.5 log (TSAYE) to 2.5 log (SO); *Salmonella* 1.7 ± 0.6 log; *E. coli* 1.0 ± 0.4 log.
- **Agar bias:** Selective chromogenic media overestimated kill vs TSAYE for several treatments (e.g. *E. coli* immediately after HPP or AEHPP).
- **Additivity vs cross-protection:** For most *Listeria* strains AEHPP ≈ AE + HPP; 12MOB089LM was 1.0 log *less* than the sum (possible acid-induced protection). *E. coli* CTC1030 AEHPP (~4 log) was also less than the sum of singles.
- **Sublethal injury (apple, HPP, immediate):** *Listeria* 0.6 ± 0.2 to 1.7 ± 0.2 log (strain-dependent); *Salmonella* 0.9–4.5 log (CCUG34136T 4.5 ± 0.5; CIP106188 4.3 ± 0.1); *E. coli* 1.3 ± 0.6 log after HPP, not strain- or time-dependent.

## Key findings

- Matrix acidity (apple pH 3.32 vs banana–apple pH 4.02) multiplied HPP lethality at only 300 MPa / 2 min (Figure 1).
- Delayed 24 h enumeration in acid puree *increased* measured inactivation (injured cells die in acid), opposite of recovery sometimes seen in low-acid foods (Discussion).
- Target organism for validating HPP of acid fruit puree, in this panel, is *E. coli* — especially O157:H7 CECT5947 — not *Listeria* (conclusions).
- Nutrient-rich TSAYE should be used for validation so injured cells are not scored as dead (Section 3.2, Discussion).
- Applying HPP immediately after packaging is preferred if acid adaptation can blunt later pressure (Discussion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Single mild HPP condition (300 MPa / 2 min); authors call for other pressures.
- Purees were pre-sterilised by 600 MPa / 6 min then frozen; ice-crystal and prior-pressure history of the matrix are not those of a fresh packed puree.
- Inocula were frozen cultures thawed into puree.
- Infant-food claim is analogical (homemade puree, not a commercial recipe with starch/ascorbate beyond 0.5%).
- Two independent experiments only; *Salmonella* variability was large (wide SDs, many non-significant strain contrasts).
- No sensory or nutrient data.

## Relevance to our research question

Central for interpreting HPP “log reductions” in acid fruit: measured inactivation of vegetative pathogens depends on pH, strain, whether cells sat in acid before pressure, when you plate, and which agar you use. Without those, literature numbers are not comparable. *E. coli* O157:H7 can be the survivor in acid puree even when *Listeria* and *Salmonella* look easy to kill.
