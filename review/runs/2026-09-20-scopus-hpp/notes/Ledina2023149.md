# Non-thermal technologies for milk and dairy processing

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Ledina T, Đorđević J, Kovandžić M, Bulajić S. Meat Technology — Special Issue 64/2 (2023). doi: 10.18485/meattech.2023.64.2.27
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Ledina2023149.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Which non-thermal technologies (HPP, ultrasound, UV, cold plasma, PEF, supercritical CO2) are being applied to milk and dairy, and what are their processing roles?

## Methods
Review paper (conference special issue) surveying non-thermal dairy options versus conventional heat, with sustainability context.

## Sample / data
Cited dairy studies; no PRISMA n.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Narrative review.
- **Population / model:** Milk and dairy products.
- **n:** not reported.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP, ultrasound (including thermosonication), PEF plus mild heat, UV, cold plasma, high-intensity ultrasound and supercritical CO2 versus thermal dairy processes.
- **Primary endpoint:** Microbial/enzymatic inactivation and quality/sustainability arguments.
- **Primary result:** Heat remains routine for dairy safety but harms nutrition/sensory quality; several non-thermal options are described as promising. High-intensity ultrasound and supercritical CO2 are singled out as particularly noted for some dairy applications (introduction/closing). PEF plus mild heat can exceed PEF or heat alone for microbicidal effect (cited).
- **Cannot show:** Regulatory equivalence of any listed technology to legal milk pasteurisation (contrast with the EFSA opinion in this review).

## Key findings
- Dairy carbon-footprint argument is used to motivate alternatives (Poore and Nemecek cited).
- Combinations (ultrasound+heat; PEF+heat) are treated as more realistic than single mild fields.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Short review; limited original data; special-issue venue.

## Relevance to our research question
Places HPP among other dairy nonthermals; useful as context, weaker as primary inactivation evidence.
