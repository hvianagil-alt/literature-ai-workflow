# Macroalgae-Fortified Sausages: Nutritional and Quality Aspects Influenced by Non-Thermal High-Pressure Processing

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Marçal, Catarina; Pinto, Carlos A.; Silva, Artur M. S.; Monteiro, Carla; Saraiva, Jorge A.; Cardoso, Susana M. 2021. Foods 10(2): 209. doi:10.3390/foods10020209. PMC7909550
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Marçal2021.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Does adding European macroalgae flour change sausage nutrition (especially minerals and Na/K), and can HPP (600 MPa, 6 min) extend refrigerated shelf-life of the fortified products without harming color, pH, or fatty-acid profile?

## Methods
Industrial sausages from Irmãos Monteiro S.A. Plain vs fortified: meat frankfurter (F-MFS, 3.0% algae), vegetable frankfurter (F-VFS, 7.8%), traditional Portuguese “chouriço” (F-TPS, 2.6%). Mix: *Ulva* spp., *Gracilaria* spp., *Fucus vesiculosus* (AlgaPlus). Vacuum PA/PE bags. Nutrition on frozen samples (protein N×6.25, ash, Soxhlet fat, ICP-MS minerals). HPP: Hiperbaric 55 L, 600 MPa / 6 min / room temperature vs non-pressurized fortified controls, stored 4 ± 1 °C. TAM on PCA, 37 °C, 72 ± 3 h; spoilage threshold 6.49 log CFU/g. pH, CIELab, FAME. Frankfurters sampled to 30–45 d; chouriço to 180 d. Nutrition: t-test, ≥3 independent assays; HPP data: Sidak tests, p = 0.05.

## Sample / data
Commercial-style frankfurters and chouriço, with or without algae flour. Moisture 50–70%; meat products protein 20–22% and fat 19–24%; vegetable frankfurter protein/fat 8.0% class and carbohydrates ~81% (Table 1).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Industrial product reformulation (algae flour) plus HPP vs no HPP refrigerated storage
- **Population / model:** Macroalgae-fortified meat frankfurter, vegetable frankfurter, and Portuguese chouriço
- **n:** nutritional means of at least 3 independent assays; HPP microbial/quality time series (figures; replicate n not stated beyond that)
- **Intervention / comparator:** ~2.6–7.8% algae mix vs plain sausages; HPP 600 MPa / 6 min / room temperature vs non-pressurized fortified sausages at 4 °C
- **Primary endpoint:** TAM (log CFU/g) vs 6.49 log threshold; minerals (mg/100 g); surface color and FA profile
- **Primary result:** Algae raised Mg, K, Ca, Mn, Fe and lowered Na/K (plain Na/K 1.8–3.6 vs fortified 1.6–3.0). Example Ca: F-MFS 28.6 ± 3.0 vs MFS 12.5 ± 1.4 mg/100 g; Mg: 80.0 ± 1.3 vs 19.0 ± 0.4. Immediately after HPP, F-MFS TAM <1.0 log CFU g−1 vs control ~2.5. Time to 6.49 log: control F-MFS <15 d vs HPP ~45 d (~3-fold). F-VFS at day 15: HPP 4.0 vs control 6.3 log CFU g−1; at day 45 both ~8.1–8.2. F-TPS control 2.2–2.7 log over 180 d; HPP remained below detection. HPP prevented frankfurter pH drop; no significant HPP vs control color change; FA profiles not meaningfully harmed.
- **Cannot show:** Sensory/consumer tests; named-pathogen challenge; that HPP caused the mineral gains (those come from algae, not pressure); spore control.

## Key findings
- Protein and fat were not changed by algae; ash/minerals increased (Section 3.1; Table 1).
- Na: F-MFS 1153.2 ± 13.6 vs MFS 1178.8 ± 8.2 mg/100 g (significant but small); F-VFS and F-TPS Na increased vs their plains (Table 1).
- pH at T0: frankfurters 6.3–6.4 vs chouriço <6. Non-HPP frankfurters fell to 5.2 ± 0.1 (F-MFS) and 5.9 ± 0.0 (F-VFS) at T30; HPP kept pH stable (Section 3.2.2).
- Meat sausages SFA 51–59% of FA; F-VFS unsaturated ~70% (Section 3.2.4).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Exact meat formulations confidential.
- Microbial n per time point not numerically stated beyond “at least 3” for nutrition.
- Vegetable frankfurter still reached high TAM by day 45 even after HPP.
- No sensory.

## Relevance to our research question
Meat (and vegetable analogue) matrix: HPP 600 MPa/6 min roughly triples frankfurter refrigerated TAM shelf-life and keeps chouriço below detection for 180 d, without color/FA damage. Nutrition change is from seaweed fortification, not HPP.
