# Reduced-temperature pasteurization of apple juice using a flow-through sonicator

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Maghami, Sara; Rumpunen, Kimmo; Johansson, Örjan. 2026. Ultrasonics Sonochemistry 131:107926. doi:10.1016/j.ultsonch.2026.107926
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Maghami2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Can a cylindrical flow-through sonicator that combines acoustic cavitation (single- vs dual-frequency) with a five-hole Venturi nozzle inactivate yeast, mold, and aerobic bacteria in cloudy apple juice at 30–55 °C, and how do excitation mode, temperature, and efficient residence time change microstructure and sedimentation?

## Methods
Pilot-scale experimental study with structural–acoustic COMSOL design of an 18-sonotrode cylindrical sonicator (primary ~27.7 kHz chirp; secondary 42.3 kHz; 300–350 W) plus a 5-hole Venturi (mass flow 36 L/min; ~47 W hydrodynamic power). Treatments: SF-1/DF-1 (30–40 °C), SF-2/DF-2 (40–45 °C), DF-3 (45–50 °C), DF-4 (50–55 °C, 350 W). Cavitation zone is 17% of circuit volume; “efficient time” 0–600 s. Yeast/mold Petrifilm (23 °C, 5 days); selected samples sent to Eurofins for yeast, mold, and aerobic plate count at 30 °C. SEM (200 and 500 µm) and 10-day sedimentation. ANOVA not described for the microbial table. No matched thermal-only controls (authors state all temperatures were below 60 °C).

## Sample / data
Fresh Ingrid Marie apples, hydraulically pressed, frozen before trials. Sampling every 15 min over 1 h (plus 5 and 10 min in the final treatment). Petrifilm DF-4 time series shown with three replicates. Blank counts (Eurofins, Table 4): yeast 4000 CFU/mL; mold 909 CFU/mL; aerobic 3700 CFU/mL. Number of independent juice batches is not reported.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Pilot-scale experimental thermosonication / hydrodynamic-acoustic cavitation study (no RCT; no thermal-only arm)
- **Population / model:** Cloudy juice from Ingrid Marie apples (native spoilage flora, not inoculated pathogens)
- **n:** Petrifilm DF-4 time series: three replicates; Eurofins confirmatory counts: n not reported; COMSOL/acoustic spectra: engineering measurements, n not reported
- **Intervention / comparator:** Dual-frequency DF-4 (27.6 + 43.7 kHz, 350 W, 50–55 °C, 450 s efficient time) vs DF-3 (45–50 °C) vs lower-temperature single/dual-frequency runs vs untreated blank; no isothermal thermal pasteurization control
- **Primary endpoint:** Log reduction of yeast, mold, and aerobic microorganisms (CFU/mL)
- **Primary result:** After DF-4, yeast fell from 4000 to <1 CFU/mL (>3.6 log); mold 909 to 2 CFU/mL (2.7 log); aerobic 3700 to 6 CFU/mL (2.8 log). DF-3: 1.7, 1.8, and 1.5 log, respectively (Table 4). Specific energy stated as 0.044 kWh/L at 450 s DF-4. Did not meet FDA 5-log pathogen pasteurization criterion.
- **Cannot show:** 5-log kill of juice pathogens (E. coli O157:H7 / Salmonella / Cryptosporidium); contribution of heat vs cavitation (no thermal control); industrial continuous-line performance; nutritional/sensory endpoints (not measured)

## Key findings
- Dual-frequency spectra were more complex than single-frequency; measured dual-frequency pressures included p(f1)=225 kPa and p(f2)=283 kPa (Section 6.2).
- 30–40 °C showed no inactivation trend; DF-2 at 40–45 °C could increase colony counts (sublethal growth) (Section 6.3; Fig. 7).
- DF-4 Petrifilm showed progressive reduction after 300 s, near-complete at 450–600 s across three replicates (Fig. 8).
- SEM showed time-dependent surface disruption from 150 s to extensive fragmentation at 600 s (Fig. 9); treated juice had a less compact 1 mL sediment line after 10 days vs untreated (Fig. 10), without a monotonic time trend.
- Authors recommend avoiding 35–40 °C sonication at limited power because growth can be enhanced (Conclusion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Reductions missed the 5-log pathogen target; juice solids/macromolecules may shield cells.
- No pure thermal controls at the same temperatures.
- Residual colonies on some DF-4 plates attributed to plating contamination, not quantified.
- Bubbly-water acoustic parameters used as a first-order stand-in for bubbly apple juice.
- Quality limited to SEM and visual sedimentation; PPO, vitamins, and sensory not reported.

## Relevance to our research question
Direct ultrasound/cavitation evidence in a juice matrix: dual-frequency thermosonication at 50–55 °C can give ~2.7–>3.6 log spoilage-flora reduction and better cloud stability, but it is not a validated 5-log pathogen pasteurization process and lacks a thermal comparator.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Whether Eurofins counts are single measurements or means (n missing in Table 4).
- Absolute Petrifilm CFU vs the Eurofins log table.
- How “efficient time” maps to industrial mean residence time at 36 L/min.
- Pathogen challenge data are absent; do not treat yeast log reductions as pathogen kill.
