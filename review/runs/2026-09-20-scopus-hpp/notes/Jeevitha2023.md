# Non-thermal processing as a preservation tool for health-promoting beverages

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Jeevitha GC, Saravanan R, Mittal A, Venkat Kumar S. 2023. Discover Food 3:26. doi:10.1007/s44187-023-00066-4
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Jeevitha2023.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

How do non-thermal methods (ultrasonication and its pressure/heat variants, cold plasma, pulsed light, high hydrostatic pressure, supercritical CO2, membrane technology, pulsed magnetic field) affect microbial load, enzymes, nutrients, sensory quality, and shelf life of health-promoting beverages, relative to thermal pasteurization?

## Methods

Open-access narrative review. Each technology has a working-principle subsection plus beverage applications and summary tables. The authors note most cited work is laboratory-scale. No PRISMA protocol. Conclusion recommends combining non-thermal processes with aseptic packaging and cold storage.

## Sample / data

Cited beverage trials (fruit/vegetable juices, milk, smoothies, coconut water, spirits, whey drinks). Included-study N is not reported.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Open-access narrative review of athermal processing for functional/health-promoting beverages
- **Population / model:** Plant- and dairy-based beverages (juices, smoothies, milk, coconut water, spirits) in the cited experiments
- **n:** not reported (narrative review)
- **Intervention / comparator:** Ultrasound (including thermosonication, manosonication, manothermosonication), cold plasma, pulsed light, HHP (stated 100–1000 MPa), SC-CO2, membranes, pulsed magnetic fields vs thermal pasteurization/HTST and untreated controls in source papers
- **Primary endpoint:** Microbial counts (log CFU/mL), enzyme residual activity, bioactive retention (ascorbic acid, anthocyanins, phenolics), and shelf life (days)
- **Primary result:** Authors argue non-thermal methods can match thermal preservation with better nutrient/sensory retention. Recited HHP examples: soy smoothie 650 MPa / 20 °C / 3 min, 55% ascorbic acid retained, color/bioactives unchanged; mulberry juice 500 MPa / 10 min, aerobic mesophiles from 4 log CFU/mL to undetectable with yeast/mold eliminated; milk 600 MPa / 3 min, 5-log reduction of E. coli, Salmonella, and L. monocytogenes and 7-day shelf life comparable to commercial pasteurized milk; goat milk 200–600 MPa / 5–15 min / <40 °C, 7-log S. aureus; Shiikuwasha juice 600 MPa / 150 s, >5-log E. coli, 28-day stability. Ultrasound examples: strawberry juice 10 min, 96.8% anthocyanin and 89% ascorbic acid retained; orange juice 0.3–0.81 W/mL / 2–10 min, predicted shelf life 27–33 days vs 19 days thermal.
- **Cannot show:** Cannot provide industrial-scale efficacy, a ranked technology, or a pooled pasteurization equivalent; cannot treat this as primary HHP kinetic data.

## Key findings

- Thermal processing of fruit/vegetable beverages harms organoleptic properties and nutrient bioavailability; non-thermal aims to inactivate microbes/enzymes via membrane/DNA/non-covalent-bond disruption without cooking. (Introduction)
- Ultrasound classes: low 20–100 kHz (large shear), medium 100 kHz–1 MHz (radicals; lipid/protein oxidation risk), high 1–100 MHz. Manosonication 100–300 kPa + US; manothermosonication adds heat for thermophiles. (Ultrasonication)
- Cheng et al. guava: carbonation + US improved ascorbic acid retention but microbial reduction was low. Mango juice 600 W / 40 min: complete microbial reduction with superior nutrient retention (as cited). Coconut water 60% amplitude / 10 min: quality profile unaffected; 4.79 log CFU/mL microbial reduction. (US applications; Table 1)
- HHP principle: ~10–20 min in a closed chamber; adiabatic heating ~3 °C per 100 MPa. (HHP working principle)
- Additional HHP recited results: cucumber juice 500 MPa / 5 min better sensory than HTST; watermelon 50 °C / 30 MPa / 30 min PPO to 4% residual; strawberry puree 400–600 MPa higher anthocyanin/phenolics/ascorbic acid; aronia puree 400 and 600 MPa / 5 min yeast/mold to 1 log CFU/g, aerobes <2 log CFU/g; fruit–vegetable smoothies 630 MPa / 20 °C / 6 min POD 31.4%, PPO 9.7%, PME 83.9%; apple juice 300 MPa / 10 min, 3.7-log A. acidoterrestris; beetroot 500 MPa / 10 min, betacyanin −12.2%, betaxanthin −8.9%, LAB −4.5 log; orange juice 600 MPa / 10 min, A. acidoterrestris spores −3.5 log, shelf life 60 days; raspberry 600 MPa / 10 min, 22% PME inactivation, molds 1.85–3.72 log, yeast 3.19 log; tender coconut water 593 MPa / 3 min, shelf life 120 days. (HHP applications; Table 4)
- SC-CO2 examples: pomegranate 12.7 MPa / 45 °C / 15 min, yeast/mold <2 log CFU/mL, aerobes <2.4 log CFU/mL. (SC-CO2)
- Pulsed magnetic field: orange juice 7 T / 20 pulses, 0.61-log aerobes; cucumber juice 8 T / 60 pulses, 1.36-log E. coli. (Table 7)
- Authors: HHP applicable to packaged foods; PEF mainly liquids; US and membranes more economically scalable; HHP and SC-CO2 suitable for microbes and enzymes at 35–55 °C (conclusion statement). (Conclusion)

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Most studies laboratory-scale; industrial trials needed.
- HHP: heat generation causes process instability/non-uniformity; lack of data on pressure–packaging synergy.
- Pulsed-light and pulsed-magnetic-field beverage data are limited; membrane fouling at small MWCO.
- Regulatory pathways described as slow/expensive.
- Internal table/text inconsistencies (see Open questions).

## Relevance to our research question

On-scope for HHP and other nonthermals in juices/milk, including some combined US + heat/pressure language (manothermosonication) relevant to hurdles, though the paper is beverage-quality focused rather than a dedicated hurdle review.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Table 3 lists “Pomegranate juice Pressure: 600 MPa” under a SC-CO2 table — likely a mis-filed HHP row; do not cite 600 MPa as SC-CO2.
- Table 4 cucumber juice “30 MPa / 30 min” conflicts with narrative “500 MPa / 5 min” for cucumber; watermelon PPO row also mixes 30 MPa with cucumber table caption. Prefer narrative for 500 MPa cucumber sensory vs HTST and 30 MPa watermelon PPO, and flag tables as unreliable without the primary papers.
- Conclusion “HHP and SC-CO2 … 35–55 °C” does not match many recited HHP runs at 20 °C.
- Sheep-milk 121 MPa / 30 min and sorghum-spirit 30–90 MPa are far below typical pasteurization HHP; treat as specialized, not general HHP.
