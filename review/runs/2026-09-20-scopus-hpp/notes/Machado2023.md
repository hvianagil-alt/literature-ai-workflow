# High Pressure and Pasteurization Effects on Dairy Cream

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Machado, Fernanda; Duarte, Ricardo V.; Pinto, Carlos A.; Casal, Susana; Lopes-da-Silva, José A.; Saraiva, Jorge A. 2023. Foods 12(19): 3640. doi:10.3390/foods12193640. PMC10572675
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Machado2023.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Can HPP pasteurize raw dairy cream as well as or better than commercial heat pasteurization (75 °C, 15 s) for endogenous and inoculated microbes, while tracking pH, color, viscosity, fatty acids, and volatiles during refrigerated storage?

## Methods
Two cream batches from a Portuguese producer. Batch 1: raw vs heat (75 °C/15 s) vs HPP 450 MPa or 600 MPa for 5 min at 7 °C; storage 4 °C, days 5, 9, 18, 33, 51. Batch 2: 600 MPa for 5 or 15 min at 7 °C vs heat; storage days 3, 10, 28, 52; plus inoculated *E. coli* ATCC 25922 and *L. innocua* ATCC 33090 (~10^8 cells/mL). Hiperbaric 55; come-up 200 MPa/min; decompression nearly instant. Endpoints: TAP, ENT, LAB; inoculated pathogens; pH; CIELab ΔE*; apparent viscosity (cone-plate, 25 °C, compared at 33 s−1); FAME GC; HS-SPME–GC-MS volatiles. Triplicate packs, each analyzed in duplicate; two-way ANOVA, Tukey p ≤ 0.05. Spoilage threshold 6.00 log CFU/mL; detection limit 1.00 log CFU/mL.

## Sample / data
Industrially homogenized raw and commercially pasteurized cream; 20 mL in PA/PE bags. Initial pH batch 1 6.74 ± 0.05; batch 2 6.91 ± 0.14. Raw cream major FA: palmitic 24.50 ± 0.12%, oleic 20.03 ± 0.11%, myristic 11.25 ± 0.07%. 39 VOCs identified.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Two-batch refrigerated shelf-life trial of HPP vs commercial thermal pasteurization, plus inoculation challenge
- **Population / model:** Bovine dairy cream (high aw ≈ 0.97, pH ≈ 6.7 as stated in introduction)
- **n:** triplicate samples per storage day, each analyzed in duplicate
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 450 MPa/5 min or 600 MPa/5–15 min at 7 °C vs thermal 75 °C/15 s vs raw; 4 °C storage up to 51–52 d
- **Primary endpoint:** Endogenous TAP/LAB/ENT (log CFU/mL) and inoculated *E. coli* / *L. innocua*; quality (pH, ΔE*, viscosity, FA, VOCs)
- **Primary result:** Heat dropped TAP/LAB/ENT below 1.00 log CFU/mL until day 9; by day 51 TAP and LAB >6.00 log CFU/mL. 450 MPa/5 min: TAP similar to raw immediately; TAP/LAB >6 log from day 18 (spoiled). 600 MPa/5 min: slower TAP/LAB recovery; TAP rose significantly only after day 51. Batch 2: 600 MPa/15 min cut TAP ~1.4-fold and LAB ~1.8-fold vs raw; day 28 TAP 4.53 ± 0.11 log CFU/mL vs heat >6.00. HPP reduced inoculated *E. coli* and *L. innocua* by more than 6 log to undetectable (1.00 log CFU/mL) as stated in the abstract. Viscosity at day 28: HPP 0.028 ± 0.003 Pa·s vs heat 0.016 ± 0.002 Pa·s. ΔE* variation up to 8.43; FA profile largely stable; PUFAs decreased (p ≤ 0.05) on day 52.
- **Cannot show:** Spore inactivation; phosphatase / *Coxiella* legal pasteurization endpoints; sensory/consumer data; vitamin destruction (discussed as a heat risk, not measured here).

## Key findings
- ENT stayed <1.00 log CFU/mL after all treatments through storage (Batch 1) (Section 3.1).
- *E. coli* on HPP samples rose by day 10 then fell below 1.00 log by later days; *L. innocua* increased from day 28 to 52, consistent with Gram-positive pressure resistance (Section 3.1).
- No statistical ΔE* differences among treatments on a given storage day (Section 3.2).
- HPP cream was more viscous than heat-treated cream after storage; shear-thinning non-Newtonian flow (Section 3.3).
- VOCs tended to increase during storage (acids, aliphatic hydrocarbons); alcohols appeared after either process (Section 3.5).
- Abstract claims ≥15 extra shelf-life days vs thermal; conclusions also mention commercial cream usually <3 weeks and HPP could add ≥30 days—two different framings of the same storage data.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors state dairy pasteurization regulations (e.g., US *Mycobacterium* / *Coxiella*, phosphatase) still need dedicated work (Section 3.1).
- Two cream batches differed in color a* ranking.
- *L. innocua* recovery during late cold storage.
- Spores not targeted.

## Relevance to our research question
Core dairy-matrix HPP paper: 600 MPa holds endogenous flora longer than 75 °C/15 s pasteurization, with modest viscosity increase and stable FA/color. Nutrition here is fatty-acid profile, not vitamins.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Abstract “more than 6 log to undetectable” vs body text describing later *E. coli* rebound then die-off and *L. innocua* growth after day 28—cite time-specific counts, not only the abstract.
- Shelf-life extension “≥15 days” (abstract) vs “at least 30 days” (conclusions) are author interpretations of the same 51–52 d study; 450 MPa did not last 51 days.
- Exact initial TAP of raw cream is in figures, not a single number in the extract.
