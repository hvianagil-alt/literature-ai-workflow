# Exploration of high-pressure processing (HPP) for preservation of the Swedish grown brown macroalgae *Saccharina latissima*

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Jönsson, Madeleine; Allahgholi, Leila; Rayner, Marilyn; Nordberg Karlsson, Eva. 2023. Frontiers in Food Science and Technology 3: 1150482. doi:10.3389/frfst.2023.1150482
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Jönsson2023.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Does HPP preserve Swedish-cultivated *Saccharina latissima* as an alternative to traditional drying/freezing, and what does it do to texture, color, composition, and microbes?

## Methods
*S. latissima* harvested 12 April 2021, Kosterhavet (Nordic Sea Farm), cooled to Lund, 4 °C overnight. Indigenous 16S V1–V3 and ITS1 (n = 3, Eurofins). Spontaneous proliferation: 80 g fresh or frozen seaweed + 420 mL water, 37 °C, 7 d (n = 3). HPP: 10.0 ± 0.1 g in PA/PE vacuum bags, Avure AV-10, 200, 400, or 600 MPa for 180 s, ~24 h after harvest; untreated vacuum packs as controls. Texture puncture hardness (g) and compression (mm); CIELab. Proximate/carbohydrates on DW. Shelf-life plating (unspecific bacteria, Enterobacteriaceae, lactobacilli, mold/yeast) under refrigeration and accelerated conditions. n = 3 for several microbial setups.

## Sample / data
Large blades 100–200 cm2 to limit handling contamination. Water ~90% wet weight (Table 5). Initial microbial load of fresh material described as low (abstract).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Exploratory HPP processing trial on fresh kelp with texture, composition, and culture-based shelf-life
- **Population / model:** Cultivated brown macroalga *Saccharina latissima* (Swedish west coast)
- **n:** microbial profiling n = 3; fermentation n = 3; texture/color means ± SD (Table 4; replicate count for texture not stated as a single n in extract)
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 200, 400, 600 MPa / 180 s vs untreated vacuum-packed seaweed
- **Primary endpoint:** Texture (hardness g; compression mm); microbial growth during shelf-life; composition (g/100 g DW)
- **Primary result:** Hardness (unwashed): untreated 312.3 ± 31.2 g; 200 MPa 100.8 ± 21.1; 400 MPa 81.3 ± 5.3; 600 MPa 38.3 ± 11.9 (87.7% hardness drop at 600 MPa). Compression: 5.5 ± 0.4 mm untreated vs 2.2 ± 0.4 mm at 600 MPa (60.0% reduction). Color retained (L*a*b* similar; RGB still dark). Water 90.0–91.5%; protein 9.9 ± 0.1 (untreated) to 11.1–11.6 after HPP; total carbohydrates 38.4 ± 0.8 vs 33.2–36.6 (ns pattern). Mannitol lower only at 400 MPa (4.83 ± 0.1 vs 8.71 ± 0.8 g/100 g DW). Fresh material had low microbes; PCR often failed to amplify; spontaneous growth limited. Shelf-life: inconsistent CFU, overall low rise in unspecific bacteria, increasing Enterobacteriaceae, no lactobacilli, low mold/yeast.
- **Cannot show:** A validated commercial shelf-life for HPP kelp; pathogen challenge; that HPP is superior to drying/freezing on energy or cost (not measured); sensory liking.

## Key findings
- Color L* untreated unwashed 5.62 ± 1.0 vs 600 MPa 6.36 ± 1.3 (Table 4)—still very dark product.
- Ash 42.6 ± 0.4 untreated; 400 MPa 44.9 ± 0.2 (significant) (Table 5).
- MRS plates never showed significant growth (Section 3.2).
- Authors frame HPP as a candidate post-harvest method for new seaweed food markets, not a finished process (Abstract/Introduction).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors: little prior HPP work on this species; seaweed microbial quality data scarce (Introduction).
- Inconsistent plate growth complicates shelf-life claims.
- 180 s hold is short vs many food HPP processes (3–6 min).
- No thermal/drying arm in the same experiment.

## Relevance to our research question
Not dairy/meat/juice/seafood muscle; **seaweed** HPP. Composition mostly retained but texture collapses at 600 MPa. Microbial starting load was already low, so inactivation benefit is hard to see. Peripheral to the stated matrices; useful only if algae-fortified foods or seaweed as food are in scope.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Texture replicate n is not explicit beside the SD in Table 4.
- Accelerated-study CFU spikes (e.g., ~6.5–6.7 log CFU/mL in some diluted 200/600 MPa replicates at week 3) should not be generalized as typical refrigerated behavior.
