# High-Pressure-Based Strategies for the Inactivation of Bacillus subtilis Endospores in Honey

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Scepankova, H., Pinto, C.A., Estevinho, L.M., Saraiva, J.A. (2022). Molecules 27(18):5918. doi: 10.3390/molecules27185918
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Scepankova2022.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Can nonthermal HPP, pressure-assisted thermal processing (PATP), or hyperbaric storage (HS) inactivate *Bacillus subtilis* endospores in honey, and what water activity is needed for pressure to work?

## Methods

Multifloral Portuguese honey (Castanea sativa 53.3% of pollen; raw 84.6 °Brix, a_w 0.57). Honey–water preparations (HWP) were adjusted to a_w 0.80, 0.85, 0.90 and 0.96 (63.3 to 33.9 °Brix). *B. subtilis* ATCC 6633 spores (not heat-activated) were inoculated at about 10^4–10^5 cells/mL (reported loads after inoculation ~5 log CFU/g or CFU/mL). Treatments: HPP 600 MPa / 15 min at 20–22 °C inlet (Hiperbaric 55); PATP 600 MPa / 15 min at 70, 75 or 85 °C after preheat (adiabatic heating estimated to ~92.2, 97.2 and 107.2 °C at those set-points); HS 50–150 MPa up to 60 days at 18–23 °C; thermal processing 75 or 85 °C / 15 min at 0.1 MPa. Germinated vs dormant spores distinguished by an 80 °C / 20 min heat shock; plating on HiCrome Bacillus agar at 30 °C up to 72 h. Endogenous *B. subtilis* in raw honey ~10^2 CFU/g; TEL after heat shock <2.3 log CFU/g.

## Sample / data

Raw honey plus four diluted honeys. Indigenous TML 2.36 log CFU/mL, essentially equal to TEL. After inoculation, PATP honey TEL 5.65 log CFU/mL; HS honey TEL 5.04 ± 0.01 log CFU/g. Quantification limit 2.30 log CFU/g; detection limit 1.30 log CFU/g in diluted PATP work.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Inoculated spore challenge in honey and honey–water; comparison of HPP, PATP, HS and thermal pasteurisation.
- **Population / model:** *B. subtilis* ATCC 6633 endospores (plus indigenous *B. subtilis*) in multifloral honey.
- **n:** Inoculum targeted 10^4–10^5; exact biological replicate count for every bar is not stated in the extracted text; HS TEL given as 5.04 ± 0.01.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 600 MPa HPP at room temperature; 600 MPa PATP at 70–85 °C; HS 50–150 MPa; 75/85 °C thermal; undiluted vs a_w 0.80–0.96.
- **Primary endpoint:** log(N/N0) of total microbial load and total endospore load (after 80 °C / 20 min).
- **Primary result:** Undiluted honey (a_w ≈ 0.55–0.57): PATP 600 MPa / 15 min / 85 °C and thermal 85 °C / 15 min did not reduce inoculated TEL, including after 24 h. Diluted honey at a_w 0.85 (52.9 °Brix) and 0.90: PATP 600 MPa / 15 min / 75 °C drove spores to the 1.30 log CFU/g detection limit (≥3.70 log reduction; abstract states at least 4.0 log). HPP and 75 °C thermal did not cut endospore loads at a_w up to 0.90.
- **Cannot show:** Inactivation of *Clostridium botulinum* (the infant-botulism concern in honey); commercial sterility of undiluted honey by nonthermal HPP; HMF or enzyme quality after PATP (discussed, not measured here).
- **PATP at a_w 0.80:** About 1.74 log spore reduction at 600 MPa / 75 °C / 15 min.
- **PATP 70 °C / 600 MPa / 15 min on HWP a_w 0.96:** About 2 log reduction; undiluted honey unchanged; room-temperature HPP appeared to *increase* recoverable spores (activation of superdormant spores, not new sporulation).
- **Hyperbaric storage:** 75–125 MPa for 21 days, and 125 MPa to 60 days, did not change spore load or heat resistance in undiluted honey; 150 MPa / 21 days at a_w 0.80 also failed to germinate or kill spores, but prevented the visible spoilage seen at atmospheric pressure.
- **Indigenous load:** Raw honey TML 2.36 log CFU/mL, almost all spores.
- **Adiabatic estimate:** Authors estimate mean PATP sample temperature ≈ 92–107 °C at 600 MPa for 70–85 °C set-points (Patazca et al. 3.7 °C/100 MPa for honey).

## Key findings

- Nonthermal 600 MPa HPP does not inactivate *B. subtilis* spores in honey, consistent with low a_w / high sugar baroprotection (Sections 3.2–3.3, 4.1).
- PATP only becomes useful after water is added to a_w ≥ 0.80, and is strong at a_w 0.85–0.90 (Figure 4).
- HS neither germinates nor kills these spores in honey, unlike prior carrot-juice HS work cited by the authors (Section 3.2.2).
- Diluted honey at a_w 0.80 still spoiled at atmospheric room temperature; HS blocked that growth (Section 3.3.2).
- Authors position PATP of *diluted* honey for medical/pharmaceutical preparations, not as a way to pasteurise table honey without heat.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- *B. subtilis* is a model, not *C. botulinum*.
- Dilution destroys the identity of honey as a low-a_w food; biomedical use is the stated rationale.
- PATP temperatures after adiabatic heating overlap conventional sterilisation; “nonthermal” does not apply to the successful PATP cells.
- HMF, phenolics, enzymes and colour were not measured.
- Endogenous flora was not removed before inoculation; differential agar was used.
- Replicate structure is thinly reported.
- Equipment heating uniformity for industrial PATP is flagged as an unsolved engineering issue.

## Relevance to our research question

Important negative result for HPP microbiology: commercial-style 600 MPa, even with 85 °C PATP, does not kill spores in undiluted honey. Water activity is a hard gate on pressure lethality. Successful spore kill required dilution plus heat under pressure, which is PATP, not nonthermal HPP.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Abstract “at least 4.0 log” vs body “at least 3.70 log” to a 1.30 log CFU/g detection limit from ~5 log inoculum — both describe below-detection, but the log figure is not identical.
- Units mix CFU/mL and CFU/g.
- Figure 4 caption says whole honey a_w = 0.60 while Table 1 lists 0.57.
- Whether 70 °C PATP of a_w 0.96 honey stays below 100 °C; authors give both 93–105 °C and the 3.7 °C/100 MPa estimate.
