# Inactivation of Escherichia coli K12 in phosphate buffer saline and orange juice by high hydrostatic pressure processing combined with freezing

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Bulut, S. and Karatzas, K. A.G. (2021) Inactivation of Escherichia coli K12 in phosphate buffer saline and orange juice by high hydrostatic pressure processing combined with freezing. LWT-Food Science and Technology, 136 (1). 110313. doi: 10.1016/j.lwt.2020.110313
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Bulut2021.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Does freezing PBS or orange juice before high hydrostatic pressure (HHP) increase inactivation of *Escherichia coli* K12 at relatively low pressures, and is the extra kill explained by physical rupture of cells?

## Methods

Laboratory challenge study. Stationary-phase *E. coli* K12 was washed and resuspended in sterile PBS (pH 7.1) at about 10^9 CFU·ml^-1, packed (12 ± 1 ml) in double bags, then held overnight at 4 °C, −24 °C, or −80 °C. HHP used a Stansted Fluid Power unit (55 ml vessel) with 80% alcohol / 20% castor oil as pressure-transmitting medium; all PBS runs were at 4 ± 1 °C. PBS inactivation was modelled with a rotatable central composite design (α = 1.4142) of pressure 159–441 MPa and time 1–11 min, with three centre-point replicates (300 MPa, 5 min), analysed in Design-Expert; significance p < 0.05. Fresh squeezed orange juice was inoculated (~10^9 CFU·ml^-1), pH adjusted to 3.2, 4.5 or 5.8, packed, held at 4 ± 1 °C or −80 °C, and treated at 250 MPa for 5, 10 or 15 min (factorial design, 3 replicates). Survivors were counted on LB agar (spot plate, duplicate). Mechanism work used SEM and NanoDrop DNA/RNA in PBS supernatants after 13 000 rpm centrifugation. Model predictions at 180 and 250 MPa were validated experimentally.

## Sample / data

Non-pathogenic *E. coli* K12 in PBS (pH 7.1) and in supermarket orange juice. Orange-juice uninoculated counts were below 10 CFU·ml^-1; after inoculation and overnight 4 °C storage mean counts were 8.5 ± 0.04 log CFU·ml^-1 (n = 9). CCD centre-point standard errors used n = 3.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Laboratory inoculated challenge; response-surface (CCD) in PBS plus factorial HHP in orange juice; SEM and nucleic-acid leakage for mechanism.
- **Population / model:** *E. coli* K12 in PBS (pH 7.1) and in orange juice at pH 3.2, 4.5 and 5.8.
- **n:** CCD with three centre-point replicates; orange-juice factorial used 3 pH × 3 times × 3 replicates; NanoDrop readings averaged from three measurements per sample.
- **Intervention / comparator:** HHP at 4 ± 1 °C after overnight 4 °C vs freezing at −24 °C or −80 °C; orange juice also compared 250 MPa at 4 °C vs 20 °C after −80 °C freeze; freeze–thaw without pressure as a control.
- **Primary endpoint:** Log reduction of CFU·ml^-1 vs untreated inoculum; DNA/RNA (ng µl^-1) in PBS supernatant; SEM morphology.
- **Primary result:** In PBS, 400 MPa / 9 min / 4 °C gave 1.83 log CFU·ml^-1 reduction after 4 °C storage vs 5.63 log (−24 °C freeze) and 6.83 log (−80 °C freeze). In orange juice, 250 MPa / 15 min of −80 °C frozen samples gave 4.88, 4.15 and 4.61 log CFU·ml^-1 reductions at pH 3.2, 4.5 and 5.8; without freezing the same treatment gave ≤ 0.42 log at pH 4.5 and 5.8.
- **Cannot show:** Inactivation of *E. coli* O157:H7 or other juice pathogens; industrial-scale packs; enzyme inactivation or sensory quality of juice; a complete ice-polymorph map (phase change inferred from a ~55 MPa / 2.5 °C drop near 315 MPa).
- **Freeze-only kill (PBS):** Overnight freeze–thaw at −24 °C and −80 °C reduced *E. coli* K12 by 1.73 ± 0.12 and 1.49 ± 0.07 log, respectively; freeze–thaw of orange juice at −80 °C did not change counts (8.51 ± 0.03 log CFU·ml^-1).
- **Brief pressure on ice:** 1 s at 300 MPa gave 2.60 and 3.52 log reductions in −24 °C and −80 °C PBS vs 0.01 log in unfrozen PBS.
- **Nucleic-acid leakage:** Control DNA/RNA 28.43 ± 0.57 / 24.37 ± 0.06 ng µl^-1; freeze–thaw 181.37 ± 3.07 / 151.40 ± 1.93 ng µl^-1; pressurised frozen samples 537.73 ± 7.60 / 450.60 ± 6.56 ng µl^-1.
- **Low-pH unfrozen juice:** Unfrozen orange juice at pH 3.2 reached 3.84 ± 0.33 log reduction after 15 min at 250 MPa; frozen pH 3.2 juice reached 4.88 log under the same pressure/time.
- **20 °C vessel, frozen juice:** Frozen pH 3.2 juice pressurised at 20 °C (PTM) reached 5.0 log reduction (10 min at 250 MPa reported in conclusions) vs 4.6 log at 4 °C.

## Key findings

- Freezing before HHP converted a modest PBS kill (max 1.83 log at 400 MPa / 9 min) into 5–7 log reductions at the same conditions (abstract; Section 3.1.3).
- Unfrozen PBS inactivation followed a two-factor interaction model (r^2 = 0.94); frozen PBS followed linear models in P and t (r^2 = 0.93) (Eqs. 1–3).
- SEM showed wall disintegration only when frozen samples were pressurised; unfrozen pressurised cells looked intact (Section 3.1.5).
- In unfrozen juice, pH 3.2 drove most of the kill; after freezing, pH 4.5 and 5.8 also reached about 4 log reduction, so pH was less critical on ice (Section 3.2.3).
- Authors argue ice I → denser ice polymorphs (volume drop near 315 MPa) plus cell-wall rigidity at −80 °C explain extra mechanical rupture (Section 3.1.3).
- They report a 5-log reduction of this relatively pressure-resistant K12 strain at 250 MPa, well below typical commercial 600 MPa (Section 3.2.4; conclusions).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)

- Test organism is *E. coli* K12, not O157:H7; authors cite Torres et al. that K12 can be more pressure-resistant in orange juice than ATCC 700728.
- Packs were 12 ml laboratory bags; authors say industrial pack size still needs study.
- Orange-juice proximate composition was not measured (sugar cited from literature).
- Quality, enzymes, and shelf-life of frozen-then-pressurised juice were not measured.
- “Cell rapture” in the keywords is a spelling of rupture; mechanism is morphological, not a molecular pathway map.
- Temperature of the sample interior was inferred from PTM thermocouple, not an in-pack probe.

## Relevance to our research question

Directly relevant: HHP inactivation of a vegetative Gram-negative in a real juice, plus a clear mechanistic claim that freezing before pressure kills by physically rupturing membranes, allowing large log reductions at 180–250 MPa. Quality of the juice itself is not characterised.
