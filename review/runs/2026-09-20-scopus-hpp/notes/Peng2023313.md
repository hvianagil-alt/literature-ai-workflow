# Ultra-high-pressure technology for preservation of fresh aquatic foods: A review

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Peng et al. Czech Journal of Food Sciences, 2023 (citekey Peng2023313). doi: 10.17221/87/2023-CJFS
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/Peng2023313.pdf
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How does ultra-high pressure (UHP/HPP) preserve fresh aquatic foods (microbial inactivation, enzymes, quality, shelf-life)?

## Methods
Narrative review of UHP on fish, shellfish, and related aquatic products.

## Sample / data
Cited aquatic-food HPP studies. Study n not reported.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Narrative review.
- **Population / model:** Fresh aquatic foods (fish, shellfish, etc.).
- **n:** not reported.
- **Intervention / comparator:** UHP versus conventional thermal or chilled storage as discussed.
- **Primary endpoint:** Microbial inactivation, quality (texture, colour, enzymes), shelf-life.
- **Primary result:** UHP can extend shelf-life of aquatic foods while remaining a non-thermal option; industrial-relevant pressures discussed include the 400–600 MPa band typical of HPP (body). Exact pooled log reductions are not reported as a single number.
- **Cannot show:** One UHP schedule that both sterilises spores and preserves raw-fish texture.

## Key findings
- Quality control and microbial inactivation are both in-scope; endogenous enzymes and colour/texture trade-offs are emphasised (keywords/body).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Narrative review; aquatic matrices differ strongly (fat, salt, shell).

## Relevance to our research question
Supports the seafood/fish HPP quality papers in the sample (Castrica, Wang, Nilsuwan, Lian).
