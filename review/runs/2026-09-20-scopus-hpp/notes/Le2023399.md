# Influence of high pressure processing on qualities of celery-based juices during refrigerated storage

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Le, Uyen Ngoc Phuong; Ratphitagsanti, Wannasawat; Therdthai, Nantawan. 2023. Agriculture and Natural Resources 57: 399–408. https://doi.org/10.34044/j.anres.2023.57.3.04
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-scopus-hpp/extracted/Le2023399.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Can natural acidification (lime or apple juice) plus HPP improve microbial and enzyme inactivation and extend refrigerated shelf life of celery-based juices without large losses of phenolics and antioxidant capacity?

## Methods
3×3 factorial completely randomized design: three formulations × three HPP pressures. Juices packed in LLDPE pouches, double-bagged, treated in HPP600MPa/5L (Bao Tou KeFA) at 400, 500, or 600 MPa for 5 min, then stored at 4 ± 2 °C for 8 weeks (sampled weeks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8; quality also 1 d after HPP). Endpoints: TAC (PCA, 35 °C, 48 h) and yeasts/molds (PDA, 25 °C, 120 h); pH; TSS; L*a*b* and ΔE*; residual PPO and POD; TPC (Folin–Ciocalteu, mg GAE/100 mL); FRAP and DPPH. ANOVA and Tukey, p < 0.05. At least two replications.

## Sample / data
Celery (*Apium graveolens*), Granny Smith apple, and lime from a Bangkok supermarket. Formulations: celery-lime (90% celery / 10% lime), celery-apple (85% / 15%), celery (100%). Target pH bands: high-acid <3.5, acidic <4.5, low-acid ~5.0–6.0. Untreated fresh juices: pH 3.30 ± 0.08, 4.50 ± 0.08, 6.02 ± 0.06; TAC 3.39 ± 0.09, 5.74 ± 0.01, 5.58 ± 0.03 log CFU/mL; YM 3.37 ± 0.07, 2.95 ± 0.23, 3.13 ± 0.03 log CFU/mL (Table 1).

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Factorial laboratory HPP trial with refrigerated storage vs untreated juice
- **Population / model:** Celery-based juices (celery, celery-lime, celery-apple)
- **n:** at least two replications (mean ± SD)
- **Intervention / comparator:** HPP 400, 500, or 600 MPa / 5 min vs untreated; acidification via 10% lime or 15% apple juice; 8 wk at 4 ± 2 °C
- **Primary endpoint:** Total aerobic count (log CFU/mL) and enzyme residual activity (PPO/POD %); also TPC and antioxidant capacity
- **Primary result:** Untreated juices spoiled after 1 week (gas, fermented odor). HPP reduced TAC to <2 log CFU/mL in all formulations and kept it there for 8 wk; YM not detected in any HPP juice throughout storage. Residual PPO/POD reduced to under 36% (celery-lime) and under 50% (celery juice) in the authors’ summary; celery-lime POD RA after 600 MPa/5 min was 30.37% (wk 0) and 31.15% (wk 8). Antioxidant capacity was maintained at more than 50%; FRAP declined ~14% (celery-lime), ~46% (celery), ~60% (celery-apple) by week 8. Authors judged 600 MPa most suitable.
- **Cannot show:** Comparison with thermal pasteurization (no heat arm); 5-log pathogen challenge; commercial-scale packs; vitamin C or individual phenolics.

## Key findings
- Juice formulation and HPP (and their interaction) significantly affected TAC, YM, TSS, ΔE*, PPO, POD, FRAP, and DPPH; pH was affected by formulation only (Table 2).
- After HPP, pH unchanged through storage; TSS slightly reduced then stable (celery-lime 3.57–3.80 °Brix; celery-apple 5.20–5.40; celery 3.30–3.43) (Results).
- Post-treatment ΔE* of celery-lime 3.03 ± 0.45 (medium visible difference); celery juice 1.50 ± 0.17 (below JND 2.3). Celery-apple ΔE* >5 from the start of storage (Results).
- TPC of celery-lime and celery held at 37–38 mg GAE/100 mL over 8 wk; celery-apple fell from 46–47 to ~38 mg GAE/100 mL (Results). Celery juice at 600 MPa had 38.45 mg GAE/100 mL at week 8, higher than other pressures.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors state longer holding times may be needed for stronger enzyme inactivation (Discussion).
- Only ≥2 replications; enzyme data for PPO in celery juice described as “data not shown.”
- No thermal comparator; untreated controls spoiled too fast for 8-week quality tracking.

## Relevance to our research question
Juice-matrix HPP evidence with a pH hurdle: 600 MPa/5 min plus lime acidification kept TAC <2 log CFU/mL and YM undetectable for 8 weeks while holding phenolics better than the apple blend. Directly relevant to juice quality, enzymes, and refrigerated shelf-life.
