# Acceptability of and attitudes to the therapeutic use of cannabis and cannabidiol in people with Parkinson’s disease: A French survey

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Barré T, Cazorla G, Di Beo V, Lopez F, Radoszycki L, Maradan G, Baunez C, Carrieri P. Clinical Parkinsonism & Related Disorders 11 (2024) 100286. doi:10.1016/j.prdoa.2024.100286
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Barré2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How acceptable is therapeutic cannabis (THC above 0.3%) and CBD (THC ≤ 0.3%) to people with Parkinson’s disease (PD) in France, and which clinical and knowledge factors are associated with acceptability?

## Methods
Nationwide cross-sectional online survey CANNABAPA, 22 May–14 July 2023 (Voxco). Inclusion: age ≥18 years and PD diagnosis. Recruited via France Parkinson (>35,000 emails; ~5,000 members), Carenity (1,857 contacts), local associations, and snowballing. Ethics: INSERM IRB00003888, CD/EB 23-045. Outcomes from two questions each for cannabis and CBD (use if prescribed; use over the counter): low / moderate / absolute acceptability. Binary logistic regression of moderate/absolute vs low (p < 0.05 after backward selection). Cannabinoid knowledge: 0–4 ad hoc true/false items. GAD-2, PHQ-2, fatigue, disability, pain, sleep items.

## Sample / data
n = 1136 completers (54.8% men; median age 68 years, IQR 62–74; median time since PD diagnosis 7 years, IQR 4.0–11.0). 34.6% had limitations performing basic daily activities or worse. 89.1% reached via France Parkinson; 2.9% via Carenity.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Cross-sectional online survey (attitudes; not a treatment trial).
- **Population / model:** Self-reported PD in France (association-enriched web sample).
- **n:** 1136.
- **Intervention / comparator:** None; cannabis vs CBD acceptability compared within respondents.
- **Primary endpoint:** Acceptability of medical cannabis and of CBD (moderate/absolute vs low), derived from prescription and OTC inclination questions.
- **Primary result:** Cannabis acceptability 81.7% (moderate 49.3% + absolute 32.4%; low 18.3%); CBD 87.4% (moderate 45.0% + absolute 42.4%; low 12.6%). Absolute acceptability higher for CBD than cannabis (z-test p < 0.001); low acceptability higher for cannabis (p < 0.001). Moderate/absolute acceptability associated with GAD-2 ≥ 3 (cannabis aOR 1.48, 95% CI 1.03–2.11, p = 0.033; CBD aOR 1.92, 1.24–2.98, p = 0.003), cannabinoid knowledge (aOR 1.25, 1.09–1.43, p = 0.001; CBD 1.44, 1.22–1.69, p < 0.001), informing oneself about medical cannabis (aOR 1.8, 1.27–2.55, p = 0.001; CBD 2.73, 1.80–4.14, p < 0.001), and high/very high perceived dependence risk (aOR 0.16, 0.09–0.28, p < 0.001; CBD 0.23, 0.12–0.44, p < 0.001) (Table 2). CBD also associated with better household economic status (comfortable aOR 3.18, 1.63–6.22, p = 0.001).
- **Cannot show:** Clinical efficacy or safety of cannabis/CBD in PD; nationally representative prevalence of use; causal predictors of future uptake.

## Key findings
- “Do not know” to prescription-based question: 14.1% cannabis, 9.7% CBD; OTC: 23.7% and 19.5% (Results).
- Fear of dependence was a main barrier; healthcare providers were rarely sources of medical-cannabis information (Abstract).
- PD is not an eligible indication in France’s medical cannabis experiment (Introduction).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Not representative of all French people with PD; France Parkinson oversampling may overestimate information-seeking and acceptability (Discussion).
- Web format may select less disabled patients; people interested in cannabinoids likely overrepresented (Discussion).
- Male-to-female ratio slightly lower than expected nationally (Discussion).
- Self-report diagnosis and symptoms.

## Relevance to our research question
Patient-level survey evidence on cannabis/CBD acceptability in PD in a jurisdiction where THC is illegal for PD. Complements clinical outcome trials; does not measure PET or efficacy.
