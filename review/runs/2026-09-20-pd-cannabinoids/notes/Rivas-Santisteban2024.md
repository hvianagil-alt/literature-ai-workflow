# Cannabinoid regulation of angiotensin II-induced calcium signaling in striatal neurons

- **Citation:** Rivas-Santisteban R, Muñoz A, Lillo J, Raïch I, Rodríguez-Pérez AI, Navarro G, Labandeira-García JL, Franco R. npj Parkinsons Dis. 2024;10:220. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41531-024-00827-7
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Rivas-Santisteban2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Do CB1 and AT1 receptors form heteromers that let cannabinoids blunt angiotensin II–evoked Ca2+ signals in striatal neurons, and does heteromer abundance change in the 6-OHDA rat PD model with or without levodopa-induced dyskinesia?

## Methods
HEK-293T heterologous expression: cAMP (forskolin), ERK1/2, BRET2 (CB1-Rluc + AT1-GFP2 vs ACE2 negative control), GCaMP Ca2+ imaging with Ang II 100 nM, ACEA 100 nM, candesartan 1 µM, rimonabant 1 µM. Primary striatal neurons: PLA and Ca2+ imaging. Male Wistar rats, 8 weeks: 12 µg 6-OHDA in 4 µL into right MFB; amphetamine rotation and cylinder (<20% impaired paw) to confirm lesion; 16 lesioned rats received levodopa methyl ester 6 mg/kg + benserazide 10 mg/kg s.c. for 3 weeks, split dyskinetic (AIM score ≥2) vs non-dyskinetic. Striatal PLA in NeuN+ cells. Statistics when group size at least n = 5; HEK Ca2+ n = 5 independent experiments in duplicate.

## Sample / data
HEK assays: 5 independent experiments. Rat groups: (i) non-lesioned, (ii) lesioned-vehicle, (iii) lesioned levodopa non-dyskinetic, (iv) lesioned levodopa dyskinetic. Lesion >95% striatal TH loss. Exact n per PLA group not listed beyond n ≥ 5 rule and 16 levodopa-treated lesioned animals.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** preclinical molecular/cellular plus 6-OHDA rat tissue (no cannabinoid *treatment* of PD animals)
- **Population / model:** HEK-293T; primary striatal neurons; male Wistar 6-OHDA hemiparkinsonian rats ± levodopa
- **n:** HEK Ca2+ = 5 experiments; rat PLA ≥5/group; 16 lesioned rats on levodopa
- **Intervention / comparator:** ACEA vs Ang II ± rimonabant/candesartan in vitro; in vivo comparator is lesion and dyskinesia status, not a cannabinoid drug
- **Primary endpoint:** AT1-driven cytosolic Ca2+ (ΔF/F0) and PLA AT1CB1Het dots per NeuN+ striatal neuron
- **Primary result:** In striatal neurons, ACEA cut the Ang II ΔF/F0 peak by approximately half and returned fluorescence to baseline in <90 s (Figure 5). Lesioned striatum: neurons with AT1CB1Hets fell from >95% (non-lesioned) to ≈45% (lesioned). Text then states complexes in ≈23% of levodopa-treated non-dyskinetic neurons and ≈70% in “levodopa-treated non-dyskinetic” again (likely a dyskinetic/non-dyskinetic mix-up; Figure 6J). TH lesion virtually complete (>95%) in the right striatum (Figure 6A–D).
- **Cannot show:** that giving cannabinoids in vivo reduces dyskinesia or protects DA neurons in this paper; BRET/cross-antagonism is mechanistic, not a PD outcome trial.

## Key findings
- BRET2 saturation between CB1 and AT1; linear (no interaction) with ACE2 (Figure 2E–F).
- Cross-antagonism on cAMP and ERK when both receptors are coexpressed (Figure 1C, F).
- In HEK cells coexpressing both receptors, ACEA reduced AT1-mediated Ca2+ mobilization (Figure 3; vs Ang II ***p < 0.001 in panels D/F).
- PLA confirmed AT1CB1Hets in primary striatal neurons (Figure 4).
- Heteromer load depended on 6-OHDA lesion and on whether levodopa produced dyskinesia; authors interpret partial recovery in dyskinetic rats as a compensatory mechanism (Abstract).

## Limitations
- Cannabinoid was applied only in cells (ACEA), not as a therapeutic in the 6-OHDA rats.
- PLA quantification sentence lists ≈70% twice for “non-dyskinetic,” conflicting with the Abstract’s partial recovery in dyskinetic animals.
- Male rats only; 6-OHDA is a toxin model.
- Ca2+ “approximately half” is not a fitted IC50.

## Relevance to our research question
Mechanism paper: CB1 can restrain AT1/Ca2+ signaling relevant to striatum and PD/LID tissue. Supports cannabinoid *receptor* biology in a 6-OHDA model but is not an efficacy study of CBD/THC/CBG.
