# Cannabinoids: Potential for Modulation and Enhancement When Combined with Vitamin B12 in Case of Neurodegenerative Disorders

- **Citation:** Kaszyńska AA. Cannabinoids: Potential for Modulation and Enhancement When Combined with Vitamin B12 in Case of Neurodegenerative Disorders. Pharmaceuticals. 2024;17(6):813. doi:10.3390/ph17060813
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Kaszyńska2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Can cannabinoids (especially CBD) that modulate protein aggregation, microglia, mitochondria, and homeodynamics be combined with vitamin B12 to enhance neuroprotection across neurodegenerative diseases, including Parkinson’s disease (PD)?

## Methods

Single-author narrative review of ECS pharmacology (CB1/CB2, orphan GPCRs, TRPV1, PPARs), cannabinoid effects on aggregation and microglia, disease-by-disease discussion (HD, ALS, AD, PD, MS), then vitamin B12 pathways and hypothetical CBD–B12 combinations (including liposomes/micelles). No search protocol, dates, databases, or included-study count. No meta-analysis. Combination of CBD with B12 is framed as a hypothesis (including MS-related CBD–B12 observations and delivery systems from cited papers).

## Sample / data

Cited preclinical and some clinical literature across AD, PD, HD, ALS, and MS, plus B12/homocysteine/BBB studies. PD-specific cannabinoid evidence is a small subset (α-synuclein/ECS rodent work; 6-OHDA CBD/THC; C. elegans CBD). No n of included studies.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative review / mechanistic synthesis (not RCT; not systematic).
- **Population / model:** Neurodegenerative diseases as a class (PD, MS, AD, HD, ALS); PD discussed via α-synuclein, 6-OHDA, and C. elegans models.
- **n:** not reported.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Cannabinoids (Δ9-THC, CBD, CBG, THCV) ± hypothesized vitamin B12 co-therapy versus no quantitative comparator.
- **Primary endpoint:** Conceptual: protein aggregation, microglial phenotype, mitochondrial/Cx43 function, and (for PD) dopaminergic survival and locomotion in cited models.
- **Primary result (PD-specific, as stated):** PD is marked by α-synuclein aggregation in Lewy bodies. Kelly et al. (2022) α-synuclein overexpression in rats: decreased 2-AG and OEA with concurrently diminished neuroinflammatory markers. In 6-OHDA PD, Δ9-THC and CBD reduced dopaminergic neuron loss; CBD increased striatal dopamine independently of CB1R/CB2R. da Cruz Guedes et al. (2023) C. elegans: CBD reversed reserpine- and α-synuclein–induced locomotor deficits; effects on pharyngeal pumping did not alter defecation or egg production. Conclusions: CBD “shows promise in reversing locomotor changes in Parkinson’s disease independently of NPR-19 receptors, while also protecting dopaminergic neurons and reducing reactive oxygen species accumulation.” Elevated homocysteine from B12 deficiency is linked to PD risk in the B12 section (oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, dopaminergic degeneration)—this is B12 pathophysiology, not a cannabinoid trial result. No PD clinical trial of CBD plus B12 is presented.
- **Cannot show:** That combining cannabinoids with vitamin B12 improves PD in humans; clinical motor or non-motor efficacy of CBD/THC in PD; a count or quality appraisal of PD trials; disease modification.

## Key findings

- CBD is synthesized as anti-inflammatory (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β down; IL-10 up), shifting microglia M1→M2, with antioxidant and calcium-homeostasis effects relevant to aggregation diseases including PD (Section 2.4).
- GPR55 is discussed as a PD-relevant target (dopamine release/motor behavior in animal models).
- CBD’s calcium-homeostasis role is “assumed” beneficial in PD (citation in cannabinoids section).
- Combination strategy (CBD + B12, nanoparticles, micelles, RNA aptamers) is prospective; authors call for RCTs and rigorous testing before clinical use.
- Clinical trials in neurological motor disorders are said to have a high failure rate (HD context), cautioning translation.

## Limitations

- No systematic search or n.
- PD human evidence is not synthesized as trials; PD conclusions rest on a few cited preclinical papers.
- CBD–B12 synergy is hypothesized from MS/other contexts, not demonstrated in PD.
- Broad disease coverage dilutes PD-specific clinical interpretation.

## Relevance to our research question

Relevant as a 2024 neurodegeneration review that includes PD α-synuclein/ECS and preclinical CBD/THC neuroprotection. It does not answer whether cannabinoids work in people with PD and should not be used as clinical PD efficacy evidence. The B12 combination is out-of-sample for PD trials.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Whether NPR-19 independence is only the C. elegans finding (yes in the PD paragraph).
- No PD RCT of CBD+B12 appears in the text—confirm none is hidden in figures.
- Homocysteine–PD association is B12 literature, not cannabinoid outcome.
