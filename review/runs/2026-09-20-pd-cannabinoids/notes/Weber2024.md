# Oral Cannabidiol for Seborrheic Dermatitis in Patients With Parkinson Disease: Randomized Clinical Trial

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Weber I, Zagona-Prizio C, Sivesind TE, Adelman M, Szeto MD, Liu Y, Sillau SH, Bainbridge J, Klawitter J, Sempio C, Dunnick CA, Leehey MA, Dellavalle RP. JMIR Dermatology 2024;7:e49965. doi:10.2196/49965. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03582137.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Weber2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Is short-term oral cannabidiol (CBD)-rich cannabis extract associated with a decrease in seborrheic dermatitis (SD) presence or severity among people with Parkinson disease (PD)?

## Methods
Secondary dermatologic analysis of a randomized (1:1), parallel, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of oral sesame-oil CBD-rich extract (100 mg/mL CBD and 3.3 mg/mL THC) at 2.5 mg/kg/day CBD for PD motor symptoms. Dosing: 1.25 mg/kg each morning for 4 ± 1 days, then twice daily for 10 ± 4 days (~16 days). Deidentified facial photographs before and after treatment were scored independently by two board-certified dermatologists using SEDASI (0–60). Presence of SD and SEDASI averages were analyzed with GEE relative-risk models and longitudinal regression / paired and two-sample t-tests. Plasma cannabinoids were measured at the final dose visit.

## Sample / data
Adults 40–85 years with idiopathic PD at University of Colorado Hospital, September 2018–January 2022. n = 26 CBD and n = 27 placebo. Mean age 70.6 (SD 6.3) CBD vs 68.7 (SD 7.5) placebo (P = .34); female 10/26 (38%) vs 7/27 (26%). CBD group trended toward longer disease duration (P = .07) and higher total MDS-UPDRS (P = .08). Mean final CBD blood level used in GEE: 49.29 ng/mL.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Nested secondary analysis of a parallel double-blind placebo-controlled RCT (motor trial; SD outcomes secondary).
- **Population / model:** Adults with idiopathic PD (trial eligibility also mentions concurrent SD in one Methods paragraph; analyzed cohort is the motor-trial sample with photographs).
- **n:** 53 (26 CBD, 27 placebo).
- **Intervention / comparator:** Oral CBD-rich extract 2.5 mg/kg/day CBD (with 3.3% THC relative to CBD concentration in the 100 mg/mL formulation) vs matching sesame-oil placebo for ~16 days.
- **Primary endpoint (this paper):** Presence of SD and SEDASI average (0–60); parent trial primary was PD motor efficacy (not reported here).
- **Primary result:** No treatment effect. SD present: CBD 12/26 (46.2%) pre vs 9/26 (34.6%) post (P = .26); placebo 15/27 (55.6%) vs 18/27 (66.7%) (P = .26); treatment-effect P = .12. Post vs pre risk ratio for SD: CBD 0.69 (95% CI 0.41–1.18; P = .15); placebo 1.20 (95% CI 0.88–1.65; P = .26); groups differed at P = .07. SEDASI mean: CBD 3.63 to 3.79 (P = .81); placebo 5.39 to 4.65 (P = .35); treatment effect P = .38 (Table 2). GEE with final CBD level: trend toward reduced SD vs placebo at mean CBD 49.29 ng/mL (P = .07).
- **Cannot show:** Adequately powered effect of oral CBD on SD; motor efficacy (not in this report); long-term dermatologic benefit.

## Key findings
- SD severity was low at baseline in both groups (Results; Table 2).
- No unexpected serious AEs; no significant dermatologic AEs; no notable liver-test changes (Results).
- Authors state the parent trial was powered for motor symptoms, not SD (Conclusions).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- 16-day exposure limited by drug availability; may be too short (Limitations).
- Low SD severity limited detection of change (Limitations).
- Concurrent topical medications as possible confounders (Limitations).
- Underpowered for SD (Conclusions).
- Placebo slightly differed in appearance/odor despite masking procedures (Methods).

## Relevance to our research question
Clinical RCT of oral CBD (with low THC) in PD; the reported endpoints are cutaneous (SD), not motor/non-motor PD scales. Relevant as cannabinoid exposure in PD with null SD result.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Methods say eligibility required concurrent SD, but Table 2 shows SD present in only ~46–56% at baseline—possible mismatch between eligibility wording and analyzed presence definition.
- Parent-trial MDS-UPDRS motor results are not in this text.
