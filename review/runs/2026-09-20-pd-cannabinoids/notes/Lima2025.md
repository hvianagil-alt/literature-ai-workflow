# Preventive beneficial effects of cannabidiol in a reserpine-induced progressive model of parkinsonism

- **Citation:** Lima AC, Bioni VS, Becegato MS, Meier Y, Cunha DMG, Aguiar NA, Gonçalves N, Peres FF, Zuardi AW, Hallak JEC, Crippa JA, Smaili SS, Abilio VC, Silva RH. Front Pharmacol. 2025;16:1539783. doi: 10.3389/fphar.2025.1539783
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Lima2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Does CBD (0.5 mg/kg) delay or prevent motor and nigral tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) changes in a low-dose repeated-reserpine progressive parkinsonism model, when given concurrently with reserpine versus before reserpine?

## Methods
Six-month-old male Swiss mice received subcutaneous reserpine 0.1 mg/kg or vehicle and intraperitoneal CBD 0.5 mg/kg (1% Tween 80/saline) or saline. Two protocols: concomitant (CBD during reserpine) and preventive (CBD started before reserpine). Outcomes: catalepsy bar, vacuous chewing, open-field distance, SNpc TH+ cell counts (Mann–Whitney). Repeated-measures ANOVA with Sidak post hoc.

## Sample / data
N = 75 mice started; 1 euthanized for fight injuries. Figure/table n typically 9–10 per group (concomitant catalepsy/vacuous chewing n = 10; OFT and TH n = 9–10). Immunohistochemistry reported as median with min–max.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** preclinical in vivo (two-arm pharmacological parkinsonism; concomitant vs preventive CBD)
- **Population / model:** 6-month-old male Swiss mice; repeated low-dose reserpine (0.1 mg/kg s.c.)
- **n:** total 75; analyses n = 9–10/group
- **Intervention / comparator:** CBD 0.5 mg/kg i.p. daily vs saline; reserpine vs vehicle
- **Primary endpoint:** catalepsy latency (s, phased over reserpine injections) and SNpc TH+ cell count
- **Primary result:** Preventive CBD reduced catalepsy vs Res/Sal at initial (p = 0.029) and intermediate (p = 0.05) phases; Res/Sal catalepsy was elevated at initial, intermediate, and motor phases (p = 0.03, 0.003, 0.005). Concomitant CBD delayed catalepsy onset (Res/CBD vs control only at intermediate p = 0.002 and motor p < 0.001) but Res/Sal vs Res/CBD was not significant. TH+: concomitant Res/CBD > Res/Sal (U = 19.000, p = 0.019, r = 0.53); preventive Res/CBD > Res/Sal (U = 21.000, p = 0.028, r = 0.50). Absolute TH+ counts not reported in text. (Figures 2, 4, 5, 7)
- **Cannot show:** human PD, α-synuclein pathology, or that CBD restores locomotion (OFT: no CBD rescue of reserpine hypolocomotion).

## Key findings
- Concomitant CBD did not reduce vacuous chewing (both reserpine groups elevated at intermediate and motor phases; Sal p = 0.024/0.001, CBD p = 0.012/0.002) (Section 3.1.2).
- Preventive CBD: vacuous chewing increased only in Res/Sal at motor phase (p = 0.013); Res/Sal vs Res/CBD at motor phase not significant (Section 3.2.2).
- Open field (Table 1, n = 9–10): concomitant motor-phase distance Res/Sal 2.525 ± 1.070 m vs Veh/Sal 10.467 ± 0.786 m; Res/CBD 2.669 ± 0.592 m (no CBD rescue). Only Veh/CBD vs Veh/Sal locomotion increase (p = 0.016; concomitant motor CBD 11.956 ± 1.437 m).
- Res/Sal reduced SNpc TH+ vs Veh/Sal (concomitant U = 14.000, p = 0.040, r = 0.51; preventive U = 13.500, p = 0.01, r = 0.60).

## Limitations
- Male mice only; single CBD dose from a prior acute-reserpine study.
- Reserpine is a vesicular monoamine-depletion model, not a synucleinopathy.
- Authors note catalepsy vs oral-movement dissociation and that motor scores can look similar to Res/Sal even when TH is preserved.
- Outliers/n drop to 9 not fully itemized beyond one fight-injury death.

## Relevance to our research question
One of the few CBD studies in a *progressive* pharmacological PD-like model. Preventive CBD more clearly cut catalepsy; both schedules preserved nigral TH. Supports timing of cannabinoid dosing as a modifier.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Exact TH+ cell numbers (only U statistics in text).
- Precise number of reserpine injections per protocol (text references 10th and 20th injections and 40 catalepsy assessments).
- Whether 0.5 mg/kg is relevant to human CBD exposure.
