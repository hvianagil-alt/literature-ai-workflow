# Impaired Gait, Postural Instability, and Rigidity in Relation to CB1 Receptor Availability in Parkinson's Disease

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Ajalin R, Al-Abdulrasul H, Tuisku J, Hirvonen J, Lahdenpohja S, Rinne JO, Brück A. Movement Disorders 2025;40(1):163–167. doi:10.1002/mds.30042
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Ajalin2025163.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Is cerebral CB1R availability on [18F]FMPEP-d2 PET related to specific motor features of Parkinson’s disease (PD)—especially postural instability and gait disorder (PIGD) and rigidity—versus tremor or bradykinesia?

## Methods
Cross-sectional [18F]FMPEP-d2 PET (VT) in idiopathic PD (UK Brain Bank plus confirmatory [18F]FDOPA HRRT PET). Motor scores from UPDRS-III; PIGD, rigidity, tremor, and bradykinesia subscores. Voxel-wise multiple regression of VT vs subscores with age and sex as covariates; FDR P < 0.05. Patients on individual standard PD medication; none used hormone replacement or reported smoking. Authors previously reported lower CB1R OFF medication vs controls and increase toward normal ON medication.

## Sample / data
15 PD (8 male / 7 female). Age 66 ± 6 years (52–74); disease duration 10 ± 5 years (2–18); H&Y 2.5 (1.5–3); UPDRS-III OFF 23 ± 8 (8–36); gait + postural instability score 2 ± 1 (0–4); rigidity 5 ± 3 (0–12); bradykinesia 2 ± 1 (Table 1).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Cross-sectional observational PET–motor correlation (brief report); not a drug trial.
- **Population / model:** Idiopathic PD with nigrostriatal hypofunction on FDOPA PET.
- **n:** 15.
- **Intervention / comparator:** None (no cannabinoid administered); motor-subtype contrasts within PD.
- **Primary endpoint:** Correlation of [18F]FMPEP-d2 VT with UPDRS-III total and motor subscores (FDR P < 0.05).
- **Primary result:** Negative correlation of VT with PIGD score (P = 0.002; cluster 266,770 voxels over both hemispheres: temporal, parietal, frontal, cerebellum, white matter) and with rigidity (P < 0.001; cluster 627,855 voxels including thalamus and basal ganglia). Tremor P = 0.995; bradykinesia P = 0.980; UPDRS-III total P = 0.144 (not significant). CB1R–FDOPA correlation did not survive FDR P < 0.05.
- **Cannot show:** That targeting CB1R improves PIGD or rigidity; causal pathway; independence from dopaminergic state (patients were on medication; OFF UPDRS listed in Table 1).

## Key findings
- Lower CB1R availability associated with worse gait/postural instability and rigidity, not tremor or bradykinesia (Abstract; Results).
- Findings replicated with non-parametric testing, ROI analysis, and partial-volume correction (Results).
- Discussion argues dopaminergic deficit alone is unlikely because correlations extended to cerebellar and occipital regions with few dopaminergic projections.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors call for larger samples and mixed motor/non-motor phenotypes (Discussion); n = 15 is small.
- Brief report; limited multivariable clinical covariates beyond age and sex.
- Medication state: PET on usual medication vs UPDRS-III labelled OFF in Table 1—interpretation of ON/OFF pairing needs care.
- No healthy-control correlation arm in this paper.

## Relevance to our research question
CB1 PET evidence that the endocannabinoid system tracks PIGD and rigidity in PD, not tremor/bradykinesia. Pathophysiology relevant to cannabinoids in PD; no clinical outcome of cannabis/CBD/nabilone.
