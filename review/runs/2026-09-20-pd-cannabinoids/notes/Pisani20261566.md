# Gray Matter Volume Loss in Parkinson's Disease Psychosis and Cannabinoid Receptor Gene Expression in the Brain

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Pisani S, Váša F, Velayudhan L, Bhattacharyya S. Movement Disorders 2026;41(6):1566–1571. doi:10.1002/mds.70222
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Pisani20261566.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Is the spatial pattern of gray-matter volume loss in Parkinson’s disease psychosis (PDP), relative to PD without psychosis (PDnP), associated with regional brain expression of genes coding for CB1 and CB2 receptors (from an independent healthy postmortem atlas)?

## Methods
Secondary analysis. Hedges’ g maps of gray-matter volume loss came from the authors’ prior coordinate-based SDM-PSI meta-analysis (search date 7 June 2021; 10 voxel-based morphometry MRI studies comparing PDP vs PDnP). CB1 (CNR1) and CB2 (CNR2) mRNA from Allen Human Brain Atlas microarray data in 6 healthy donors, parcellated to Desikan–Killiany cortex plus subcortical regions. Pearson correlations of effect-size maps with gene-expression maps; significance via BrainSMASH spatial-autocorrelation-preserving surrogates (10,000 maps). Analyses unadjusted and LEDD-adjusted.

## Sample / data
Meta-analysis: 211 PDP (age 69.01 ± 4.90 years; 52.1% male; LEDD 651.94 ± 318.14; MDS-UPDRS III 28.72 ± 11.69) and 298 PDnP (age 67.34 ± 4.59; 49.1% male; LEDD 577.06 ± 263.11; MDS-UPDRS III 25.94 ± 8.63) from 10 MRI studies. Gene expression: 6 healthy brain donors (Allen atlas)—not PD patients. No cannabinoid intervention.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Secondary spatial correlation of a VBM meta-analysis with postmortem gene-expression maps (observational; not a clinical trial).
- **Population / model:** PDP vs PDnP MRI cohorts (pooled); CB1/CB2 expression from healthy donors.
- **n:** 211 PDP + 298 PDnP (volume maps); 6 donors (mRNA); 10 MRI studies.
- **Intervention / comparator:** None. Contrast is PDP vs PDnP volume loss vs CNR1/CNR2 expression.
- **Primary endpoint:** Pearson r between regional Hedges’ g (PDP-related volume loss) and CB1/CB2 expression, with spatial-autocorrelation-corrected P.
- **Primary result:** Unadjusted CB1: r = 0.337, t(76) = 3.122, P = 0.003 (uncorrected); spatial-autocorrelation-corrected P < 0.001. LEDD-adjusted CB1: r = 0.357, t(76) = 3.328, P = 0.001; corrected P = 0.015. Unadjusted CB2: r = 0.221, t(76) = 1.976, P = 0.052; corrected P = 0.090. LEDD-adjusted CB2: r = 0.229, t(76) = 2.055, P = 0.043; corrected P = 0.132 (not significant after spatial correction). Abstract reports CB1 r = 0.337, t(76) = 3.122, P < 0.001 and CB2 P = 0.132 corrected.
- **Cannot show:** That CB1 expression was measured in PDP brains; that cannabis/CBD treats or causes PDP; in vivo receptor availability (this is mRNA in healthy donors vs MRI in patients).

## Key findings
- Prior meta-analysis: lower gray matter in parietal, occipital, and temporal areas in PDP vs PDnP, unchanged after LEDD and cognitive-status control (Results).
- Positive association: higher regional CB1 expression density aligned with larger effect size of volume difference in PDP vs PDnP (Discussion).
- No CB2 association after spatial autocorrelation correction (Results).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Secondary analysis: volume and CB1 data from different individuals (Limitations).
- Coordinate-based peaks are less informative than statistical maps (Limitations).
- Healthy-donor mRNA is a proxy for receptor density, not PD pathology.
- Cross-level inference (meta-analytic MRI + atlas) cannot establish mechanism (excitotoxicity vs neurodegeneration hypotheses untested).

## Relevance to our research question
Links PDP-related atrophy topography to CB1 gene expression, supporting ECS involvement in PD psychosis pathophysiology. Not a clinical cannabinoid, PET occupancy, or survey study; no patient cannabinoid exposure.
