# Cannabidiol in Neurology: Current Insights and Translational Perspectives

- **Citation:** Białoń M, Kędziora M, Starowicz K. Cannabidiol in Neurology: Current Insights and Translational Perspectives. Pharmaceuticals. 2026;19(2):330. doi:10.3390/ph19020330
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Białoń2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

What are the preclinical and clinical outcomes of CBD (as distinct from THC or whole-plant cannabis) across neurological conditions, including Parkinson’s disease, and what are the translational risks (PK, drug–drug interactions, placebo/blinding)?

## Methods

Literature search of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar through December 2025; English-language; keywords including cannabidiol/CBD and Parkinson’s disease among other neurology terms; reference-list screening. Eligible: in vitro, animal, or human CBD studies; original research, reviews, and clinical trial reports. Excluded: THC-only, whole-plant cannabis without distinguishing CBD, non-neurological indications, non-peer-reviewed sources. PRISMA-style flow: 420 database records + 12 from citation searching; 107 studies included in what the authors call a systematic review. Table 2 catalogs clinical studies by condition.

## Sample / data

107 included studies spanning epilepsy, MS, AD, stroke/TBI, PD, and neuropathic pain. PD clinical rows in Table 2 include (as listed): 60 PD patients randomized CBD-enriched sublingual product vs placebo (12 weeks); 61 PD patients (CBD/THC n=31 vs placebo n=30) oral sesame oil to 2.5 mg/kg/day (2 weeks); and additional PD tremor/open-label designs described in the table. Preclinical PD: 6-OHDA, reserpine, rotenone, PC12.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Systematic-style narrative/translational review of CBD in neurology (PRISMA flow; mixed preclinical + clinical; not an RCT).
- **Population / model:** In vitro/animal neurological models and human clinical populations; PD is one of several indications.
- **n:** 107 studies included (420 + 12 identified). Number of PD-only studies is not given as a separate total.
- **Intervention / comparator:** CBD (sometimes with THC in clinical PD extracts) versus placebo or disease-model controls. Epidiolex/Sativex discussed as approved products for epilepsy/MS, not PD.
- **Primary endpoint:** Condition-specific (seizures, spasticity, cognition, motor/non-motor PD, pain); PD synthesis emphasizes motor/non-motor symptoms, α-synuclein/TH preservation in animals, and clinical UPDRS/cognition/sleep.
- **Primary result (PD):** Preclinical: CBD increased PC12 viability after 6-OHDA (Nrf2/Bcl-2, lower Casp3/Bax); chronic CBD in rat PD reduced nigrostriatal degeneration/inflammation and improved motor performance via astrocytic TRPV1/CNTF; CBD reduced 6-OHDA hyperalgesia/allodynia; in 6-OHDA/reserpine/rotenone models CBD reduced α-synuclein, preserved TH+ neurons, delayed motor deficits; CBD and PECS-101 reduced LID without blocking L-DOPA benefit (blocked by CB1/PPARγ antagonists); CBD lipid nanoparticles restored dopamine and motor/memory comparably to L-DOPA in PD rats (Lapmanee et al.). Clinical: mixed. Some studies suggest QoL, anxiety, or task-related tremor benefits; larger RCTs failed to show superior motor or cognitive benefit vs placebo. Example: high CBD/low THC extract—no benefit, worsened cognition and sleep, many mild AEs vs placebo. Authors’ PD summary: preclinical promising; clinical trials “do not have such direct positive effects”; dosing/duration heterogeneous.
- **Cannot show:** Disease-modifying CBD therapy in PD; a pooled PD effect size; that isolate CBD matches nanoparticle or entourage preclinical success; superiority of CBD over placebo for core PD motor signs.

## Key findings

- Only approved pure-CBD drug is Epidiolex (severe epilepsies); Sativex (CBD+THC) for MS spasticity.
- Oral CBD bioavailability ~6–13%; ~4× with high-fat meal; smoking ~31%.
- Meta-analysis of RCTs (cited): after excluding childhood epilepsy, diarrhea was the only AE associated with CBD.
- Strongest clinical CBD evidence is epilepsy; for PD, MS, AD, stroke, chronic pain evidence is preliminary/mixed (Conclusions).
- Translational gap attributed to low oral bioavailability, variable protocols, isolate vs multi-target preclinical designs, placebo/expectancy, and CYP3A4/CYP2C19 (and P-gp) interactions.

## Limitations

- Inclusion of reviews plus original studies (heterogeneous evidence hierarchy).
- PD clinical n per trial is modest (e.g., 60; 61; smaller tremor studies).
- Mixed CBD vs CBD/THC products in PD rows.
- No PD-specific meta-analysis.

## Relevance to our research question

High: recent systematic-style CBD neurology review with an explicit PD section concluding a preclinical–clinical gap. Useful for ranking evidence (epilepsy >> PD) and for stating that RCTs have not confirmed motor benefit.
