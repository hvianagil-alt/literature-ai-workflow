# Therapeutic use of medical Cannabis in neurological diseases: a clinical update

- **Citation:** Hidding U, Mainka T, Buhmann C. Therapeutic use of medical Cannabis in neurological diseases: a clinical update. Journal of Neural Transmission. 2024;131(2):117–126. doi:10.1007/s00702-023-02719-1
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Hidding2024117.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

What is the clinical evidence for medical cannabis/cannabinoids across neurological diseases, and what do German prescription-survey data and daily-practice constraints add—especially for Parkinson’s disease?

## Methods

Clinical narrative update (neurology). Reviews licensed indications, RCTs/meta-analyses by disease, and the German BfArM accompanying survey after the 2017 “Cannabis as Medicine” law (published July 2022). Table 1 rates evidence (PD marked “+” / n/a in the extracted table fragment). No original systematic search protocol or total n of studies.

## Sample / data

Multiple neurological indications. PD: self-use prevalence cited 8.3–37% across surveys. Two meta-analyses: Thanabalasingam et al. 2021—15 studies including 6 RCTs; Urbi et al. 2022—18 studies including 5 RCTs. NMS-Nab: nabilone for PD non-motor symptoms (Peball et al.). Almeida et al. 2021 CBD for PD-RBD (negative); post hoc RLS also negative. BfArM: PD (ICD-10 G20.-) <1% of cannabinoid prescriptions, mostly dronabinol or Sativex, so PD cannot be analyzed further from that survey.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative clinical review / update (not an RCT).
- **Population / model:** Neurological patients (MS, epilepsy, pain, tics, PD, HD, ALS, etc.); PD subsection uses surveys, meta-analyses, and named RCTs.
- **n:** not reported as one review-wide n. PD meta-analyses: 15 studies (6 RCTs) and 18 studies (5 RCTs). Self-use 8.3–37%.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Medical cannabis flowers/extracts, THC, CBD, nabilone, Sativex, dronabinol versus placebo or usual care in cited studies.
- **Primary endpoint:** Disease-specific; PD motor MDS-UPDRS and non-motor (anxiety, sleep, RBD).
- **Primary result (PD):** Patients use cannabinoids for tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity, LID, freezing, pain, anxiety, sleep; inhaled THC-containing products preferred; some reduce anti-PD medication; many stop for ineffectiveness. AEs: dry mouth, dizziness, cognitive changes. Contrary to patient reports, two meta-analyses found no convincing evidence for various cannabis preparations on PD motor scores. Those MAs are limited by few RCTs and heterogeneous application and THC/CBD content (sometimes unknown, e.g. Lotan 2014). Nabilone (NMS-Nab): positive effects on anxiety and sleep; common mild AEs fatigue, drowsiness, dizziness, dry mouth, confusion. CBD did not benefit PD-RBD (Almeida 2021) or RLS (de Almeida 2023 post hoc). Unclear whether cannabinoids increase hallucination risk in PD. CBD antioxidant/anti-inflammatory effects reduced striatal neurodegeneration in animal models; “translation to humans has not yet been attempted.”
- **Cannot show:** Motor efficacy of medical cannabis in PD; BfArM-based PD effectiveness (too few prescriptions); human neuroprotection; a standardized product/dose.

## Key findings

- Reliable neurology approvals: Epidyolex CBD for Dravet/LGS/TSC seizures; Sativex for MS spasticity.
- Neurodegenerative/neuroimmune animal hope has not translated (Conclusions).
- German law allows prescription without RCTs if a “not entirely remote prospect” of benefit exists; authors note current evidence still allows reasonable use only in rare cases.
- Driving advice during THC titration is discussed (practice section).

## Limitations

- Narrative; PD MA heterogeneity of products.
- BfArM data descriptive only.
- PD <1% of prescriptions limits real-world German evidence.
- Animal neuroprotection not tested as translation in humans (authors’ wording).

## Relevance to our research question

High for clinical PD: meta-analyses negative on motor outcomes; nabilone signal on anxiety/sleep; CBD negative in RBD RCT; self-use common but often stopped.
