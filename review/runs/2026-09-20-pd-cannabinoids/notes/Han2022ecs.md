# Roles of the Cannabinoid System in the Basal Ganglia in Parkinson’s Disease

- **Citation:** Wang M, Liu H, Ma Z. Front. Cell. Neurosci. 2022;16:832854. doi:10.3389/fncel.2022.832854
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids-gapfill/Han2022ecs.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20
- **Gap-fill:** g4 — basal-ganglia endocannabinoid circuit map to interpret CB1 PET and CB2 preclinical papers

## Research question
How do endocannabinoids, CB1/CB2 and related receptors, and eCB-metabolizing enzymes act in basal-ganglia circuits relevant to Parkinson’s disease, and what does that imply for cannabinoid pharmacology?

## Methods
Narrative physiology review (no PRISMA n). Synthesizes eCB synthesis/degradation (AEA, 2-AG, FAAH, MAGL), receptor localization (CB1 on corticostriatal terminals and MSNs; CB2 more inducible on glia/periphery), interactions with dopamine/glutamate/GABA, and cannabinoid analogues in PD models.

## Sample / data
Not a primary dataset. Preclinical PD models (6-OHDA, MPTP) and human imaging/biochemistry as cited by the authors.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Narrative review of basal-ganglia endocannabinoid physiology in PD
- **Population / model:** Basal-ganglia circuits in PD and toxin/genetic models (as cited)
- **n:** not reported (review)
- **Intervention / comparator:** Modulation of eCB tone, CB1, CB2, TRPV1, GPR55, PPAR vs untreated PD pathophysiology
- **Primary endpoint:** Circuit-level account of motor and inflammatory cannabinoid actions
- **Primary result:** Authors argue that altering CB receptor activation by modulating eCB levels can have anti-movement-disorder effects, but eCB regulation is complex because CB1/CB2 functions diverge and dopamine, glutamate, and GABA signals interact with cannabinoid signaling in the same nuclei. Both CB1 agonists and antagonists have been reported to modify levodopa-induced dyskinesia in models, illustrating bidirectional pharmacology.
- **Cannot show:** A single recommended PD cannabinoid dose or receptor; new human RCT data.

## Key findings
- AEA and 2-AG are the principal eCBs; FAAH and MAGL are major hydrolases (Introduction/sections on metabolism).
- CB1 is abundant in striatum/GP/SNr; CB2 is more inflammation-linked (receptor sections).
- Authors flag LID as a setting where cannabinoid drugs have inconsistent directions of effect.

## Limitations
- Narrative, not systematic; 2022 cutoff.
- Relies on heterogeneous animal models.

## Relevance to our research question
Provides the circuit vocabulary needed to interpret Ajalin CB1 PET (lower VT with PIGD/memory deficits) and Joers CB2 agonist data without treating CB1 and CB2 as interchangeable.
