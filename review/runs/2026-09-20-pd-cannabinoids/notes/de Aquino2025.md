# Therapeutic Potential and Challenges of Cannabis sativa in Neurological Disorders: Pathways to Integration into Health Systems

- **Citation:** de Aquino PEA, Xavier JMC, Fonteles MMF, Leal LKAM, Viana GSB. Rev Bras Plantas Med / Braz J Med Plants. 2025;27:e2025002. https://doi.org/10.70151/np6wdt13
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/de Aquino2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
What does the published literature say about Cannabis sativa / phytocannabinoids (THC, CBD, CBG) for neurological diseases (including Parkinson’s), and what would it take to integrate cannabis medicines into Brazil’s Unified Health System (SUS)?

## Methods
Narrative review. Databases: PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Web of Science, SciELO; no date restriction; Portuguese, Spanish, English. Keywords: Cannabis sativa, cannabinoids, CBD, THC, neurological diseases, Unified Health System, Brazil, regulation. Screening January–October 2024; tables used research from 2006 to October 2024. 150 articles screened → 55 used to build results tables → 42 cited in the discussion. No new experiment.

## Sample / data
Literature set: 55 articles tabulated (epilepsy, MS, PD, ALS, anxiety, etc.). PD-related rows include secondary citations (e.g., Ferreira-Junior 2020 animal p < 0.01; Patricio 2020 p < 0.05; Rieder 2020 mixed clinical significance). Those p-values belong to the *cited* papers, not to an analysis performed here.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** review (narrative literature review; journal labels the PDF “ORIGINAL ARTICLE” but Methods are a review protocol)
- **Population / model:** not applicable (human/animal primary data are those of cited studies)
- **n:** 150 records identified; 55 tabulated; 42 discussed — not a patient or animal n
- **Intervention / comparator:** not applicable; describes THC vs CBD properties and regulatory options (e.g., Brazilian Bill No. 399/2015)
- **Primary endpoint:** not applicable (synthesis of pharmacological, preclinical, clinical, and policy literature)
- **Primary result:** Authors conclude C. sativa components, especially THC and CBD, have therapeutic potential across neurological conditions; CBD is framed as non-psychoactive with anti-inflammatory, anxiolytic, and anticonvulsant effects. For PD they summarize cited work as neuroprotective/motor signals in animals (reported p < 0.05 or p < 0.01 in their table) and mixed clinical CBD evidence (“some studies show significance, others do not”). No pooled effect size. SUS inclusion is described as facing regulation/production barriers.
- **Cannot show:** original efficacy of cannabinoids in PD or toxin models; this paper did not generate new n, p, or dose–response data.

## Key findings
- Guiding question is dual: neurological therapeutics *and* Brazilian health-system implementation (Methodology).
- PD is listed among target conditions in the Abstract/Introduction alongside epilepsy and multiple sclerosis.
- Table entries on PD are second-hand (biological exploration / evidence review / clinical discussion of CBD), not a systematic PD meta-analysis.
- Policy: Farmácia Viva / Anvisa / Bill 399/2015 discussed as pathways, not trial outcomes.

## Limitations
- Narrative, not PRISMA systematic review; no quality scoring of PD trials.
- Header “ORIGINAL ARTICLE” vs review methods.
- p-values in tables are imported from other papers and cannot be re-checked here without those sources.
- Heavy Brazil-SUS focus; limited critical appraisal of PD trial heterogeneity.

## Relevance to our research question
Background/policy review of cannabis in neurology, with PD as one mentioned indication. Useful for context, not as primary evidence of cannabinoids in PD toxin models.
