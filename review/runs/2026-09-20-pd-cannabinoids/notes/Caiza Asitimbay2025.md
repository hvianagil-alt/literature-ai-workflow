# An Examination of the Application of Cannabis in Therapeutic Use for Parkinson’s syndrome

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Caiza Asitimbay MA, Kapoor T, Maqsood R, Reddy P, Sahoo LK, Aggarwal P, Lyngdoh NNK. Health Leadership and Quality of Life. 2025; 4:772. doi:10.56294/hl2025772. Bilingual Spanish/English original article.
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Caiza Asitimbay2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
The stated aims are to evaluate cannabis use in Parkinson’s disease (PD) and multiple sclerosis (MS) and to compare self-reported neurological impairment (emotional variability, cognitive function, fatigue, physiological weight) between cannabis-using groups. The English abstract also frames a survey of demographics, physical activity, neurological function, and fatigue.

## Methods
The text mixes a survey of 900 respondents with pre/post “three weeks of cannabis-based therapy” analyses. Authors report linear regression, hierarchical regression, and between-subjects two-way ANCOVA (SPSS 25), with ANCOVA adjusted for baseline pre-test scores. Group 1 = MS (n = 450); Group 2 = PD (n = 450). Outcomes are Likert-type self-report scales (20 items mentioned for daily activities). Sampling frame, cannabis dose, product, THC/CBD ratio, and blinding are not described in usable detail. One Methods sentence refers to “cloud-based healthcare adoption,” which does not match the cannabis/PD aims (internal inconsistency).

## Sample / data
N = 900 (MS 450, PD 450). Table 1: PD group 260 male / 190 female; age bands 18–35 years n = 130 and 36–55 n = 320 in the PD group (no ≥56 band tabulated). Smoking, BMI, education, and employment also tabulated. How PD/MS diagnoses were verified is not stated.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Unclear mixed survey / pre–post observational analysis (authors label ORIGINAL); not a randomized clinical trial. Methods quality is poor and internally inconsistent.
- **Population / model:** Self-identified or survey-classified MS (Group 1) and PD (Group 2); cannabis users. Not restricted to clinically confirmed PD.
- **n:** 900 total; PD subgroup n = 450 as tabulated.
- **Intervention / comparator:** “Cannabis-based therapy” over three weeks (Discussion/Results narrative) vs pre-test; no placebo described. Dose/formulation not reported.
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in self-reported Emotional Variability, Cognitive Function, Fatigue, and Physiological Weight (Likert means); ANCOVA F and p.
- **Primary result (PD Group 2, ANCOVA Table 4):** Emotional variability pre 3.50 (SD 0.85) to post 3.10 (0.80); adjusted mean difference −0.40; F = 6.12; p = 0.001. Cognitive function 2.80 (0.75) to 3.25 (0.70); difference +0.45; F = 5.20; p = 0.003. Fatigue 3.35 (0.90) to 2.95 (0.85); difference −0.40; F = 6.30; p = 0.001. Physiological weight 2.80 (0.70) to 3.10 (0.65); difference +0.30; F = 4.25; p = 0.009. Hierarchical post-test p-values for Group 2 were <0.001 (emotional, cognitive, fatigue) and 0.005 (weight) (Table 3). Linear-regression “odds ratios” for Group 2 post-test are tabulated (e.g., emotional variability post-test OR 3.10, p = 0.001) but the metric is not a standard clinical endpoint.
- **Cannot show:** Causal benefit of cannabis in PD; MS vs PD treatment effects with a valid RCT design; motor UPDRS outcomes; a defined cannabinoid product. Demographic age bands for “PD” (many 18–35) are not compatible with typical PD epidemiology, so clinical PD inference is not supported.

## Key findings
- Abstract: current cannabis consumers were younger and less often considered obese; users reported lower disability on emotional variability, cognitive function, and fatigue (Abstract)—this user vs non-user contrast is not clearly separated from the pre/post tables.
- Authors conclude cannabis “may benefit” PD and MS in those domains but call for longitudinal clinically measured assessments (Abstract).
- Paper is bilingual; Spanish abstract matches the 900-respondent claim.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Authors: cannot infer causality; need longitudinal clinical measures; survey to guide future RCTs, not to conclude effectiveness (Discussion/Conclusions).
- Evident: no dose, product, or diagnostic verification; possible placebo; methods text appears copy-pasted (cloud adoption); PD age structure implausible; “odds ratios” in a linear-regression table; Likert “physiological weight” is not body mass.
- Low percentage with a medical cannabis card; much self-medication (Conclusions).

## Relevance to our research question
Nominally a clinical/survey paper on therapeutic cannabis in PD (and MS). Relevance is limited by uninterpretable methods and likely non-PD age mix. Do not treat Table 4 p-values as high-quality PD trial evidence.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Whether this venue paper is a legitimate reanalysis of a prior MS/PD cannabis web survey (references include web-based PD/MS cannabis studies) versus generated/garbled text.
- Confirm PD diagnosis criteria and age—Table 1 conflicts with known PD demographics.
- Translation: Spanish and English abstracts agree on n = 900 and direction of self-reported disability, not on a specific milligram dose.
