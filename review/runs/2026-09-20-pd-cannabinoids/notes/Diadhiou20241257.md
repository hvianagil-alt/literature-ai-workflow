# Natural Health Products for Symptomatic Relief of Parkinson’s Disease: Prevalence, Interest, and Awareness

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Diadhiou S, Maas BR, Schootemeijer S, Bloem BR, de Vries NM, Calon F, Darweesh SKL, de Rus Jacquet A. Journal of Parkinson’s Disease 14 (2024) 1257–1264. doi:10.3233/JPD-240102
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Diadhiou20241257.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Among people with Parkinson’s disease (PD) in a population-based Dutch cohort, what is the prevalence of natural health product (NHP) use for PD symptoms (including cannabis), interest in NHPs, awareness of herb–drug interactions, and discussion with PD clinicians?

## Methods
Cross-sectional 4-item survey embedded in PRIME-NL (Radboudumc ethics 2019-5618). Eleven herbal products listed (availability in the Netherlands and possible motor/non-motor effects) plus “other.” Items asked only about use to alleviate PD-related symptoms (not recreational). Descriptive statistics; logistic regression for determinants of NHP use; sensitivity assuming non-responders were non-users. H&Y from self-administered items; education ISCED collapsed to three levels.

## Sample / data
566 invited; 367 (65%) responded. Mean age 70.3 ± 8.1 years; disease duration 7.1 ± 4 years; 201 men, 166 women. Participants vs 199 non-participants: more women (p = 0.005) and shorter disease duration (p < 0.001); similar age, education, H&Y.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Cross-sectional 4-item survey nested in a population-based PD cohort.
- **Population / model:** People with PD in the Netherlands (PRIME-NL).
- **n:** 367 respondents (of 566 invited).
- **Intervention / comparator:** None; self-reported ever-use of listed NHPs vs non-use.
- **Primary endpoint:** Prevalence of ever NHP use for PD symptoms; interest; awareness of interactions; discussion with neurologist/PD nurse. Cannabis is one listed product, not the sole primary endpoint.
- **Primary result:** 36% (132/367) ever used ≥1 listed/other NHP for PD symptoms. Coffee 15.8% (58/367); cannabis 13.1% (48/367); both coffee and cannabis 4.9% (18/367); turmeric 10%; velvet bean 7%; chamomile 5% (Table 2 / Results). Conservative sensitivity (none of 199 non-responders used NHPs): 23% (132/566). NHP use associated with younger age (OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.94–0.99), not gender (OR 1.25, 0.75–1.96), education (1.17, 0.95–1.47), disease duration (0.99, 0.93–1.05), or H&Y (1.16, 0.93–1.44). 71% interested in learning more about NHPs. Among NHP users, 39% (n = 51) aware of possible interaction with PD medication (women > men, p = 0.049); 39% had discussed use with a PD professional. Velvet-bean users: 76% had discussed herbal use.
- **Cannot show:** Efficacy or safety of cannabis (or other NHPs) for PD; dose, THC:CBD ratio, or route; causal determinants beyond associations.

## Key findings
- Cannabis was the second most reported NHP after coffee (Results).
- Cannabis gender split: men 10.4% (21/201), women 16.3% (27/166), p = 0.10 (Table 2).
- Authors compare 13% cannabis use with 11% in Norway and 25% in a US survey (Discussion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Optional survey, 65% response; possible interest bias (Limitations).
- Gender imbalance vs non-responders (Limitations).
- Four items cannot capture frequency, duration, dose, formulation, target symptoms, or cannabis THC/CBD ratio and inhaled vs ingested route (Limitations).
- Self-reported H&Y.

## Relevance to our research question
Survey evidence that 13.1% (48/367) of a Dutch PD cohort used cannabis specifically to relieve PD symptoms, with low disclosure and low interaction awareness among NHP users. Not a clinical outcome or PET study.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Cannabis product composition is unknown by design.
- 36% NHP use includes many non-cannabinoid products; do not treat 36% as cannabis prevalence.
