# Advice to People with Parkinson’s in My Clinic: Cannabis

- **Citation:** de Freitas MET, Fox SH. Advice to People with Parkinson’s in My Clinic: Cannabis. Journal of Parkinson’s Disease. 2024;14(4):873–881. doi:10.3233/JPD-230358
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/De Freitas2024873.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

What can clinicians tell people with Parkinson’s disease (PD) about cannabis products for motor and non-motor symptoms, given current preclinical and clinical evidence, product variability, and harms?

## Methods

Clinic-oriented narrative review / clinical update (not a systematic review). Synthesizes cannabinoid pharmacology and licensed synthetics (nabilone, dronabinol, nabiximols), preclinical basal-ganglia/neuroprotection literature, patient surveys, open-label studies, RCTs, and a cited meta-analysis of three RCTs on UPDRS III. Ends with Box 1 recommendations. No database search dates or included-study n.

## Sample / data

PD-focused. Survey examples: 1,373 people with PD (cannabis ratio known in 86.7%; 54.6% higher CBD, 30.2% higher Δ9-THC, 15.2% similar); 261 PD cannabis users (66% used for tremor; 23% stopped in prior 6 months); Norway survey n=530 (sleep 52.5% and pain 37% perceived benefits). RCTs/open-label: meta-analysis of 3 RCTs on UPDRS III; 2 small open-label inhaled cannabis; open-label CBD up to 25 mg/kg/day; LID trials of THC/CBD or CBD 75/300 mg; nabilone dyskinesia n=7 single-dose then n=38 over 4 weeks; nabilone NMS n=38; RBD CBD 75–300 mg 14 weeks; phase-1b pain oils n=8; cognitive RCT high-dose CBD 100 mg + low Δ9-THC 3.3 mg n=29. Umbrella review of 101 meta-analyses (multiple indications) cited for safety.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative clinical review of cannabis in PD (not an RCT).
- **Population / model:** People with PD (surveys, open-label, RCTs) plus PD animal models for mechanism.
- **n:** not reported as a single included-study count. Specific cited samples as above.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Variable cannabis products (smoked/vaped, oils, CBD, Δ9-THC, nabiximols, nabilone) versus placebo, baseline, or usual care.
- **Primary endpoint:** Motor (UPDRS III, tremor, LID) and non-motor (pain, insomnia, anxiety, RBD) plus adverse effects.
- **Primary result:** Surveys: subjective tremor benefit, no subjective benefit on other motor issues including dyskinesia; higher Δ9-THC more often reported beneficial (tremor OR 1.7, 95% CI 1.17–2.57, p=0.01 vs similar THC/CBD in the 1,373-person survey). Meta-analysis of 3 RCTs: small non-significant improvement in UPDRS III vs placebo. Two small open-label inhaled-cannabis trials: UPDRS III improved within 30 min. Open-label CBD: UPDRS III improved vs baseline over 2–4 weeks. Three small RCTs: no LID improvement with CBD/THC-containing products. Nabilone: small LID improvement in 7-subject single-dose crossover; no significant motor change over 4 weeks in 38 patients. Nabilone 0.25 mg daily to 1 mg twice daily: efficacy on UPDRS non-motor subscale, particularly sleep/insomnia/fatigue (n=38). CBD 75–300 mg for 14 weeks in RBD: better sleep satisfaction, no difference vs placebo in nights with RBD or RLS burden. Pain: VAS reduction after 18:0 THC/CBD oil (n=8) and after smoking/vaping in prior studies. Authors do not endorse cannabis for motor symptoms; may consider for insomnia, anxiety, and pain after other options fail. Parkinson Foundation does not endorse medical cannabis in PD.
- **Cannot show:** High-level RCT proof of motor efficacy; standardized dose or THC:CBD ratio; that survey benefits survive placebo control; disease modification. Legalization is described as making large placebo-controlled trials less feasible.

## Key findings

- CB1 is concentrated in basal ganglia; Δ9-THC partial CB1/CB2 agonist; CBD low-affinity negative allosteric CB1 modulator with anti-inflammatory/antioxidant potential.
- Licensed synthetics: nabilone/dronabinol (CINV/AIDS anorexia), nabiximols (spasticity)—not PD-licensed; side effects include orthostatic hypotension, psychosis, dizziness.
- Preclinical: CB1 agonists and antagonists both reported to reduce LID—pharmacology is complex; little evidence cannabinoid agonists/antagonists are antiparkinsonian per se; a rodent meta-analysis suggested bradykinesia/postural instability benefits of unclear clinical applicability; no validated PD rest-tremor model.
- Common AEs: abdominal pain, asthenia, blurred vision, constipation, drowsiness, dry mouth, headache, palpitations, orthostatic hypotension, psychosis, vertigo.
- Hallucinations in surveys: 425/1881 respondents, slightly more with Δ9-THC than CBD; small RCTs did not show increased PD psychosis. Cognitive RCT: CBD/THC worse verbal fluency and concentration vs placebo; AEs ≥2× placebo.
- Advise against smoking/vaping; caution in cognitive impairment, behavioral disorders, orthostatic hypotension, daytime sleepiness; addiction/respiratory risk.

## Limitations

- Not systematic; product and dose heterogeneity.
- Heavy reliance on biased surveys.
- Small, underpowered RCTs and open-label designs (type I/II error).
- Benefit may wane after a few weeks (survey finding).
- Unregulated personal-use products vary in THC/CBD.

## Relevance to our research question

Core clinical PD-cannabis review: the most directly clinic-facing synthesis among this set. PD-specific conclusions are explicit and conservative on motor outcomes.
