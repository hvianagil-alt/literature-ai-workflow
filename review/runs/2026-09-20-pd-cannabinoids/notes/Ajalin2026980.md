# Deficits in episodic memory and executive functions in relation to CB1 receptor availability in Parkinson's disease

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Ajalin RM, Al-Abdulrasul H, Tuisku JM, Karrasch M, Hirvonen J, Kirjavainen AK, Zetterberg H, Rinne JO, Brück AE. Journal of Parkinson’s Disease. 2026. doi:10.1177/1877718X261457830
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Ajalin2026980.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Is cerebral cannabinoid receptor type 1 (CB1R) availability, measured with [18F]FMPEP-d2 PET, related to neuropsychological test performance (especially episodic memory and executive function) in Parkinson’s disease (PD)?

## Methods
Cross-sectional [18F]FMPEP-d2 PET (volume of distribution VT) plus a standardized neuropsychological battery (episodic and working memory, executive function, language, visuospatial function, attention). Voxel-wise multiple regression of VT vs test scores with LEDD as covariate; FDR at voxel level p < 0.01 (Benjamini–Hochberg q < 0.05). UK Brain Bank PD plus [18F]FDOPA PET confirmation. Patients remained on usual antiparkinsonian medication; none used cannabis or hormone replacement. Related to the authors’ prior PD vs control CB1R PET work.

## Sample / data
23 people with PD (16 male / 7 female); Table 1 also lists healthy controls 4/5 but cognitive–CB1R correlations are reported for PD. PD age 65 ± 6 years (53–75); disease duration 7.7 ± 4.5 years (2–18); H&Y 2.5 (1.5–3); UPDRS-III 23.6 ± 8.5 (8–39); LEDD 555 ± 274 mg; injected activity 196 ± 10 MBq. Three of 23 met PD-MCI; group PD-MCI vs PD-NC analyses were not done. CSF AD biomarkers in 15/23 (five with two or three abnormal biomarkers).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Cross-sectional observational PET–neuropsychology correlation study (not an intervention trial).
- **Population / model:** Idiopathic PD on standard dopaminergic medication; no cannabis products.
- **n:** 23 PD (PET and cognition); HC demographics in Table 1 (9) were not the primary correlation sample.
- **Intervention / comparator:** None (imaging/biomarker); LEDD covariate; no cannabinoid drug given.
- **Primary endpoint:** Voxel-wise association of [18F]FMPEP-d2 VT with cognitive domain scores (FDR p < 0.01).
- **Primary result:** Poorer episodic memory associated with lower CB1R availability: WMS-R Logical memory I, two clusters (p < 0.001, Tpeak = 5.07, kE = 166177, q = 0.006; p = 0.007, Tpeak = 4.75, kE = 42916, q = 0.008) in thalami, temporal, cingulate, orbitofrontal, visual cortices; Logical memory II one cluster (p < 0.001, Tpeak = 5.12, kE = 288007, q = 0.002); CERAD word-list learning (p = 0.01, Tpeak = 4.03, kE = 54481, q = 0.01). Poorer executive function (TMT B–A) associated with lower VT (p < 0.001, Tpeak = 5.11, kE = 229035, q = 0.004) in insulae, caudate, thalami, brainstem, lingual gyri, predominantly right temporal cortex. No relationship with language, visuospatial function, or working memory (Abstract/Results). All listed results remained significant after FDR; partial-volume correction essentially replicated findings.
- **Cannot show:** That CB1R loss causes cognitive decline; treatment effects of cannabinoids; prediction of dementia (cross-sectional; authors state they cannot conclude CB1R predicts decline without follow-up).

## Key findings
- None of the neuropsychological assessments showed significant group differences between PD and HC; cognition of the PD sample was still relatively good (Discussion).
- TMT B–A had one extreme outlier (132, z > 3) winsorized to 83; without winsorization the cluster was larger (p < 0.001, Tpeak = 6.23, kE = 397421) (Results).
- Analyses excluding two escitalopram users were essentially unchanged (Discussion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Relatively small sample (Discussion).
- Cross-sectional; cannot infer prediction of decline (Conclusions).
- Possible concomitant AD pathology in a CSF subset (5/15) (Discussion).
- All imaging/testing ON medication (LEDD covaried).
- PD-MCI n = 3 precluded subgroup analysis.

## Relevance to our research question
In vivo CB1 PET in PD linking lower receptor availability to episodic memory and executive deficits. Relevant to cannabinoid-system pathophysiology, not a clinical cannabinoid treatment study.
