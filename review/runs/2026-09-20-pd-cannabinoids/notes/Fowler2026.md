# Cannabidiol and other non-psychotropic cannabinoids from Cannabis sativa as therapeutics for microglial-mediated neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration

- **Citation:** Fowler DK, Savage TM, Mackie DI. Cannabidiol and other non-psychotropic cannabinoids from Cannabis sativa as therapeutics for microglial-mediated neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration. Journal of Cannabis Research. 2026;8:82. doi:10.1186/s42238-026-00445-5
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Fowler2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

How do non-psychotropic phytocannabinoids (CBD, CBG, CBC and varin/acidic analogs) act on microglia and neuroinflammation, and what is the preclinical and clinical evidence in AD, PD, MS, and HD?

## Methods

Narrative mechanistic review: biosynthesis/genetics of non-psychotropic cannabinoids; microglial molecular targets; then disease sections with tabulated preclinical models (Table 1) and clinical trials (Table 2) of non-psychotropic phytocannabinoids in AD, PD, MS, HD. No PRISMA search dates or total included n. Authors note they found only two PD preclinical studies that directly assayed microglia after non-psychotropic phytocannabinoids.

## Sample / data

Four diseases. PD preclinical: MPTP (indirect inflammation markers, no direct microglia), rotenone rat nanodelivery CBD (Iba1), 6-OHDA CBD (microglial morphology), THCV in Pitx3ak LID (no microglia). PD clinical Table 2 examples: CBD 75 or 300 mg/day, 6 weeks, n=7 per group (Chagas); CBD-enriched sublingual product (Mitarnun); Epidiolex titration (Leehey); CBD titrated to 2.5 mg/kg, 3 weeks (NCT03582137); other CBD dose/duration rows including MTD up to 800 mg/day.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative review of non-psychotropic cannabinoids, microglia, and four neurodegenerative diseases (not an RCT).
- **Population / model:** Microglia-focused in vitro/in vivo models and human trials in AD, PD, MS, HD.
- **n:** not reported as a total included-study count. PD microglia-direct preclinical studies: two (as stated). PD clinical: “several recent” CBD trials listed in Table 2.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Non-psychotropic phytocannabinoids (almost entirely CBD in PD; THCV once in LID mice) versus disease models or placebo.
- **Primary endpoint:** Microglial activation/phenotype and clinical motor/cognitive/QoL scales (UPDRS, PDQ-39, MoCA, liver enzymes).
- **Primary result (PD):** MPTP+CBD: better function, lower TNF-α/IL-1β/IL-6 and NLRP3/caspase-1/IL-1β, higher IL-10; microglia not directly measured (Wang et al., 2022a). Rotenone rat: CBDne 5 mg/kg p.o. reduced striatal/duodenal α-synuclein, attenuated iNOS/nNOS/NO, normalized Iba1 (Dos Santos et al., 2025). CBD 10 mg/kg i.p. in 6-OHDA: attenuated striatal terminal and dopaminergic cell loss, increased microglial endpoints, did not alter CD11b+ density or CD32/inflammatory polarization (details truncated mid-sentence in extract). THCV 2 mg/kg i.p. reduced LID in Pitx3ak mice without microglia assays (Espadas et al., 2020). Clinical: “acute CBD at liver-safe doses generally does not appear to be effective at treating PD motor and cognitive symptoms.” Chagas 2014: 21 PD, CBD 300 mg/day 6 weeks (n=7) no UPDRS/BDNF/MRS benefit; PDQ-39 QoL improved. Mitarnun 2025: 12-week sublingual ~26 mg CBD/day in 27 treated PD—no overall MoCA/UPDRS/mood/inflammatory-marker benefit vs placebo; naming improved; alkaline phosphatase rose. Leehey 2020 open-label Epidiolex 20–25 mg/kg/day: UPDRS improved; 5/13 high-dose patients had elevated cholestatic enzymes that resolved after stopping. Authors: unaware of PD clinical trials of non-psychotropic phytocannabinoids other than CBD.
- **Cannot show:** That CBD modifies microglial PD pathology in patients; motor efficacy of liver-safe CBD; effects of CBG/CBC in PD; a pooled clinical effect.

## Key findings

- Chronic microglia–α-synuclein–NLRP3 signaling is framed as a PD driver.
- Translational limits: poor/variable oral bioavailability; in vitro micromolar potencies vs clinical plasma levels; LPS-microglia models may poorly match PD; metabolites understudied.
- Outside CBD (and limited CBG in HD), microglial pharmacology of other phytocannabinoids is “profoundly incomplete.”
- Industry affiliations disclosed (cannabis manufacturer funding).

## Limitations

- Narrative; Table 2 is a trial catalog, not a meta-analysis.
- PD microglia evidence is two animal papers.
- Clinical PD rows are small and mixed open-label/RCT.
- Extract cuts one 6-OHDA microglial-phenotype sentence.

## Relevance to our research question

Relevant for PD neuroinflammation mechanism and for a blunt clinical statement that liver-safe CBD generally fails motor/cognitive endpoints, with open-label high-dose motor signals offset by cholestatic enzyme elevations.
