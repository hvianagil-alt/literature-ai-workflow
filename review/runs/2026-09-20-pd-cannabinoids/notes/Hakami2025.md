# Therapeutic potential of cannabinoids in neurological conditions: a systematic review of clinical trials

- **Citation:** Hakami AY, Alshehri FS. Therapeutic potential of cannabinoids in neurological conditions: a systematic review of clinical trials. Frontiers in Pharmacology. 2025;16:1521792. doi:10.3389/fphar.2025.1521792
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Hakami2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

What completed ClinicalTrials.gov trials of cannabinoids (focus CBD and THC) in neurological conditions show regarding efficacy, safety, phases, and outcome measures—including Parkinson’s disease among the most studied conditions?

## Methods

Systematic review of ClinicalTrials.gov only (not PubMed full texts). Initial 507,934 trials; 67,640 neurological; 132 cannabinoid interventions; 47 completed Phase 1–4 trials with cannabinoids as primary intervention and relevant outcomes (pain, cognition, spasticity, etc.). Filters: completed; phases 1–4; search terms included “cannabinoids”, “neurological disorders”, “Multiple Sclerosis”, “Parkinson’s Disease”. Comparative analysis emphasized 13 CBD/THC trials (~27% of sample). Cochrane RoB assessed per included study. No meta-analysis of PD endpoints. Registration: clinicatrials.gov (as printed).

## Sample / data

47 completed trials. Most common conditions listed: psychomotor impairment, Parkinson’s disease, fibromyalgia. Most common interventions: nabilone, dronabinol, CBD oil, THC. Phases: Phase 2 = 18 (38.3%); Phase 3 = 7 (14.9%); phase not available 13 (27.6%); Phase 1 = 3; Phase 1/2 = 4; Phase 2/3 = 1; Phase 4 = 1. Interventional 45 (95.7%). Adults and older adults 38 (80.9%). Duration 4 weeks to 2 years. Table 3 RoB includes two rows “Nabilone for Non-motor Symptoms in Parkinson’s Disease.” Authors say although nabilone and dronabinol were in the dataset, analysis centered on CBD and THC.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Systematic review of completed ClinicalTrials.gov cannabinoid trials in neurology (not an RCT; not a full-text PD efficacy meta-analysis).
- **Population / model:** Human clinical trial participants with neurological conditions, including PD.
- **n:** 47 trials included. Number of PD-only trials is not stated as an integer (PD is among “most common conditions”). 13 trials in the CBD vs THC comparison subset.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Cannabinoids (CBD, THC, and in the registry nabilone/dronabinol) versus trial-defined comparators (often placebo); this review does not re-analyze patient-level outcomes.
- **Primary endpoint:** Registry primary outcomes—pain relief, tremor reduction, spasticity, cognitive function, safety/tolerability.
- **Primary result:** Cannabinoids have broad investigational use in neurology; most trials Phase 2; promising symptom-management signals claimed especially for MS and fibromyalgia. Standardized protocols, dosing, and outcome measures are lacking, limiting implementation. PD is a frequent trial indication and nabilone NMS-PD studies appear in RoB tables, but the paper does not report a PD-specific efficacy conclusion, UPDRS change, or count of PD trials with results. Common AEs across trial types: dizziness, dry mouth (and related).
- **Cannot show:** Whether cannabinoids improve PD motor or non-motor symptoms (no PD pooled result). Cannot replace full-text appraisal of PD RCTs. Cannot support nabilone/dronabinol efficacy because the authors de-centered those drugs despite listing them as common interventions. ClinicalTrials.gov-only sampling misses published trials not registered there or incomplete records.

## Limitations

- Registry-based; “secondary data analysis.”
- No quantitative PD synthesis despite PD being a top condition.
- Focus on CBD/THC while nabilone is both a common intervention and present in PD NMS trials.
- No search date for the ClinicalTrials.gov pull in the extracted methods (completion filter only).
- Typographical issues (clinicatrials.gov; “efﬁcacy”).

## Relevance to our research question

Relevant as a systematic map of clinical-trial activity: PD is a major indication for cannabinoid trials, including nabilone for non-motor symptoms, but this paper does not itself establish PD efficacy. Use for trial-landscape context, not for PD effect sizes.
