# CBD’s potential impact on Parkinson’s disease: An updated overview

- **Citation:** Hafida EG, Rachid S, Halima G, Najib K. CBD’s potential impact on Parkinson’s disease: An updated overview. Open Medicine (Poland). 2024;19(1):20241075. doi:10.1515/med-2024-1075
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Hafida2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

Does cannabidiol improve motor symptoms (including L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia) and non-motor symptoms of Parkinson’s disease, based on animal studies and clinical trials?

## Methods

PD-focused comprehensive narrative review compiling preclinical animal/cellular CBD studies and clinical trials on motor and non-motor PD. No named databases or search end-date in Methods (abstract: “compiles data”). Clinical evidence is tabulated by symptom (motor Table 1, pain Table 2, depression, sleep, psychosis, anxiety, QoL). Authors state they aim to present “essentially all” animal and clinical CBD–PD studies.

## Sample / data

Preclinical: 6-OHDA, MPTP, LID, catalepsy, reserpine, and later models (doses e.g. CBD 3 mg/kg 2 weeks 6-OHDA Lastres-Becker 2005; 5 mg/kg 5 weeks MPTP negative 2016; 15–60 mg/kg LID with capsazepine). Clinical: abstract says seven clinical trials to date. Motor subsection: four studies concluded no UPDRS motor-severity improvement. Pain: one Cannador study n=17. Depression: one trial, 10 depressed PD patients within n=33 RBD study, 14 weeks, no positive effect. Sleep: four CBD/cannabinoid studies. Anxiety: one RCT n=24, 300 mg CBD vs SPST (de Faria 2020). Open-label Epidiolex 5 to 20–25 mg/kg/day, 10–15 days, generally minor AEs.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative PD overview of CBD (not an RCT; not a registered systematic review).
- **Population / model:** PD animal models and people with PD.
- **n:** Seven clinical trials (abstract). Preclinical n not totaled. Motor clinical: four studies; sleep: four studies (as “according to our literature search”).
- **Intervention / comparator:** CBD (sometimes Cannador 2.5 mg THC + 1.25 mg CBD) versus placebo or lesion-only.
- **Primary endpoint:** Motor UPDRS; LID; pain, depression, sleep, psychosis, anxiety, quality of life.
- **Primary result:** Preclinical generally suggests CBD can alleviate PD-related motor/biochemical/cognitive features, but methods/doses vary; MPTP 5 mg/kg×5 weeks did not protect DA neurons/motor; CBD alone did not reduce LID unless combined with TRPV1 antagonist capsazepine 30 mg/kg (CB1/PPAR-dependent). Clinical motor: among trials so far, four concluded no improvement in UPDRS motor severity. Pain: Carroll 2004 Cannador n=17, no McGill pain benefit. Depression: no benefit in the one identified PD trial. Sleep: Carroll Cannador no sleep benefit; Chagas 2014 four PD-RBD patients rapid reduction in agitation/limb movements/nightmares with pure CBD; de Almeida RCT prolonged CBD no difference vs placebo on primary sleep outcomes; later phase II/III exploratory similar (negative). Anxiety: 300 mg pure CBD attenuated SPST-induced anxiety (de Faria 2020, n=24). Psychosis: CBD discussed as generally antipsychotic in broader literature; PD-specific trials are few/small (Zuardi-type open-label covered in tables). Abstract: preclinical promising; seven clinical trials insufficient for definitive efficacy. Clinical impact of cannabinoids on PD motor symptoms “still unclear.”
- **Cannot show:** Definitive clinical efficacy of CBD for PD motor or most non-motor domains; CBD-only pain trials in PD (authors note a lack); disease modification in humans; an optimal dose.

## Key findings

- PD prevalence cited: 4.5 million worldwide; 1% over age 60.
- ECS rearranges with DA depletion; CB1 down early, CB1/CB2 and eCB tone up in intermediate/late stages (animal and human studies cited).
- CBD: low CB1/CB2 affinity; FAAH blockade raising AEA; negative allosteric CB1; TRPV/dopamine D2 NAM discussed.
- Safety: after excluding pediatric epilepsy, diarrhea was the sole AE linked to CBD in a cited meta-analysis; elderly polypharmacy/CYP interactions emphasized.
- Epidiolex open-label PD: generally minor, none severe AEs in that study; clobazam interaction (3× active metabolite) flagged.

## Limitations

- No systematic search documentation.
- “Seven trials” vs “four motor studies” need care not to double-count overlapping trials across symptom tables.
- Mix of CBD isolate and THC+CBD (Cannador) under a CBD-focused title.
- Preclinical dose/model heterogeneity; one MPTP study negative.

## Relevance to our research question

Highest PD specificity in this review set: CBD-only PD overview with symptom-stratified clinical tables. Supports preclinical promise and clinical uncertainty, especially motor UPDRS nulls and mixed sleep/psychosis/anxiety signals.
