# Recent Preclinical Evidence on Phytocannabinoids in Neurodegenerative Disorders: A Focus on Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s Disease

- **Citation:** Blebea NM, Pușcașu C, Hancu G, Stăniguț AM, Chiriță C. Recent Preclinical Evidence on Phytocannabinoids in Neurodegenerative Disorders: A Focus on Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s Disease. Pharmaceuticals. 2025;18(6):890. doi:10.3390/ph18060890
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Blebea2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

The authors ask whether recent preclinical work supports phytocannabinoid (especially CBD and THC) modulation of the endocannabinoid system as a neuroprotective strategy in neurodegenerative disease, with a stated focus on Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and Parkinson’s disease (PD).

## Methods

Narrative review of phytocannabinoid pharmacology, cannabinoid receptors, and experimental AD/PD (plus briefer HD/ALS) models. The authors state they emphasize “the most relevant data from the past five years.” No database list, search date, PRISMA flow, or eligibility algorithm is reported. Findings are organized by disease (Section 4.2 for PD) with a tabulated summary of PD animal studies (Table 2). No meta-analysis.

## Sample / data

Secondary synthesis of published preclinical (animal and some cellular) studies of phytocannabinoids in AD and PD models (6-OHDA, MPTP, transgenic PD, pain/orofacial PD models). Number of included studies is not reported as a single n. Clinical PD trials are not the evidence base; the authors note a lack of quality clinical trials in Conclusions.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative review of preclinical phytocannabinoid studies (not an RCT; not a systematic review with a reported search).
- **Population / model:** Experimental AD and PD (and to a lesser extent HD/ALS) models; human PD mentioned mainly for ECS changes, not as trial outcomes.
- **n:** not reported (no count of included studies; Table 2 lists multiple PD animal experiments without a stated total).
- **Intervention / comparator:** Phytocannabinoids (CBD, THC, VCE-004.8, Δ9-THCV, and related compounds) versus untreated or lesion-only disease models; doses exemplified include CBD 10 mg/kg i.p. (6-OHDA) and CBD 100 mg/kg (MPTP).
- **Primary endpoint:** Neuroprotection, neuroinflammation, motor/cognitive behavior, and pain thresholds in PD models (review-level synthesis, not a pooled primary endpoint).
- **Primary result:** In PD models, chronic CBD (10 mg/kg i.p.) in 6-OHDA reduced nigrostriatal degeneration and neuroinflammation and improved motor function, attributed mainly to astrocytic TRPV1 and CNTF (Giuliano et al., as cited). Intrapallidal CBD reduced motor asymmetry and improved fine motor skills with reduced GAD-67 in striatum/GPe (GPR55-related). Acute and chronic CBD mitigated 6-OHDA hyperalgesia/allodynia via CB1/TRPV1 and AEA. THC improved motor and cognitive measures in 6-OHDA with D1R/CB receptor and PSD-95 changes. VCE-004.8 preserved TH+ neurons and suppressed astro-/microgliosis in 6-OHDA. CBD 100 mg/kg in MPTP improved cognition and locomotion, raised 5-HT, DA, and IL-10, lowered TNF-α/IL-1β/IL-6, and inhibited NLRP3. Δ9-THCV 2 mg/kg i.p. delayed and reduced L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia in Pitx3ak mice. Authors also state CB2 is upregulated in PD patients while CB1 levels appear stable, and that FAAH inhibitors and CB1 antagonists improve motor symptoms in PD patients (as cited from prior literature, not new data).
- **Cannot show:** Clinical efficacy, safety, or disease modification of phytocannabinoids in people with PD; pooled effect sizes; standardized dosing; or that preclinical motor/pain benefits translate to humans. The review cannot rank CBD vs THC vs other phytocannabinoids in patients.

## Key findings

- ECS dysregulation is presented as linked to AD, PD, HD, and ALS pathophysiology (Introduction; Conclusions).
- PD section (4.2): cannabinoids modulate basal ganglia GABA/glutamate via CB1/CB2 even though CB1 is not on dopaminergic neurons themselves.
- Pain is stated to affect ~60% of people with PD; 6-OHDA mice had lower thermal/mechanical thresholds that CBD reversed (as synthesized from cited experiments).
- Sex differences: female 6-OHDA rats showed greater orofacial allodynia/hyperalgesia; local CBD reversed pain in both sexes, with different dose sensitivity (Vivanco-Estela et al.).
- Conclusions: preclinical neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and neuromodulatory effects are described as consistent in AD/PD/HD models, largely via ECS plus TRPV1, 5-HT1A, and PPARs.
- Translation is limited by variable cannabinoid content, formulation PK, lack of standardized dosing, and few quality clinical trials.

## Limitations

- Narrative design without reported databases, dates, or inclusion counts.
- Focus is preclinical; human PD claims about ECS and antagonists are cited, not independently extracted as trials.
- Heterogeneous models, doses, and compounds (CBD, THC, VCE-004.8, THCV).
- HD/ALS preclinical literature is described as older and in need of updating.
- Authors cannot conclude efficacy or safety in humans.

## Relevance to our research question

Directly relevant as a 2025 neurodegenerative phytocannabinoid review with a dedicated PD preclinical synthesis. It supports mechanistic plausibility (neuroprotection, inflammation, pain, LID with THCV) but explicitly does not establish clinical PD benefit.
