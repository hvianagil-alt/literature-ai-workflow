## Research question

What is the current level of evidence for therapeutic CBD (and related cannabis literature) across mental and neurological disorders, including Parkinson’s disease, and what gaps remain before clinical implementation?

## Methods

Authors label it a narrative review in the Introduction but Methods (Section 8) follow PRISMA 2020 identification/selection. Search: ISI Web of Science, PubMed, ScienceDirect, Scopus; articles January 2000–November 2025; MeSH/free-text including cannabidiol and Parkinson’s disease. Manual reference screening. Included: original research, clinical trials, observational studies, relevant preclinical work. Excluded: editorials, conference abstracts, duplicates, insufficient methods. Titles/abstracts then full text screened. PRISMA flow is Figure 2 (counts are in the figure, not given as numbers in the extracted prose). No formal risk-of-bias assessment (explicitly because of broad heterogeneous overview, not quantitative synthesis).

## Sample / data

Preclinical and clinical CBD studies across anxiety/sleep, epilepsy, AD, PD, and emerging COVID-19 adjunct use. PD clinical and preclinical studies are summarized in Table 4. No numeric total of included papers in running text.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Narrative review with PRISMA-guided search (not an RCT; not a quantitative meta-analysis).
- **Population / model:** Mental and neurological disorders; PD patients in small pilots/RCTs and PD animal models (6-OHDA and others in Table 4).
- **n:** not reported as a single included-study count in the text (PRISMA diagram exists). PD examples given include inhaled cannabis n=22 (Lotan); open-label CBD psychosis n=6; CBD 75 or 300 mg/day n=7+7 for 6 weeks; Epidiolex dose-escalation 13 PD (10 completers).
- **Intervention / comparator:** CBD (various oral doses; Epidiolex) and some whole-cannabis/inhaled products versus placebo or baseline; preclinical CBD versus lesion models.
- **Primary endpoint:** PD motor (UPDRS) and non-motor (psychosis, anxiety, sleep, QoL) plus preclinical locomotion/neuroprotection.
- **Primary result (PD):** Lotan et al.: 22 PD patients, assessments before and 30 min after smoking cannabis: ~30% improvement in UPDRS motor subsection; tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia reduced; pain, sleep, well-being improved; no serious AEs (preliminary, inhaled cannabis not isolated CBD). Open-label CBD 150–400 mg/day added to antiparkinsonian therapy (n=6): reduced BPRS/PPQ psychosis without significant motor/cognitive change or serious AEs. CBD 75 vs 300 mg/day: authors note no group differences on some outcomes (details in Table 4; Chagas-type design). Leehey open-label Epidiolex 5 mg/kg/day to 20–25 mg/kg/day: total UPDRS improved 17.8%, motor subscale 24.7%; nighttime sleep improved; mostly mild AEs (diarrhea, somnolence, fatigue); 10 of 13 completed. Authors’ PD wrap-up: CBD generally well tolerated; possible benefits on psychosis, anxiety, sleep, emotional well-being, discomfort; moderate doses linked to QoL; high-dose CBD acceptable tolerability with preliminary motor improvement. Core motor evidence “limited and inconsistent.” Firm efficacy conclusions not possible because of small pilots/RCTs.
- **Cannot show:** Pooled PD efficacy; that smoked cannabis benefits equal purified CBD; long-term safety; optimal dose; superiority of CBD for core motor PD vs placebo in adequately powered RCTs.

## Key findings

- Strongest CBD clinical evidence remains drug-resistant epilepsy (Epidiolex RCTs).
- AD/PD/anxiety/sleep evidence is weaker, preclinical-heavy or small trials with heterogeneous protocols (Conclusions).
- CBD PK: peak 2–5 h oral; CYP2C19/CYP3A4; drug–drug interaction risk.
- Search window 2000–November 2025 is stated (Methods only).

## Limitations

- PRISMA without RoB or meta-analysis.
- Included observational and preclinical with clinical PD data.
- Inhaled cannabis (Lotan) mixed into a CBD-focused PD narrative.
- n of studies not in prose.
- Structured abstract reports “comprehensive literature search” without naming databases (those appear in Section 8).

## Relevance to our research question

High for a 2026 CBD mental/neurology review with a dedicated PD section and Table 4. PD takeaway matches other clinical reviews: tolerability and possible non-motor signals; motor evidence weak/inconsistent.
