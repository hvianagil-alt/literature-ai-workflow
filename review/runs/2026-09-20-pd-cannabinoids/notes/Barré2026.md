# Healthcare Providers’ Acceptability of Cannabis And Cannabidiol to Manage Parkinson’s Disease in France

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Barré T, Cazorla G, Di Beo V, Bianchi C, Baunez C, Carrieri P. Current Therapeutic Research 104 (2026) 100830. doi:10.1016/j.curtheres.2026.100830
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Barré2026.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
How acceptable is therapeutic cannabis (THC > 0.3%) versus CBD (THC ≤ 0.3%) for Parkinson’s disease (PD) among French healthcare providers who manage PD, and which factors (occupation, knowledge, perceived dependence) are associated with acceptability?

## Methods
Cross-sectional online survey, 16 September 2023–30 January 2024 (Voxco). Inclusion: healthcare provider in France actively managing PD patients (gate question). Disseminated via France Parkinson professional list (~5,000), regional unions, neurologist societies, website, snowballing. No IP duplicate check. Ethics: INSERM IRB00003888, CD/EB 23-045. Acceptability from two hypothetical questions (encourage if prescription-only; encourage if OTC) coded low / moderate / high. Binary logistic regression moderate/high vs low; backward selection then common covariate set; Hosmer–Lemeshow; ordinal logistic sensitivity. Knowledge score 0–4 dichotomized <4 vs 4 (56.9% scored 4; Cronbach’s α = 0.656).

## Sample / data
324 consented; 218 completed (79.4% women; median age 41 years, IQR 34–50). Occupations: 82 speech therapists, 58 physiotherapists, 45 physicians (42 neurologists), 21 nurses, and smaller groups (dieticians, OT, neuropsychology, pharmacy, psychology, psychomotor, naturopath). Physicians 20.6% (n = 45); nonphysicians n = 173. Response rate not estimable.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Cross-sectional provider survey (attitudes; not a treatment trial).
- **Population / model:** French healthcare professionals who report managing people with PD (convenience sample).
- **n:** 218 (45 physicians, 173 nonphysicians).
- **Intervention / comparator:** None; physician vs nonphysician occupation is the main contrast.
- **Primary endpoint:** Moderate/high vs low acceptability of cannabis and of CBD for PD.
- **Primary result:** Cannabis: low 27.5% (60/218), moderate 49.5% (108), high 22.9% (50). CBD: low 11.9% (26), moderate 36.7% (80), high 51.4% (112). Physicians more often low acceptability than nonphysicians (P < 0.001). Being a physician: cannabis aOR 0.13 (95% CI 0.06–0.28), P < 0.001 (87% lower odds); CBD aOR 0.10 (0.04–0.27), P < 0.001 (90% lower odds). Very serious perceived cannabis-dependence risk: cannabis aOR 0.26 (0.08–0.88), P = 0.031 (75% lower odds of moderate/high cannabis acceptability). Ordinal models: good cannabinoid knowledge associated with CBD acceptability 1.98 (1.13–3.48), P = 0.017, not cannabis (P = 0.126). Among physicians, primary barrier was lack of evidence of therapeutic effectiveness (Abstract).
- **Cannot show:** Whether providers would actually prescribe if legal; patient outcomes; nationally representative French provider opinion (unweighted convenience sample).

## Key findings
- Low cannabis acceptability: physicians 57.8% vs nonphysicians 19.7%; low CBD: 33.3% vs 6.4% (Table 1).
- 91.3% supported easing medical-cannabis legal restrictions absolutely or under conditions; only 25.7% supported easing nonmedical restrictions. Physicians less often “absolutely” in favor of easing medical restrictions (11.1% vs 33.5%, P = 0.038).
- 82.2% of physicians vs 93.6% of other providers supported easing medical-cannabis restrictions in some form (Discussion).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Modest n, especially physicians (n = 45); wide CIs (Strengths and limitations).
- Cross-sectional; no causal inference.
- Heterogeneous nonphysician group; disproportionate physician vs nonphysician n.
- Response rate unknown; no duplicate-response check; not weighted for representativeness.
- Residual confounding (physicians older, more male, less rural) (Discussion).
- Hypothetical “might you encourage” items, not observed prescribing.

## Relevance to our research question
Provider-side acceptability of cannabis/CBD for PD in France, complementary to Barré 2024 patient survey. Does not measure clinical efficacy or PET.
