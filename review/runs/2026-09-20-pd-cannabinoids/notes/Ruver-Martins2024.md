# Low doses of cannabis extract ameliorate non-motor symptoms of Parkinson’s disease patients: a case series

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Ruver-Martins AC, Martinez IAR, Holla VG, Souza BL, Silva EG, Novoa DMA, Souza-Silva E, Araujo FS, Prediger RD, Nascimento FP. Front. Hum. Neurosci. 18:1466438 (published 24 February 2025). doi:10.3389/fnhum.2024.1466438
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Ruver-Martins2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Do low oral doses of a THC-rich cannabis extract change cognition (MoCA), insomnia (ISI), and daytime sleepiness (ESS) over 90 days in people with moderate Parkinson’s disease (PD)?

## Methods
Prospective case series with 1:1 randomization to two extract doses (no placebo). Ethics: CAAE 87333418.9.0000.5515. Oral THC:CBD 250:28 μg/day (group 1, n = 3) or 1000:112 μg/day (group 2, n = 3). Assessments at baseline and days 15, 30, 60, and 90. HPLC of extract: THC 8.7% ± 0.5 m/m; CBD 0.75% ± 0.15. Statistics: Friedman repeated-measures test with Dunn post-hoc versus baseline (non-parametric because of small n).

## Sample / data
Six Brazilian adults with idiopathic PD, MDS-UPDRS Part III >20 in best ON, Hoehn and Yahr III–IV, age >40 years, stable levodopa ≥30 days. Four men and two women, ages 49–76 years, PD ≥5 years. Exclusions included schizophrenia/psychosis, epilepsy, first-degree relatives with those comorbidities, and any cannabis or illegal psychoactive use.

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Randomized two-dose case series (open-label; no placebo or untreated control).
- **Population / model:** Moderate–severe idiopathic PD (H&Y III–IV) on stable levodopa.
- **n:** 6 (3 per dose).
- **Intervention / comparator:** Oral cannabis extract THC:CBD 250:28 μg/day vs 1000:112 μg/day for 90 days; no placebo.
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in ISI (insomnia); also MoCA and ESS at 15, 30, 60, 90 days vs baseline.
- **Primary result:** Significant ISI improvement only for 1000:112 μg/day after 60 days (p = 0.03 vs baseline; Friedman + Dunn; Figure 2C; text also marks *p < 0.05). MoCA increased more in the 250:28 μg/day group but authors state no significant difference. ESS: no significant effect in either group. No significant adverse effects reported.
- **Cannot show:** Efficacy versus placebo; dose–response with adequate power; motor-scale change with statistics (motor comments are patient-reported).

## Key findings
- ISI decreased vs baseline in both groups descriptively; only group 2 at 60 days reached p = 0.03 (Results).
- All six patients wished to continue extract after 90 days (Results).
- Authors describe MoCA improvement over 90 days at both doses as a trend, not statistically significant (Results/Discussion).
- No self-reported relevant side effects (Results).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Sample size and lack of a control group; need for placebo-controlled trials (Discussion).
- Non-parametric analysis of n = 3 per arm.
- Motor “improvement” not quantified on MDS-UPDRS in the results text.
- Unblinded; self-reported sleep and qualitative interviews.

## Relevance to our research question
Clinical PD series of combined THC/CBD at microgram-range daily doses with a statistically reported insomnia signal at 60 days (higher dose). Relevant to cannabinoid non-motor outcomes; very small n.

## Open questions / things to verify
- Exact ISI/MoCA/ESS numeric means are in Figure 2, not tabulated as means in the extracted text—do not invent scale points.
- Randomization of six patients does not make this an RCT with a control.
