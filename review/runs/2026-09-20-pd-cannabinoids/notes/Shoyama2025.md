# Therapeutic Potential of the Cannabinoid System and Acupuncture in Neurodegenerative Brain Disorders: a Comprehensive Review

- **Citation:** Shoyama Y. Therapeutic Potential of the Cannabinoid System and Acupuncture in Neurodegenerative Brain Disorders: a Comprehensive Review. Innovations in Acupuncture and Medicine. 2025;18:11. doi:10.1186/s44424-025-00013-3
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Shoyama2025.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question

How do phytocannabinoids (THC/CBD) and electroacupuncture (EA), separately and together via CB1/CB2, relate to therapy of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and Parkinson’s disease (PD)?

## Methods

Single-author comprehensive/narrative review covering cannabis history and biosynthesis, CB1/CB2 and endocannabinoids, acupuncture/EA mechanisms, then EA–cannabinoid interactions and Kampo/Chinese medicine combinations. No databases, search dates, PRISMA, or included-study n. Abstract and conclusion assert in vitro, in vivo, and clinical support for cannabinoids in AD and PD (PD citations [101, 102] in the conclusion) and that EA acts in part through CB receptors. Combination of EA plus cannabinoids is described as “the first reported case of a synergistic therapeutic effect,” without a new clinical trial in this paper.

## Sample / data

Cited laboratory, animal, and selected clinical/meta-analytic literature on EA in PD (neurotransmitter, apoptosis, oxidative stress, α-synuclein, gut microbiota) and cannabinoids in AD/PD. PD clinical cannabinoid evidence is not extracted study-by-study in the body; conclusion cites prior systematic reviews/meta-analyses of cannabis in PD ([101, 102] in the reference list: e.g., effects of cannabis in PD SR/MA 2022; medical cannabis as alternative therapeutics SR). EA+Madopar scalp EA: significant motor improvement (Qiang et al.), with a call for larger samples.

## Claim-ready facts

- **Design:** Comprehensive narrative review (not RCT; no systematic search reported).
- **Population / model:** AD and PD (clinical and experimental); inflammatory-pain models for EA–CB2; PC-12 and Aβ mouse models for CBD/THC in dementia-related mechanisms.
- **n:** not reported.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Phytocannabinoids (CBD, THC) and/or electroacupuncture (points exemplified GB30/GB34 in pain studies; scalp EA + Madopar in PD) versus cited controls in source papers.
- **Primary endpoint:** Review-level: neuroprotection, motor function in PD, cognition in AD, CB1/CB2-mediated analgesia/inflammation.
- **Primary result (PD-specific):** EA is “often used” for PD; mechanisms listed include neurotransmitter modulation, anti-apoptosis, reduced oxidative stress, inflammatory cytokines, reduced α-synuclein, mitochondrial/UPS, autophagy, gut microbiota [77–80]. Patient surveys/clinical use of EA in PD are asserted (“adopted extensively in PD therapy”) without new n. Scalp EA plus traditional Chinese medicine/Madopar improved PD motor function in cited work [86]; authors say combination therapy “may be recommended” but larger experiments are needed. CBD is said to inhibit oxidative stress and tau phosphorylation, “both characteristic features of PD,” and may help AD patients without THC-like hallucinations. Conclusion: cannabinoids have shown therapeutic effects on PD in cited studies [101, 102]; EA efficacy in AD/PD is “primarily through the modulation of CB1 receptor activity”; combining cannabinoids and EA is claimed to surpass either alone. Abstract wording that EA “has been clinically Parkinson’s disease” is grammatically broken in the extracted text.
- **Cannot show:** That combining EA with cannabinoids is proven in a PD RCT; independent clinical effect sizes for CBD/THC in PD (those are deferred to cited SRs); disease modification; a search-based evidence map.

## Key findings

- EA or CB2 agonist AM1241 at GB30/GB34 reduced inflammatory hyperalgesia/allodynia and IL-1β/IL-6/TNF-α; CB2 antagonist AM630 abolished EA analgesia (Ho et al.; related CB2 work).
- PD is treated in this review partly as overlapping with dementia biology (explicit statement: “PD is considered a form of dementia”), which drives AD/PD mechanism mixing.
- Kampo plus acupuncture is suggested as possibly more effective than synthetic-drug combinations for PD [86, 95–99].

## Limitations

- No methods for study identification; citation-driven.
- Mechanistic claims for CBD in PD sometimes imported from AD/Aβ experiments (tau/oxidative stress).
- Synergy of EA + cannabinoids is asserted without presenting a PD trial of the combination in this paper.
- English in the abstract/PD sentences is occasionally corrupted, lowering confidence in a few phrases.

## Relevance to our research question

Peripheral: cannabinoids in PD are mentioned, but the unique contribution is EA–ECS linkage, not a clinical synthesis of CBD/THC/nabilone trials. Use for mechanism/context, not for PD cannabinoid efficacy ranking.

## Open questions / things to verify

- What [101] and [102] actually concluded (SR/MA of cannabis in PD)—this paper does not reproduce their numbers.
- Whether any human trial of EA plus a cannabinoid in PD is described (text emphasizes EA+Madopar/Chinese medicine more than EA+CBD).
- “PD as a form of dementia” is the author’s framing, not a standard nosology.
