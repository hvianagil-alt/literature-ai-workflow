# Long-term safety and efficacy of open-label nabilone on sleep and pain in Parkinson´s Disease

- **Citation (as given in the paper / filename):** Peball M, Heim B, Carbone F, Schorr O, Werkmann M, Ellmerer P, Marini K, Krismer F, Knaus HG, Poewe W, Djamshidian A, Seppi K. npj Parkinson’s Disease (2024) 10:61. doi:10.1038/s41531-024-00665-7
- **Source file:** papers/2026-09-20-pd-cannabinoids/Peball2024.txt
- **Extracted:** 2026-09-20

## Research question
Does open-label nabilone remain safe and continue to improve non-motor symptoms—especially sleep and pain—over six months in Parkinson’s disease (PD) patients who had already responded to nabilone in a prior randomized withdrawal trial?

## Methods
Single-center open-label extension (NMS-Nab2) of the preceding placebo-controlled, double-blind, enriched-enrollment randomized withdrawal NMS-Nab trial. Eligible patients were prior treatment responders who completed the double-blind phase without a drug-related serious, moderate, or severe adverse event (AE). Nabilone was re-titrated until overall non-motor symptom (NMS) burden improved, then continued for six months with clinic visits every 3 months (V1 after titration; V3 at 6 months). AEs were captured by self-report and clinical assessment. Efficacy used CGI-I, MDS-UPDRS, NMSS, King’s PD Pain Scale (KPPS), HADS, PDQ-8, ESS, FSS, QUIP-RS, MMSE, and MoCA. Analyses included drop-outs until discontinuation.

## Sample / data
Twenty-two patients enrolled (August 10, 2018–January 31, 2020); 1 non-responder discontinued before V1; 2 drop-outs; 19 completed six months. Analyses used n = 21 at screening (demographics Table 1) and n = 19 at V3 for change scores. Mean age 67.23 ± 6.15 years (median 68.33); 52% female (n = 11); mean PD duration 9.30 ± 6.04 years (median 7.42); mean LEDD 761 ± 637 mg (median 500) at screening/V1. Mean daily nabilone after titration: 0.87 ± 0.44 mg (median 0.75 mg).

## Claim-ready facts
- **Design:** Open-label extension of a prior RCT (enriched-enrollment randomized withdrawal); no concurrent placebo arm.
- **Population / model:** Adults with PD who were nabilone responders in NMS-Nab and completed double-blind treatment without qualifying drug-related AEs.
- **n:** 22 enrolled; 21 started open-label analysis set; 19 completed 6 months.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Oral nabilone, titrated then continued 6 months (mean 0.87 mg/day at V1); no placebo comparator in this extension; LEDD allowed to change (mean LEDD at V3 667 ± 521 mg, p = 0.943 vs screening).
- **Primary endpoint:** Long-term safety (AEs/SAEs); key efficacy: CGI-I NMS improvement and change from screening to V3 in sleep and pain scales (NMSS Domain 2, MDS-UPDRS 1.7, KPPS total, MDS-UPDRS 1.9).
- **Primary result:** Concentration difficulties were the most common treatment-related AE (possibly related n = 2, not related n = 1 between V1 and V3). Five SAEs in 4 patients, all rated unrelated to nabilone. CGI-I showed NMS improvement in 78.90% at V3 (21.10% worse vs screening; CGI V1 vs V3 p = 0.002). NMSS Domain-2 (sleep/fatigue) −8.26 points (95% CI −13.82 to −2.71; p = 0.004; ES = −0.72); MDS-UPDRS-1.7 (night-time sleep) −1.42 points (95% CI −2.16 to −0.68; p = 0.002; ES = −0.92); KPPS total −8.00 points (95% CI −15.05 to −0.95; p = 0.046; ES = −0.55); MDS-UPDRS-1.9 (pain) −0.74 points (95% CI −1.21 to −0.26; p = 0.008; ES = −0.74) (n = 19 at V3). MDS-UPDRS-I total −2.58 (95% CI −5.00 to −0.16; p = 0.052). MDS-UPDRS II/III and motor sum all p > 0.513.
- **Cannot show:** Causal efficacy versus placebo at 6 months; generalizability beyond prior nabilone responders; motor benefit.

## Key findings
- 19/22 completed 6 months; one drop-out for night-time sleep problems requiring prohibited brotizolam; one discontinued for worsening pre-existing MCI that did not resolve off drug (Results).
- No deaths, no SUSARs; C-SSRS suicidality did not occur (Safety results).
- No significant change MMSE (p = 0.298) or MoCA (p = 0.775) from NMS-Nab screening to V3 (n = 19).
- HADS-D improved V1 to V3 (−1.00 ± 2.08; 95% CI −2.00 to 0.00; p = 0.044); HADS-A, PDQ-8, ESS, FSS, QUIP-RS not significant (Table 3).
- Former nabilone vs former placebo groups did not differ on efficacy measures (all p > 0.427; Supplementary Table 4).

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, or evident from the methods)
- Small sample derived from prior trial; limited generalizability (Discussion).
- Pain/anxiety burden (MDS-UPDRS I item ≥2) was an inclusion criterion of NMS-Nab and may be overrepresented (Discussion).
- Open-label design; authors note placebo may influence pain results (Discussion).
- Enriched responder sample only.

## Relevance to our research question
Clinical PD evidence for a synthetic THC analog (CB1/CB2 partial agonist) on sleep and pain over 6 months, with systematic AE reporting. Directly relevant to cannabinoid clinical outcomes in PD; not PET or survey.
