# Synthesis rationale — EXAMPLE (fictional papers)

> Every paper named here is made up. Do not cite this file as research.

**Research question:** Does structured note-taking improve recall of technical material compared with unstructured note-taking?

**Included studies:** Fictional Al Researcher 2021; Fictional Bea Scholar 2022; Fictional Chen Example 2023; Fictional Dee Sample 2024.

**Not used as evidence:** Fictional Eli Notincluded 2020 (creative writing, out of scope).

## (a) What each included study actually measured

- **Al Researcher 2021** — between-subjects lab experiment; 1-week delayed recall; N=40 undergraduates; outline vs free-form notes.
- **Bea Scholar 2022** — mixed methods; STEM vs humanities as a moderator; N=85.
- **Chen Example 2023** — meta-analysis of 12 earlier studies (not including Al/Bea); aggregate N≈900; outcome is a pooled d.
- **Dee Sample 2024** — within-subjects digital vs paper structured notes; immediate and 1-week recall; N=52 graduate students.

## (b) Themes the data support vs themes that would be forced

**Supported:** a small positive recall effect in the same direction in three papers; domain as a possible moderator in one paper; medium (paper vs digital) tested in one paper.

**Would be forced:** “structured notes work for all students in all subjects” (Bea’s STEM null is a single-study observation; Chen’s pooled d averages domains).

## (c) Real disagreements and why

Bea 2022’s STEM null vs Chen 2023’s positive pooled effect — **population/endpoint mix**: Chen aggregates across domains and does not test the STEM split Bea was designed to detect.

## (d) What this sample cannot answer

Semester-long retention; K–12 learners; whether the STEM split replicates.

## (e) Outline of the review

**Kind:** narrative journal review (not a systematic review).

1. Introduction — delayed recall as the phenomenon; tools (outline, free-form, paper, screen); aim last.
2. Brief Methods.
3. Overall direction of the recall effect (Al, Chen, Dee), with **Table 1** (design, n, endpoint, result).
4. Domain as a moderator (Bea vs Chen).
5. What this literature still lacks.
6. Discussion / Conclusions.

## (f) Interpretation gaps

G1 — STEM split rests on one study.
